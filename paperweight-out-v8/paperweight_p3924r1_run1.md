Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin basis for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on an asserted distinction between declarations and non-declarations and a single national-body comment. The case is thinnest where it should be most concrete: it does not identify who is harmed, why the standard is the right place to act, or how the problem could be addressed outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the observation that some constructs, such as `struct S`, are not declarations even though they may be mistaken for them.
- The paper points to NB comment US 11-400 as a request for review, but does not develop that into a demonstrated need for normative change.
- The paper never establishes who is affected by the alleged misuse or what practical consequence follows.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, coordination analysis, or explanation of why a library or non-normative guidance would be insufficient.
