Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a solid foundation for why the problem is real and how its approach differs from existing work, but it does not yet make a complete case for standardization. The strongest material concerns motivation, prior art, and implementation experience, while the thinnest support appears in the areas of why a library solution is insufficient and how the feature would coordinate with existing standard facilities.

- The paper clearly establishes that dynamic structural interfaces are a recurring need and that its reflection-based approach is distinct from existing type-erasure solutions.
- The reference implementation and its use of code generation to simulate post-C++26 reflection provide credible evidence of implementation experience.
- The paper only claims, rather than demonstrates, who is affected and why the standard is the right venue, leaning on general references to existing facilities without specific user or ecosystem impact.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library cannot adequately provide the proposed functionality.
