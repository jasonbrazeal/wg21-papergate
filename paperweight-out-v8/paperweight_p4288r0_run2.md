Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some grounding for its motivation and points to relevant prior art, but it leaves the core case for standardization largely unargued. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected audience, interoperability story, or reason a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the motivation that asynchronous functions should be able to return references just as synchronous functions do.
- The discussion of `std::execution::split` and its removal provides concrete prior art for the problem being addressed.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the issue or what coordination with existing practice would be required.
- Most glaringly, it never explains why this capability cannot be provided by a library rather than through standardization.
