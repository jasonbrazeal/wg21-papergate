Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin basis for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on an asserted perception problem and a handful of informal terminology discussions. The case is thinnest where it matters most: there is no demonstration of who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the work would interoperate, or that a library solution is insufficient.

- The strongest support is the observation that users may misunderstand what activating a profile does, though even this is asserted rather than shown.
- The discussion of prior art gestures at alternative vocabulary and related removal proposals, but does not establish that these amount to a worked-out design space.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the proposed standardization or what problem they concretely face.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, coordination plan, or argument for why the standard rather than a library or separate document is needed.
