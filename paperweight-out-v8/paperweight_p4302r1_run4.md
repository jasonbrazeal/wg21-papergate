Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a partial account of the procedural problem it wants to solve, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case almost entirely unargued. The strongest material concerns the real risk that in-meeting revisions can shift consensus before absent reviewers have seen the change, while the thinnest support surrounds the absence of any demonstration that a standards-level rule is the right remedy.

- The paper establishes why the issue matters by showing how recording a poll on an unmailed revision can create a new status quo before stakeholders know the change exists.
- It gestures toward affected parties and prior practice, but the supporting evidence is anecdotal or only loosely connected to the proposed rule.
- The paper does not establish why the standard is the necessary place for the fix, as opposed to existing procedures or guidance.
- It offers no implementation experience or interoperability analysis, leaving the practical consequences of standardization unexamined.
