Verdict: Adequate to Strong (7/14)

The paper offers solid grounding in the problem space and in prior art, and it has enough implementation experience to show the idea is more than speculative. The support is thinnest where the paper needs to show that this belongs in the standard rather than in a library, and why existing or third-party facilities cannot carry the design.

- The paper clearly establishes that dynamic structural interfaces are a recurring need and that the proposed reflection-based approach differs meaningfully from existing alternatives such as `proxy`.
- The reference implementation, even with a custom build step, gives credible evidence that the core design has been worked through in practice.
- The paper only claims, rather than demonstrates, who is affected and how the feature would coordinate with existing standard library type erasure facilities.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for why a library cannot provide this capability, which leaves the central standardization question unanswered.
