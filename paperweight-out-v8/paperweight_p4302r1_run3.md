Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a clear rationale for why in-meeting design changes create a procedural problem, and it grounds that concern in existing WG21 practice and rules. Its support is thinnest when it moves from the general problem to the specific need for a standing rule: the affected population is illustrated by one meeting’s anecdote, and the case for why this must be addressed in the standard itself is not made.

- The strongest support is the explanation of how recording a poll on changed text can shift the consensus burden before national body experts have seen the change.
- The paper also establishes that its approach is narrower than existing SD-4 permissions and leaves room for the group to proceed when nobody objects.
- The main weakness is that the affected population is asserted through a single personal tally rather than shown as a recurring, broadly felt problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why this procedural constraint belongs in the standard rather than in WG21’s own rules or practices.
