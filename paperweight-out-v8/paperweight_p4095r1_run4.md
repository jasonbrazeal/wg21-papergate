Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow but genuine basis for its standardization case: it establishes why the problem matters within its chosen framing and shows that the proposed alternative was not already covered by the prior work that motivated the pivot. Beyond that, the support is thin, with no demonstrated affected audience, no argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and no implementation experience to validate the design.

- The strongest support is the identification of a real error-propagation gap under the work framing and the absence of any generic remedy in the existing `execute(F&&)` surface.
- The paper also credibly establishes that the continuation framing and the proposed basis operation were not present in the three papers that drove the pivot, nor in the earlier `async_result` and N3747 work.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience, leaving the proposal without evidence that the design is usable or that its claimed zero-allocation and type-erasure trade-offs hold in practice.
- Equally absent is any case for why a library cannot suffice, since the paper documents the limitations but does not establish that standardization is the necessary remedy.
