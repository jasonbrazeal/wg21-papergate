Verdict: Weak (3/14)

The paper offers a narrow but real foundation for its standardization case: it explains why the current `std::execution::task` design cannot express stopped completion from within the coroutine body, and it situates the work as a follow-up enabled by the already-adopted P3950. Beyond that, however, the support is thin—most of the burden of showing who needs this, why it belongs in the standard rather than a library, and how it fits with existing practice is left unaddressed.

- The strongest support is the clear motivation, which ties the gap directly to the semantic mismatch between `co_return` and `co_yield`.
- The paper also establishes a credible prior-art and alternatives path by grounding itself in the accepted P3950 and framing the proposal as a revision of that design.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any identified affected audience or use case that would demonstrate real-world demand for the change.
- Equally unestablished are the standard-necessity arguments: why a library cannot provide the behavior, how the feature coordinates with adjacent facilities, and whether there is any implementation experience to validate the design.
