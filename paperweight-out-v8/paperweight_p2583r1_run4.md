Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers solid support for the technical problem and the shape of a protocol-level solution, but its case for standardization rests on several assertions about industry practice, ABI risk, and the impossibility of library-only mitigation that are stated rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the mechanism itself and the scope of the required changes; the thinnest concerns who is actually affected and why the work cannot happen outside the standard.

- The paper clearly establishes that synchronous sender completion defeats symmetric transfer and that a protocol-level change returning `coroutine_handle<>` is a viable fix with concrete implementation experience.
- The paper establishes the interoperability burden by showing that the concept changes ripple through twenty-five sender algorithms, coroutine bridges, and every third-party `receiver` or `operation_state`.
- The paper claims but does not establish that every major coroutine library uses symmetric transfer, leaving the breadth of the affected audience asserted rather than evidenced.
- The paper claims but does not establish that the change becomes ABI-breaking once `std::execution` ships, and that a library-level trampoline is an unacceptable cost, leaving the necessity of standardization as the most glaring omission.
