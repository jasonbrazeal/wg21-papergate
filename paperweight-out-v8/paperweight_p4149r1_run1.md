Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin, fragmentary case for its own standardization: it gestures at a definitional goal and at a desire for consistency with related language features, but leaves almost every other question about need, affected users, alternatives, and feasibility unaddressed. The support is thinnest where a proposal normally carries the most weight—showing who is concretely affected and why the standard is the only viable venue.

- The strongest support is the paper’s stated aim to define “immediate context,” which at least identifies the term it wants to standardize.
- The paper also claims a consistency rationale by linking annotations to noexcept-specifiers, default arguments, and function-contract-specifiers, though it does not establish that rationale beyond assertion.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the practical motivation for standardization entirely unstated.
