The paper’s strongest backing comes from concrete implementation experience, including a reference implementation, libunifex, and stdexec, which lends real weight to the claim that the proposed lifetime behavior is already workable in practice. Its case is much thinner, however, when it comes to explaining why the problem matters, who is affected, and why a library-only solution would be insufficient; those points are asserted rather than demonstrated. The discussion of prior art and the need for a standard is adequately grounded, but the paper leans heavily on the same libunifex reference to support several distinct claims without showing how broadly the issue arises or how users are harmed today.

- The paper clearly establishes implementation experience through multiple existing implementations and a reference implementation output.
- The need for standardization is supported by the observation that users must currently write code as if the minimal lifetime option were chosen, making portability difficult.
- The paper does not establish who is affected beyond a single library’s internal practice, leaving the scope of the problem unclear.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of a demonstrated reason why a library cannot solve the problem, since the only evidence offered is that a library already does so.
