Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a reasonably clear account of the problem it wants to solve and the design space around it, but it leaves several parts of the standardization argument almost entirely unaddressed. The strongest material concerns motivation and prior art, while the thinnest concerns who would actually be affected, how the feature would interact with existing practice, and why a library solution is insufficient.

- The paper establishes why the change matters by tying it to type safety, strong typedefs, enumerations, `std::byte`, and consistency with scalar operations.
- It also establishes meaningful prior art and alternatives through the standard library’s existing variable-template customization pattern and the discussion of customization points.
- The paper claims implementation experience, but the evidence is mostly a repeated assertion about Intel’s implementation and compiler behavior rather than a demonstrated body of portable, independently verified practice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for who is affected, how the feature coordinates with existing code and tooling, or why the same goal cannot be met by a library outside the standard.
