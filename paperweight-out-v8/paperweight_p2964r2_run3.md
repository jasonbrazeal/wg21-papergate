The paper offers a solid rationale for why the change matters and shows credible prior art, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case unaddressed, particularly around affected users, interoperability, and why a library solution cannot suffice. The thinnest areas are those that would normally ground the proposal in real-world need and integration, rather than in the design itself.

- The strongest support is the established motivation, which clearly connects the proposal to type safety, strong typedefs, enumerations, `std::byte`, and the closed list currently limiting `std::simd`.
- The paper also establishes prior art and alternatives by pointing to existing standard library patterns and the limitations of the current expression-only checks.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established discussion of who is affected, leaving the actual user community and impact unclear.
- Equally missing is any established case for why a library cannot solve the problem, along with no coordination or interoperability analysis.
