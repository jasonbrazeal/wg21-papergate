Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a concrete account of how in-meeting design changes can distort consensus and disadvantage absent participants, but it does not build a comparable case for standardizing the remedy it proposes. The strongest material concerns the procedural problem itself, while the argument thins out almost entirely around the need for a standard, interoperability, and implementation experience.

- The paper establishes why the problem matters by showing that recording an unmailed change can reverse consensus outcomes and force delegates to vote on text different from what they prepared.
- It establishes who is affected through specific examples of papers changing during a meeting and implementers asking for a slower feature cadence.
- Its treatment of prior art and alternatives is suggestive rather than settled, since the cited proposal lacked consensus and the relevance of other practices is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper offers nothing to establish why the standard is the right vehicle, how the change would coordinate with existing processes, why a library or non-standard practice would not suffice, or what implementation experience supports it.
