The paper offers only a thin, largely rhetorical case for standardization: it gestures at the motivation for profiles but does not develop the surrounding evidence that would connect that motivation to a standards-track proposal. The support is thinnest where a proposal normally needs to be concrete—audience, prior work, standardization rationale, interoperability, library alternatives, and implementation experience are all left unaddressed.

- The strongest element is a single sentence asserting that profiles offer an incremental path to safety beyond base-language changes, though even this is only claimed rather than demonstrated.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by the proposed standardization or what existing practice it builds on.
- It offers no account of why standardization, rather than a library or other mechanism, is the right vehicle.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no implementation experience or interoperability analysis, leaving the practical basis for the proposal entirely unsupported.
