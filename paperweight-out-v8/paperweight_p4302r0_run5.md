Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper makes a credible case that the current process creates a real, recurring problem and that its proposed bright-line rule addresses that problem more cleanly than the alternatives it surveys. Its support is strongest on the need for an objective mechanism and on the history of prior attempts, but it thins out considerably when it comes to showing that the rule belongs in the standard itself, that affected parties beyond a single anecdotal delegate would actually change their behavior, or that the approach has been tried anywhere.

- The paper establishes why the problem matters by tying it to the pre-meeting mailing’s review function and to the costs of judgment-based rules.
- It also establishes that prior art and alternatives exist and that the proposed rule avoids weaknesses that contributed to a near-miss in an earlier proposal.
- The paper claims but does not establish who is affected, resting the delegate-side evidence on a single recorded case.
- The most glaring omissions are the absence of any case for why the standard is the right instrument, why a library or other non-standard mechanism would not do, and any implementation experience with the proposed rule.
