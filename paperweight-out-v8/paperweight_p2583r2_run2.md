Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers a reasonably grounded case in the areas that matter most for a protocol-level change: it shows why the current sender completion model causes unbounded stack growth, identifies a concrete fix, and demonstrates that independent coroutine libraries have already converged on the same mechanism. The support is thinnest where the paper needs to connect that technical convergence to the standardization process itself, particularly in showing who is actually affected and why the change cannot be absorbed by libraries or a later revision without breaking the standard.

- The strongest support is the independent replication across five of six surveyed libraries of the same `coroutine_handle<>`-returning `await_suspend` mechanism, which gives the proposed fix real implementation weight.
- The paper also clearly establishes the scope of the change by enumerating the concept-level expressions, sender algorithms, receivers, and operation states that would need to be modified.
- A notable gap is that the claim about every major coroutine library using symmetric transfer is asserted rather than demonstrated with evidence from the surveyed libraries.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish why the fix must be standardized now rather than left to libraries, since the ABI-break argument rests on the unestablished premise that `std::execution` will ship with void-returning completions.
