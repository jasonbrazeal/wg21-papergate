Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on the observation that `span` and `string_view` are conceptually similar and that `first` and `last` appear to be absent without explanation. Beyond that analogy, the case is thin: it does not identify who would benefit, why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, how it would interact with existing practice, or whether anyone has tried implementing it.

- The strongest support is the established prior-art point that `span` and `string_view` both model non-owning references into contiguous memory, and that `subspan` already has a conceptual counterpart in `string_view::substr`.
- The paper’s claim that the missing APIs matter is asserted mainly through phrases like “little to no reason” and “for no apparent reason,” but it does not demonstrate practical need or user impact.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any case for why this must be standardized rather than supplied by a library, alongside no discussion of affected users, interoperability, or implementation experience.
