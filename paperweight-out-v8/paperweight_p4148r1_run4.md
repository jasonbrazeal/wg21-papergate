Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives a reasonably grounded account of the problem space and of its own implementation approach, but it leans heavily on a small set of passages and does not close the gap between showing that type erasure is common and showing that this particular protocol mechanism belongs in the standard. The thinnest part is the absence of any argument that a library solution cannot suffice.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation experience, including a reference implementation and a clear statement that code generation currently depends on a custom build step.
- The paper also establishes prior art and alternatives by situating itself against existing type-erasure facilities and the `proxy` proposal.
- The case for why the standard should adopt this is only claimed, resting on broad statements about the absence of a language-supported mechanism rather than a specific standardization need.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never establishes why a library will not do, leaving the central rationale for standardization unaddressed.
