Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some useful framing around reducing false positives and preserving guarantees, but it does not yet make a complete case for standardization. The strongest material concerns motivation and relationship to prior work, while the practical and procedural dimensions are almost entirely absent.

- The paper clearly explains why suppressing safety checks can undermine whole-program guarantees and why a lower-false-positive approach would matter.
- It situates the idea credibly against borrow checking and P1179R1, and it borrows established definitions from related proposals.
- It does not identify who would be affected by the proposed change or what implementation experience exists.
- Most notably, the paper never explains why this needs to be in the standard rather than delivered through a library or tooling.
