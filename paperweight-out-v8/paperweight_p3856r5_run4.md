Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a clear motivation for exposing a structural-type query, but its supporting case is thin in most other respects: several important claims are asserted rather than demonstrated, and some standard justification categories are not addressed at all. The strongest material concerns the gap in current reflection facilities and the value of such a query for compile-time programming, while the weakest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, coordination with existing features, or real implementation experience beyond a sample implementation.

- The paper establishes why the feature matters by identifying a concrete gap: there is no way to query whether a type is structural, despite the importance of structural types for non-type template parameters.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is only claimed, not established, because the references to P2996 and the need for lower-level metafunctions are asserted without a fuller comparison or demonstration of what existing facilities can and cannot do.
- The argument for why a library solution will not suffice rests entirely on the statement that library implementers must have the functionality internally, but the paper does not show that this internal capability is inaccessible or insufficient for users in practice.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of any account of who is affected by the problem or how the proposed feature would coordinate and interoperate with existing standardization efforts.
