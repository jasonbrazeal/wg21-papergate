Verdict: Adequate (7/14)

The paper offers a mixed but uneven case for standardization, with its strongest grounding in prior art, implementation experience, and the practical need for safer UTF transcoding interfaces. The thinnest areas are coordination and interoperability, which are not established at all, and several key claims about why the standard library is the right home for this work rest on a single assertion about replacing removed `codecvt` facilities.

- The paper is most convincing on implementation experience, citing a reference implementation and adherence to its specification.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is well supported, including alignment with Unicode substitution methodology and explicit design tradeoffs.
- The argument for why this belongs in the standard leans heavily on the claim that it can replace deprecated and removed `codecvt` facets, but the paper does not develop that into a fuller standardization rationale.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any discussion of coordination and interoperability with existing or proposed library facilities.
