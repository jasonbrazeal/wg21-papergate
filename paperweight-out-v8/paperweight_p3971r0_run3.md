Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for the general problem and for the existence of relevant precedent, but it leaves the standardization case largely implicit where it most needs to be explicit. The strongest material concerns prior art and the conceptual gap being filled, while the thinnest concerns who is actually affected and why existing library-level solutions cannot suffice.

- The paper clearly establishes that a generalized rebinding mechanism addresses a real gap in generic programming, particularly for tuple-like types designed to hold heterogeneous element types.
- It also credibly grounds the design in prior art, especially the `std::simd` rebinding facility and existing standard library rebinding terminology.
- The claim that only standardization can provide the needed combination of type computation and conversion support is asserted rather than demonstrated against plausible non-standard alternatives.
- The paper does not establish who is affected, how the feature would interoperate with existing code or proposals, or that there is implementation experience supporting the design.
