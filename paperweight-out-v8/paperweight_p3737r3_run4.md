Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably solid account of the practical problem and existing implementation behavior, but it is much thinner when it comes to showing why the change belongs in the standard rather than in guidance or a library-level fix. The strongest material concerns what libc++ and libstdc++ already do, while the weakest area is the absence of any real argument that the goal cannot be achieved outside the standard.

- The paper clearly establishes that zero-length `std::array` behavior matters because the current specification is overly permissive and misses useful guarantees such as trivial copyability.
- It also establishes who is affected and what existing practice looks like, with concrete evidence that libstdc++ and libc++ already match the proposed direction.
- The case for standardization itself is asserted mainly through appeals to precedent and the oddity of not standardizing common practice, but it is not developed into a compelling necessity.
- The paper does not establish why a library cannot address the problem, leaving that essential part of the standardization case essentially unargued.
