Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a clear and well-supported account of the procedural problem it targets, particularly the tension between in-meeting revisions and the review role of the pre-meeting mailing. Its support is thinnest where it needs to connect that procedural concern to the formal standardization process itself, and it does not establish why a change to the standard is the necessary remedy.

- The strongest support is the established explanation of why the issue matters, including the risk that in-meeting revisions shift consensus before national body experts can review them.
- The paper also establishes relevant prior art and alternatives, showing that existing rules and WG14 practice already recognize the value of pre-meeting review time.
- The paper claims but does not establish who is affected, relying on a single meeting’s anecdote and a general implementer sentiment rather than a demonstrated pattern of harm.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for why the standard itself must change, as opposed to the committee’s internal procedures or guidance.
