The paper gives a reasonably clear account of why the current overflow rules are inconsistent and why aligning them with existing library behavior would be sensible, but it does not fully demonstrate that this is a problem requiring a core language change rather than a compiler or library matter. The strongest material concerns the conceptual mismatch and the precedent from mathematical functions, while the thinnest support appears in the areas of affected users, implementation experience, and the necessity of standardization.

- The paper clearly establishes that the current specification of floating-point overflow is unclear and that the proposed direction is consistent with how mathematical functions already handle range errors.
- It offers some evidence from compiler behavior and prior committee discussion, but does not convincingly establish who is affected or how widespread the practical impact is.
- The argument for why this must be addressed in the standard, rather than through implementation or library choices, is asserted more than demonstrated.
- The paper provides no case for why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving a notable gap in the standardization rationale.
