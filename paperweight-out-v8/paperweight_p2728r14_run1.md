The paper’s strongest support comes from its implementation experience, with a reference implementation and experimental work already available, but its case for standardization is otherwise thin. The argument for why the standard library is the right home, who specifically benefits, and why existing libraries cannot suffice is asserted rather than demonstrated, and coordination with related standardization efforts is left unaddressed.

- The paper clearly documents implementation experience through a reference implementation and experimental branches.
- Prior art and alternatives are meaningfully discussed, including the relationship to removed `codecvt` facilities and the Unicode Standard’s substitution methodology.
- The paper claims relevance and audience impact but offers little concrete evidence of who is affected or why the problem matters at scale.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of coordination and interoperability with other proposals or existing standards.
