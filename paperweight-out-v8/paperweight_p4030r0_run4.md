Verdict: Weak (3/14)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly asserted case for standardization: its motivation rests on a plausible need for endianness-aware UTF conversion, but it does not demonstrate who is affected, what existing practice or alternatives show, or that a library solution would be insufficient. The support is thinnest around the absence of implementation experience and any evidence that the proposed facility cannot be adequately provided outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the paper’s identification of a real user need for converting between UTF encodings with explicit endianness.
- The paper gestures toward avoiding a combinatorial explosion of UTF adaptors, but it does not establish that this design pressure actually exists or that the proposed views are the right response.
- The paper claims broad applicability to network protocols and file formats, but it offers no concrete examples, user reports, or interoperability analysis to substantiate that reach.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience or evidence that a library cannot already satisfy the need, leaving the standardization case essentially ungrounded.
