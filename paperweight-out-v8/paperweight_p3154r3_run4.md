Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a focused motivation for changing how `signed char` and `unsigned char` are treated, but it leaves most of the case for standardization unaddressed. The strongest material concerns the surprising current behavior and the precedent set by `std::format`, while the discussion of affected users, the need for a standard change rather than a library solution, interoperability, and implementation experience is essentially absent.

- The paper clearly establishes that the current treatment of `signed char` and `unsigned char` as characters is surprising and difficult to justify, especially when integer aliases like `int8_t` are involved.
- The paper credibly points to `std::format` and the C versus C++ handling of `char8_t` as relevant prior art and alternatives.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the current behavior or how widespread the problem is in practice.
- The paper offers no argument for why the change requires standardization rather than a library-level solution, nor any evidence of implementation experience or coordination concerns.
