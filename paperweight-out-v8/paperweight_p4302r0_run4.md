Verdict: Adequate to Strong (6/14)

The paper offers real support for the need to replace a judgment-based deadline with an objective rule, and it engages seriously with the prior process proposal and the committee’s existing mailing framework. The case is thinnest where it reaches beyond the author’s own experience: the affected population, the necessity of a standard-level change, and the impossibility of addressing the problem through non-standard means are asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the established argument that the committee’s scarce prepared attention justifies an objective deadline and that judgment calls impose avoidable costs.
- The paper also credibly establishes prior art by treating P2138R4 as a direct ancestor and grounding the deadline in SD-4’s existing pre-meeting mailing purpose.
- The claim that delegates broadly stopped preparing rests on a single personal account, so the affected population is not established.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established reason the change must be made in the standard itself rather than through committee process or other non-standard mechanisms.
