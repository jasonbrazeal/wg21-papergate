Verdict: Adequate to Strong (7/14)

The paper offers real support in the places that matter most for a proposal of this kind: it can point to working implementations and to a concrete, widely recognized failure mode in existing composed asynchronous code. The case is much thinner, however, when it moves from “this is a genuine problem” to “this specific mechanism belongs in the standard,” with the claims about affected users, prior alternatives, and the necessity of standardization resting largely on assertion rather than demonstrated evidence.

- The strongest support is implementation experience, where the paper names maintained libraries and grounds its motivation in the layered state-machine burden seen in Boost.Beast.
- The paper establishes why the problem matters by connecting completion-token generality to foreclosed optimizations and by showing how work framing imposes costs that continuation framing does not.
- The weakest part of the case is the near-total reliance on assertion for who is affected, what alternatives were considered, and why a library solution cannot suffice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of established evidence that the proposed mechanism is needed in the standard specifically, rather than as a library built on existing or near-term language features.
