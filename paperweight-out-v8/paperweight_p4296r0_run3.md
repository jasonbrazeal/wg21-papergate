Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a narrow but real foundation for its standardization case, centered on motivation and prior art, while leaving most of the practical and evidentiary questions unanswered. The thinnest areas are those that would show the feature is ready for the standard: who is affected, how it interoperates, why a library cannot suffice, and whether anyone has actually implemented it.

- The strongest support is the motivation, which clearly explains why reducing false positives while preserving provable safety matters and why suppression-based approaches undermine guarantees.
- The paper also does a credible job situating itself against prior work, especially P1179 and the container/pointer definitions from P3274R0/P3446R0, and it identifies a claimed advantage over earlier borrow-checker-style approaches.
- The case for standardization itself rests on a single unsupported assertion about the difficulty of standardizing “Positive Rules,” without evidence or elaboration.
- The most glaring omissions are the complete absence of discussion about affected users, interoperability with existing code and tooling, why a library solution would not work, and any implementation experience.
