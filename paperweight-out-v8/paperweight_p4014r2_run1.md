Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in the areas where it engages with existing practice and theory, particularly through its references to `stdexec`, `sender-examples`, and the monadic framing of the Sender Sub-Language. However, the case for standardization is thinnest where it needs to show that a library solution is insufficient and that the affected developer population is broad enough to justify a language feature.

- The strongest support comes from concrete implementation experience, with examples drawn from real repositories and a maintained coroutine-native I/O library.
- The paper also grounds its design in established prior art, connecting its abstractions to Moggi’s monadic framework and existing sender/receiver patterns.
- The argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library is not established, leaving a central justification unaddressed.
- The most glaring omission is any account of who is affected, so the paper never demonstrates that the problem reaches beyond a narrow set of asynchronous programming specialists.
