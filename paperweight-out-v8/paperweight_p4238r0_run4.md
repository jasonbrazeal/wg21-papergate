Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some support for its own standardization, chiefly by showing that the surrounding process has failed to preserve the conditions under which a contested design could be legitimately advanced. That support is real but narrow: it establishes why the question matters and that alternatives were available, while leaving the actual case for standardization—who is affected, why the standard is the right vehicle, and whether the design has been tried—largely unstated.

- The strongest support is the paper’s demonstration that recorded rationale and external scrutiny are essential to the decision, and that the existing record does not show the opposition was answered.
- The paper also credibly establishes that recognized alternatives, including a Technical Specification and the precedent of withdrawing a contested feature, were available and not adequately pursued.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established account of who is affected by the proposal or what implementation experience exists to ground it.
- The paper likewise does not establish why the International Standard, rather than a library or another venue, is the necessary place for this work.
