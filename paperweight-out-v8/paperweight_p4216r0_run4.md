Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a narrow but real foundation for its proposal, chiefly by situating `span` comparisons alongside existing non-owning reference types and by documenting the prior inclusion and later removal of such comparisons. Beyond that historical and design context, however, the case for standardization is largely asserted rather than demonstrated, with no substantive treatment of affected users, library alternatives, interoperability, or implementation experience.

- The strongest support comes from prior art: the paper shows that `string_view`, `reference_wrapper`, and `optional<T&>` all provide deep value comparisons, and that `span` itself originally included them before their removal.
- The paper establishes why the issue matters by pointing to the inconsistency among standard non-owning reference types and the real concerns raised by the earlier removal.
- The thinnest part of the case is the absence of any established argument for who is affected or why a library solution would be insufficient.
