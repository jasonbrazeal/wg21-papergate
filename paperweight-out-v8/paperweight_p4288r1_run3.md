Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow but genuine foundation for its standardization case, centered on the conceptual need for reference-returning asynchronous functions and some relevant prior art, but it leaves most of the practical and procedural questions unanswered. The thinnest areas are the absence of any demonstrated user population, the lack of a clear argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and the failure to address coordination or interoperability with the broader ecosystem.

- The strongest support is the paper’s articulation of why reference returns matter for asynchronous functions, grounded in the uncontroversial synchronous analogue and the ownership-model distinction it draws.
- The discussion of prior art is credible, particularly the cautionary example of `std::execution::split` and the note that the design has been implemented against a reference implementation.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established audience or affected community, leaving the proposal without a demonstrated constituency.
- Nearly as significant is the failure to explain why standardization, rather than a library solution, is necessary or how the proposal would coordinate with existing and adjacent standardization efforts.
