Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on assertions about community expectations and references to other documents rather than on evidence developed in the proposal itself. The support is thinnest where a standards document would normally need to be most concrete: identifying affected users, explaining why the standard is the right venue, showing how the feature would coordinate with existing practice, ruling out a library solution, and demonstrating implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s gesture toward prior art and alternatives, though even that consists mainly of citations to other proposals rather than an analysis of how they bear on this one.
- The claim that the community has waited over a decade for networking support is asserted as motivation, but the paper does not connect that general desire to the specific need for this proposal.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by the proposed standardization or what problem they would have in practice.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no account of why a library would be insufficient, why the standard itself must act, or what implementation experience exists to ground the design.
