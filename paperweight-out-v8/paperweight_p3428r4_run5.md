Verdict: Adequate (7/14)

The paper offers concrete support in the areas of motivation, affected users, and implementation experience, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case largely unargued. The thinnest support concerns why this needs to be in the standard rather than a library, how it coordinates with the existing hazard pointer interface, and what the standard specifically enables.

- The strongest support is the demonstrated production use and measured latency benefit of batched hazard pointer construction and destruction.
- The paper identifies a clear gap in the current C++26 interface, which only supports individual nonempty hazard pointers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library implementation would be insufficient, despite the feature already existing as a library class in Folly.
