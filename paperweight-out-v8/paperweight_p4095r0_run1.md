Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in a few areas, particularly in establishing why the problem matters and in showing that the relevant prior work and alternatives have been examined. However, the case for standardization is thin where it counts most: the paper does not establish who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the proposal would coordinate with existing facilities, or that a library solution is insufficient. The implementation-experience claim rests on the reputation of earlier authors rather than on experience with the proposed design itself.

- The strongest support is the demonstration that error handling is currently implementation-defined and that the four identified deficiencies are real under the work framing.
- The paper also credibly shows that the prior proposals driving the pivot did not engage with the continuation framing or `async_result`.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established need for standardization itself, leaving the central question of why this belongs in the standard unanswered.
