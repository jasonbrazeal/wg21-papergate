Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a narrow but genuine basis for its motivation, chiefly by showing that existing stream behavior is surprising and that `std::format` already points toward the desired treatment of character types as integers. Beyond that, however, the case for standardization is largely undeveloped: the affected audience, the need for a standard change rather than a library solution, and any implementation experience are all absent, while the claim about coordination rests on a very thin observation.

- The strongest support is the established motivation, which identifies the unexpected behavior and the difficulty of finding cases where the current behavior is actually wanted.
- The paper also credibly cites `std::format` as prior art and notes the C and C++ divergence over `char8_t`, giving the proposal some grounding in existing practice.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any established audience or demonstrated need for a standard change, leaving the proposal without a clear constituency or urgency.
- The absence of implementation experience and of an argument for why a library cannot address the problem leaves the standardization path essentially unsupported.
