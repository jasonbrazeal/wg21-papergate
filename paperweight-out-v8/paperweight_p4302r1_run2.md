Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers meaningful support for its procedural concern, particularly in explaining why in-meeting design changes can distort consensus, and it does useful work in situating the proposal against existing rules and adjacent committee practice. Its case thins considerably once it moves from identifying a problem to showing who is concretely affected and why standardization is the necessary remedy, with several standard requirements left entirely unaddressed.

- The strongest part of the paper is its articulation of the procedural risk: recording a poll on an unmailed design change can shift the consensus burden before absent stakeholders even know the change exists.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also well grounded, showing how SD-4 and WG14 handle related timing questions and distinguishing the proposed rule from those practices.
- The claim about who is affected rests mainly on anecdote and a divided poll, without establishing the breadth or severity of harm across the committee.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why this needs to be a standard at all, as opposed to a change in committee policy or operating procedure.
