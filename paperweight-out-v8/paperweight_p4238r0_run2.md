Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a clear and well-grounded account of the procedural failures it wants to address, and it draws effectively on external definitions of consensus and on the history of the Contracts work. Its support is thinnest where a standardization document would normally need to show that the proposed remedy belongs in the standard, that it can be implemented, and that it coordinates with existing processes.

- The strongest support is the paper’s demonstration that the committee’s handling of Contracts departed from documented consensus requirements and that alternatives such as a Technical Specification were available but declined.
- The paper also establishes that the affected community extends beyond the committee itself, though it does so mainly by assertion rather than by showing the practical consequences for users or implementers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case that the proposed change requires standardization rather than a change to committee practice or policy outside the International Standard.
