Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its own standardization, centered on the fact that draft wording has been developed collaboratively. Most of the case for why this work belongs in the standard is asserted rather than demonstrated, and several essential justifications are simply absent. The thinnest areas concern the intended audience, the need for normative standardization rather than another form of publication, and the absence of any demonstrated reason a library or separate reference could not serve the same purpose.

- The paper gestures at prior efforts and a hoped-for common vocabulary, but it does not establish that those efforts succeeded, failed for lack of standardization, or that stable names require a place in the standard.
- The paper never identifies who is concretely affected by the absence of this annex or what practical problem they face today.
- Most glaringly, it does not explain why the standard itself is the right home, nor why a library, database, or maintained reference document would not be sufficient.
