Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper makes a real case for the procedural problem it identifies and for the specific remedy it prefers, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization argument largely unaddressed. The strongest material concerns the costs of judgment-based rules and the relationship to existing SD-4 practice, while the thinnest concerns who is actually affected, why a standards change rather than a library or process change is needed, and what implementation experience supports the proposal.

- The paper establishes why the problem matters by connecting judgment calls to wasted delegate preparation and to the loss of the train model’s safety valve at the most consequential meeting.
- The paper establishes prior art and alternatives by engaging SD-4, competing papers, and the history of P2138R4, and by scoping its change narrowly to the poll.
- The paper does not establish who is affected beyond a single delegate’s reported behavior, leaving the breadth of the claimed harm unsupported.
- The paper does not establish why the standard is the right instrument or why a library or ordinary process change would not do, which is a glaring omission for a standardization proposal.
