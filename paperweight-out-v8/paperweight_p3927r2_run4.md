Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some concrete grounding for its proposal, but the case for standardization is uneven: it clearly identifies a real behavioral problem and shows implementation experience, yet leaves most of the surrounding justification unstated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the feature coordinates with existing specifications, and why a library solution would not suffice.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with the solution already present in the `std::execution` reference implementation as of early 2026.
- The paper also establishes why the problem matters by showing that `task_scheduler` silently loses parallelism when wrapping a `parallel_scheduler` for `bulk` work.
- The prior art and alternatives discussion is only gestured at through a passing comparison to `parallel_scheduler`, without a substantive exploration of other approaches.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of evidence about who is affected, why standardization is necessary, or why an out-of-standard library cannot address the need.
