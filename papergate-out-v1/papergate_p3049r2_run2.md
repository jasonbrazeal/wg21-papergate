Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for standardization: it names a consistency concern but does not develop it, and it leaves most of the evidentiary burden unaddressed. The strongest support is the existence of an implementation, though even that is merely cited rather than described or evaluated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, prior art, or why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper points to a concrete implementation as evidence of design feasibility.
- It acknowledges a known consistency criticism but does not substantiate why the new API’s advantages outweigh that cost.
- It asserts transferability between compatible containers without explaining how that coordination would work in practice.
- It never addresses who is affected, what alternatives exist, or why standardization rather than a library is necessary.
