Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a mixed case for standardization: it grounds several key arguments in concrete examples and prior art, but leaves some of its most load-bearing claims about user demand and design constraints asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the paper relies on general statements about community need and mandatory design requirements without evidence or elaboration.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of prior art and alternatives, which cites specific limitations in D and explains why those approaches do not transfer to C++.
- The implementation-experience section also provides useful specificity by identifying a concrete mechanism from D’s history and its practical failure.
- The paper asserts that class invariants are among the most commonly requested extensions and that any solution must avoid layout or runtime costs, but offers no supporting evidence or reasoning for either claim.
