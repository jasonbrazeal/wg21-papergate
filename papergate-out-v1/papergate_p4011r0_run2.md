Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, focusing on terminology and the relationship between contracts and undefined behavior while leaving most practical and procedural questions untouched. The strongest material concerns prior art and conceptual framing, but the absence of implementation experience, library alternatives, and standards-process rationale leaves the proposal largely unmoored from the evidence typically needed to justify committee action.

- The paper grounds its discussion in prior work such as N3248, showing awareness of existing terminology around narrow and wide contracts.
- It offers a specific conceptual point about syntax that both encodes preconditions and removes undefined behavior, which gives the discussion some analytical focus.
- It does not address implementation experience, leaving no evidence that the proposed direction is feasible in practice.
- It does not explain why a library solution would be insufficient or why standardization is the right venue, which is a fundamental gap for a proposal of this kind.
