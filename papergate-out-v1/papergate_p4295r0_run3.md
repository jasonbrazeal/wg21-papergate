Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives only a narrow, partial basis for its own standardization, resting most of its case on a single motivating observation and a reference to earlier concurrent queue history. The support is thinnest around the questions that would actually justify committee action: who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and how the feature would interoperate with existing or future library components.

- The strongest support is the citation of prior art showing that single-ended concurrent queue interfaces were considered as early as N3353 and deliberately separated by SG1.
- The paper offers a concrete, if minimal, motivating scenario for why producers and consumers often need only one side of a queue interface.
- The implementation experience is asserted through a link, but the paper provides no description of what was implemented, tested, or learned.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of why a library solution would not suffice or why standardization is necessary.
