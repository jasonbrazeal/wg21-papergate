Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers very little support for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on asserted observations about user perception and informal terminology rather than evidence or analysis. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion of prior art, implementation experience, and any argument for why the feature belongs in the standard rather than in a library or separate tool.

- The paper’s strongest support is its identification of a conceptual confusion about when profile effects apply, though even this is asserted without supporting examples or data.
- The claim that users use terms like “dialecting,” “epochs,” and “subset-of-a-superset” is offered as hearsay rather than documented evidence.
- The paper does not address prior art or alternative approaches, leaving unclear whether the problem has known solutions elsewhere.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any rationale for standardization, including why a library or external tooling would not suffice.
