Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers almost no direct support for its own standardization, resting instead on a single comparison to other languages and a broad assertion about pursuing the direction holistically. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of motivation tied to C++ users, standardization rationale, implementation experience, or consideration of alternatives beyond the brief language comparison.

- The strongest support is the specific observation that expression-oriented returns are common in languages such as Kotlin and Rust.
- The paper gestures toward a standardization rationale by saying the direction should either be dropped or pursued holistically, but does not develop that into a case for the standard.
- It does not identify who would be affected by the change or what problem it would solve for them.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience, library alternative analysis, or coordination discussion to ground the proposal in C++ practice.
