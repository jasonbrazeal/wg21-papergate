Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a single motivating sentence, repeated nearly verbatim across every section where justification is expected, so it does little to build a case for standardization. The thinnest areas are those that require evidence or reasoning beyond convenience: who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, why a library cannot suffice, and whether the feature is implementable.

- The strongest support is the reference to P2830R10’s `type_order`, which at least grounds the idea in existing reflection-related ordering work.
- The paper asserts that direct comparison would make sorting metaprogramming entities more convenient, but does not develop this into a concrete need or example.
- It does not address who would use the feature, what code it would simplify, or what problems arise without it.
- The most glaring omission is the admission that there is no implementation experience for the proposed built-in comparison, leaving feasibility and design consequences entirely unexplored.
