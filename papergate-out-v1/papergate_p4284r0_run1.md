Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on the observation that the prior catalog became stale after concurrent changes. That support is thin because the document does not explain who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the work coordinates with related efforts, or whether any implementation experience exists.

- The strongest support is the concrete identification of a real maintenance problem: the catalog was current when approved but immediately outdated due to papers landing at the same meeting.
- The paper gives some prior art by citing P3596R3 and its annexes cataloging undefined behavior and ill-formed no-diagnostic-required constructs.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected by the problem or why standardization is the appropriate remedy.
