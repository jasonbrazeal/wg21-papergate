Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper provides only a narrow slice of the justification needed for standardization, concentrating on conceptual distinctions and prior terminology while leaving most practical and procedural questions untouched. Its strongest material concerns the relationship between preconditions and undefined behavior, but the absence of discussion about affected users, implementation experience, or why a library solution would be insufficient leaves the standardization case largely unbuilt.

- The paper grounds its central distinction in prior work by Meredith et al., giving the terminology a traceable foundation.
- It offers a specific rationale for why encoding preconditions in syntax can be problematic, which supports the motivation for the proposed direction.
- It does not address who is affected by the proposal or what practical problem it solves for them.
- It provides no implementation experience, no interoperability considerations, and no argument for why the standard rather than a library is the right venue.
