Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its procedural argument in concrete committee history, citing a specific failed poll and a real case where revisions arrived only after adoption, which gives the standardization case a factual backbone. The support is thinnest where the paper leans on general statements about the pre-meeting mailing and the papers index without connecting them to the particular harm the proposed rule would prevent.

- The strongest support comes from the recorded P2435R0 poll tally, which shows the prior process proposal lacked consensus and motivates revisiting the issue.
- The Croydon example of `std::execution` papers adopted before their final revisions were published offers a specific, recent failure that the proposal could address.
- The repeated appeal to the pre-meeting mailing’s purpose is asserted rather than tied to evidence that the proposed change would improve national body review.
- The paper does not show that the open-std.org mailing date and disposition columns are insufficient for tracking the problem it describes.
