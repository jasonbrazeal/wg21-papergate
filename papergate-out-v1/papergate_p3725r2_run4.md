Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper grounds its motivation in concrete failures of the current filter view and points to prior SG9 discussion and direction, but it does not build a complete case for standardization. The strongest support is for the existence of a problem and a committee-endorsed direction, while the argument for why this must be a standard library change is essentially asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper identifies specific broken or risky use cases and ties them to real committee discussion, giving its motivation a factual basis.
- It cites an SG9 vote and discussion notes, showing that the proposed direction has some prior committee consideration.
- The claim that ordinary programmers need a safe, self-explanatory filter is presented without supporting examples or explanation of why current alternatives fail.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient, nor does it discuss implementation experience or coordination with existing facilities.
