Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a partial account of why the change should be standardized, but it leans heavily on assertions about implementation acceptance and user dependence without demonstrating either. The strongest material concerns the precedent set by CWG3003 and the naturalness of named specializations, while the case for standardization itself is largely assumed rather than argued.

- The paper grounds its motivation in a concrete, familiar example and ties the issue to a recently accepted core issue fix.
- It asserts that most implementations already accept the behavior, but offers no survey, version details, or vendor documentation to substantiate that claim.
- The argument that users may already depend on the behavior is presented as likely rather than evidenced, with no examples of real-world code or compatibility concerns.
- The paper does not address coordination with implementations, the standard library, or why a library-level solution would be insufficient.
