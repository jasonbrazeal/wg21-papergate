Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides a reasonably grounded case for its standardization, with concrete references to the standard’s wording, compiler-only enforceability, and prior implementation experience. The support is thinnest around the affected audience and the broader consensus picture, since the earlier rejection is acknowledged but not meaningfully re-examined beyond the author’s feeling that new information exists.

- The strongest support comes from the precise citation of the working draft’s “names” and “declarations” language, which directly ties the restriction to compiler enforcement rather than ordinary library code.
- The implementation experience is concrete, though it is presented only as the author’s own work without independent or user-facing validation.
- The discussion of prior art identifies a directly relevant earlier paper and its lack of consensus, but does not analyze why that consensus failed or how the new information resolves the earlier objections.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected by the change, leaving the practical motivation and user impact largely implied rather than demonstrated.
