Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives a narrow but concrete justification for its existence, mainly by pointing to the immediate staleness of a recently merged catalog after concurrent changes landed at the same meeting. Beyond that, the case for standardization is largely unbuilt, with almost no discussion of affected users, alternatives, or why this belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere.

- The strongest support is the specific claim that the prior catalog was current when approved but instantly outdated due to other papers landing at the same meeting.
- The paper offers no discussion of who is affected by the problem or what practical harm the staleness causes.
- It does not address why the standard is the right venue or why a library or separate reference document would not suffice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed mechanism works in practice.
