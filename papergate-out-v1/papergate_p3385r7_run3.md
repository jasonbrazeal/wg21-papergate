Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its standardization case: it gestures at the growing use of attributes and points to a related proposal, but it does not substantiate the claimed prevalence, explain why the standard is the right venue, or show that a library solution is insufficient. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about coordination, interoperability, and the “why the standard” question, along with an implementation-experience claim that is asserted without detail.

- The strongest support is the reference to merging P3678R0 following an SG7 recommendation, which at least anchors the work in prior committee discussion.
- The paper asserts that attributes are widely used and regularly added, but offers no examples, counts, or references to support that claim.
- It does not address why a library solution would not work, leaving a central part of the standardization rationale unexamined.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of coordination, interoperability, or why the standard itself is the necessary vehicle for this design concern.
