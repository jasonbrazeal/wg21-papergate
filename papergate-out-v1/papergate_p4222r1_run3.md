Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete reasoning in some technical areas but little evidence for the claims that matter most to committee adoption. The thinnest support concerns who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and whether the feature has meaningful implementation experience.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of why a library-only approach is insufficient, where the paper gives a specific performance rationale involving buffers and initialization overhead.
- The paper also grounds its proposal in prior art and interoperability by pointing to existing standard-library functions and vector’s initialized/uninitialized partition as a model.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the scope and audience of the proposal unclear.
- The claim that the initialization profile “will be very widely used” and the assertion of an existing implementation are both stated without supporting evidence, weakening the case for standardization.
