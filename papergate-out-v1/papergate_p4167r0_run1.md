Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives a narrow but concrete rationale for exploring a design change, grounded in a specific ADL limitation and a comparison with prior work, but it leaves most of the standardization case unstated. The strongest support is the technical problem statement and the acknowledgment of an alternative proposal, while the thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion about why the standard—rather than a library—must change, and the lack of implementation experience beyond a prototype.

- The paper clearly identifies a specific ADL problem with value representations and connects it to a concrete library example.
- It situates itself against P2822R2 by explaining the main design difference and the choice to avoid new syntax.
- It offers no argument for why the standard is the right venue or why a library solution would be insufficient.
- It provides no coordination or interoperability analysis, and the implementation experience is limited to an asserted prototype with no supporting detail.
