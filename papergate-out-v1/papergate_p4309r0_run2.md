Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a concrete account of the awkward workaround it wants to eliminate, but it rests much of its standardization case on assertion rather than demonstrated need or broader validation. The strongest support is the specific description of the current API limitation and the silliness of creating a container only to extract its node, while the thinnest areas are the lack of evidence about who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and any implementation experience.

- The paper clearly identifies the current workaround and explains why it is an unreasonable burden in ordinary use.
- It acknowledges at least one alternative design space by mentioning factory functions or additional constructors.
- It does not substantiate the claim that this annoyance has been encountered multiple times or that it affects a meaningful population of users.
- It offers no coordination or interoperability discussion, and no evidence that the feature cannot be adequately provided outside the standard.
