Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a concrete sense of what the facility is for and what to call it, but it does not build a sustained case for standardization. The thinnest parts are the claims about who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and whether existing practice is sufficient.

- The strongest support is the naming rationale, which is tied to established practice across Intel, LLVM, and RISC-V.
- The paper identifies plausible application areas such as CRC, AES-GCM, parsing, and bit manipulation.
- The claim of widespread hardware support is asserted without evidence of the platforms, compilers, or instruction sets involved.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of implementation experience, library feasibility, or interoperability concerns.
