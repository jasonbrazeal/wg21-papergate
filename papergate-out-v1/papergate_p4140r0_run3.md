Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides some concrete grounding for its standardization case by explaining the historical wording change and the intent to restore consistency with a previously adopted design, but it leaves several important evidentiary areas entirely unaddressed. The support is thinnest around who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and whether any implementation or usage experience exists.

- The strongest support comes from the specific account of how the allowance for incomplete types was inadvertently dropped and why the earlier formulation had to change.
- The paper asserts that this is merely a wording fixup consistent with P2830R10, but offers no supporting rationale for why the standard is the appropriate place for that fixup.
- The absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience, or coordination leaves the practical impact and readiness of the change largely unexamined.
