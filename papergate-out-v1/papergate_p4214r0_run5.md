Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper leans heavily on one conceptual argument—that progress guarantees belong in the standard’s correctness vocabulary—but offers little else to justify standardization, leaving most of the practical and procedural case unstated. The support is thinnest where it matters most for a committee audience: motivation, affected users, and evidence that a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the specific appeal to Lamport’s safety/liveness distinction as a framing for treating progress guarantees as part of the standard’s correctness contract.
- The paper notes that the standard already contains forward-progress vocabulary, which at least gestures toward a natural place for the work.
- The most glaring omission is any discussion of why the standard should change rather than leaving the facility to a library.
