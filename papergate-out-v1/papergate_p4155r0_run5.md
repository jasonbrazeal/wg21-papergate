Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides some concrete support for its position, particularly around implementation experience and prior committee discussion, but it leaves several foundational questions about motivation and affected users entirely unaddressed. The case for standardization is therefore uneven: it gestures at real history and feasibility while offering almost no affirmative argument for why the standard should adopt the proposal now.

- The strongest support comes from the claim that the approach was previously implemented and then removed from clang, which at least suggests practical feasibility.
- The discussion of prior art and committee history around trivial relocation gives some context for why the proposed semantics were considered.
- The paper is thinnest on why the change matters and who is affected, making it hard to judge the actual need for standardization.
- It also does not explain why the standard, rather than a library or existing practice, is the right vehicle for the proposed behavior.
