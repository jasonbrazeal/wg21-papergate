Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper grounds its central design rationale in concrete examples and prior proposals, but it leaves the affected audience unspecified and offers only an unsupported assertion about implementation experience. The strongest support appears in the discussion of why existing library mechanisms and earlier contract designs are insufficient, while the thinnest support concerns evidence that the proposed approach has actually been implemented or validated.

- The paper gives specific, relevant examples of why prior contract inheritance rules and current library facilities cannot express the intended behavior.
- It explains with concrete reasoning why a native language design is preferable to adapting mechanisms from elsewhere.
- It does not identify who would be affected by the change or what codebases would benefit.
- The claim that all earlier implementations failed is asserted without any supporting detail or reference to demonstrated experience.
