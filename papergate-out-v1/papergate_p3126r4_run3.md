Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, leaning on the general importance of graphs and the authors’ prior experience while leaving the central argument—why this belongs in the standard rather than a library—largely unsupported. The strongest material concerns prior art and implementation experience, but the rationale for standardization itself is asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper gives concrete grounding in prior graph libraries and a reference implementation, which at least shows the design is informed by real experience.
- The claim that no existing library meets the same requirements is stated without examples of those requirements or how alternatives fall short.
- The paper never addresses who would be affected by standardizing a graph library or what coordination with existing practice would require.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any supported argument for why a standard library component is necessary when a library could evidently serve the same purpose.
