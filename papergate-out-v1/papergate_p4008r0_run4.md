Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper grounds its case most concretely in the persistence of legacy compatibility problems, but much of the surrounding rationale is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the proposal claims advantages over alternatives, relies on C++20 Modules, and skips questions of interoperability, library feasibility, and implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the specific identification of legacy C-compatible behaviors as an ongoing source of bugs and complexity.
- The claim that existing safe-subset efforts either remove necessary power or remain unenforceable is presented without examples or evidence.
- The reliance on C++20 Modules as mature infrastructure is asserted but not substantiated.
- The paper does not address coordination, interoperability, why a library solution would be insufficient, or any implementation experience.
