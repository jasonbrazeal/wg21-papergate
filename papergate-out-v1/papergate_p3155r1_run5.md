Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of why the rule should remain in force and what would be lost by abandoning it, but it does not connect that rationale to a specific, actionable standardization change. The strongest material concerns the standard library’s foundational role and the practical testing mechanism, while the weakest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users or coordination with other proposals.

- The paper supports its case most strongly by grounding the argument in a survey of narrow-contract functions across the current working draft and in a concrete, portable testing technique.
- It also explains clearly why the standard library, rather than a separate library, is the right place for the rule to apply.
- The discussion of prior art is specific but limited to a single earlier proposal, leaving the broader design history largely unexamined.
- The paper does not address who would be affected by retaining or relaxing the rule, nor how the change would coordinate with existing practice or other standardization efforts.
