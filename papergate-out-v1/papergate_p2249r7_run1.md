Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on a repeated claim about practical need while leaving most of the evidentiary categories unaddressed or asserted without support. The thinnest areas are prior art, why the standard is the right venue, coordination concerns, and any demonstration of implementation or usage experience beyond a single prototype link.

- The strongest support is the concrete identification of a real usability gap: mixed smart-pointer/raw-pointer comparisons currently require manual extraction of the raw pointer.
- The paper points to a prototype implementation, though it does not describe what that implementation revealed about feasibility, costs, or design tradeoffs.
- The claim that the situation “commonly occurs in practice” is repeated but never substantiated with examples, user reports, code surveys, or ecosystem data.
- Prior art and alternative approaches are effectively unexamined, and the paper does not explain why a library solution or existing comparison mechanisms would be insufficient.
