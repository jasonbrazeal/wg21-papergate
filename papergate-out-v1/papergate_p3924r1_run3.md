Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper provides only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, resting almost entirely on a single national-body comment and a cross-reference to a CWG issue. The strongest support is the concrete observation that the term “declaration” has been used incorrectly in places, but the paper does not explain who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or whether the change is implementable. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about affected users, alternatives, coordination, library solutions, and implementation experience.

- The paper’s strongest support is its citation of NB comment US 11-400, which gives a specific request to review usage of “declaration” for correctness.
- The paper also points to CWG3132 as the place where the relevant paragraph is being updated separately, offering a narrow path for coordination.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never says who is affected by the inconsistency or what practical harm it causes.
- The paper also offers no discussion of why the core language rather than a library or editorial process is the right place to address the issue.
