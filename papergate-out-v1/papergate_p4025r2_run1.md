Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, resting most of its argument on broad assertions about C++’s role in AI and the risk of fragmentation rather than on demonstrated need or evidence. The strongest support comes from its identification of prior art and a concrete external DataFrame project, but the paper does not explain why that existing work is insufficient or why a library approach cannot meet the stated goals. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about who is affected, the lack of implementation experience, and the unsupported claim that only a standard can prevent fragmentation.

- The paper’s most concrete support is its reference to an existing DataFrame library, which at least grounds the idea in prior art.
- The claim that C++ needs a native DataFrame to remain the engine of AI is asserted repeatedly but never backed with examples of current failures or user demand.
- The paper offers no implementation experience or prototype evidence to show the proposal is feasible or well understood.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the audience and impact of the proposed standardization entirely unclear.
