The paper offers almost no support for its own standardization, as it repeatedly gestures toward a future goal without supplying the evidence or reasoning needed to justify action now. The thinnest area is the complete absence of discussion about prior art, implementation experience, or why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving the reader with no basis for evaluating whether the proposed direction is viable or necessary.

- The paper at least states a clear long-term ambition of building existing practice toward eventual adoption of transactional memory in C++.
- It does not address who would be affected by the proposal or what problem it solves for them.
- It provides no implementation experience, prior art, or alternatives, making the case for standardization essentially unsupported.
- It never explains why the standard is the right venue or why a library approach would not suffice, which is a glaring omission for any standardization proposal.
