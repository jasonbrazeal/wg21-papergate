The paper offers a narrow but concrete basis for standardization, chiefly through implementation experience and a plausible explanation for why the restriction originally existed. Its support is thinnest on the questions that matter most for a standards change: who is actually affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and how the change interacts with existing specifications or implementations.

- The strongest support is the claim that most implementations already accept these template specialization names, which suggests low practical risk.
- The paper also grounds the original restriction in a specific historical rationale from the CTAD feature’s author.
- It does not address why a library-level workaround would be inadequate for the stated use case.
- Most glaringly, the paper asserts rather than demonstrates the affected audience and the ease of change for non-conforming implementations.
