Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization: it grounds some design choices in concrete reasoning and points to a companion document for alternatives, but several key justifications are simply asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected and the lack of evidence for implementation experience beyond a bare claim.

- The strongest support comes from the explanation that an iterator-based approach cannot provide the desired property because an iterator must point to an actual object.
- Coordination and interoperability are addressed by pointing to the companion Graph Containers proposal, which catalogs concrete containers, trade-offs, and examples.
- The case for standardization itself is only asserted, with no supporting argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of discussion of who is affected by the proposal.
