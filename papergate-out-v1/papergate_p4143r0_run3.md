Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow, anecdotal basis for standardization, resting its case on a single observation about current compiler behavior while leaving nearly every other justification unaddressed. The support is thinnest around the fundamental questions of who is affected, what alternatives exist, and why the standard is the right venue.

- The strongest support is the specific claim that compilers already evaluate the relevant expression just once in practice.
- The paper does not discuss prior art or alternative approaches that might already solve the problem.
- It never explains why a library solution would be insufficient or why standardization is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected by the issue or what coordination and interoperability concerns arise.
