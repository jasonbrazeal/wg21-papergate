Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers almost no support for its own standardization, resting its motivation on an unattributed anecdote and leaving every substantive question about scope, alternatives, and feasibility unexamined. The thinnest part is not any single weak argument but the absence of argument across the board: there is no audience analysis, no comparison to existing practice, and no reason given for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library or a separate tool.

- The only concrete support offered is a single quoted sentence asserting that the author has heard certain terms in a dozen interactions, with no context, attribution, or explanation of what those terms mean for the proposal.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by the proposed change or what problem it would solve for them.
- It provides no prior art, alternatives, implementation experience, or interoperability discussion, leaving the reader with no basis to judge whether the approach is viable or precedented.
- Most glaringly, the paper never explains why standardization is the right venue, nor why a library or non-standard mechanism would not suffice.
