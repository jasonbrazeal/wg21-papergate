Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of its technical lineage, implementation status, and expected standard-library impact, but it does not consistently connect those details to the case for standardization. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected and the lack of an argument for why the feature belongs in the standard rather than in a library.

- The strongest support comes from the implementation experience, with a linked Clang fork and a Compiler Explorer example demonstrating the proposal in practice.
- The paper also grounds itself well in prior art and alternatives, explicitly building on earlier proposals and identifying the insights that distinguish this approach.
- Coordination and interoperability are addressed with a specific list of standard library types that would become usable under the proposal.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never says who is affected by the change or why the standard is the right venue, leaving the standardization rationale largely asserted rather than argued.
