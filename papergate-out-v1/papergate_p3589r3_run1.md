Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers a mixed case for its own standardization, with its strongest support resting on a single, repeated argument about the need for a common framework across suppliers. That argument is used to justify several distinct sections, but it is not expanded or differentiated, and the paper provides little concrete evidence for the claimed demand from educators or for the implementation experience it cites. The thinnest support appears where the paper asserts benefits or experience without offering specifics, leaving the reader to take those claims on faith.

- The paper’s strongest support is its specific citation of prior art in Stroustrup and Dos Reis’s design alternatives paper, which grounds the proposal in existing discussion.
- The argument that supplier-specific options will diverge without a common framework is concrete and repeated, giving it weight as a standardization rationale.
- The claim that educators have asked for profiles is asserted without any supporting examples, surveys, or named sources.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of detail about implementation experience, since the reference to `[[gsl::suppress]]` is presented as evidence but no deployments, users, or outcomes are described.
