Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper leans heavily on one interoperability argument, repeating it across several sections, but offers little concrete evidence for the claimed educational demand, the insufficiency of a library solution, or the implementation experience behind the proposed form. The strongest support is the cited prior art and the general concern about divergent supplier-specific safety options, while the thinnest support concerns the actual need for standardization as opposed to a shared convention or library.

- The paper’s most substantiated point is that without a common framework, supplier-specific safety options would fragment guarantees and portability, and it cites relevant prior work by Stroustrup and Dos Reis.
- The claim that educators have independently requested profiles is asserted without examples, surveys, or named contexts, leaving that audience’s needs unverified.
- The assertion that a library cannot address the problem is presented with no technical argument or comparison against library-based alternatives.
- The implementation experience is reduced to a single sentence referencing `gsl::suppress`, with no details about deployment scale, failure modes, or lessons that would justify standardization.
