Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of why the function belongs in the standard rather than in user code, and it is strongest when explaining the pitfalls of library-level alternatives and the implementation freedom that standardization would provide. The support is thinnest around evidence of real-world use and around how the proposal fits with existing or adjacent standardization efforts.

- The clearest support comes from the worked examples showing that common library-level approaches can silently fail or require target-specific care.
- The discussion of rejected names and the rationale for standardizing the operation itself adds useful context for reviewers.
- The claim of implementation experience at Intel is asserted but not substantiated with any detail about usage, longevity, or lessons learned.
- The paper does not address coordination or interoperability with related proposals or existing practice outside the author’s implementation.
