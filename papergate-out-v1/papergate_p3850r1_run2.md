Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives only partial support for its own standardization, resting most of its case on the existence of a mature design and implementation in another proposal while leaving several key arguments unstated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of prior art, alternatives, library solutions, or why this belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere.

- The strongest support comes from the reference to P3097R3, which is described as mature, scalable, easy to teach, fully worded, and implemented in GCC.
- The paper asserts that the extension is useful and frequently requested, but offers no evidence or examples to substantiate that claim.
- It does not address why a library-based approach would be insufficient or why standardization is the right venue.
- Most glaringly, the paper omits any treatment of prior art, alternatives, or coordination and interoperability concerns, leaving the proposal’s context and necessity largely unargued.
