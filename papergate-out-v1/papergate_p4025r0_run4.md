Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers only a skeletal case for standardization, with its strongest moments coming from concrete references to prior art and a specific interoperability goal, but it repeatedly asserts rather than demonstrates the need for a standard. The thinnest support lies in the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library cannot suffice, or what implementation experience exists.

- The paper’s most concrete support is its citation of an existing DataFrame library and its stated aim of zero-copy exchange with Python.
- The claim that C++ lacks key components to prevent fragmentation is asserted without evidence or examples of actual fragmentation.
- The paper never addresses who would be affected by standardization or what constituencies are asking for it.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that a library-level solution has been tried and found inadequate.
