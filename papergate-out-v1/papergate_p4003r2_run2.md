Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial, concrete support for its standardization in several areas, particularly through implementation experience, interoperability guarantees, and a clear explanation of why a library solution falls short. The support is thinnest around the fundamental justification for standardization itself and the affected audience, leaving the reader to infer why the language must act and who would benefit.

- The strongest support comes from the reported complete implementation on three platforms, which grounds the proposal in working practice rather than speculation.
- The interoperability section makes a compelling, specific case by showing how the two-argument signature enforces compliance at compile time in both directions.
- The discussion of type erasure and heap allocation gives a concrete reason why a library cannot adequately solve the problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the proposal without a clear constituency or impact analysis.
