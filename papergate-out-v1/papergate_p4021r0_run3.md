Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for standardization, with its strongest support resting on the existence of a reference implementation and a concrete contrast with current language facilities. The argument thins considerably around who is affected, why the standard is needed, and why a library solution is insufficient, since those points are repeated as claims without evidence or elaboration.

- The clearest support comes from the description of a header-only implementation and its use since 2023, which at least grounds the idea in practice.
- The comparison with static_assert, assert, contracts, and profiles gives a useful, specific picture of the existing landscape.
- The paper does not address coordination or interoperability with related standardization efforts, leaving a significant gap in the case for adoption.
- The most glaring omission is the unsupported repetition that no mechanism exists and that a library will not do, which is the central justification but is never substantiated.
