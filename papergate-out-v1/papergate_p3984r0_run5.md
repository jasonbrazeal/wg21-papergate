Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives a mixed account of its own readiness for standardization: it grounds some points in concrete examples and references, but several of the claims most central to justifying a standard are simply asserted. The thinnest support appears where the paper needs to show that existing mechanisms cannot suffice and that the described approach has been meaningfully implemented.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of coordination and interoperability, where the paper identifies concrete friction from divergent tool chains, annotations, and build settings.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives is also reasonably specific, particularly in framing Profiles as an open framework that can accommodate local extensions.
- The claim that a library solution will not do is asserted as a list of general shortcomings without evidence tying those shortcomings to the proposed design.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of why the standard is the right venue, leaving the core standardization rationale unstated.
