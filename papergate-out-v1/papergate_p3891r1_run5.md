Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives a narrow but concrete rationale for the feature by showing how the current grammar suffers without grouping and repetition, and it documents at least one alternative syntax that was considered. Beyond that, however, the case for standardization is largely undeveloped: the proposal does not explain who would be affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the change would interact with existing practice, or whether there is any implementation experience to draw on.

- The strongest support is the specific example of grammatical bloat caused by the absence of grouping and repetition.
- The discussion of an alternative syntax shows some engagement with design trade-offs.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience or evidence that the proposed syntax is workable in practice.
- The paper also never addresses why a library solution would be insufficient or how the change would coordinate with existing standards and implementations.
