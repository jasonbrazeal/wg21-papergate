The paper gives uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete technical grounding in some areas but little evidence for the broader need or viability of the proposal. The thinnest parts are the absence of any discussion of affected users, coordination with related work, or implementation experience beyond a bare assertion.

- The strongest support comes from specific references to existing standard facilities and prior art, showing how the proposed utility relates to and improves upon current practice.
- The claim that a library solution is insufficient is backed by a concrete comparison to a common idiom, though it does not demonstrate why that idiom is inadequate for standardization purposes.
- The paper asserts that C++ needs asynchronous objects to achieve parity with its synchronous ecosystem, but offers no supporting argument or evidence for this foundational premise.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of discussion about who is affected by the proposal or how it coordinates with existing and in-progress standardization efforts.
