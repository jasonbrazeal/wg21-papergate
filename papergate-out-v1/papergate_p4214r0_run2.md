Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the justification needed for standardization, resting its case almost entirely on a conceptual appeal to Lamport’s safety/liveness distinction while leaving the practical and procedural questions untouched. The thinnest areas are those that would tell a committee whether the idea is actionable: who is affected, why the standard is the right vehicle, and whether anyone has tried to implement it.

- The strongest support is the specific reference to Lamport’s framework as a principled basis for treating progress guarantees as part of a concurrency facility’s correctness contract.
- The paper does not address why the standard, rather than a library or guidance document, is the appropriate mechanism.
- The paper offers no implementation experience or evidence that the proposed treatment is feasible in practice.
- The paper does not explain who would be affected by the change or what problem it solves for them.
