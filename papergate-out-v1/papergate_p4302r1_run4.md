Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper grounds its case in concrete procedural history, particularly the Croydon meeting and the earlier P2138R4 attempt, which gives the problem real texture and shows who is affected and when. The support is thinnest where the paper never explains why this belongs in the standard itself rather than in WG21’s standing rules or administrative practice, and it offers no implementation experience with the proposed mechanism as a normative requirement.

- The strongest support comes from specific, dated examples showing papers changing after national body review windows, making the process gap tangible.
- The paper also cites prior art in P2138R4, showing the committee has already recognized and attempted to address a related cooling-period problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard, as opposed to committee policy or a standing document, is the right vehicle for the change.
