Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, repeated justification for its standardization—that expression-oriented returns exist in other languages—and leaves most of the evidentiary burden unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about affected users, implementation experience, interoperability, and why a library solution cannot suffice.

- The strongest support is the concrete reference to Kotlin and Rust as prior art for omitting `return` in expression contexts.
- The paper also gestures toward a standardization rationale by arguing that this direction should either be abandoned or pursued holistically.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience or evidence that the feature is feasible in real compilers.
- Equally absent is any consideration of who would be affected or how the change would coordinate with existing C++ syntax and tooling.
