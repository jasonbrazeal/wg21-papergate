Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper offers substantial, concrete support for its standardization case, drawing on production incidents, author track record, and comparisons with existing libraries to justify the need for a standard. The support is thinnest around formal coordination with adjacent standards or committees and around the practical path from the proposed design to a widely implementable specification.

- The strongest support comes from specific, named failure modes and the authors’ demonstrated success with widely adopted libraries in the same domain.
- The paper also grounds its claims in direct comparisons with Boost.Units, nholthaus/units, Pint, and JSR 385, showing where existing tools fall short.
- The most glaring omission is any substantive discussion of how this proposal would coordinate with other standardization efforts or existing interfaces beyond the C++ ecosystem.
