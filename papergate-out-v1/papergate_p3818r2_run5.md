Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a reasonably specific account of why the interaction it targets matters and why the proposed direction is necessary for the standard, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case largely unargued. The strongest material concerns prior art, alternatives, and the need for a language-level change, while the thinnest support is around affected users, implementation experience, and coordination with other work.

- The paper most concretely supports its case by citing a rejected alternative and explaining why a library-only approach would not suffice.
- It also ties the proposal to a concrete prerequisite for future `constexpr` coroutine behavior, giving the change a forward-looking standardization rationale.
- The most glaring omission is any discussion of who is affected by the current behavior or the proposed change.
- The paper also offers no implementation experience or coordination and interoperability analysis, leaving practical and ecosystem concerns unaddressed.
