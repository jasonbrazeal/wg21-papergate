Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper provides only a narrow sliver of justification for its standardization, resting almost entirely on a single reference to prior art while leaving the core rationale, affected users, and practical viability unexamined. The thinnest areas are those that would normally anchor a proposal: why the standard needs to change, who benefits, and whether the change is implementable in practice.

- The strongest support is the citation to CWG 3112, which at least grounds the discussion in existing committee work and prior consideration.
- The paper does not explain why the change matters or who is affected, leaving the motivating problem unstated.
- It offers no implementation experience or evidence that the proposed direction is feasible in real compilers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard, rather than a library or other mechanism, is the right place to address the issue.
