Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem, the affected audience, and the design history, but it leaves key standardization questions unanswered and offers no implementation evidence for the proposed check. The strongest support comes from the recorded LEWG poll and the discussion of prior approaches, while the thinnest areas are the absence of any rationale tied to the standard itself and the lack of coordination or interoperability analysis.

- The paper documents real committee interest with a specific poll result, which grounds the proposal in actual working-group attention.
- It explains why a pure library solution is insufficient, using a concrete conversion example to support that claim.
- The prior-art discussion shows the evolution from a more ambitious R0 design, giving useful context for the narrowed proposal.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper does not address why the standard is the right venue or how the change would coordinate with existing specifications and implementations.
