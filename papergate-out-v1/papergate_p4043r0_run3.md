The paper grounds its motivation in concrete, recent standardization activity and identifies a broad affected population, but it does not build a case that the proposed direction is ready for the standard. The strongest material concerns why the current situation is problematic, while the document is largely silent on implementation experience, library alternatives, and interoperability.

- The paper offers specific evidence of widespread concern, citing more than twenty national body comments related to C++26 Contracts.
- It situates the problem within active committee discussion by referencing recent papers and unresolved alternative proposals.
- The paper does not address implementation experience, leaving the practical viability of the proposal unsupported.
- It also omits any discussion of why a library solution would not suffice or how the feature would coordinate with existing standard facilities.
