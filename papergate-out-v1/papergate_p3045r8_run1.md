Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper offers substantial, concrete support for standardizing a quantities-and-units vocabulary type, drawing on production incidents, prior art, and audience analysis to justify both the problem and the proposed scope. The support is thinnest where it leans on the same Mars Orbiter anecdote to cover multiple distinct arguments, which makes the case feel more rhetorical than evidentiary in places.

- The strongest support comes from specific production feedback linking the feature to prevention of real, named failure modes such as warehouse robot dimension errors and flight parameter mix-ups.
- The Teachability chapter’s audience tables give a credible, structured account of who would be affected and how the feature would reach different user populations.
- Prior art is well represented with direct references to Au, Boost.Units, and mp-units, grounding the proposal in existing practice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of distinct evidence for why a standard library facility is necessary, since the paper reuses the Mars Orbiter example rather than showing what a non-standard library cannot achieve.
