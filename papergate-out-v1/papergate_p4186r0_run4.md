Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow justification for standardization, resting almost entirely on a general appeal to memory safety and a brief list of unsuccessful prior profile efforts. It does not explain who would be affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the feature would interoperate, or whether any implementation experience exists, leaving the case for standardization largely unbuilt.

- The strongest support is the citation of several recent, unsuccessful profile-related papers, which at least situates the proposal within an active standardization conversation.
- The paper asserts that profiles are the best known approach to memory safety, but offers no reasoning or evidence to connect that belief to a need for standard action.
- The discussion of prior art stops at listing papers without explaining what was learned from their failure or how this proposal differs.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, coordination, or interoperability discussion, leaving no practical grounding for the proposal.
