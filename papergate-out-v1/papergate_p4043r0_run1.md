Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of why the feature’s current state is contested and why delaying or redesigning may be preferable, but it does not build a positive case for standardizing anything in its present form. The strongest material concerns process risk and precedent, while the discussion of what the standard should actually contain is largely absent.

- The paper supports its concern about timing and readiness with specific evidence, including more than twenty national body comments on C++26 Contracts.
- It grounds its argument in a relevant historical precedent by pointing to Concepts’ removal from C++11 and later refined reintroduction in C++20.
- It identifies affected audiences and teachability concerns, but does not explain why the standard is the right venue for the proposed direction.
- It offers no implementation experience, no interoperability or coordination analysis, and no explanation of why a library-based or non-standard solution would be insufficient.
