The paper offers a mixed case for standardization, with its strongest support coming from concrete implementation experience and real-world library evidence, while its weakest moments rely on assertions that are either unsupported or repeated without development. The thinnest support appears where the document claims no mainstream units library distinguishes absolute quantities, deltas, and points, yet offers no evidence or reasoning for why that absence justifies standardization rather than caution.

- The paper grounds its case most convincingly in implementation experience, noting that most features are already implemented in mp-units and that Au provides real-world evidence for a stricter approach.
- The discussion of coordination and interoperability is supported by a specific concern about generic programming and substitutability of numeric types.
- The claim about no mainstream units library distinguishing absolute quantities, deltas, and points is asserted twice without supporting evidence or explanation of its relevance to standardization.
- The paper does not clearly establish why a library solution is insufficient, since the same unsupported claim about other languages is reused rather than developed into an argument for language-level standardization.
