Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers a mixed case for its own standardization, with concrete reasoning in several areas but notable gaps where key claims are simply asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns the motivating importance of the problem and the implementation history, both of which are stated without evidence.

- The strongest support comes from the coordination and interoperability section, which identifies specific existing standard library functions that would align with the proposed return type.
- The “why the standard” and “why a library will not do” sections provide concrete, technically grounded justifications tied to existing API patterns and hardening guarantees.
- The paper does not address who is affected by the proposed change, leaving the scope and impact of the problem unclear.
- The initial claim that the new functions matter is asserted without supporting argument, and the implementation experience section offers no evidence for its conclusion about the only sensible choice.
