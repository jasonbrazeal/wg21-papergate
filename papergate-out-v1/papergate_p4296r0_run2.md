The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization, grounding its motivation and comparison to prior art in concrete examples but leaving several key claims about prevalence, feasibility, and experience asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the proposal argues for standardizing rather than using a library solution and where it relies on unstated implementation experience.

- The strongest support comes from the concrete code example and the specific contrast with P1179R1, which give the reader a clear sense of the problem and the intended improvement.
- The claim that unrelated types are an extremely common pattern is asserted without evidence, weakening the case that the issue is widespread enough to warrant standardization.
- The argument that a library solution cannot address the problem is stated as a conclusion rather than supported with reasoning or examples.
- The paper does not address coordination or interoperability with existing or proposed features, leaving a significant gap in the standardization case.
