Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper makes only a partial case for its own standardization, grounding its naming argument in concrete library precedent but leaving most of the practical and procedural questions untouched. The thinnest support concerns why this belongs in the standard at all, since the paper asserts rather than argues that point and offers no implementation experience, interoperability analysis, or explanation of why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the specific, well-reasoned argument that an unbounded `get` returning `optional` would be inconsistent with existing library conventions.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is adequately grounded, citing named proposals and the reception they received.
- The paper does not address who is affected by the proposed change or what coordination with other library components would be required.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that a standard facility, rather than a library, is necessary.
