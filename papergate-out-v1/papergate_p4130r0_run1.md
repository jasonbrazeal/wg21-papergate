Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, grounding its argument in the compounding effects of the Bandwidth Gap and the contrast between fixed-term Directives and indefinite SD-4 tenure, while leaving most of the surrounding rationale unstated. The support is thinnest where the paper should connect its procedural concerns to the actual standard and to the people or workflows affected.

- The strongest support comes from the specific claim that the Bandwidth Gap amplifies the burden on median delegates, who then rely on social signals rather than absorbing the paper volume.
- The paper also cites a concrete inconsistency: the Directives require fixed terms with confirmation, while SD-4 grants subgroup chairs indefinite tenure with neither.
- The most glaring omission is any discussion of who is affected by the proposed change, which leaves the practical stakes unclear.
- The paper likewise does not address why the standard is the right venue, why a library or other mechanism would not do, or what implementation experience exists.
