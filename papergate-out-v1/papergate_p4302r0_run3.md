Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its procedural concerns in concrete committee history, with specific citations to polls, proposals, and mailing practices that make the problem tangible. The support is strongest when describing the cost to national body experts and the confusion caused by unpublished or mismatched revisions, but it thins considerably when explaining why a formal rule is the necessary remedy rather than a refinement of existing practice.

- The paper gives a clear, sourced account of how the current pre-meeting mailing and review process can waste expert time and create confusion about which revision is under consideration.
- It points to a real failed poll and a specific prior proposal, showing that the issue has been live in the committee rather than invented for this paper.
- The most glaring omission is any sustained argument for why the proposed standardization is the right fix, as opposed to better chairing, clearer links, or informal process adjustments.
