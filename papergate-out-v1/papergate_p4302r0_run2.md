Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its standardization case in concrete committee history and process evidence, though that support is unevenly distributed: several sections lean on the same mailing-deadline argument, while the treatment of alternatives and implementation experience is comparatively thin. The strongest material concerns the need for national-body review before decisions, but the paper does not develop that into a full account of why the proposed rule, specifically, belongs in the standard rather than in committee policy or tooling.

- The paper gives specific, verifiable evidence that late publication has affected real standardization decisions, including the March 2026 Croydon adoption of `std::execution` papers.
- The public adoption poll for P3826R5 is cited with a clear recorded result, showing prior committee interest in the direction.
- The discussion of prior art is limited to a single 2021 proposal, with little comparison to other process rules or later refinements.
- The paper does not clearly distinguish why this requirement must be normative in the standard rather than enforced through WG21 procedures or the paper tracker.
