Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its standardization case in concrete implementation experience and a clearly identified gap in the current ecosystem, though its support is uneven: the strongest evidence concerns feasibility and prior art, while the weakest concerns the specific design choices being proposed. The document reads more like a positioning paper for a particular async model than a fully argued proposal for a specific standard interface.

- The paper’s strongest support comes from working implementations in Capy and Corosio, which demonstrate that the proposed model can be built and used across multiple platforms.
- The historical context—LEWG’s rejection of the Networking TS async model and weak consensus toward sender/receiver—provides a plausible motivation for seeking a new foundation.
- The thinnest support is the near-total absence of discussion about what the proposed standard interface actually is, how it relates to existing sender/receiver work, or what specific wording changes would be required.
