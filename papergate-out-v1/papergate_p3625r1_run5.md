Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin, largely anecdotal case for standardization, with its strongest material going to naming alternatives rather than to the need for or viability of the feature itself. The support is thinnest where a proposal would normally carry the most weight: motivation, implementation experience, and the reasons a library solution or existing standard machinery cannot suffice.

- The most concrete support is the discussion of alternative names and why they were rejected, which at least shows some design reflection.
- The paper asserts real-world need and implementation experience but provides no projects, code, or data to substantiate those claims.
- It does not explain why the standard is the right venue or why a library facility would be inadequate.
- It omits any treatment of coordination, interoperability, or interaction with existing concepts and type traits.
