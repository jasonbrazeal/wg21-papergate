Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem and the intended scope, but its case rests more on analogy and specification tidiness than on demonstrated practice. The thinnest parts are the absence of any implementation experience and the lack of discussion about why a library-level solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support comes from the identification of 53 candidate operations and the reference to a companion paper exploring their defaulting.
- The paper also grounds its approach in the precedent of C++20 defaulted comparisons, which helps situate the proposal within existing standard practice.
- The argument that defaulting could shrink library specification is mentioned but not developed with concrete examples of savings.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience or prototype evidence to show the feature is practical and beneficial in real code.
