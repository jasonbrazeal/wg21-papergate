Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides a generally well-supported case for standardizing its approach, with concrete reasoning behind the need for language support, the limitations of prior proposals, and the interoperability concerns. The support is thinnest where the paper asserts breadth of use-case coverage and implementation experience without offering evidence or detail.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of prior art and alternatives, which clearly identifies a recurring failure mode in earlier proposals and implementations.
- The rationale for standardization is also well grounded, explaining why independent component evolution requires language-level semantics rather than coordinated changes.
- The interoperability section gives a specific, illustrative example that makes the practical stakes of the design choice easy to follow.
- The most glaring omission is the claim that the proposal already covers a broad range of use cases and provides correct defaults, which is asserted without any supporting examples or analysis.
