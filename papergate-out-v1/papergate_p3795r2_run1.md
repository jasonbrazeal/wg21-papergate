Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives a narrow but concrete rationale for its existence by identifying inconsistencies introduced when related facilities were adopted together, but it does not build a broader case for standardization. The strongest material concerns prior art and the specific gap in P2996R13’s access-checking mechanism, while most other sections that would justify a standards change are left unaddressed.

- The paper supports its motivation with specific examples of gaps and inconsistent APIs arising from simultaneously adopted proposals.
- It grounds its approach in a concrete limitation of P2996R13’s `std::meta::access_context`, showing relevant prior art.
- It does not say who is affected by the problem or why the standard, rather than a library, is the right place to address it.
- It offers no implementation experience, coordination discussion, or interoperability analysis to support the proposed direction.
