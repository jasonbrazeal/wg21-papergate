Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably grounded account of why the proposed facility belongs in the standard, with concrete references to existing practice and the limits of current range adaptors, but it leaves several parts of its standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is around implementation experience and the affected audience, where claims are made without enough detail to judge their weight.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of prior art and interoperability, which names specific libraries and explains how existing parallel algorithms impose iterator requirements.
- The rationale for standardization is also well supported, since the paper explains concretely why C++20 range adaptors are a poor fit for parallel execution.
- The implementation experience claim is asserted without evidence about what the implementation revealed or how it validates the design.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving a central part of the standardization argument unexamined.
