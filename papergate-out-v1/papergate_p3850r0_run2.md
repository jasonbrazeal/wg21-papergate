Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides solid, specific support for its standardization case in the areas of prior art, coordination, and the motivating need for a C++26 follow-up, but it leaves several important justification gaps unaddressed. The thinnest support concerns why a library solution is insufficient, who the affected users are, and why standardization is necessary at all, while the implementation experience claim is asserted without evidence.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s grounding in a prior comprehensive analysis and its clear explanation of how the library-only hook addresses known C++26 design concerns.
- The motivation section is specific about the minimal-viable-product nature of C++26 contract assertions and the urgency of extensions.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of why a library will not suffice, especially for a proposal explicitly framed as library-only.
- The implementation experience is merely asserted, with no supporting details about the GCC implementation or its use in practice.
