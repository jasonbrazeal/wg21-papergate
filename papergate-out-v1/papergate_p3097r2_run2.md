Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper grounds several key arguments in concrete technical reasoning, particularly around C++’s undefined behavior, low-level domains, and the practical burden of repeating contract sequences. However, its support is uneven: claims about historical recognition and implementation failures are asserted without evidence, and the affected audience is described rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support appears in the discussion of why C++’s general-purpose nature and undefined behavior make inherited assertions a distinct design problem compared with Eiffel or D.
- The paper also gives specific, plausible reasons why affected developers would reject contracts rather than refactor toward unconditional substitutability.
- The thinnest support is the assertion that previous proposals and implementations “failed to do this correctly,” which is stated without examples or analysis.
- The claim that the importance of this integration has been “recognised repeatedly” is likewise unsupported by citations or historical detail.
