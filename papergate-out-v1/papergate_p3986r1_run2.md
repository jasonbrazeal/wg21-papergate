The paper provides a moderately developed case for standardization, with concrete references to the standardese and a recorded poll outcome, but it leaves several important evidentiary gaps. The strongest support concerns the core problem statement and the direction chosen by the committee, while the thinnest areas are implementation experience and the rationale for why this must be solved in the standard rather than in a library.

- The paper grounds its central problem in specific standard clauses and shows that the receiver must be passed to completion functions, making the lifetime issue concrete.
- It records committee attendance and a poll outcome indicating stronger support for the “Allow” wording, which gives some evidence of directional consensus.
- It sketches an alternate approach and a coordination mechanism through the `inlinable_receiver` protocol, though the treatment remains brief.
- The paper does not address implementation experience at all, leaving no evidence that the proposed direction has been tried in practice.
- It also does not explain why the standard is the necessary venue, despite the availability of library-level mechanisms that might address the same lifetime concern.
