Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides some context for why the feature is contentious and why revisiting it may be warranted, but it does not build a case that the proposed change is ready for standardization. The strongest material concerns existing dissatisfaction and historical precedent, while the practical, implementability, and standards-process questions are left entirely open.

- The paper grounds its relevance in concrete evidence, citing more than twenty national body comments and recent concerns about semantics, safety, deployability, and teachability.
- It offers a meaningful prior-art comparison by pointing to Concepts as a feature that was removed and later standardized only after further design work.
- It does not explain why a library solution would be insufficient or why standardization, rather than further design exploration, is the right next step.
- It provides no implementation experience, no coordination or interoperability analysis, and no discussion of how the proposal would fit into the standard.
