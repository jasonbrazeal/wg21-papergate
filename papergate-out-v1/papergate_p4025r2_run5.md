Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, leaning on broad assertions about C++’s role in AI and a single external project as prior art, while leaving the affected audience, implementation experience, and the insufficiency of a library largely unexamined. The strongest material is the identification of strategic priorities and the reference to an existing DataFrame implementation, but the argument for why this must be a standard rather than a shared library is essentially asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper’s most concrete support comes from naming a prior DataFrame project and listing features that SG19 discussions identified as strategic priorities.
- The claim that C++ needs a native DataFrame to prevent fragmentation is stated as a conclusion without evidence or examples of actual fragmentation.
- The paper does not address who would be affected by standardization or how existing users would migrate or benefit.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or prototype evidence to show the proposed components are viable and ready for standardization.
