Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow evidentiary base for standardization, resting almost entirely on prior related work and a link to draft wording, while leaving the motivating rationale, affected audience, and design context largely unargued. The thinnest support is around the actual need for normative inclusion, since the paper asserts importance without demonstrating who benefits or why existing non-normative resources are insufficient.

- The strongest support comes from the cited prior attempts and formalization requests, which establish that this is a recurring community concern.
- The existence of collaboratively developed draft wording provides some concrete evidence of implementation feasibility.
- The paper does not address who is affected or provide the totals it says would be useful for decision-making.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard, rather than a library or separate reference document, is the right home for this information.
