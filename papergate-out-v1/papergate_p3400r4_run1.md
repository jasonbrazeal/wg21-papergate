Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper grounds most of its central claims in concrete technical scenarios, but its case for standardization leans heavily on a few well-developed examples while leaving the breadth of affected users asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest support appears where the proposal connects its mechanism to existing practice, prior art, and implementation constraints, while the thinnest support is the unsupported claim that widespread Contracts adoption depends on this feature.

- The paper gives specific, technically grounded reasons why existing Contracts semantics cannot express the desired optimization behavior without an assume construct.
- It draws on prior language feature macros and implementation experience to show that the proposed mechanism fits established patterns and real-world build constraints.
- The discussion of platform-specific calling conventions and postcondition checking provides a concrete interoperability argument for the proposal’s necessity.
- The assertion that the feature is “essential” to widespread Contracts adoption is stated without evidence or examples from affected domains.
