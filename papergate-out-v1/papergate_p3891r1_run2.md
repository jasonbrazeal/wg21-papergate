Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives only a narrow, illustrative case for its own standardization: it identifies a real expressiveness problem in the grammar and mentions one alternative syntax, but leaves the broader rationale largely unstated. The support is thinnest around the practical and procedural questions that would normally justify a standards-track change.

- The strongest support is the concrete observation that grouping and repetition would reduce the many existing X-seq and X-list rules.
- The paper also briefly documents a considered alternative syntax, showing some design reflection.
- It does not address who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or why a library cannot provide the capability.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or interoperability discussion to ground the proposal in practice.
