Verdict: Weak (1/14, close to None)

The paper offers almost no support for its own standardization, resting its case on a single unsupported assertion about profiles while leaving every practical question about scope, feasibility, and precedent unanswered. The thinnest part is the complete absence of any discussion of who would be affected, how the feature would interoperate with existing code, or why standardization is the right venue at all.

- The only substantive claim in the paper is that profiles are an incremental approach to safety, but even that is asserted without evidence or elaboration.
- The paper does not address prior art, alternatives, or implementation experience, leaving no basis for evaluating whether the approach is viable or novel.
- It never explains why a library solution would be insufficient or why the feature belongs in the standard rather than in a separate specification or tooling ecosystem.
- The most glaring omission is the total silence on who is affected and how the proposal would coordinate with existing language features, implementations, or other standardization efforts.
