Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with its strongest material concentrated in the motivation and a brief nod to related work, while leaving most of the case for committee action unstated. The argument is thinnest where it matters most: it does not explain who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, or whether anyone has actually tried the approach.

- The paper grounds its motivation in a concrete ergonomic problem around changing rounding modes for correctly rounded calculations.
- It cites a related proposal on reproducible floating-point behavior, giving at least some prior-art context.
- It asserts that the standard is the right venue but offers no supporting reasoning for that claim.
- It never addresses implementation experience, affected users, or why a library cannot solve the problem.
