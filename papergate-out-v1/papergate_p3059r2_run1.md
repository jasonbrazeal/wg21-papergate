Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper leans heavily on the author’s own reasoning and a favorable mailing-list quotation, but it does not assemble the kind of evidence—implementation experience, interoperability analysis, or a clear argument for why a library solution is insufficient—that would make the standardization case persuasive. The thinnest areas are precisely those where a proposal needs concrete grounding: who would actually be affected, why the standard is the right venue, and whether the change has been tried in practice.

- The strongest support comes from the specific example used to illustrate why the exposed constructors provide no observable value.
- The paper also cites direct support from an SG9 mailing-list participant, which gives some indication of committee-facing backing.
- The claim about likely breakage is asserted through a quotation rather than supported with any survey, code search, or concrete evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, coordination and interoperability discussion, or an explanation of why a library-only approach would not suffice.
