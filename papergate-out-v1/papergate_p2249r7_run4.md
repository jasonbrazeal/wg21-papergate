The paper provides some concrete grounding for the practical need it identifies, but much of its case rests on assertions rather than demonstrated evidence, leaving several important justifications undeveloped. The thinnest support appears where the proposal claims common real-world usage, feasibility, and the necessity of standardization without offering corroborating detail.

- The strongest support is the specific claim that mixed smart-pointer/raw-pointer comparisons occur commonly in practice, even though that claim is not substantiated.
- The availability of a working prototype on a GCC branch is asserted but not described in enough detail to establish implementation experience.
- The paper does not address prior art, alternative approaches, or coordination and interoperability concerns.
- The assertion that a library solution cannot suffice is made without supporting reasoning or examples.
