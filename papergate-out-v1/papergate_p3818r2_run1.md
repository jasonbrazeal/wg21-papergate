Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem it is trying to solve and shows that the proposed change has been prototyped, but it leaves important parts of the standardization case largely unargued. The strongest material concerns the specific breakage being addressed and the existence of an implementation, while the weakest concerns how this proposal relates to prior work and to the broader committee or ecosystem context.

- The paper identifies a specific, standards-relevant interaction and supports its relevance with a concrete example of silent breakage and a pointer to the affected specification.
- The implementation experience is the most persuasive practical support, since the change is described as implemented and available for experimentation.
- The paper does not address prior art or alternatives, leaving unclear whether this approach has been considered against other possible remedies.
- Coordination and interoperability are not discussed, so the proposal gives little sense of how it fits with related features, existing practice, or the current C++26 schedule.
