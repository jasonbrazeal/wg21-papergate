Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper makes a consistently concrete case for standardization, grounding its motivation in production failures, audience analysis, prior art, and implementation evidence rather than general enthusiasm. The support is thinnest where it leans on rhetorical counterfactuals about well-known engineering disasters, which illustrate the stakes but do not by themselves demonstrate that the proposed design would have prevented those specific failures.

- The strongest support comes from the implementation experience section, which points to verifiable compiler output showing no runtime cost compared to raw arithmetic.
- The teachability and audience analysis offers unusually specific evidence that the feature is designed for distinct user populations rather than a single imagined audience.
- The discussion of prior art is detailed and comparative, showing exactly how existing libraries diverge on the same example.
- The most glaring omission is any concrete evidence of coordination with other standards bodies or existing industry interfaces beyond retrospective speculation about past failures.
