Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, resting most of its argument on broad assertions about C++’s role in AI and the risk of fragmentation rather than on demonstrated need, implementation experience, or analysis of alternatives. The strongest support comes from its identification of a concrete prior art project and a general motivation around Python/Pandas defaults, but the sections on why the standard is the right venue, why a library will not suffice, and what implementation experience exists are essentially unsupported.

- The paper gives a specific external project as prior art, which at least grounds the idea in existing work.
- The motivation that AI workflows default to Python/Pandas because C++ lacks a native data frame is stated, though not developed into a case for standardization.
- The claim that C++ must standardize these components to prevent fragmentation is asserted without evidence or examples of actual fragmentation.
- The paper provides no implementation experience, no discussion of who is affected, and no explanation of why a library solution would be inadequate.
