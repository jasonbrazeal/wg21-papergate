Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for standardization: it identifies a real syntactic annoyance and sketches a language-level remedy, but it does not develop the surrounding evidence that would let a committee weigh the change. The support is thinnest where the proposal should connect its idea to existing practice, alternative designs, and the practical path through the standard.

- The strongest support is the concrete description of when the repeated type name becomes especially burdensome.
- The paper asserts that a pure language extension avoids library changes, but does not explain why that matters for standardization.
- The proposal does not address prior art, alternative syntaxes, or why a library-level solution would be insufficient.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or coordination discussion that would ground the change in real-world feasibility.
