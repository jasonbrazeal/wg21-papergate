Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete reasoning in a few areas but little or no justification in several places that matter for a committee audience. The strongest material concerns interoperability and the relationship to prior work, while the thinnest concerns the basic case for putting this in the standard rather than a library.

- The paper is most persuasive when it explains that standardizing named constants matters for type compatibility across separately written code.
- It also grounds its synopses in existing proposals and notes that the selected constants come from practical use in mp-units.
- It offers only an assertion, not an argument, for why shipping the framework without SI content would be a problem for the standard itself.
- It does not address who is affected or why a library cannot provide the same functionality, leaving the central standardization question largely unanswered.
