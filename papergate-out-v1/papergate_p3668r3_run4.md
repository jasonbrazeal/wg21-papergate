Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem and the intended scope, but it leaves the case for standardization incomplete because it does not show that the feature can be specified and implemented in practice. The strongest material concerns the breadth of affected operations and the possibility of simplifying the library specification, while the thinnest areas are the absence of implementation experience and the lack of a clear rebuttal to a library-based alternative.

- The paper identifies 53 candidate operations and points to a companion proposal, which gives the standardization effort a concrete target.
- It argues that defaulting postfix operations could shrink the library specification by expressing currently duplicated canonical behavior more directly.
- The discussion of prior art acknowledges existing boilerplate-reduction approaches and attempts to explain why they fall short.
- The paper does not address implementation experience, leaving open whether the proposed defaulting can be reliably specified and adopted by compilers.
