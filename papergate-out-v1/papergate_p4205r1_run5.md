Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with some useful engagement on prior art and interoperability but little direct argument for why the feature belongs in the standard or why a library solution would be insufficient. The thinnest areas are the absence of any motivation tied to user impact and the unsupported claim of implementation experience.

- The strongest support comes from the concrete discussion of prior art and the specific gap in Boost.Ranges and range-v3 that explains why the Ranges library lacks a `Searcher` overload.
- The paper also shows some engagement with coordination by noting the SG9 request to investigate compatibility between range-based and existing searchers.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any stated rationale for why the standard should adopt this feature or who would benefit from it.
- The implementation experience is merely asserted with a link, offering no evidence of usability, completeness, or lessons learned.
