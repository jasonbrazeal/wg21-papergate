Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers some concrete grounding for its standardization case, chiefly through references to existing standard library facilities and a reference implementation, but much of the argument rests on repeated assertions rather than demonstrated need or analysis. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of why a library solution would not suffice and the lack of evidence for claims about widespread use or interoperability.

- The strongest support comes from the cited implementation experience, including a publicly available reference implementation.
- The discussion of prior art is usefully specific in naming `proxy` and describing the overlapping design space.
- The paper repeatedly asserts that the need is real and recurring but does not substantiate the claimed widespread use of type-erasure.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never addresses why a library-based approach would be inadequate, leaving a central standardization question unanswered.
