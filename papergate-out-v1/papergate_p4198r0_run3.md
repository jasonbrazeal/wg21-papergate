Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers only a narrow, repeated rationale for standardization, centered on ABI constraints and runtime indexing, while leaving most of the case for the proposal unsubstantiated. The strongest support is the concrete claim about ABI limitations, but nearly every other category—prior art, implementation experience, affected users, and the need for a standard rather than a library—is asserted without evidence or elaboration.

- The paper’s most concrete support is its repeated assertion that existing tuple implementations cannot optimize runtime indexing without breaking the ABI.
- The mention of a reference implementation is the only nod toward implementation experience, but it is not described or linked in any useful way.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the proposed change or why existing alternatives are insufficient beyond the single ABI claim.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any supporting detail for prior art, library-based solutions, or interoperability concerns, leaving the standardization case largely unsupported.
