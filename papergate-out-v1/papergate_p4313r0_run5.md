Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives concrete evidence that the boilerplate problem is real and that implementers have working prototypes, but it does not connect those facts to a need for standardization rather than a library or shared utility. The thinnest parts are the unsupported assertions about why the standard is the right venue and why existing library facilities are insufficient.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with links to working prototypes on current compilers and a public repository.
- The paper also grounds the problem in real code, citing repeated boilerplate in LLVM’s standard library headers.
- It points to prior art and a clear design lineage, showing the idea has been explored and refined outside the committee.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any argument for why standardization is necessary, since the paper merely asserts the feature is sought after and dismisses library alternatives without explanation.
