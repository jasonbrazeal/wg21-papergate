Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives only a narrow slice of the justification needed for standardization, focusing almost entirely on conceptual distinctions around undefined behavior while leaving the practical and procedural case largely unbuilt. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, why the standard is the right venue, or how the proposal would interact with existing library and language machinery.

- The strongest support is the concrete reference to prior art in N3248 and the distinction between narrow and wide contracts.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by the change or what problem they would experience in practice.
- It offers no discussion of why a library solution would be insufficient or why standardization is necessary.
- There is no implementation experience, coordination strategy, or interoperability analysis to ground the proposal in real-world use.
