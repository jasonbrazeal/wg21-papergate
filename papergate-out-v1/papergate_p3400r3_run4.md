Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper generally grounds its standardization argument in concrete technical gaps and prior decisions, though the support is uneven: several sections cite specific mechanisms and history, while the claim about widespread adoption is asserted without evidence. The thinnest part of the case is the assertion that the functionality is essential across domains, which is never connected to actual user needs, existing practice, or reported friction.

- The strongest support comes from the explicit linkage to the C++26 Contracts MVP and the stated intent to fill a gap deliberately left for this framework.
- The discussion of why a library solution is insufficient is concrete, pointing to build-time configuration burdens and optimization-related link failures.
- The most glaring omission is the unsupported claim that the proposal is essential to unhindered and widespread adoption of Contracts, with no examples, domains, or user reports to substantiate it.
