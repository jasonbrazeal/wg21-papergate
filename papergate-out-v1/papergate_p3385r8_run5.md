Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a partial account of why a uniform mechanism would be useful, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case unstated, especially around affected users, library solutions, and the need for standard rather than implementation-specific action. The strongest support is concrete but narrow, and the thinnest areas are exactly those a committee would need to weigh before taking on new language machinery.

- The paper points to existing compiler explorer availability as concrete implementation experience.
- It identifies prior work in P3678R0 and notes an SG7 recommendation to merge that material.
- It asserts that a uniform vehicle would save repeated committee design effort, but does not develop that claim with examples or evidence.
- It does not address who is affected, why a library cannot suffice, or what specifically requires standardization rather than a shared convention.
