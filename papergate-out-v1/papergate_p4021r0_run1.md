Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers a narrow but concrete case for its mechanism, grounded mainly in the absence of an existing language feature and a small amount of implementation experience. The support is thinnest around the claimed user base, the need for standardization rather than a library solution, and any discussion of how the feature would interact with the rest of the language.

- The strongest support is the header-only implementation experience, which at least demonstrates the intended behavior in practice.
- The discussion of existing alternatives is specific, though it mostly restates the gap rather than showing why filling it requires a standard language feature.
- The claim that the feature has been in use since 2023 is asserted without evidence of scale, context, or lessons learned.
- The paper does not address coordination or interoperability with other C++ features, leaving the standardization case incomplete.
