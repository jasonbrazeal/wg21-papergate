Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a partial but uneven account of why the proposed change belongs in the standard, with its strongest material concentrated in the discussion of language-level limitations and prior wording approaches. The case thins considerably around real-world impact, implementation experience, and coordination with existing practice, leaving several central questions unaddressed rather than answered.

- The clearest support comes from the explanation that existing template-based workarounds cannot reliably distinguish a type-aware allocation function from ordinary declarations.
- The discussion of CWG1676 and CWG1669 offers concrete prior-art grounding for the proposed wording direction.
- The paper asserts implementation experience but provides no evidence of prototypes, usage, or compiler support.
- The affected audience and standardization rationale are largely absent, with no account of who encounters the problem or why a standard mechanism is preferable to the status quo.
