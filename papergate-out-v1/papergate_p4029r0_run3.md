Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper leans heavily on assertion rather than evidence, offering only a few concrete references to prior work while leaving its central claims about incompatibility and performance largely unsubstantiated. The thinnest support appears wherever the argument turns from describing existing models to explaining why standardization—rather than a library or a different proposal—is necessary.

- The strongest support is the specific identification of P4003 or similar Direct Style concepts as a preferred alternative with claimed performance and ergonomic benefits.
- The paper asserts that P2300’s allocation patterns are incompatible with low-latency networking, but provides no measurements, examples, or analysis to back that claim.
- Implementation experience is invoked only through a general statement about Asio/Beast performance, with no details about actual use, testing, or deployment.
- The paper does not address coordination or interoperability with existing networking or sender/receiver work, leaving a major standardization concern entirely open.
