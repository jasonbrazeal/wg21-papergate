Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives concrete, specific support for the problem it addresses and for existing practice, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case largely unargued, especially the need for a standard facility rather than a library solution and the affected user population. The strongest material concerns real-world duplication and prior art, while the rationale for standardizing this particular mechanism is asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper clearly identifies the forced duplication between contract assertions and regular code, and cites a directly relevant prior proposal.
- It offers credible implementation experience by pointing to widely used always-on assertion mechanisms in production codebases.
- It does not address who is affected beyond a general reference to users needing always-enforce assertions.
- It never explains why a library cannot provide the desired behavior, leaving a central part of the standardization case unexamined.
