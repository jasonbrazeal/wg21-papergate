Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, concentrating on a specific wording strategy and a single technical necessity while leaving most of the broader justification unstated. Its support is thinnest where it matters most for a standards-track document: who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the change interacts with existing practice, and whether anyone has actually tried it.

- The strongest support is the concrete observation that receivers must be passed to the completion functions, which anchors the proposed wording change in a real requirement.
- The paper also identifies a genuine gap between what implementers must compute and what the current wording provides.
- It does not address who is affected by the change or what the practical consequences would be for users and implementers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, coordination discussion, or argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than in guidance or a library.
