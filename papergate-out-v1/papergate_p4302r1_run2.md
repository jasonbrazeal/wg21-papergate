Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives concrete, specific support for the existence of a process problem and for the likely audience affected, but it leaves the standardization rationale largely implicit and does not explain why the proposed remedy belongs in the standard rather than in WG21’s standing rules or tooling.

- The strongest support comes from the Croydon example, where six of nineteen papers changed during the meeting, directly illustrating the timing gap the proposal targets.
- The paper also cites prior art in P2138R4 and identifies the pre-meeting mailing as the coordination point that gives national bodies time to review.
- The thinnest part is the absence of any argument for why the standard itself is the right vehicle, as opposed to a change in committee process or practice.
