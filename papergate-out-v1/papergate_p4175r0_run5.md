The paper offers some concrete support for its standardization, particularly around existing implementation practice and the historical rationale for the original restriction, but it leaves several important parts of the case asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the lack of evidence for widespread user dependence, the absence of any discussion about why a library solution would not suffice, and the complete silence on coordination or interoperability concerns.

- The strongest support is the specific claim that most implementations already accept the construct and that the remaining ones would find the change easy.
- The paper grounds the origin of the restriction in a named prior proposal and explains it as a specification-simplicity choice rather than a deliberate design constraint.
- The argument for user dependence is asserted as “highly likely” without examples, surveys, or concrete reports of code that would break or benefit.
- The paper does not address why a library-level workaround is insufficient or how the change interacts with other standardization efforts.
