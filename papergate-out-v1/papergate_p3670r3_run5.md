Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides some concrete grounding for its motivation by citing related proposals and a specific limitation in P2662R3, but it leans heavily on assertion for the parts that would justify standardization, such as user impact, implementation confidence, and the absence of viable alternatives. The thinnest support is around who is affected and why the standard is the right venue, where the argument is stated rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the specific identification of a gap in P2662R3 and the reference to related in-flight proposals that frame the design space.
- The claim of positive feedback from Clang and GCC implementations is asserted without examples, links, or discussion of what that feedback addressed.
- The paper does not discuss coordination with other proposals, interoperability concerns, or why a library-level solution would be inadequate.
- The implementation experience section offers only the author’s confidence that Clang can implement the feature, with no prototype, patch, or compiler vendor statement to support that claim.
