Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for standardization: it identifies one concrete design concern and gestures at prior naming alternatives, but leaves the central questions of why this belongs in the standard, why a library cannot suffice, and how it would interoperate with existing practice essentially unexamined. The support is thinnest where it matters most for a standardization proposal, namely in demonstrating need, implementability, and coordination.

- The strongest support is the specific observation that this would be the first fallible `get()` in the standard library, which at least frames a real design tension.
- The discussion of prior art is concrete in naming P3091’s alternatives, though it does not explain why those failed beyond a lack of enthusiasm.
- The paper does not address why the standard is the right venue, leaving the core standardization rationale unstated.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, coordination, or interoperability discussion, which leaves the proposal’s practical viability unsupported.
