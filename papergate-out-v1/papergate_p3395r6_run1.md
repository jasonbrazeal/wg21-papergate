Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper grounds several of its central claims in concrete implementation details and prior work, but its case is uneven because the discussion of who would actually benefit is asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest support appears where the proposal explains why existing mechanisms fall short and why a library-only solution is insufficient, while the thinnest support concerns the absence of any evidence that users have wanted or requested this functionality.

- The paper gives specific, technically grounded reasons for why the current inserter behavior is problematic and why addressing encoding through the standard is preferable to alternatives.
- The proposal is backed by implementation experience in {fmt} and engages with prior art such as P2930 in a substantive way.
- The claim that no one has requested this functionality over several years is stated without supporting evidence, leaving the affected-user case largely unsubstantiated.
- The document does not establish a demonstrated demand or clear constituency for the proposed standardization, which is the most glaring omission in its argument.
