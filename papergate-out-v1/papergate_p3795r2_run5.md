Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper gives a narrow but concrete rationale for its existence by pointing to inconsistencies and gaps introduced when several related proposals were adopted together, but it does not build a broader case for standardization. The support is thinnest around the practical and procedural questions that would normally justify taking this work into the standard.

- The strongest support is the specific observation that independently adopted papers created inconsistencies, gaps in coverage, and inconsistent APIs.
- The paper cites relevant prior art in the form of the Reflection for C++26 proposal, grounding the work in an existing standardization context.
- It does not explain who is affected by the problem or why the standard, rather than a library or other mechanism, is the right place to address it.
- It offers no implementation experience, coordination notes, or interoperability discussion, leaving the practical viability of the proposal largely unexamined.
