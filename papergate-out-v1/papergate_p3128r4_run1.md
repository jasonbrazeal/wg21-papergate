Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow, unsupported claim about algorithmic efficiency, leaving nearly every question relevant to standardization unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of motivation for putting this in the standard, any discussion of affected users, or evidence that a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper’s strongest support is a specific, if brief, comparison of merge-based intersection against nested-loop and hash-based alternatives for sparse graphs.
- It does not explain why the standard, rather than an existing or potential library, is the right home for these algorithms.
- It provides no implementation experience, usage data, or interoperability considerations to ground the proposal in practice.
- It never identifies who would be affected by standardization or what problem in the current ecosystem the proposal is meant to solve.
