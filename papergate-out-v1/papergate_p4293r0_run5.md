Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow, indirect justification for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on its relationship to P4288 rather than on independent evidence of need, usage, or design validation. The support is thinnest where a proposal normally needs to be strongest: in showing who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and that the design has been tested in practice.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s engagement with P4288’s specific claim and its attempt to supply the missing algorithm that P4288 posits.
- The paper does not address who would use the proposed facility or what problem they currently face without it.
- It offers no implementation experience, no interoperability discussion, and no argument for why a library solution would be insufficient.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why this belongs in the C++ standard at all, beyond its rhetorical role in another proposal’s argument.
