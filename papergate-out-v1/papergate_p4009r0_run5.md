Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, grounding its motivation in the intensity of objections to the current draft and in the existence of alternative designs, but leaving several essential parts of the standardization case unstated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience, library alternatives, or interoperability, and the assertion that alternatives are palatable is not backed by evidence.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s concrete account of why the current draft causes severe objections and why a different direction is being considered.
- It also points to prior alternative designs and explains some major concerns with them, which helps frame the design space.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper does not address who is affected by the proposal or what implementation experience exists, leaving the practical case for standardization largely unexamined.
