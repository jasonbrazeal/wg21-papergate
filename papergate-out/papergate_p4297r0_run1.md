Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper provides substantial, concrete support for its standardization case, grounding its claims in deployment history, existing vendor practice, and the current state of EWG review. The support is thinnest where it relies on the assertion that foundational clauses will pass and that remaining cases are mechanical, since that forward-looking claim is not yet demonstrated by committee action.

- The strongest support comes from a decade of shipping implementations under vendor names, which gives the named-check-set architecture real deployment credibility.
- The paper also makes a clear case that existing library mechanisms cannot deliver what it proposes, since none of the shipped checkers route through the C++26 contracts handler or use Labels.
- The most glaring omission is any evidence that the foundational clauses have actually gained consensus in EWG, leaving the claim that the rest is mechanical dependent on an outcome the paper itself says is still under review.
