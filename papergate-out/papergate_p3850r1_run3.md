Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of implementation readiness and prior design work, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case largely implicit, especially around why this needs to be in the standard rather than addressed by existing or library-level mechanisms. The strongest material concerns feasibility and prior analysis, while the weakest concerns motivation and necessity.

- The paper’s strongest support comes from its claim of complete wording and a working GCC implementation, which at least signals that the proposal is implementable.
- It also grounds the design discussion in a prior comprehensive analysis of use cases and trade-offs, giving the proposal some technical context.
- The thinnest support is the absence of any discussion of why a library solution would not suffice or why standardization is the right venue.
- The paper also asserts the feature is frequently requested and broadly useful without offering evidence, leaving the affected-user case largely unsupported.
