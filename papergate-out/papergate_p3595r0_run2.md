Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives concrete, useful evidence that the feature has been explored in real implementations and that it addresses a genuine need for consumers of annotated code, but it stops short of making the affirmative case for why this belongs in the C++ standard. The strongest material concerns implementation experience and the specific needs of code consumers, while the argument for standardization itself is essentially absent.

- The paper supports its relevance with concrete examples of build-time controls serving code consumers whose needs differ from the author’s.
- It offers tangible implementation evidence through partial GCC and Clang branches available on Compiler Explorer.
- It does not address why the standard is the right venue, how the feature would coordinate with existing or future standards work, or why a library solution would be insufficient.
