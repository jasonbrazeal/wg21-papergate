Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some support for its naming argument, but it does not build a broader case for standardization, leaving most of the practical and procedural questions untouched. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience, or why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the specific reasoning about why a `get` returning `optional` would be inconsistent with existing library conventions.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is concrete, including a named alternative and an explanation of why it is not proposed.
- The paper asserts that the inconsistency matters for the standard, but does not explain what problem that inconsistency causes in practice.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed facility has been tried and found useful.
