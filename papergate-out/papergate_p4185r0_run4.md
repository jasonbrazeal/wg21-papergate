Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper grounds much of its case in concrete implementation experience, community feedback, and prior design discussion, but it leaves the central argument for why this must be standardized—rather than remain a library capability—largely asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the paper contrasts its approach with other languages and where it must justify standardization specifically, since those sections offer claims without the same level of specific evidence found elsewhere.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience, with most features already built in mp-units and the remaining two described as design-complete.
- Real-world feedback and independent user requests, including a linked discussion and input from multiple national bodies, give the ergonomic and modeling problems credible weight.
- The discussion of alternatives and prior art is specific about trade-offs, though it does not clearly establish why the proposed model is the right one for the standard.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of supporting evidence for the claim that no mainstream units library distinguishes absolute quantities, deltas, and points, which is presented as a reason for standardization without citation or elaboration.
