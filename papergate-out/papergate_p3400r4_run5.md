Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper grounds several of its standardization arguments in concrete implementation experience and specific technical constraints, but the overall case is uneven because the claimed breadth of impact is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the proposal argues for essential, widespread adoption without offering evidence from users or domains.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience with C++26 Contracts, particularly the need for translation units and violation handlers to work across different standard library implementations.
- The discussion of why a library solution is insufficient is also concrete, citing the burden of maintaining supplementary configuration files for selective hardening.
- The weakest point is the claim that the functionality is essential to unhindered and widespread adoption, which is stated without supporting examples or domain evidence.
