Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers concrete support for its standardization case mainly through implementation experience and a specific account of the types it would enable, but several arguments that matter for committee adoption are asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and why a library solution would not suffice.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, which names the implementation, the compilers, and the range of user-defined types tested.
- The paper also grounds its motivation in specific type categories and references a related proposal for missing transparent shift function objects.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any coordination or interoperability discussion, leaving the proposal’s relationship to adjacent standardization work unclear.
