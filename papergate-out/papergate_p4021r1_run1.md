Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers a moderately developed case for standardization, with its strongest grounding in the absence of an existing language mechanism and the existence of a reference implementation, but it leaves several important evidentiary and coordination questions largely untouched. The thinnest support concerns who is actually affected and how the feature would fit with ongoing or adjacent standardization efforts.

- The clearest support comes from the specific claim that no general mechanism currently allows compile-time assertions inside ordinary functions from regular compilers.
- The reference implementation and reported use since 2023 provide some practical backing, though the paper does not identify the code bases or scale of use.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of coordination or interoperability with related efforts such as contracts or profiles.
