Verdict: Excellent (13/14)

The paper provides substantial, concrete support for its standardization case in most areas, particularly in explaining why the feature cannot be achieved through library-only means and in detailing the coordination required across working groups. The support is thinnest when the paper asserts the proposal’s essential role in widespread Contracts adoption without offering evidence or examples of affected domains or users.

- The strongest support comes from the specific explanation of how C++26 Contracts currently forces supplementary configuration files for hardening, making the need for in-source control clear.
- The paper also grounds its case well in implementation realities, such as the tendency of unevaluated expressions to be optimized away before link-time errors surface.
- The most glaring omission is the unsupported claim that the functionality is essential to unhindered and widespread adoption, which is asserted rather than demonstrated with affected users or domains.
