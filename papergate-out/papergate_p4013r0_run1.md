Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization: it provides concrete implementation evidence and some discussion of alternatives, but leaves the motivating problem, affected users, and interoperability almost entirely unstated. The thinnest parts are the justifications for why this belongs in the standard and why a library solution is insufficient, both of which are asserted rather than argued.

- The strongest support is the linked compiler implementation and compiler explorer availability, which shows the change is at least technically realizable.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is grounded in a specific existing codebase and a related proposal, giving the reader some context for the design space.
- The paper does not explain why the problem matters or who is affected, leaving the core motivation for standardization unclear.
- The claim that a library cannot adequately solve the problem is asserted without supporting reasoning, which is a glaring omission for a proposal aimed at changing the core language.
