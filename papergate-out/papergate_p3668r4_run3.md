Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of why defaulted postfix operators would reduce boilerplate and why existing alternatives fall short, but it leaves some important parts of the standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the problem being solved and the limitations of library-based workarounds, while the thinnest support surrounds the claimed breadth of applicability and the absence of implementation experience.

- The paper most convincingly supports its motivation by explaining how defaulting would reduce user error and make unusual hand-written operators more visible.
- Its discussion of prior art and library alternatives is grounded in specific shortcomings of current approaches.
- The claim that 53 candidate operations could be defaulted is presented without evidence or examples tying that number to the proposal’s scope.
- The paper does not address implementation experience, leaving the practical viability of the feature unsupported.
