Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper grounds its motivation in concrete production practice and relevant prior work, but it does not directly argue why the proposed facility belongs in the standard rather than in a library or coding convention. The strongest support appears in the discussion of existing always-on assertion patterns and the cited proposal for reliably non-ignorable contracts, while the case for standardization itself remains largely asserted.

- The paper offers specific, production-oriented examples of terminating enforcement semantics, which lends credibility to the claimed need.
- It cites prior art in WG21, showing awareness of related standardization efforts and positioning the proposal within that conversation.
- The claim that the proposal is a pure extension is stated without explaining what standardization would enable that existing practice cannot already achieve.
- The paper does not address why a library-level or project-level always-on assertion facility would be insufficient.
