Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a partial but uneven account of why this facility belongs in the standard, with concrete implementation and prior-art details doing much of the persuasive work, while the rationale for standardization itself remains largely asserted rather than argued. The thinnest areas are the absence of discussion about affected users, coordination with other proposals, and why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support comes from the sample implementation and the demonstration of how existing reflection facilities fall short.
- The paper points to library mandate clauses as evidence that implementers already need this capability, though it does not develop that point into a full standardization argument.
- It does not address who would be affected by adding the trait or how it would interact with related standardization efforts.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any explanation for why this cannot be provided as a library facility rather than a core or standard-library addition.
