Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for standardization: it asserts that the proposed API’s advantages outweigh consistency concerns, but it does not develop that claim with evidence, comparisons, or user impact. The support is thinnest where the proposal should be most persuasive—prior art, implementation experience, and the reasons a library solution or existing standard facility cannot suffice.

- The strongest support is the availability of an implementation link, though the paper does not describe what that implementation demonstrates.
- The paper asserts that the new API’s advantages are clear, but it does not explain what those advantages are or why they justify the inconsistency it acknowledges.
- The proposal does not address who is affected, what alternatives were considered, or how the feature would coordinate with existing node-based sequences.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library.
