Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper provides a mixed but uneven case for standardization, with its strongest material concentrated in discussions of prior art, interoperability, and the concrete existence of a specification and compiler implementation. The argument becomes notably thinner when it turns to why a library solution is insufficient and what implementation experience actually demonstrates, where broad assertions stand in for evidence.

- The paper gives specific support for coordination and interoperability by citing an existing specification and an experimental implementation in a major compiler.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is grounded in concrete reasoning about profiles as an open framework and the need to accommodate local needs.
- The claim that a library will not do is asserted as a list of general shortcomings without connecting those shortcomings to the proposed design.
- The paper offers no substantive implementation experience beyond naming prototypes and guidelines, leaving the practical viability of the proposal largely unestablished.
