Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper makes a reasonably well-supported case for standardization, drawing on concrete implementation experience, cross-language precedent, and clear motivation tied to existing standard behavior. The support is thinnest around coordination and interoperability, where the paper does not address how the proposed change interacts with other parts of the standard or with existing user expectations.

- The strongest support comes from the proposal’s grounding in a widely used implementation that has been available for over six years.
- The paper also benefits from clear evidence of committee interest and from prior art in several major languages and runtimes.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of coordination with related standardization efforts or interoperability concerns.
