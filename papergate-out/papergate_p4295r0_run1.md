Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives only partial support for its own standardization, mainly by connecting the idea to earlier concurrent queue history and offering a link to a partial implementation. The case is thinnest on the questions that matter most for a standards proposal: who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and how the feature would coordinate with existing or future library components.

- The strongest support is the reference to N3353, which grounds the single-ended interface in prior committee discussion and explains why it was separated out.
- A partial implementation is mentioned, but no details are given about its completeness, use, or lessons learned.
- The paper does not address who would be affected by the proposal or what problem they face beyond a brief statement about unidirectional communication.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library.
