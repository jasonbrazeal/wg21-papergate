Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives concrete support for its formal-safety claims and for its relationship to prior work, but it leaves several standardization-relevant questions largely asserted rather than demonstrated, particularly around prevalence, implementability, and library alternatives. The thinnest support concerns who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and whether the approach has any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s specific explanation of how its approach upgrades P1179R1 and proves invalidation impossible in the covered cases.
- The discussion of why the standard is the right venue is weakened by an unsupported claim that standardization can proceed gradually despite the large volume of rules.
- The paper asserts that the pattern of unrelated types is extremely common without offering evidence for that prevalence.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or coordination and interoperability discussion, leaving the practical path to standardization unclear.
