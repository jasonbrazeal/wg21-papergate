Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper backs its standardization case with unusually concrete evidence, from public poll records and issue-tracker history to decade-old vendor implementations and the C++26 contracts handler. The support is strongest on prior art, implementation experience, and the limits of library-only approaches, while it is thinnest on showing that the proposed layering has actually been exercised in practice rather than described architecturally.

- The paper grounds its relevance in specific, dated WG21 activity, including a recorded EWG poll and a public papers-tracker issue, which makes the standardization context tangible rather than assumed.
- It demonstrates real-world precedent through named vendor check-sets shipped since 2016, tying the proposal to a decade of implementation history.
- The most glaring omission is any evidence that the proposed layering or configuration mechanism has been prototyped or used beyond the paper’s own diagrams and wording review.
