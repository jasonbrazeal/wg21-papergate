Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides some useful naming precedent for the proposed operations, but it leaves most of the standardization rationale unstated, so the case for adoption rests on a narrow foundation. The strongest material concerns discoverability and cross-language terminology, while the absence of any discussion of implementation experience, library feasibility, or why the standard specifically must act leaves the proposal largely unsupported.

- The paper’s clearest support comes from its survey of other languages using the terms ceiling and floor division, which establishes naming precedent and discoverability.
- It also argues, briefly, that users will search for these operations under those established names, supporting the choice of interface.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving the need for standardization itself unexplained.
- It offers no implementation experience or discussion of prior art and alternatives, which are glaring omissions for a proposal seeking committee adoption.
