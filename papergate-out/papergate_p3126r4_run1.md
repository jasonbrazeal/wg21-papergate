Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of its design influences, implementation experience, and intended minimality of requirements, but it does not build a persuasive case for why this work belongs in the C++ standard rather than in a library. The strongest support is practical and technical, while the argument for standardization itself is largely asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper grounds its approach in specific prior art and provides a reference implementation, which gives the proposal tangible technical substance.
- It explains that the named requirements are deliberately minimal to accommodate a wide range of graph data structures.
- The discussion of affected users is absent, leaving no clear picture of who would benefit from standardization.
- The central claim that no existing library meets the same requirements is repeated as a reason for standardization without explaining why that gap cannot be filled by a non-standard library.
