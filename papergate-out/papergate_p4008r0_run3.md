Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly asserted case for standardization: it names a real motivation and gestures at a plausible technical foundation, but it does not substantiate its central claims about alternatives, library solutions, or implementation feasibility. The support is thinnest where the paper should be most concrete—prior art, interoperability, and evidence that the proposed approach can actually be standardized and adopted.

- The strongest support is the specific motivation that C++ must retain compatibility with legacy code while addressing well-known unsafe C-compatible behaviors.
- The claim that existing safe-subset efforts either remove necessary low-level power or are unenforceable is asserted without examples or analysis.
- The paper offers no discussion of coordination with existing standards, affected users, or interoperability concerns.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposal is viable beyond a conceptual sketch.
