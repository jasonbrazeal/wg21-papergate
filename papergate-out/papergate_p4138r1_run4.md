Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the language problem and the implementation landscape, but it leaves important parts of the standardization case largely implicit, especially around affected users and the need for a standard change rather than an implementation or coding convention fix.

- The strongest support comes from the compiler survey, which shows broad agreement among major implementations and gives the proposal a factual anchor.
- The discussion of why a library solution cannot work is specific and directly tied to overload resolution behavior.
- The historical reference to N1821 grounds the design question in prior committee thinking.
- The most glaring omission is any treatment of who is affected and how common the problematic pattern is in real code, which weakens the urgency of standardization.
