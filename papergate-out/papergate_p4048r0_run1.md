Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper grounds its standardization case in concrete history, shipping implementations, and real adoption signals, but it leaves the central rationale for ISO standardization largely implicit. The strongest material concerns ecosystem traction and interoperability requirements, while the thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why a standard is needed or why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper offers specific evidence of long-standing committee interest and real-world library adoption, which supports the relevance of the work.
- It clearly identifies the interoperability constraint between coroutine-native and sender-based code as a coordination requirement.
- It does not address why standardization, rather than continued library evolution, is necessary.
- It provides no implementation experience from the proposed design itself, leaving the practical viability of the proposal unsubstantiated.
