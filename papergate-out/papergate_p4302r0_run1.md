Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper provides a fair amount of concrete support for its standardization case, drawing on specific committee history, public polling, and recent adoption patterns. The support is strongest when it points to observable process failures and existing precedent, but it is thinnest when connecting those observations to the particular remedy being proposed.

- The paper grounds its relevance in a specific public adoption poll that passed unanimously, showing clear committee interest.
- It cites a concrete 2026 case where execution papers were adopted from revisions that had not been available for pre-meeting national body review.
- It references prior work by Ville Voutilainen on design-to-specification engagement, establishing that the underlying concern has a recognized history.
- The most glaring omission is a direct explanation of how the proposed standardization change would have prevented the cited Croydon adoption problem or would operate in practice.
