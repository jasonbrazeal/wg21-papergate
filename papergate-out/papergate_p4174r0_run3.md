Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives only a thin account of why this belongs in the standard, resting most of its case on a single motivating pain point and leaving the broader standardization questions largely unexamined. The strongest material concerns the problem statement, while the argument becomes noticeably weaker when it moves from “this is useful” to “this must be standardized.”

- The paper is most concrete when explaining the current awkwardness of fold-expanded constraints and the lack of deduplication in concept-based checks.
- It asserts implementation experience across GCC, Clang, and MSVC, but offers no details, links, or evidence of real use.
- It does not engage with Boost.Mp11 as prior art beyond a passing mention, nor explain why a mature library solution is insufficient.
- The case for standardization itself is essentially absent: there is no discussion of why a library cannot serve, no coordination or interoperability analysis, and no supported claim about who is affected.
