Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides some useful context for why a stable catalog of undefined and IFNDR behavior would be valuable, but it does not build a complete case for standardizing the proposed annex. The strongest support is its citation of prior efforts and its concrete claim that the wording was developed collaboratively against the working draft, while the thinnest areas are the absence of discussion about who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and what implementation experience actually shows.

- The paper grounds its motivation in the real risk posed by IFNDR and undefined behavior, and it points to earlier proposals that called for formalizing this information in the standard.
- It offers a specific collaborative development history by linking to a draft branch, though it provides no evidence of implementation or use beyond that assertion.
- The paper does not address why a library or separate reference document would be insufficient, leaving the choice of the standard itself largely undefended.
- It never identifies the affected audiences or explains how the annex would coordinate with existing standard structure, tooling, or other working group efforts.
