Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a reasonably specific account of why out-of-thin-air behavior matters and how prior models have fallen short, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case largely unargued, particularly around affected users, interoperability, and implementation experience. The strongest support is in its engagement with prior work and its claim that avoiding OOTA cycles requires no standard changes, while the thinnest support concerns whether the described implementation approach has actually been tried.

- The paper grounds its motivation and prior-art discussion in concrete references and a clear explanation of the fixed-point nature of OOTA cycles.
- Its central standardization claim is direct: portable code and the standard itself would not need changes, with only minimal implementation adjustments.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the issue or what coordination or interoperability concerns might arise.
- Implementation experience is asserted rather than demonstrated, with no evidence that the compiler-based freezing of nondeterministic pathways has been realized in practice.
