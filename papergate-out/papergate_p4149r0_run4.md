Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper provides only a thin evidentiary basis for standardization, with concrete detail concentrated in procedural history, related work, and implementation divergence rather than in motivation or design rationale. The thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why the standard needs the proposed definition and no discussion of why a library-level or non-normative solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support comes from the cited CWG direction to unify treatment of related language features, which grounds the work in an active committee concern.
- The implementation status section gives a useful, specific signal about current compiler behavior, though it is presented without analysis of what that divergence implies.
- The paper asserts the importance of defining “immediate context” but offers no supporting reasoning or examples showing the consequences of leaving it undefined.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of discussion about why a standard change, rather than a library or guidance document, is the right vehicle.
