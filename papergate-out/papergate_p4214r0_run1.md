Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, resting almost entirely on a conceptual appeal to prior work while leaving the practical and procedural questions untouched. The support is thinnest where a proposal most needs to be concrete: why the standard is the right venue, what would actually change, and whether anyone has tried to build it.

- The strongest support is the specific invocation of Lamport’s safety/liveness distinction as a framing for treating progress guarantees as part of a concurrency contract.
- The paper does not address why this belongs in the C++ standard rather than in guidance, libraries, or a separate specification.
- It offers no implementation experience, interoperability analysis, or indication of who would be affected by the change.
