Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper gives substantial, concrete support for its standardization case, particularly in connecting its layering claim to named, current proposals and to a decade of shipped vendor experience. The support is thinnest where it leans on procedural assumptions about how accumulated approvals affect future revisitation, since that argument is stated as a testable assumption rather than demonstrated from committee practice.

- The strongest support comes from tying the proposal’s layering claim directly to specific published papers and their interoperation requirements.
- The paper also grounds its “why a library will not do” argument in named vendor guarantees and the absence of routing through the C++26 contracts violation handler.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of direct evidence for the claim that a design settled through accumulated approvals is harder to revisit than one decided by an explicit ballot.
