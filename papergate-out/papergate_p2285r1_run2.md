Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides a reasonable but uneven case for standardization, with its strongest support coming from concrete compiler divergence and links to existing core issues, while its weakest area is the complete absence of implementation experience. The argument that the standard currently lacks a clear stance is repeated across several sections, which gives the problem credibility but also makes the supporting material feel somewhat circular.

- The paper most convincingly grounds the problem in observable compiler disagreement and an unresolved core issue, which establishes that standardization is addressing a real gap.
- The rationale connecting default arguments to failed constructor overload use cases in STL containers offers a specific, practical motivation for the change.
- The discussion of implementer notions of separately instantiated entities shows some engagement with implementation constraints, though only secondhand.
- The paper offers no implementation experience, leaving unclear whether the proposed direction is feasible or already reflected in any compiler’s behavior.
