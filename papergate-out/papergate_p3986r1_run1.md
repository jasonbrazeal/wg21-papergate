Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete reasoning about why a library-only solution is insufficient but little engagement with the broader standardization context. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of implementation experience, affected-user discussion, and interoperability considerations.

- The strongest support is the specific argument that the receiver must be passed to the standard completion functions, grounding the need in existing standardese.
- The paper also engages with prior art by explicitly considering an alternate approach, though it does not develop that comparison in depth.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience, leaving the practical viability of the proposal unexamined.
- The paper does not address who is affected or how the change would coordinate with existing practice, making the case for standardization feel narrowly framed.
