Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a clear, if narrow, rationale for why contract enforcement belongs in statements rather than declarations, but it does not build a complete case for standardization because it leaves out the affected audience, alternatives, and implementation experience. The strongest support is conceptual, while the practical and comparative grounding is almost entirely absent.

- The paper most convincingly supports its position by explaining that C++ declarations should express agreements, while behavior should come from statements.
- It offers a specific reason that a library solution would not suffice, namely that the problem is making enforcement part of the agreement rather than the enforcement behavior itself.
- The discussion of prior art is asserted rather than demonstrated, with no concrete examples of the failed proposals or why they foundered.
- The paper does not address who would be affected by the change or provide any implementation experience, leaving the standardization case thin where it matters most.
