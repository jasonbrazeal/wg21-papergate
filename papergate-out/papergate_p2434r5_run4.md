Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete discussion of alternatives and interoperability but little evidence that the proposed model is necessary or grounded in implementation reality. The thinnest areas are the absence of any motivation tied to real users or affected code, and the reliance on bare assertions where implementation experience and the need for a standard change should be demonstrated.

- The strongest support comes from the specific comparison with the PVI model and the explanation of how a provenance fence becomes implementable under the proposed approach.
- The discussion of why a library solution will not suffice is also grounded in the rejected PVI alternative, giving at least one concrete point of comparison.
- The paper asserts rather than demonstrates that existing implementations would be formalized rather than changed, offering no evidence from actual practice.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of explanation of why the change matters or who is affected, leaving the standardization rationale unstated.
