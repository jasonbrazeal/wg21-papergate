Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, relying heavily on broad assertions about C++’s role in AI rather than concrete evidence or implementation experience. Its strongest support is a single external reference to prior art, while the arguments for why a standard is needed, why a library would not suffice, and how the proposal would interoperate are largely unsupported.

- The paper points to an existing DataFrame implementation as prior art, which at least grounds the idea in real-world practice.
- It asserts that C++ lacks key components to prevent fragmentation, but provides no examples of current fragmentation or how standardization would resolve it.
- The claim that AI workflows default to Python because C++ lacks a native DataFrame is repeated as justification for standardization without evidence linking the two.
- The paper offers no implementation experience, no coordination plan with other proposals or libraries, and no explanation of why a library solution would be inadequate.
