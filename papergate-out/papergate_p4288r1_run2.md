Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem and its implementation context, but it leans heavily on assertion at the points where standardization rationale matters most. The strongest support appears in the discussion of prior art and interoperability, while the case for why this must be standardized rather than supplied as a library is notably underdeveloped.

- The paper grounds its motivation in familiar synchronous behavior and connects it directly to a gap in asynchronous functions.
- It offers specific evidence of implementation experience and prior standardization history, including a reference implementation and a removed C++26 algorithm.
- The argument for why a library solution is insufficient is asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the central standardization question thinly supported.
- The paper does not substantiate its claim about implementation experience beyond repeating the same reference-implementation statement without detail.
