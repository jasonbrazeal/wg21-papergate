Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers some concrete framing for why a common framework would improve consistency across toolsets, but it leans heavily on a single quoted rationale and leaves several parts of the standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the paper claims that a library solution would be insufficient and where it cites implementation experience without detailing that experience.

- The strongest support is the repeated, specific argument that without a common framework, supplier-specific options would diverge in guarantees and portability.
- The paper identifies its relationship to prior work by explicitly stating it is not competing with Sutter’s proposal, which helps situate it among alternatives.
- The claim that a library will not do is asserted with nothing supporting it, weakening the case for why standardization is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of detail behind the cited implementation experience, since the reference to `gsl::suppress` and in-field deployments is not expanded into evidence.
