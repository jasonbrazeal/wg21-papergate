Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper grounds its central motivation and the need for a standard mechanism in concrete examples and prior proposals, but it leaves important parts of the standardization case largely unstated. The thinnest support concerns who is actually affected and whether the feature has been tried in practice.

- The strongest support is the specific history of prior proposals, showing this is a recurring language need rather than a new idea.
- The paper also explains clearly why a library-only approach cannot provide the required temporal separation for postcondition captures.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed mechanism has been validated in a compiler or large codebase.
- The claim that such postconditions are relatively common in the standard library is asserted without examples, leaving the affected-user argument unsupported.
