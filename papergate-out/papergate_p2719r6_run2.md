Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper provides a narrow but concrete rationale for its proposed change, grounded in specific wording issues and a known core-language defect, but it leaves several important dimensions of the standardization case unexamined. The strongest support concerns the technical motivation and the connection to prior CWG discussions, while the thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience beyond an assertion, or why the standard is the right venue.

- The paper gives specific reasoning for why the type being allocated matters and why a library-level solution is insufficient.
- It identifies a plausible path for resolving CWG1676 by analogy with CWG1669, offering prior art within the committee’s own history.
- The claim about implementation experience is asserted without supporting detail, weakening confidence that the change has been exercised in practice.
- The paper does not address who is affected, why the standard should change rather than remain as is, or how the proposal coordinates with existing practice and interoperability.
