Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives concrete, specific support in a few areas—particularly prior art, implementation experience, and the core problem of duplicated logic—but leaves several foundational questions about affected users, standardization rationale, and library alternatives unaddressed. The overall case is therefore uneven: it shows that the issue is real and has precedent, but does not fully explain why the standard is the right venue or who would be served by the change.

- The strongest support comes from the cited prior art and the representative production examples, which ground the proposal in existing practice.
- The paper also clearly identifies the duplication burden for users needing always-enforce assertions, though it does not develop that point beyond a single repeated sentence.
- The thinnest support is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why the standard is necessary, or why a library solution would not suffice.
