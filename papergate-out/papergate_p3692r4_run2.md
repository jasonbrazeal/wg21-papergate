Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, uneven case for its own standardization: it grounds the problem and some prior art in specifics, but leaves the central request for a normative change largely unsupported by evidence about affected users, implementation experience, or why the standard is the right venue. The thinnest support surrounds the very mechanism being proposed, where assertions stand in for demonstration.

- The strongest support is the concrete framing of the OOTA problem and the reference to P0442R0’s perturbation-based decision procedure.
- The paper asserts implementation experience but does not show which implementations, models, or programs were actually tested.
- It never addresses who is affected by the change or why a library-level solution would be insufficient.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any supporting argument for why the standard itself must change, even non-normatively.
