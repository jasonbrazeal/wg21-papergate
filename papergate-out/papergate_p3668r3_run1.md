Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers a reasonably concrete case for why defaulted postfix operations would reduce boilerplate and simplify specification, but it does not fully establish that the proposed mechanism is ready for standardization. The strongest support lies in the identification of affected operations and the discussion of prior art, while the thinnest areas concern practical implementation experience and the explicit rejection of a library-based alternative.

- The paper grounds its motivation in a specific set of 53 candidate operations and links the proposal to a companion paper exploring their defaulting.
- It engages with existing approaches and explains why the authors believe standardization is preferable to those alternatives.
- The discussion of why a library solution would be insufficient is present but underdeveloped, leaving a key design question only partially addressed.
- The absence of any implementation experience leaves the feasibility and real-world impact of the proposal largely unsubstantiated.
