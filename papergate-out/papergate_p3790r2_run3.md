Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, resting on broad assertions about existing practice and an alternative naming discussion rather than on evidence or analysis of the problem’s scope. Its support is thinnest where it matters most: explaining why the standard must change, why a library solution is insufficient, and whether the proposed approach is viable in practice.

- The strongest support is the concrete discussion of alternative names and the reference to prior work in P2188R1.
- The paper asserts that long-standing concurrent algorithms rely on the described pointer operations, but provides no supporting examples, data, or citations.
- It does not address why a library-only solution would fail, despite acknowledging that the standard currently invalidates such pointers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed facility has been tried, measured, or adopted.
