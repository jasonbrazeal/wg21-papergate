Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers a moderately developed case for standardization, with its strongest material concentrated in the technical rationale and implementation landscape, while the institutional and interoperability arguments remain largely asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and who exactly would be affected beyond a generic appeal to JSON and UTF-8 prevalence.

- The paper gives concrete technical justification by pointing to the absence of standard transcoding facilities and the numerical equivalence with existing ASCII-based implementations.
- It situates the proposal within prior art by citing stale related proposals, showing awareness of the design space.
- The discussion of affected users is vague, leaning on the popularity of UTF-8 and `char8_t` without identifying specific constituencies or use cases.
- The case for standardization itself is essentially unaddressed, leaving unclear why a library solution would be insufficient or inappropriate.
