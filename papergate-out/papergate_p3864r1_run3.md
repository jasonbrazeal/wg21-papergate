Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a partial account of why the feature would be useful, but it does not build a complete case for standardization, leaving several essential questions about scope, feasibility, and precedent unanswered. The strongest material concerns the ergonomic motivation and a related proposal, while the thinnest support appears wherever the paper should justify working through the standard rather than through other means.

- The paper supports its motivation with a concrete example of unergonomic rounding-mode changes and connects the idea to P3375R3’s reproducibility goals.
- The claim that the proposal solves the stated problem directly is asserted without explaining why standardization is the necessary or appropriate mechanism.
- The paper does not address who is affected, how the feature would coordinate with existing practice, or whether a library solution could suffice.
- It offers no implementation experience or interoperability discussion, leaving the practical path to standardization largely unsupported.
