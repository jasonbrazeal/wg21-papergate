Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives uneven support for its standardization case, grounding its technical comparisons and algorithm categorization in concrete detail while leaving several key justifications as bare assertions. The thinnest support appears around implementation experience, affected users, and the standard-specific rationale, where claims are made without evidence or explanation.

- The strongest support comes in the prior art and alternatives section, which names specific `std::execution` algorithms and orders them against the standard’s presentation.
- The coordination and interoperability discussion is also concrete, identifying the storage and child-operation requirements a redesigned algorithm would impose.
- The claim of implementation against nVidia’s reference implementation is asserted twice but never substantiated with details, scope, or lessons learned.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving a central standardization question unanswered.
