Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers very little concrete support for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on a single asserted motivation about generic algorithms. That claim is repeated across several sections without elaboration, examples, or evidence, leaving the proposal’s rationale thin where it should be most persuasive. The thinnest areas are those that would normally ground a change in practice or precedent: prior art, implementation experience, and the affected audience are either asserted or absent.

- The strongest support is the identification of a specific standardese status quo, namely that `std::execution::when_all()` is currently ill-formed.
- The paper does not develop its central claim that the ban creates a special case in generic algorithms with any code, use case, or concrete consequence.
- Prior art and alternatives are merely asserted, with no comparison to other models, libraries, or languages.
- Implementation experience and the affected population are not addressed at all, leaving the practical case for standardization unsupported.
