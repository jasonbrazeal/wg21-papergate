The paper provides some concrete grounding for the problem and for why a library-only approach is insufficient, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case unstated or asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is around who is affected, implementation experience, and the absence of any discussion of why the standard is the right venue or how the change would coordinate with existing rules.

- The strongest support is the specific explanation of how value representations like `awesomeness/s` fail to add associated entities for ADL.
- The discussion of prior art is useful because it distinguishes this design from P2822R2 by noting that it avoids new syntax and reuses existing annotation syntax.
- The claim about affected users rests only on a mention of a presentation and a library paper, with no independent evidence of broader impact.
- The paper does not address why the standard should change, nor does it discuss coordination or interoperability with existing ADL and template rules.
