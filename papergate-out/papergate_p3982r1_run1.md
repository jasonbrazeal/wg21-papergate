Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s strongest support comes from concrete implementation experience and a clear statement of why the facility belongs in the standard, but it leaves the motivating problem largely implicit and does not explain what user need or pain point the proposal addresses. The case for standardization rests more on precedent and interoperability than on a demonstrated demand or a well-articulated rationale.

- The paper provides a nonbinding poll showing unanimous or near-unanimous interest in pursuing `range_slice` for C++26, which is direct evidence of committee appetite.
- It cites implementation experience in libstdc++, showing the design has been tested in practice rather than remaining purely theoretical.
- The discussion of prior art in Fortran, Python, Matlab, and Rust is specific and helps situate the proposal among established language features.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any explanation of why the feature matters to users, leaving the reader to infer the problem being solved.
