Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, uneven case for standardization: it gestures at a real inconsistency and cites one relevant precedent, but leaves most of the evidentiary burden unaddressed. The strongest material concerns the motivating problem and prior art, while the sections on affected users, implementation experience, and the necessity of a standard-language solution are essentially silent.

- The paper grounds its motivation in a concrete inconsistency in how the STL handles temporaries and lifetime extension.
- It cites P2266R3 as prior art, showing at least some awareness of related standardization history.
- It asserts that formalizing pure alias types would remove inconsistencies but provides no supporting reasoning or examples.
- It does not identify who is affected, offer implementation experience, or explain why a library-only approach would be insufficient.
