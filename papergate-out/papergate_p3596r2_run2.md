Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, leaning almost entirely on prior references and a collaborative drafting branch rather than on evidence or argument developed within the document itself. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library solution would not suffice, or why the standard specifically is the right vehicle.

- The strongest support comes from the cited prior art and earlier calls to formalize the data in an annex, which at least grounds the effort in existing community discussion.
- The paper asserts that stable names would be useful as common vocabulary, but does not develop this into a concrete coordination or interoperability argument.
- The claim that IFNDR and undefined behavior are “always potentially problematic” is offered as motivation without any supporting reasoning or examples.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of implementation experience, affected users, or why standardization—rather than a separate reference document or library—is necessary.
