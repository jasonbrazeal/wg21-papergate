Verdict: Excellent (12/14, close to Strong)

The paper grounds its standardization case in a substantial body of prior work, implementation evidence, and unresolved committee history, giving it unusually strong support for a document of this kind. The support is thinnest where it should connect the proposal to the people and projects that would be affected, since the paper never identifies who would adopt or be constrained by the change.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s detailed reconstruction of seven committee polls and its demonstration that no prior decision adopted the architecture it now asks EWG to review.
- The paper also makes a specific, well-sourced case that existing vendor check-sets do not route through the C++26 contracts violation handler and therefore cannot be configured by a Label.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of affected users, projects, or implementation communities, leaving the standardization argument without a clear constituency.
