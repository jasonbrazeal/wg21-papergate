Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a mixed account of its own case: it explains the technical obstacle and points to a concrete implementation, but it does not establish who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or how the feature would interact with existing practice. The strongest material concerns feasibility and the inadequacy of a purely library-level solution, while the argument for standardization itself remains largely asserted.

- The paper is most concrete when showing that current `std::extents` cannot be destructured and that a working implementation exists.
- It also gives a specific rejected alternative, which helps clarify the design space.
- It does not address who would use the feature or what practical problem it solves for them.
- The thinnest part is the justification for standardization itself, which is stated rather than supported by use cases, coordination concerns, or interoperability analysis.
