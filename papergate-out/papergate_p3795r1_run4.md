Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper provides some concrete grounding for its standardization case by identifying inconsistencies introduced when related reflection proposals were adopted together, but it leaves most of the persuasive work undone. The strongest support is the specific reference to P2996R13 and the acknowledgment of gaps and inconsistent APIs, while the thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about affected users, implementation experience, or why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper points to a concrete prior proposal and notes that independently adopted reflection papers created inconsistencies, gaps, and inconsistent APIs.
- It does not address who is affected by the proposed changes or why the standard is the right venue.
- It offers no implementation experience, no discussion of coordination or interoperability, and no argument for why a library-only approach would not suffice.
