Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its standardization argument in concrete deployment evidence and direct engagement with competing proposals, though the support becomes thinner when it turns from what exists today to what the proposed model would add. The strongest material concerns shipped practice and the cost of alternatives, while the weakest concerns the absence of comparable field experience for the specific machinery being advanced.

- The paper’s strongest support is its documentation of a decade of production-default use across multiple vendors, with named deployments such as ScyllaDB pinning the terminating semantic through vendor attributes.
- It also substantiates the coordination problem well, citing the explicit acknowledgment in P3100R8 that two competing models cannot both remain without one being specified in terms of the other.
- The most glaring omission is that the proposal’s own added machinery—implicit assertions, Labels, and the replaceable handler—has no implementation experience, leaving the standardization case resting on adjacent practice rather than the thing actually proposed.
