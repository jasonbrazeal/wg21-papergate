Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper grounds its case well in concrete implementation experience, prior-art history, and the standard’s own mechanics, but it leaves the affected-audience argument and the library-alternative objection largely unexamined. The strongest support is practical and specific, while the thinnest parts rely on assertion or vague sentiment rather than demonstrated need.

- The implementation-experience section is the most persuasive, showing a working compiler branch and a small, well-scoped change.
- The prior-art and why-the-standard sections give specific historical and wording-level evidence that the proposal fits existing machinery.
- The affected-audience discussion is essentially absent, offering only a general feeling about a “simpler language” rather than who benefits and how.
- The claim that a library solution is inadequate is asserted through concision and discoverability without substantiating why those limits justify a core-language change.
