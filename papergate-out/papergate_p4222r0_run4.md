Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a partial but uneven account of why the initialization profile should be standardized, with useful technical grounding in some areas but little direct evidence for its central claims about breadth of use or foundational status. The thinnest parts concern who is actually affected, whether existing practice or implementation experience supports the design, and why standardization—rather than the already available erroneous-behavior rules—is necessary.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of why a library solution is insufficient, since it contrasts the proposal with C++26’s erroneous behavior and identifies a concrete gap.
- The prior art section is also substantive, acknowledging terminological mismatches and the need to reconcile the proposal with existing work.
- The claim that the initialization profile is foundational and widely relied upon is asserted repeatedly without evidence about affected users or codebases.
- The paper does not address implementation experience at all, leaving the practical viability of the proposed rules unsupported.
