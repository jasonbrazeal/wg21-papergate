Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization: it grounds some design choices in prior guidance and interoperability concerns, but it leaves the central motivation, affected audience, and necessity for a standard-language feature largely unstated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected or why a library solution would be insufficient, and the reliance on assertions rather than evidence for implementation experience.

- The strongest support comes from the concrete discussion of prior art and the specific design guidance the proposal follows.
- Coordination and interoperability are addressed with a specific rationale tied to mangling and compiler optimizations.
- The paper asserts the value of the guarantee for safety but does not explain why the standard is the right venue or who would be affected.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any treatment of why a library-only approach would not suffice, alongside implementation experience that is asserted without supporting detail.
