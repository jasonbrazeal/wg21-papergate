The paper leans heavily on a single, repeated claim—that users frequently need this facility and routinely get it wrong—but offers little beyond that to justify standardization. The strongest support is the existence of a complete reference implementation, while the case for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library is asserted rather than argued, and prior art is entirely absent.

- The paper’s most concrete support is a full implementation in the `eisenwave/integer-division` repository, which at least demonstrates feasibility.
- The claim that users need this constantly and fail when implementing it themselves is specific enough to suggest real demand, though it is not substantiated with examples or evidence.
- The paper does not address prior art or alternative approaches, leaving the standardization landscape unexplored.
- The argument for why a library solution is insufficient rests on the same unsupported assertion about user failure, without explaining what the standard would uniquely provide.
