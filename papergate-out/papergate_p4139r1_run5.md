Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper provides only a narrow slice of the rationale needed to justify standardization, resting most of its case on a single observation about novelty while leaving the broader questions of necessity, coordination, and feasibility largely unexamined. The thinnest areas are those that would tell a reviewer whether this belongs in the standard, whether it can be built outside it, and whether anyone has tried.

- The strongest support comes from the specific claim that this would be the first fallible, runtime-keyed `get()` in the library, which at least frames a design question worth answering.
- The discussion of prior art is concrete in naming P3091 and its rejected alternatives, giving some sense of the design space already explored.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving the central question of standardization unanswered.
- It offers no implementation experience, no interoperability or coordination analysis, and no discussion of who is affected, so the practical and ecosystem case is entirely absent.
