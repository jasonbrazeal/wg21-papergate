Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper grounds its case in concrete procedural friction, showing real churn in a recent meeting and citing prior attempts to address the same problem, but it leaves the standardization rationale largely implicit rather than arguing why this particular mechanism belongs in the standard itself. The strongest material concerns timing and review burden, while the weakest areas are the absence of any discussion of why a library or process change outside the standard would not suffice.

- The paper offers specific, recent evidence that papers change during the meeting week, making the cooling-period motivation tangible.
- It identifies prior art in WG21, including P2138R4, which shows the idea has been considered in a stronger form before.
- It connects the proposal to the pre-meeting mailing, giving national body experts a concrete review window.
- It does not address why the standard is the right vehicle, nor why a library or non-normative process change would not accomplish the same goal.
