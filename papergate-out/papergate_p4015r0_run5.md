Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a narrow but coherent rationale for standardizing its approach, grounded in the relationship between declarations and transfer of control, though it leaves several important parts of the standardization case unstated. The strongest support concerns how the proposal fits existing C++26 contracts and why a library alternative is insufficient, while the thinnest areas are the absence of implementation experience, affected users, and prior-art discussion.

- The paper gives concrete reasoning for why contract conditions belong in declarations and why a library cannot provide the same effect.
- It explains coordination with existing C++26 contracts by distinguishing agreed-upon conditions from specified behavior.
- It does not address who is affected by the proposal or what implementation experience exists.
- It omits any discussion of prior art and alternatives, leaving the proposal’s novelty and necessity less clearly established.
