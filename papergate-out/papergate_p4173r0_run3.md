Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives concrete support for implementation feasibility and alignment with existing library directions, but it leaves several parts of the standardization rationale unstated, especially around who benefits and why a non-standard library solution would be insufficient. The strongest evidence is practical and specific, while the weakest areas are the abstract claims about the need for a standard mechanism.

- The paper demonstrates implementation experience through a libstdc++-based prototype and a worked example.
- It grounds the design in prior art and current standardization trends, particularly P3349’s treatment of contiguous iterators.
- It does not identify the affected users or communities that would gain from standardizing this facility.
- It asserts that standardization eliminates reinventing access logic without explaining why a library-only solution cannot meet that need.
