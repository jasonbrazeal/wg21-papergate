Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides only a narrow, underdeveloped case for standardization, resting almost entirely on a single motivating example and an early-stage prototype. Its support is thinnest in the areas that would normally justify committee action: the necessity of core language changes, the affected audience, and any evidence of implementation or design validation.

- The strongest support is the concrete link to P3771, which at least names a real problem that a pure library solution could address.
- The paper asserts that no current mechanism exists to detect or modify values crossing constant-evaluation boundaries, but offers no explanation of why that gap requires standardization rather than a different library or wording approach.
- The discussion of implementation experience is essentially an admission that the work is too preliminary to demonstrate feasibility.
- The paper does not address who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or how the proposal would coordinate with existing language and library features.
