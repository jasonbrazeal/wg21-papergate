Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a skeletal justification for standardization, leaning almost entirely on prior work and an asserted hope for stable vocabulary rather than building a case from demonstrated need, affected users, or implementation experience. The thinnest support is in the sections that matter most for a standards change: why the standard is the right venue, who is affected, and what practical problem is being solved.

- The strongest support is the citation of prior efforts, which at least grounds the idea in an existing conversation.
- The paper asserts that IFNDR and undefined behavior are “potentially problematic” but provides no evidence or examples of how this annex would help.
- It does not address who is affected or why the standard, rather than a separate document or library, is the appropriate home.
- The claim of collaborative wording development is offered without any implementation experience or evidence of use.
