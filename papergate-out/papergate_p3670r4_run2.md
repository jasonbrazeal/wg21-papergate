Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for its own standardization: it identifies a specific gap in pack indexing and gestures at related work, but leaves the core rationale for standardizing this change largely unexamined. The thinnest support is around the absence of implementation experience, library alternatives, and any discussion of coordination or interoperability.

- The strongest support is the concrete observation that P2662R3 does not allow indexing a pack of templates, which at least anchors the proposal in a specific, identifiable limitation.
- The paper cites related in-flight proposals, showing some awareness of the surrounding design space, though it does not establish how this proposal relates to them.
- The claim of positive feedback and implementer confidence is asserted without evidence, leaving the practical viability of the change unsupported.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be insufficient or how the change would coordinate with existing and pending features, which are the most glaring omissions for a standardization argument.
