Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides only partial support for its own standardization, with concrete evidence in a few areas but little argumentation for the broader need or fit within the standard. The thinnest support concerns motivation, affected users, and why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience, where generated code is cited as concrete evidence.
- Naming and placement are tied to existing `std::simd` facilities such as `chunk` and `cat`, giving some prior-art alignment.
- The case for standardization itself is largely unaddressed, leaving the core rationale for adding this to the C++ standard unexplained.
- The rejection of a library-only approach is asserted rather than demonstrated, with no supporting detail for why users cannot reasonably build on existing mechanisms.
