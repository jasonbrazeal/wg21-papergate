Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives a partial but uneven account of why its approach should be standardized, with concrete reasoning about objections and extensibility but little evidence about affected users, implementation feasibility, or how the design fits the broader ecosystem. The strongest material concerns the motivation and the inadequacy of a rejected alternative, while the practical and coordination dimensions are largely absent.

- The paper most concretely supports standardization by tying the work to active objections and the severe dissatisfaction those objections represent.
- It also offers a clear rationale for language-level semantics by showing how ordinary function calls can supply different contract behaviors without new language magic.
- The discussion of prior alternatives is useful but limited to one rejected design and does not survey other possible directions or their tradeoffs.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any treatment of affected users, implementation experience, or interoperability, leaving the practical case for standardization largely unexamined.
