Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper leans heavily on a single piece of evidence—that major implementations already accept the code—and repeats it across several sections, which gives the proposal a narrow but real foundation while leaving large parts of the standardization case unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and why the standard itself needs to change rather than simply codifying existing practice.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation experience, including verification against specific examples.
- The paper also offers specific evidence of cross-vendor implementation behavior, though it reuses that evidence rather than developing it further.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of why the standard, rather than a library or existing practice, must change.
