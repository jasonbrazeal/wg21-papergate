Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives a partial account of why a language feature might be needed, but it leaves several core standardization questions unexamined, especially around motivation, affected users, and why the standard is the right venue. The strongest material concerns prior art and a concrete example of attributes that cannot currently be appertained, while the weakest areas are largely asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The clearest support comes from the reference to merging P3678R0 and the concrete example involving `alignas`, `[[no_unique_address]]`, `[[deprecated]]`, and `[[maybe_unused]]`.
- The claim that implementation experience exists is only an assertion pointing to Compiler Explorer, without details about scope, maturity, or lessons learned.
- The paper does not address who is affected by the problem or why standardization, rather than another approach, is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any supported argument for why the standard should adopt the feature, beyond a general statement that uniform syntax would be useful.
