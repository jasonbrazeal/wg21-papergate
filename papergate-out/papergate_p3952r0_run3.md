Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers substantial support for standardization by documenting existing practice in major libraries, identifying a real portability gap, and citing a concrete implementation with compiler support. The case is thinnest where it fails to explain why a non-standard library solution cannot suffice, leaving a key justification for standardization unaddressed.

- The strongest support comes from named, widely used implementations in libc++, Qt, and Boost, showing the facility is already a de facto standard.
- The paper also benefits from merged and parallel proposals that supply both design discussion and implementation experience.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library-only solution would be inadequate, which weakens the case for taking this to the standard.
