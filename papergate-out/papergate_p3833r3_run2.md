Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives concrete examples of the problem and sketches an alternative design, but it does not build a case that this belongs in the standard rather than in a library or that the proposed interface is the right one. The strongest support is for the existence of a real ergonomic gap, while the thinnest areas are the absence of affected-user context, implementation experience beyond a link, and any discussion of coordination with related standardization efforts.

- The paper most convincingly supports the claim that current manual management of multiple timed or deferred locks is verbose and error-prone.
- It provides at least a named alternative design, which helps frame the design space even though the alternative is not evaluated.
- The argument for standardization over a library solution is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper does not address who is affected, how the feature interoperates with existing or proposed locking facilities, or what implementation experience reveals.
