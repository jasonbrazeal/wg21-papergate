Verdict: Excellent (14/14)

The paper grounds its standardization case in a consistent deployment record: every surveyed implementation that detects the relevant violation in production terminates or traps by default, with continuation modes framed as temporary adoption aids rather than standing configurations. The support is strongest where that record is cited as evidence for both prior art and implementation experience, and thinnest where the argument relies on the same sampled population to carry several distinct justifications without additional independent reasoning.

- The strongest support comes from the deployment record showing that no surveyed production implementation defaults to continuation after detecting the violation.
- The paper clearly ties the undefined behavior of continued execution to the claim that a library-only solution would be insufficient.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of how the proposed standardization would interact with implementations or configurations not represented in the surveyed population.
