The paper provides uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete reasoning in several key areas but little evidence of real-world use or affected audiences. The thinnest parts are the unsubstantiated implementation experience and the complete absence of discussion about who would be affected by the proposed facility.

- The paper gives specific technical justification for why the standard library is the right venue and why a library-only solution would be insufficient.
- It grounds its design in concrete interactions with existing scheduler and receiver queries, showing coordination with the broader execution model.
- The claim of existing implementation experience is asserted without naming libraries, versions, or usage, leaving the maturity of the design unverified.
- The paper never addresses who is affected by the proposal, making it difficult to judge the scope of the problem or the demand for standardization.
