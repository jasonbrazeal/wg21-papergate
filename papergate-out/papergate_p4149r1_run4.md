Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the support needed to justify standardization, resting almost entirely on a single cited prior paper and a brief implementation-status note. Its thinnest areas are the absence of any rationale for why the standard needs the proposed definition, who would be affected, or how the change would interoperate with existing rules.

- The strongest support is the reference to P2285R1, which grounds the discussion in prior work on default function arguments and the immediate context.
- The implementation status gives a concrete, if minimal, signal about current compiler divergence.
- The paper does not explain why the standard itself should adopt the definition, leaving the core motivation unstated.
- It never addresses affected users, implementers, or coordination with other proposals, making the scope and impact of the change unclear.
