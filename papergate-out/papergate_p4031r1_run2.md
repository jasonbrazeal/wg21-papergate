Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with the strongest material aimed at naming concerns and prior discussion rather than at the fundamental case for putting the facility in the standard. The argument is thinnest where it matters most: there is no treatment of why a library solution would be insufficient, no implementation experience, and no discussion of coordination or interoperability.

- The paper grounds its naming objection in a specific inconsistency with the current working draft, which gives the critique concrete weight.
- It cites prior art and alternatives from P3804R1, showing that the naming question has already been explored in committee.
- It does not address why the standard, rather than a library, is the right home for the proposed facility.
- It offers no implementation experience or interoperability discussion, leaving the practical case for standardization essentially unexamined.
