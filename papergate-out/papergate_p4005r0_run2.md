Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a partial but uneven account of why its proposed guarantee belongs in the standard, with some concrete grounding in prior art, design guidance, and mangling interoperability, but it leaves several key justifications asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library solution would be insufficient, and any implementation experience beyond an admission that the full proposal has not been implemented.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s engagement with prior art and design guidance, including specific references and a stated difference from another proposal.
- The discussion of coordination and interoperability is also concrete, particularly the claim that mangling enables safe compiler optimizations.
- The rationale for standardization itself is merely asserted, with no supporting argument for why the guarantee must be in the standard rather than elsewhere.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any treatment of who is affected or why a library-only approach would not suffice, leaving important audience and scope questions unanswered.
