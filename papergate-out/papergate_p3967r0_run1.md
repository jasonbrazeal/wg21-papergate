Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably concrete account of the problem it targets and the standardization gap it hopes to fill, but its case rests on a narrow set of arguments and leaves several important audiences without enough information to judge readiness. The strongest support concerns interoperability and the limits of current C++26 semantics, while the thinnest support appears around practical experience and the people or projects most affected.

- The paper most convincingly supports standardization by identifying concrete linkage and semantic-control problems that arise from C++26’s TU-level contract evaluation model.
- It also offers a specific prior-art anchor in P3400R2, showing that finer-grained control is already being explored in the committee.
- The discussion of pre-compiled library distribution adds a practical standardization motivation, though it is stated briefly rather than developed.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or discussion of who is affected, leaving the proposal’s real-world urgency and feasibility largely unestablished.
