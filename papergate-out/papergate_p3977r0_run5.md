Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers a narrow but concrete rationale for standardization, grounded mainly in the inadequacy of existing classifications and the ABI implications of a library-only approach. Its support is thinnest on the practical dimensions that would show the feature is ready for the standard: who is affected, implementation experience, and why the standard is the right venue.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of `quick` `enforce` becoming part of the ABI, which directly motivates why a library solution would be insufficient.
- The paper also gives specific references to prior work and the wide/narrow classification, showing awareness of the surrounding design space.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, leaving the proposal without evidence that the design works in practice.
- The paper also does not address who is affected, making it hard to judge the scope or urgency of the problem for the broader C++ community.
