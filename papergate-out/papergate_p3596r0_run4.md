Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization, leaning on a general assertion that cataloguing IFNDR and undefined behavior is valuable while leaving most of the practical justification unstated. The strongest support is the existence of prior related efforts and a collaborative drafting branch, but the absence of any discussion of affected users, standardization rationale, or implementation experience leaves the proposal largely unmoored from the committee’s usual evidentiary expectations.

- The paper’s most concrete support is its citation of earlier attempts and related proposals, showing the topic has a history and some community interest.
- The claim that stable names could serve as common vocabulary is asserted hopefully but not backed by examples, coordination feedback, or demonstrated uptake.
- The paper does not address who is affected by the proposed annex or why the standard, rather than a separate reference document or library, is the right home.
- Implementation experience is reduced to a link to a draft branch, with no report of use, review, or lessons learned from that work.
