The strongest material concerns real-world precedent and implementation experience, while the thinnest concerns the absence of a positive case for standardization itself and any discussion of coordination or interoperability.

- The paper offers specific evidence from other languages and from a widely used implementation, which grounds the proposed behavior in existing practice.
- It identifies a concrete performance and correctness concern with the current default, supported by reference to committee polling.
- It asserts that the standard should fix the default behavior but provides no supporting reasoning for why standardization, as opposed to another mechanism, is required.
- Coordination and interoperability with existing specifications or implementations are not addressed at all.
