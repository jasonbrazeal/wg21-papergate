Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides a reasonable foundation by explaining why cataloguing undefined behavior and IFNDR matters and by citing prior efforts, but it leaves large parts of the standardization case unaddressed, particularly around affected users, standardizing rationale, and practical viability. The thinnest support appears where the paper asserts implementation experience without evidence and skips questions about why a library solution would not suffice.

- The strongest support comes from its motivation, which clearly ties the collection of UB and IFNDR cases to real risks in C++ programs.
- The paper also grounds itself in prior art by referencing earlier attempts and formalization proposals.
- It offers only an unsupported assertion of implementation experience, pointing to a draft branch without describing actual use or validation.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected or why standardization, rather than a non-normative reference or library approach, is necessary.
