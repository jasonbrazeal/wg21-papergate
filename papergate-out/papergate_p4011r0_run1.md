Verdict: Adequate (4/14, close to Weak)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for standardization, grounding its discussion of undefined behavior and contract terminology in prior work but leaving most of the evidentiary burden unaddressed. The strongest material concerns conceptual distinctions and references to earlier committee papers, while the practical, procedural, and interoperability dimensions are essentially absent.

- The paper’s clearest support comes from its engagement with prior art, particularly the definitions of narrow and wide contracts in N3248.
- It also provides a specific rationale for why encoding preconditions in syntax can matter for undefined behavior.
- The paper does not address who would be affected by the proposal or what implementation experience exists.
- Most glaringly, it never explains why the standard is the right venue or why a library solution would be insufficient.
