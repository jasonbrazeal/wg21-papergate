Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a mixed account of its own readiness, with concrete grounding in some design and interoperability questions but little evidence for the core claim that a standard guarantee is needed or feasible. The thinnest parts are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library cannot suffice, and any implementation experience beyond an admission that the full proposal has not been tried.

- The strongest support comes from the specific design guidance and prior-art comparison, which shows the proposal is situated within an active design conversation.
- The discussion of mangling and optimizer safety offers a concrete interoperability rationale tied to the proposed facility.
- The paper asserts that the main motivation is a guaranteed assertion facility but does not explain why that guarantee belongs in the standard rather than in a library or contract.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of implementation experience, with the paper itself stating that the full form has not been implemented.
