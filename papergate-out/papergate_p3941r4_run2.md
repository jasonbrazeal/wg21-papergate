Verdict: Strong (8/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization, grounding its technical motivation and relationship to existing proposals in concrete references but leaving several sections that would normally justify a standards change essentially unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience, or why the standard—rather than a library—is the right venue.

- The paper gives specific context for the scheduler-resumption behavior and ties its wording to N5032 and P3826r3, which anchors the proposal in the current standardization landscape.
- It acknowledges prior art and notes that the functionality exists in at least one library, though it does not develop that into meaningful implementation experience.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the change, leaving the audience and impact unclear.
- It offers no argument for why the standard library is necessary, even while conceding that the adaptation “can certainly be done” outside the standard.
