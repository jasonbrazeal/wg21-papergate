Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives credible support on the motivating problem and on the alternatives, but much of the remaining case is asserted rather than demonstrated, especially for real-world impact, interoperability, and implementability. Its strongest material grounds the need in existing CWG and reflection work, while the thinnest parts tend to rely on claims about user experience or implementation ease without enough concrete evidence.

- The clearest support is for why the issue matters, tied to concrete limitations in P3603 and CWG3150 and a specific direction for addressing them.
- Prior art and alternatives are usefully established through references to P2996, P4101R0, and existing C++26 reflection facilities.
- The paper does not adequately establish who is affected, since it concedes the problematic cases may be unlikely outside toy programs and only gestures at data-structure pain points.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience, where personal recollections and a claim of trivial implementation implications are not enough to show the proposal is viable in practice.
