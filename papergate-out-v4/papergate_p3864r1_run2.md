Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, mainly by situating itself against existing standardization efforts and a cited floating-point specification. The case is thinnest where it never identifies who would be affected or how the feature would work in practice across implementations.

- The strongest support comes from the connection to P3375R3 and ISO/IEC 60559:2020, which grounds the proposal in recognized prior work and existing specified behavior.
- The rationale for changing the standard rests on a claim of ergonomics, but the paper does not demonstrate that the current mechanism is inadequate beyond asserting it.
- The paper does not establish who is affected, so it is hard to tell whether the problem is widespread or confined to a narrow set of users.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, coordination, or interoperability discussion, leaving the practical path to standardization entirely undefended.
