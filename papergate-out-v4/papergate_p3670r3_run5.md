Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some contextual grounding for its proposal, chiefly by situating it relative to recent and pending core language decisions, but it leaves much of the case for standardization unstated. The thinnest support is around the basic motivation: why the feature matters, who would use it, and why it cannot be served outside the standard are all effectively absent from the argument.

- The strongest support is the prior-art discussion, which identifies related proposals and core issues and shows how the design presumes or depends on their outcomes.
- The paper asserts that it is completing the pack-indexing design, but it does not demonstrate why that completion is necessary or what concrete need it answers.
- It claims coordination with existing work by noting what P2662R3 omitted, but it does not show how this proposal fits into an overall coherent direction beyond the assertion itself.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established motivation, affected users, library-workaround analysis, or implementation evidence, leaving the proposal without a demonstrated rationale for standardization.
