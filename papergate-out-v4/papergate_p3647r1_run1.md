Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers very little support for its own standardization, with nearly every point resting on bare assertion rather than evidence or argument. The thinnest areas are the ones most central to a standards proposal: why the standard should be involved at all, why a library solution is inadequate, and how the feature would coordinate with existing practice.

- The strongest support is the paper’s gesture toward implementation experience, though even that is only a claim that a naive fallback is trivial.
- The paper names a few use cases and a user community, but does not develop them into a case that the feature is needed broadly enough to justify standardization.
- It cites prior art only through a broken reference, leaving the alternatives and their lessons unexamined.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library.
