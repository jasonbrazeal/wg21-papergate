Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers meaningful contextual grounding for its concerns about committee direction and language coherence, but it falls well short of demonstrating that the particular facilities or policies it discusses require standardization now. The thinnest parts are the absence of any coordination story and the failure to explain why the work could not be pursued outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the paper’s articulation of why divergent design philosophies and growing complexity matter for the long-term health of C++.
- The paper gestures toward prior art and implementation experience, but it does not connect those examples concretely enough to establish readiness for standardization.
- The claim that affected users are numerous and underrepresented is asserted through anecdotes rather than shown with evidence tied to the proposal’s own scope.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any discussion of coordination, interoperability, or why a library would be insufficient, leaving the standardization need largely unargued.
