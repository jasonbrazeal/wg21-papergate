Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin evidentiary basis for its own standardization, with every category resting on assertion rather than demonstrated need, evidence, or experience. The support is thinnest around prior art, affected users, and implementation maturity, where the paper gestures toward use cases and experiments without showing that the problem is real, broad, or adequately understood.

- The strongest support comes from the experimental availability on Compiler Explorer, though even this is only claimed and not tied to lessons learned or real-world use.
- The paper asserts that attribute introspection will matter for code generation but does not establish who is actually affected or how widespread that need is.
- The discussion of alternatives and prior art is largely a statement of philosophy rather than a comparison showing why this approach is preferable to waiting for expression reflection.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for why the standard must act now, since the motivating uniformity and the inability to appertain certain attributes are asserted without supporting evidence.
