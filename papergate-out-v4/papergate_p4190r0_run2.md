Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers solid grounding in the motivating problem, the available design choices, and evidence that the change can be implemented in practice. Its case thins considerably when it comes to showing who is concretely affected, why the standard is the only viable home for the fix, and how the change would coordinate with existing specifications and implementations.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with a concrete pull request against a Standard Library implementation cited for the proposed change.
- The paper also clearly establishes why the issue matters and why the main alternative designs were rejected.
- The weakest established area is who is affected, which rests on an assertion about a committee vote rather than direct evidence of user or implementer impact.
- The most glaring omissions are any discussion of coordination and interoperability or why a library-level solution would be insufficient.
