Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for its standardization case: it establishes why the clarification matters and gestures at current practice, but leaves most of the necessary argument—especially the need for a standard change at all—unstated. The support is thinnest around the core question of whether the standard is actually the right place to address the issue, rather than a library or existing implementation behavior.

- The clearest support is the established motivation that compilers already evaluate putative constant expressions just once and discard contract violations when the expression turns out non-constant for other reasons.
- The paper is credited with partially addressing a national body comment by clarifying which evaluations occur, though that coordination claim is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established need for a standard change, leaving the paper without an articulated reason why the standard must act.
