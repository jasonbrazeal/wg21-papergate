Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a clear motivation for connecting legacy assertion mechanisms to a future Contracts facility, but it leaves much of the surrounding case for standardization asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the lack of evidence about the affected user population, the absence of established prior art or a full alternatives analysis, and the reliance on implementation experience that is not yet publicly available.

- The paper most convincingly establishes why the migration path matters by tying it to the centralization of bug response and mitigation across large programs.
- The discussion of coordination and interoperability leans on the same motivational language rather than showing concrete binding requirements or cross-implementation guarantees.
- The claim that a library solution cannot adequately address the problem is asserted through a code-size concern without sufficient supporting argument.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any established description of who is affected by the problem, which leaves the paper without a demonstrated audience or scale for the proposed change.
