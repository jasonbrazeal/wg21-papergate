Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers meaningful support on the conceptual groundwork: it explains why the attribute-not-in-the-type question matters, acknowledges that the same effects can be achieved without embedding the attribute, and points to existing compiler divergence as relevant prior art. The case for standardization becomes much thinner when it moves from explaining the design space to showing actual need, affected users, and viable implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the discussion of prior art and alternatives, especially the recognition that implementations already diverge and that the proposal’s examples can be managed either way.
- The paper establishes why the distinction between attributes and types matters, including the compatibility surprises it can create.
- The weakest part is the absence of implementation experience, which leaves the practical path to standardization largely asserted rather than demonstrated.
- Most glaringly, the paper does not establish why a library solution would be inadequate, leaving a central standardization question unaddressed.
