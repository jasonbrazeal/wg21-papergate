Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers substantial support for its standardization case in the areas that matter most to committee process: it demonstrates real consequences for delegates, gives concrete evidence of the affected population, and engages seriously with prior art and alternatives. The support thins considerably, however, around the arguments that standardization is the only viable mechanism, with the library-alternative question left entirely unaddressed and implementation experience resting on a single anecdote.

- The strongest support is the documented harm to delegate preparation, including a specific delegate who stopped preparing once preparation stopped paying, which grounds the problem in observable committee behavior.
- The paper also establishes who is affected with concrete numbers and a clear account of how the rule would constrain the largest feature area in the C++26 cycle.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is thorough, including engagement with SD-4 and an argument that the proposed bright-line rule avoids the judgment-heavy mechanism that contributed to P2138R4’s near-miss.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never establishes why a library or other non-standard mechanism cannot address the problem, leaving the "why the standard" case incomplete.
- Implementation experience is claimed but rests on a single delegate’s reported behavior, which is thin evidence for a process rule of this scope.
