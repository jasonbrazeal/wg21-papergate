Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a solid foundation in terminology and alternative representations, but the support for standardization itself remains largely undeveloped. The case is thinnest where it matters most: showing who is affected, why a library cannot suffice, and how the standard should coordinate with existing practice.

- The strongest support is the paper’s clear grounding in established graph terminology and its explicit mapping to common representations such as edge lists and adjacency lists.
- The paper also credibly connects its design to widely used textbook conventions and shows how familiar operations can be expressed without inventing new concepts.
- The most glaring omission is any evidence of implementation experience beyond a data-file citation that does not demonstrate the proposed facility in use.
- The paper does not establish who would benefit from standardization or why existing library approaches are inadequate for those users.
