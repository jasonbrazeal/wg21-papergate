Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: its strongest material concerns prior art and alternatives, while most of the argument for user impact, standardizing authority, and interoperability rests on assertion rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest part is that it offers no case at all for why a library solution would fail, which is a central requirement for a standards proposal.

- The paper establishes that alternative type-embedded or non-embedded approaches exist and that current implementations diverge, giving a genuine basis for considering standardization.
- The discussion of ABI modification needed for attributes on function arguments gestures toward coordination and interoperability, but only as a claimed consequence rather than a worked-through interoperability case.
- The paper repeatedly frames the affected population as “limited and specialized” without evidence, leaving both the “who is affected” and “why the standard” cases unproven.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument that a library cannot achieve the desired effect, which leaves a basic justification for standardization entirely unaddressed.
