Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some groundwork by connecting its approach to recent design guidance and existing implementation experience, but it does not sufficiently establish several core parts of the standardization case. The thinnest support appears around who would be affected and around demonstrating that the proposed facility, rather than a library or a simpler guaranteed-assertion mechanism, is necessary.

- The paper’s strongest support is in prior art and alternatives, where it positions itself against related proposals and notes implementation experience with always-enforced assertions.
- Its rationale for why standardization is needed is asserted mainly through the desire for a guaranteed assertion facility, but the broader safety and certainty benefits are not backed up.
- The paper claims interoperability and mangling benefits, but does not establish how the proposal coordinates with existing or in-flight contract facilities beyond a stated non-mixing rule.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes who is affected by the proposal, leaving the audience and impact of the change unclear.
