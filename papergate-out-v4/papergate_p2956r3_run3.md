Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a preliminary sketch of a case for standardization, with every substantive point left asserted rather than demonstrated. The support is thinnest on the questions that matter most for a standard library addition: why this cannot live in a standalone library and how it would coordinate with existing or future facilities.

- The strongest indication of viability is the mention of Intel’s reference implementation and use in software products, though even this is only claimed, not shown.
- The paper gestures at the performance benefits of mapping operations to native hardware instructions, but provides no evidence beyond a statement that such instructions exist.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives repeats the existence of an earlier proposal and compiler builtins without explaining what was learned or why standardization is now warranted.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any consideration of coordination, interoperability, or the fundamental question of why a library implementation would be insufficient.
