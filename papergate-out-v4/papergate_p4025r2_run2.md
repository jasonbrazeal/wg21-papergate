Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its standardization case: it convincingly motivates the general importance of C++ in AI workflows, but it does not substantiate most of the specific claims needed to justify bringing this work into the standard. The argument leans heavily on repeated assertions about competition and fragmentation without evidence, concrete comparison, or demonstrated feasibility.

- The strongest support is the established motivation that C++ lacks native data-frame capabilities and frictionless ingestion, which drives AI developers toward Python/Pandas.
- The paper includes only a passing reference to an existing DataFrame project as prior art, without assessing its relevance or limitations.
- The thinnest part of the case is implementation experience, since no usable implementation or deployment evidence is shown.
- The most glaring omission is a failure to explain why a library cannot satisfy the stated need, leaving the central standardization question unaddressed.
