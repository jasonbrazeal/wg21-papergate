Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers substantive support for its feasibility and compatibility case, particularly through implementation experience and attention to interoperability, but it is much thinner when it comes to showing who is actually blocked today and why only standardization—rather than an ordinary library or vendor extension—can provide the migration path it envisions. The strongest material is concrete: working implementations, ABI considerations, and engagement with prior proposals. What is largely asserted rather than demonstrated is the practical demand for this integration and the inadequacy of non-standard solutions.

- The paper clearly establishes that implementations exist in both major standard libraries and that the design has been exercised against shared ABI and existing facilities.
- It also establishes meaningful prior art and coordination concerns, including an alternative proposal and cross-vendor ABI entry-point thinking.
- The thinnest support is for why the standard must act here, since the paper repeatedly asserts a migration need without supplying evidence that users are actually unable to achieve it outside the standard.
- It likewise does not establish who is affected in concrete terms, leaving the intended audience and their current constraints more assumed than shown.
