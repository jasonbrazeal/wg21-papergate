Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper’s strongest support comes from its implementation experience, but much of the surrounding case rests on assertions that are not substantiated with evidence or references. The thinnest areas are the arguments that this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and the absence of demonstrated coordination or interoperability beyond the author’s own implementation claims.

- The implementation experience is the most credible part of the paper, with concrete testing across multiple architectures and compiler versions.
- The claim that the standard is necessary is asserted largely through implementation optimism and future-type speculation rather than demonstrated standard-required behavior.
- The case for who is affected describes plausible user categories but does not establish their scale or that they are blocked without standardization.
- Prior art and alternatives are mentioned, but the paper does not show how this proposal is preferable to the cited alternatives or to non-standard solutions.
