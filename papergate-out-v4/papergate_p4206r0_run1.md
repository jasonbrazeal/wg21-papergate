Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for why the current behavior is surprising and how existing practice avoids it, but its case for standardization leans heavily on assertion rather than demonstrated need, particularly around who is actually harmed and why the standard must change. The thinnest parts are those that depend on claims about real-world impact, permanence, and interoperability that are not backed by evidence in the paper itself.

- The paper’s strongest support comes from prior art and implementation experience, with concrete examples of similar wrappers working differently and shipping implementations already in place.
- The paper clearly establishes that the current behavior is surprising and harms usability for little benefit.
- The paper only claims, without establishing, that specific users are affected and that the standard is the necessary venue because the API is permanent.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of evidence for coordination and interoperability concerns, such as symbol-size effects or diagnostic exposure, beyond a single asserted consequence.
