Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest case rests on the mismatch between the current specification and widespread implementation, and it also does a credible job of laying out the historical and alternative wording paths. Beyond that, the support becomes noticeably thinner: the claims about real-world impact, affected users, implementation experience, and the need for a standard change all lean on the same single assertion without much additional evidence. The argument that a library solution cannot address the problem is especially underdeveloped.

- The paper establishes why the issue matters by showing that the specified behavior is almost unimplemented and that existing wording leaves normative examples without a clear result.
- The prior-art discussion is reasonably complete, identifying the relevant core issue, the intended reversion, and adjacent unresolved questions.
- The paper asserts real-world breakage and user expectations but does not substantiate the scale or nature of the affected code.
- The case for why a library cannot solve the problem is essentially asserted rather than explained.
