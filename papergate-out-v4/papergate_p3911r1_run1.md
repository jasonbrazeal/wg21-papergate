Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper leans heavily on assertions about existing production practice rather than demonstrating it, so its case for standardization remains largely unproven across the board. The thinnest area is outright silence on coordination and interoperability, followed by reliance on the same short passages to support several distinct requirements.

- The most concrete support appears in the implementation experience section, which names Google Abseil and downstream projects as examples of always-on checking semantics.
- The paper’s claims about why the standard is needed rest on a single recurring statement about codebases shipping always-on assertions, with little elaboration specific to standardization.
- The need for a language feature rather than a library is supported only by an asserted duplication problem, without evidence of what library solutions fail to express.
- The paper does not address coordination and interoperability with existing or related standards at all, leaving a major gap in its standardization argument.
