Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: it gestures repeatedly at a real gap around attribute introspection, but most of the argumentation is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected and why a library-only solution would be insufficient, leaving the case for committee action largely motivational rather than evidentiary.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, since the feature is reported to be available on Compiler Explorer and includes a concrete strategy for expression equality.
- The paper repeatedly claims that attributes cannot be appertained through existing reflection facilities, but does not establish the scope of users or codebases disadvantaged by that gap.
- The prior art section leans on committee feedback and future code-generation use cases without documenting actual alternatives considered or their limitations.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of discussion of why a library cannot address the problem, which is essential for justifying standardization.
