Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, largely asserted case for its own standardization, with most of the burden carried by a few references to inconsistencies among recently adopted reflection facilities. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about who is affected, why a library solution cannot suffice, why standardization specifically is needed, and whether anyone has implemented the idea.

- The strongest support is the paper’s observation that related reflection APIs were adopted independently and produced visible gaps and inconsistent interfaces.
- The next point in its favor is the note that an existing reflection mechanism exposes the desired information only indirectly, suggesting an alternative route already exists but is awkward.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience or evidence about the affected user base, without which the urgency of fixing the inconsistency remains abstract.
