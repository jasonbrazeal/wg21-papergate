Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only an asserted platform for its standardization case, with every category resting on claims rather than demonstrated need. The support is thinnest where the paper promises performance, safety, or industry relevance without concrete evidence, measurements, or worked examples to connect those promises to what standardization would require.

- The paper’s strongest support is its repeated assertion that P2300’s allocation patterns conflict with low-latency networking, but it does not show how or for whom that conflict is unavoidable.
- The paper claims implementation experience through performance parity with Asio/Beast, yet offers no benchmarks, deployment reports, or code evidence.
- The paper gestures at industries such as games and finance as affected constituencies without identifying real users, workloads, or reported blockers.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any demonstrated reason why a library cannot satisfy the stated goals, leaving the central justification for standardization unaddressed.
