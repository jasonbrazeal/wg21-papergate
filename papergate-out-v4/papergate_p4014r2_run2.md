Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers an uneven case for standardization: it draws on credible prior art and points to substantial reference implementations, but much of its central argument about why the language itself must change rests on assertion rather than demonstration. The thinnest support is in coordination and interoperability, which the paper does not establish at all.

- The paper’s strongest ground is prior art, where it connects the design to established work like Moggi’s monadic framework and Milner’s process algebra, and situates it relative to existing proposals and coroutine discussions.
- The implementation experience section gestures at real projects and repositories, but the credited passages only name them rather than show what was learned or how that experience validates standardization.
- Several core justifications, including why the feature matters, who is affected, and why a library cannot suffice, are asserted through vivid language and short slogans rather than supported with evidence or analysis.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers nothing on coordination and interoperability, leaving unaddressed how the proposed feature would fit with existing standard facilities and the broader ecosystem.
