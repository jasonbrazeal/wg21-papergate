Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow and uneven case for standardization: it establishes that prior alternatives were considered but leaves most of the burden—importance, affected users, need for a standard, interoperability, and implementation experience—either unstated or merely asserted. The thinnest support lies in the absence of any argument for why the standard is the right home or why a library solution would not suffice, and in the failure to identify who is affected.

- The strongest support is the discussion of prior art and the considered alternative involving a `request_stop` member function on the backend.
- The paper asserts coordination among user code, frontend, and backend, but does not demonstrate how standardization would actually improve that alignment.
- The paper claims the design matters because concerns were raised and portability is lacking, but it does not establish the concrete impact.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of a case for why the standard, rather than a library, is needed.
