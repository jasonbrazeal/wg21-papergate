Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, speculative justification for standardization, mostly resting on vague references to outside interest and possible implementation strategies rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest parts are the absence of any identified affected constituency, implementation experience, or concrete argument for why the feature belongs in the standard rather than in a library or implementation extension.

- The clearest support appears in the brief acknowledgement that some people have wanted to examine the area the paper addresses, though no specifics are given.
- The discussion of alternatives gestures at ways implementations could combine this work with existing contract-checking efforts, but it does not establish that standardization is the necessary or best path.
- The paper never identifies who would use the proposed facility or what problem they concretely face, leaving the audience and impact ungrounded.
- There is no implementation experience or interoperability analysis, so the practical viability and coordination costs of standardization remain entirely unexamined.
