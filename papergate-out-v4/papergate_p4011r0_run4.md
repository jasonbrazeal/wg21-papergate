Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is largely gestural: it gestures at shared terminology and at a distinction between undefined behavior and erroneous behavior, but it does not connect those ideas to a concrete need for normative action. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about who is affected, why the standard is the right vehicle, and how the proposal would interact with existing specifications or implementations.

- The strongest material is the reference to prior terminology work in N3248 and the paper’s attempt to relate its discussion to an existing distinction between narrow and wide contracts.
- Even that prior-art discussion remains only claimed rather than established, because the paper does not show how the cited definitions support the specific standardization it appears to seek.
- The paper offers no account of affected users, domains, or codebases, leaving the practical relevance of the proposal unclear.
- Most glaringly, there is no argument for why a standard is needed at all, nor any treatment of alternatives such as library solutions, implementation experience, or coordination with other specifications.
