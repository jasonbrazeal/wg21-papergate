Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives real, if uneven, support for its standardization case: its strongest material concerns procedural precedent and the importance of documented reconciliation, while its weakest areas leave the actual standards vehicle and non-library rationale almost entirely unargued. The most serious gaps are not merely incomplete evidence but absent sections where a reader would expect the core justification for ISO action.

- The paper substantiates why committees need written reconciliation of sustained technical opposition, citing both ISO and IETF norms and a concrete removal precedent.
- The paper gestures at affected parties and implementation concerns, but leaves those claims resting on thin or secondhand support rather than direct demonstration.
- The paper offers no established case for why this belongs in the standard itself or why a library cannot address the problem.
