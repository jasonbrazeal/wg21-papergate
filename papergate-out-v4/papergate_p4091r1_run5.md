Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers firm support in the areas that are easiest to verify from existing practice: it establishes why compound I/O results matter, documents the prior art, and demonstrates real implementation experience. The case becomes much thinner where standardization itself is at stake, since the paper does not establish why the standard must act, and only claims rather than proves the affected audience and the inadequacy of library-level solutions.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation record, including maintained libraries and multiple working sender constructions that show the problem is real and implementable.
- The paper also credibly establishes the prior art and the correctness of the three-channel model for this class of operation.
- The affected audience is asserted through examples but never quantified or connected to a demonstrated need for a language or library standard.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established argument for why the standard should address this rather than leaving it to libraries, conventions, or future evolution.
