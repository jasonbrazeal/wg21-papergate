The paper makes a focused case that the current prohibition is arbitrary and inherited, and it points to a concrete implementation attempt, but much of the argument for standardization remains asserted rather than demonstrated. The most substantial gaps are in showing who is actually affected and how the change would fit with existing practice without surprising interactions.

- The paper clearly grounds the problem in the standard’s current fiat and in prior discussion, so the motivation is reasonably established.
- It points to real library limitations and prior work, which lends some support to the need for a language-level change.
- The claim that the restriction can only be lifted by the compiler is plausible but not backed by evidence about affected users or real-world code.
- The paper never establishes who is affected in practice or provides meaningful implementation experience beyond a single author’s unelaborated statement.
