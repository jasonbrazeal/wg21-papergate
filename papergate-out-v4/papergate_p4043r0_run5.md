Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives a partial account of why the design discussion is live and why deferral could be useful, but it does not connect that discussion to a concrete case for standardization of the thing it proposes. The support is reasonably concrete on background and alternatives, while the case for affected users and implementation experience rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need, and the argument for a standard rather than another vehicle is essentially absent.

- The strongest support is the recognition that unresolved design concerns, National Body feedback, and the Concepts precedent make timing and deferral a legitimate standardization question.
- The paper also establishes that prior art and alternative proposals exist, even if it does not show how they lead to standardizing this facility now.
- The claim about who is affected leans on a count of related comments without showing how those comments bear on the proposal itself.
- The most glaring omission is any developed argument for why this needs to be in the C++ standard at all, or why a library or non-standard implementation would not suffice.
