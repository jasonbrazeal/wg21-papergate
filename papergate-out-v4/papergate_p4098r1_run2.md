Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a preliminary, often indirect argument for standardization, leaning on broad historical claims and deployment references rather than demonstrating a concrete need for the proposed facility. The support is thinnest where a standardization case normally must be strongest: there is no established rationale for why the standard should contain this, why a library cannot suffice, or how it would interoperate with existing and forthcoming committee output. While the author’s implementation experience is acknowledged, it is presented as personal belief rather than as evidence that the design is ready for the standard.

- The strongest support is the long published history of executors, networking, and asynchronous APIs, even though the paper only claims rather than establishes why that history justifies this proposal.
- Less convincing is the assertion that billions of monthly users and deployment data demonstrate who is affected, since the paper surveys or repeats those claims without tying them directly to the proposal.
- The paper does not establish why a standard is needed at all, leaving the central question of standardization unaddressed.
- Most glaringly, there is no established coordination or interoperability story, nor any argument for why a library would be inadequate, which leaves the proposal’s standardization path essentially unsupported.
