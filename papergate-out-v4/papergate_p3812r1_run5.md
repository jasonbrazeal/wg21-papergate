Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on a single asserted motivation while leaving the surrounding practical and procedural questions unanswered. The support is thinnest where a proposal usually needs to be strongest: in showing who is affected, why standardization is the right mechanism, and whether the approach has any implementation or coordination backing.

- The clearest support is the stated desire to remove a limitation on defaulted copy assignment, framed as a simplification for classes with const or reference members.
- The discussion of prior art gestures toward the core guidelines and lifetime rules, but does not develop them into a comparative argument for the proposed direction.
- The paper gives no concrete sense of the user population, existing workarounds, or real-world code that would benefit.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, interoperability analysis, or explanation of why a library-level solution would not suffice.
