Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some broad framing for why asynchronous abstractions have mattered in committee history, but it does not itself close the case for standardization: most of its support is presented as claims or surveys of claims, and it falls silent on why a standard is needed or why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest material is the paper’s documentation of published claims and prior deployments, which shows that asynchronous design debates have real historical and industrial weight.
- The interoperability discussion at least gestures at a shared thread-pool concern, though it relies on previously stated committee positions rather than establishing a new standardization requirement.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established justification for standardization itself, including why the facility cannot live as a library or what specific coordination only a standard can provide.
