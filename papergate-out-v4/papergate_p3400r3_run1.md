Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper gives a solid account of the problem it wants to solve and the alternatives it is responding to, but much of the broader case for standardization rests on assertion rather than evidence. The thinnest support appears in the areas of affected users, why a library cannot suffice, and implementation experience, where the paper’s claims are not backed by concrete demonstration.

- The strongest support is the paper’s framing of the gap left by C++26 Contracts and the need for a mechanism to control individual contract assertions without external configuration.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also well established, particularly the references to P2900R14, reflection-based annotations, and the planned follow-up work.
- A notable omission is the lack of established evidence about who is affected and why the standard is the necessary venue, since the paper asserts importance and breadth but does not substantiate them.
- The most glaring gap is implementation experience, where the paper offers links and brief statements but does not establish that the proposed approach has been meaningfully validated in practice.
