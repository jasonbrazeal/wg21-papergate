Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, self-referential basis for standardization, resting almost entirely on assertions tied to another proposal rather than building an independent case. The support is thinnest where a reader would expect concrete demonstration of need: affected users, implementation experience, and coordination are absent, and the paper’s own claimed motivations are not substantiated beyond describing a wording inconvenience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s identification of a wording-strategy problem in `std::execution`, even though the significance of that problem remains asserted rather than shown.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is credited only as claimed, since it mainly summarizes P3425 without establishing how this paper’s approach compares in practice.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the proposed change or why existing library-level solutions cannot address the issue.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, leaving no evidence that the proposed wording strategy has been tested or adopted.
