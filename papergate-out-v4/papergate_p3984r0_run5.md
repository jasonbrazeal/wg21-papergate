Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers concrete support for the existence of prototypes and ongoing implementation work, but its broader case for standardization rests largely on asserted needs and problems rather than demonstrated ones. The thinnest areas are those connecting the proposed framework to actual users, alternatives, and the necessity of a standard as opposed to existing tools or libraries.

- The strongest support is the evidence of prototyping and an experimental implementation in a major compiler.
- The rationale for why the problem matters is anchored in general difficulties with existing feature-specific approaches.
- The paper asserts, but does not establish, who is affected beyond a vague “many (probably most)” of users.
- The most glaring omission is a demonstrated case for why the standard, rather than a library or existing tooling, is required to solve the stated coordination problem.
