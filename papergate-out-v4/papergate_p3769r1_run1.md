Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper provides some evidence of divergent implementation behavior, but it does not develop that evidence into a persuasive case that standardization is needed. The argument relies heavily on assertions that are not backed by sufficient detail, and whole categories of justification are simply absent.

- The clearest support comes from the table of current implementation behavior, which at least gestures toward real-world divergence among compilers.
- The paper claims that affected users and implementation experience exist, but it offers almost no elaboration about who those users are or what the practical consequences of the divergence are.
- The cases for why a library solution is impossible and why the standard itself must change are not addressed at all, which leaves the proposal without a foundation in the core question of necessity.
