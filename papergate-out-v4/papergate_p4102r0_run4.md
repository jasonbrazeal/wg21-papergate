Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper makes a credible case in its motivation and prior-art discussion, but the broader case for changing the standard is uneven: the core argument is well connected to existing work, while several practical and procedural points are asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns who actually benefits, why the standard is the only viable venue, and whether implementers have confirmed the intended change is workable.

- The strongest part is the framing of prior art, which clearly situates the proposal against existing relocation work and explains the intended deviation.
- The motivation is also well established, especially the link between relaxing assignment-based complexity and enabling relocation strategies.
- The paper is weaker on implementation experience, where only a passing reference to an existing library strategy is offered without evidence of real-world validation.
- The most glaring omission is coordination and interoperability, where the proposal provides no indication of how the change interacts with related ongoing work or broader ecosystem effects.
