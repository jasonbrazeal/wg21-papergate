Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in some foundational areas, particularly in showing that the three-channel model is correct for compound results and that the problem has recognizable prior art and real coordination across systems. The support is thinnest where standardization itself must be justified: the absence of a case for why this belongs in the standard, combined with only claimed rather than established evidence about who is affected and why a library solution is insufficient, leaves the core standardization argument largely unproven.

- The paper most convincingly establishes that the three-channel model fits the compound-result pattern and that prior work and alternatives have been examined.
- The discussion of affected users and implementation experience is suggestive but not established, relying on examples and claims rather than a demonstrated need across the intended constituency.
- The argument that a library solution cannot suffice is only claimed, leaving open whether existing library mechanisms already address the problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established reason for standardization itself, so the paper never connects its technical observations to a need for standard action.
