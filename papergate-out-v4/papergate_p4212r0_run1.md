Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a meaningful high-level motivation for standardization, but its support is uneven: the need is framed as real, yet much of the case rests on general claims rather than concrete demonstration. The thinnest areas are those that would show the work is ready and necessary specifically in C++—who is affected, what has actually been tried, and what implementation experience exists.

- The strongest support is the identification of genuine underspecification in floating-point behavior and its portability and reliability consequences.
- The paper’s reliance on the C standard and brief references to prior efforts suggests alternatives exist, but it does not establish that those alternatives have been seriously evaluated for C++.
- The case for why this must be done in the standard rather than in a library is asserted mostly through the unsuitability of the C annex, without showing that a library approach has been attempted or found inadequate.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of affected users or implementation experience, leaving the practical demand and feasibility of the proposal unestablished.
