Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial support for the existence of a real, deployed problem and for the need to reconcile two competing standardization efforts, but its case thins considerably when it argues that standardization must follow its particular architectural assumptions rather than the alternative already shipping in practice. The strongest material concerns fragmentation, production experience, and visible inconsistency between the proposals; the weakest concerns the claimed necessity of a single handler slot, menu, and configuration mechanism as the foundation.

- The paper firmly establishes that two proposals now answer the same core question and that their current wording is mutually inconsistent in the public record.
- It credibly grounds the named-guarantee approach in a decade of vendor shipping practice and production-scale measurement.
- It shows clearly that deployed checking facilities terminate rather than route through a replaceable cross-facility handler, leaving the proposed machinery without matching implementation experience.
- It does not establish why the standard must adopt its single-slot, single-menu architectural premise, nor why the profile-based alternative could not serve as the coordinating layer instead.
