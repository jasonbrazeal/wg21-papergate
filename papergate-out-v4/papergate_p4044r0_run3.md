Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper makes a focused but narrow case for why terminating enforcement of certain preconditions matters, while offering almost no supporting evidence for the broader standardization questions. Its strongest material concerns the problem statement, but the argument thins out quickly when it turns to who needs the feature, what alternatives exist, and whether the proposed mechanism has been tested or can be delivered outside the standard.

- The paper credibly establishes that C++26 contracts’ *ignore* semantics create a real gap for libraries trying to enforce UB-safety requirements.
- Its claims about standardization are more asserted than demonstrated, resting on the idea that the feature stays within already-permitted behavior.
- The document gives no account of who is affected, what prior art or alternatives were considered, or what implementation experience exists.
- Most glaringly, it never explains why a library-level solution cannot address the problem, leaving the central question of why this belongs in the standard unanswered.
