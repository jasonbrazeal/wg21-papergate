Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives some reason to think that current and proposed facilities raise design questions worth taking seriously, but it offers very little evidence that the problem described belongs in the standard, that users are concretely affected, or that standardization is the necessary remedy. The support is thinnest around the questions that matter most for a proposal: identifying the affected audience, showing why non-standard solutions are insufficient, and demonstrating any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the observation that existing and proposed math-function facilities may be accumulating incoherently and that user-defined simd types raise performance and customization concerns.
- The paper gestures at prior art and interoperation problems by discussing P2964R5 and customization limits, but it does not establish that alternatives were studied or that coordination needs are more than asserted.
- The paper never establishes who is actually harmed by the current state of affairs or why a library cannot address those harms outside the standard.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed standardization path has been tried and validated.
