Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper provides only scattered support for the need to standardize its proposed change, and much of that support is asserted rather than demonstrated. The argument is thinnest in establishing who is affected, why a library solution would not suffice, and why the standard is the right venue.

- The strongest support is the claimed implementation divergence and the cited workaround in GCC trunk, which at least gestures at real-world friction.
- The paper claims a historical intent behind the current behavior, but does not establish how that history bears on the need for standardization now.
- The paper does not establish who is affected or what the practical consequences are for users beyond a brief reference to a hardened precondition failure.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard, rather than an implementation or library-level fix, is necessary or appropriate.
