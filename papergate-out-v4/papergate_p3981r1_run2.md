Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gestures repeatedly toward benefits of `optional<T&>` and familiarity with optional references outside the standard library, but it rarely converts those gestures into a demonstrated need for changing the affected standard library returns. The strongest support is its acknowledgment of prior discussion and related proposals, while the thinnest areas concern motivation, affected users, and why the standard itself must act.

- The paper establishes some prior art by referencing existing debate, a related proposal, and its own earlier design changes.
- Its claims about practical clunkiness and external experience are asserted rather than shown with concrete evidence or examples.
- The terms “who is affected,” “why the standard,” “why a library will not do,” and “implementation experience” remain largely unsubstantiated beyond passing references.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of a developed case for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library or as a non-standard convention.
