Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a very thin case for its own standardization: it gestures at usefulness and at inconsistencies introduced by earlier papers, but it does not substantiate those claims or connect them to a demonstrated need for a standard facility. The support becomes entirely absent once the discussion moves beyond motivation, leaving the standardization rationale largely unargued.

- The strongest support is a plausible statement that the facility would be useful and occasionally asked for, though this is asserted rather than shown.
- The paper points to prior work in P2996R13 and to inconsistencies among adopted papers, but it does not establish that these create a problem requiring a new standard feature.
- It never explains why the standard is the right venue, why existing library or reflection facilities cannot suffice, or how the proposal would coordinate with related features.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience or any other evidence that the proposed facility has been tried, validated, or is ready for standardization.
