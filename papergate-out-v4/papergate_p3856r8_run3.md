Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a partial account of its standardization case, strongest when comparing reflection-based metafunctions with traditional type traits but noticeably thin in grounding the problem’s scale, the need for committee action, and real-world validation of the proposed facility. Most of the surrounding argument remains asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The comparison with traditional type traits and the promising direction of reflection metafunctions offers a credible basis for exploring the design space.
- The paper claims broad relevance for structural type queries and suggests library implementers already need such functionality, but it does not substantiate who is affected or how widely the absence is felt.
- The paper does not establish why standardization is necessary as opposed to a library solution, nor does it demonstrate meaningful implementation experience beyond a sample implementation and a brief mention of a Clang fork.
- The most glaring omission is any concrete evidence about the affected user population, which leaves the central motivation asserted without a clear audience or use-case foundation.
