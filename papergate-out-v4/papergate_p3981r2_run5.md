Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives only a thin account of why these return-type changes belong in the standard, leaning heavily on assertions that the choice is better without developing the surrounding case. Its strongest material concerns the general motivation, while nearly every other requirement rests on a passing reference or an unsupported claim rather than evidence or analysis.

- The paper does explain why optional references are a worthwhile general direction and how the existing pointer-returning APIs create awkwardness in ordinary use.
- It gestures at prior art and related proposals, but it neither examines those alternatives in enough detail nor shows how they inform the specific design decisions here.
- The clearest omission is the lack of any demonstrated implementation experience or concrete evidence of how the proposed return types behave in real code.
- The paper never establishes why a library solution outside the standard is insufficient, which leaves the central standardization question substantially unanswered.
