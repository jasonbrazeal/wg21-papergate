Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest footing is in implementation evidence, where it can point to concrete compiler agreement and a Clang rejection case. Beyond that, the case rests largely on asserted intent and informal readings of existing wording, with little developed argument for the affected audience, alternatives, or coordination. The discussion of why a library solution cannot address the problem and why the standard is the necessary venue is essentially absent.

- The clearest support is the compiler data showing broad agreement across implementations, including a concrete Clang behavior relevant to the proposed change.
- The paper claims significance and affectedness through examples and references, but does not develop them into a substantiated case about real-world impact or the community involved.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives gestures at historical intent and implementation differences, but leaves the comparison and justification mostly asserted rather than established.
- The most conspicuous omissions are any argument for why standardization is required and why a library-level approach would not suffice.
