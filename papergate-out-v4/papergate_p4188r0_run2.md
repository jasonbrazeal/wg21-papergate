Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper’s case rests most heavily on implementation experience, which it does demonstrate, but the surrounding justification is largely asserted rather than shown. The thinnest support appears wherever the paper claims community impact, existing practice, or the impossibility of out-of-standard solutions, since those arguments are repeated without evidence that connects them to the specific need for standardization.

- The strongest support is the availability of a proof-of-concept implementation compiling under GCC, Clang, and MSVC, which shows the proposed mechanism is technically realizable.
- The paper also points to independent convergence in libraries such as mp-units, Eigen, and Boost.Units, though this is presented as a claim more than a documented survey.
- The weakest area is the repeated assertion that the problem cannot be solved outside the standard or that standardization is necessary, since no concrete demonstration rules out the alternatives the paper itself mentions.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of substantiated evidence for the claimed breadth of users and libraries affected, leaving the actual demand for standardization inferred rather than established.
