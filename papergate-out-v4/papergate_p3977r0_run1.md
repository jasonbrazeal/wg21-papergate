Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a solid conceptual foundation for why the existing contract terminology is insufficient, and it credibly situates its vocabulary against the standard’s newer erroneous-behaviour category. The support becomes much thinner, however, when the paper turns to standardization-specific justifications: the affected audience is never identified, and the arguments about ABI, interoperability, and the inadequacy of library solutions are asserted rather than demonstrated. Implementation experience is likewise gestured toward rather than shown.

- The strongest part of the paper is its established motivation, drawing on concrete examples and recent contract discussions to show that current classifications fail to describe real cases.
- The treatment of prior art is also well supported, explicitly connecting the proposal to erroneous behaviour and existing wide/narrow contract categories.
- The most recurring weakness is that the ABI and binary-compatibility argument is repeated as though repetition itself established the claim, without evidence of affected users or real breakage.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established statement about who is affected by the problem, which leaves the standardization need largely abstract.
