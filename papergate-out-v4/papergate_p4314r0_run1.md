Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only the beginnings of a rationale, built around reported confusion in terminology and user expectations, but it does not develop that observation into a case that the standard must act. The support is thinnest where it matters most: there is no argument tying the perceived confusion to a defect in the standard, nor any viable path through the standardization process.

- The strongest material is the author’s report that repeated discussions have revealed confusion about what activating a profile actually does, which at least gestures toward a real communication problem.
- The paper acknowledges that other proposals could address removals independently, suggesting some awareness of alternative routes, though it does not compare them.
- The paper leaves the standard’s role entirely undefended, never showing why the confusion it describes is a standardization problem rather than a documentation or education problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence of practical interoperability concerns that would make a standards-level change necessary.
