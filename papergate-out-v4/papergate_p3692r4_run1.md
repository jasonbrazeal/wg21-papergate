Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers solid support in places, particularly around the underlying problem, prior discussion, and the motivation for constraining invented loads, but it leaves several crucial standardization arguments closer to assertion than demonstration. The thinnest support concerns implementation experience, library alternatives, and the actual consequences for users and implementers of the proposed standardese.

- The strongest support is for prior art and alternatives, where the paper traces existing discussions, folklore, and compiler flags well enough to situate its approach.
- The need for a standard change is asserted mainly through the smallness and non-normative character of the edit, rather than through a shown gap that only the standard can fill.
- The paper does not establish implementation experience, since the claim that real implementations avoid out-of-thin-air behavior is reported as unknown rather than demonstrated with evidence.
- The most glaring omission is an explanation of why a library or other non-standard mechanism cannot deliver the guidance the paper wants in the standard.
