Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest support is its clear demonstration that current implementations diverge from the CWG 1395 resolution and that the proposed direction matches widespread existing practice. Beyond that motivating observation, however, the document offers little concrete evidence about the affected user population, implementation experience, or why standardization is preferable to other remedies. The case is therefore real but largely inferential, leaning heavily on a single claim about implementer behavior.

- The paper clearly establishes that the current specification is not implemented by major compilers and that existing practice disagrees with the normative result.
- The paper establishes concrete prior art by identifying the relevant issue and the alternative of reverting most of CWG 1395 while keeping its tie-breaker.
- The paper merely claims rather than establishes who is affected, offering only a brief reference to one implementation’s bug reports.
- The paper does not establish implementation experience or why standardization is needed beyond gesturing toward existing practice.
