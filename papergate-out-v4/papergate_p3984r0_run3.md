Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a surface-level argument for its own standardization, with nearly every required element asserted rather than demonstrated and one element entirely unaddressed. The support is thinnest where the reader would expect concrete evidence: there is no case made for why a standard is necessary, and the claims about implementation experience and prior art rest on unnamed or loosely cited work rather than reproducible detail.

- The strongest support comes from the mention of an experimental implementation in a major compiler, though even that is repeated rather than substantiated with specifics.
- The paper gestures toward alternatives and prior art, but it does not engage with them beyond saying the current variety is chaotic and profiles would reduce it.
- The absence of any argument for why the standard itself is needed leaves the central standardization question unanswered.
