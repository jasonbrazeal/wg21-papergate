Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper makes a solid start on motivation and context, particularly by grounding the proposal in prior proposals, existing libraries, and the practical impossibility of a portable implementation. Its case thins considerably, however, around the arguments that standardization is necessary and that the proposed design coordinates well with the rest of C++. Much of what is marked as claimed leans heavily on a single assertion about non-portability rather than building out sustained evidence.

- The strongest support lies in prior art and alternatives, where the paper clearly positions itself against earlier proposals, Boost experience, and recent rejected or related efforts.
- The motivation is established, especially through the tooling consequences and the inability to express stack switching portably.
- The thinnest areas are standardization rationale, coordination, and implementation experience, which are asserted rather than demonstrated with enough supporting detail.
- Most glaringly, the paper does not establish who is concretely affected with the rigor needed to show widespread, urgent demand.
