Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper leans almost entirely on assertions about intent and similarity to existing syntax, but it does not develop those assertions into a demonstration that the proposed feature solves a pressing problem for identifiable users. The thinnest parts are the complete absence of discussion about who is affected, what alternatives were actually considered, and how implementation experience or library solutions bear on the question.

- The strongest support is the brief claim that there is no good reason for the current limitation and that the intent was always to be able to index all packs.
- The discussion of prior art gestures toward related proposals and an editorial prerequisite, but it stops short of explaining what workable alternatives exist or why they are inadequate.
- The paper offers no account of the affected audience or the concrete problems they face today.
- The most glaring omission is that there is no evidence of implementation experience, coordination concerns, or any reason to prefer a language change over a library-based approach.
