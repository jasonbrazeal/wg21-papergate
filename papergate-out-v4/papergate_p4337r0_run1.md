Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides some concrete grounding for its proposal through existing implementation experience and recognizable prior art, but much of the argument for why this belongs in the standard rests on assertion rather than demonstration. The thinnest support appears where the paper claims broad user impact, unique standardization value, and interoperability without substantiating those claims with evidence or examples.

- The proposal’s strongest support is its implementation experience, with a working implementation and multiple existing names for the same technique credited as prior art.
- The paper establishes alternatives by pointing to a prior abandoned proposal and the exposition-only helper already in `std::execution`.
- The most glaring omission is the repeated but unsubstantiated claim that `std::execution` will make users increasingly need this facility, with no evidence tying that to actual demand beyond the standard library’s internal use.
