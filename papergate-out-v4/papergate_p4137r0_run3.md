Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s strongest support for standardization lies in its discussion of why the problem matters and its engagement with prior art, but it leaves several essential questions unanswered. The case is thinnest around who would actually be affected, how the feature would coordinate with other parts of the language, why a library solution is insufficient, and whether the described tooling has been meaningfully exercised.

- The paper clearly establishes that the verifiable subset’s coverage of real codebases is unknown, and frames the annotation dividend as the relevant practical measure.
- It grounds its approach in existing proposals and lifetime-safety work, showing awareness of the rules and the analytical lineage.
- The argument for why standardization is necessary rests on the asserted relevance of the measurement even for new code, but that relevance is claimed rather than demonstrated.
- The implementation experience is asserted through the availability of tools and the small size of a potential implementation, but no evidence of actual use or validation is provided.
- The paper does not establish who is affected, how the feature interoperates with the broader standard, or why a library cannot achieve the same result.
