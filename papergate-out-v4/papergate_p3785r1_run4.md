Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers meaningful support for the motivating pattern and for the existence of widespread prior art, but it leaves several foundational parts of the standardization case essentially unargued. The thinnest areas are audience impact, coordination with affected specifications, and why existing library mechanisms cannot achieve the same result.

- The strongest support is the demonstrated repetition of default postfix semantics across many standard iterator operations, which establishes a plausible prior-art and wording-simplification case.
- The paper also credibly identifies a design-approved predecessor proposal and a concrete pool of affected operations.
- The case for why this belongs in the standard rather than remaining a library convention is asserted but not developed.
- The most glaring omission is any account of who is affected or how existing code and implementations would experience the change.
