Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful support for the conceptual motivation behind its taxonomy and shows familiarity with relevant prior work, but it leaves several essential standardization questions largely unargued. The thinnest areas are the lack of evidence about who is affected and the absence of implementation experience, while the case for why the standard—rather than a library or existing terminology—must change remains asserted more than demonstrated.

- The paper does establish that the existing contract classification can be counterintuitive and that current terminology is inadequate for cases such as non-ignorable contracts.
- It also credibly grounds the discussion in prior art, including the wide/narrow scheme, essential behaviour, and erroneous behaviour.
- The argument for standardization specifically relies on claims about ABI implications and caller-side versus callee-side checking that are not yet established.
- There is no implementation experience or clear picture of who is affected, leaving the practical need for standardization largely unsupported.
