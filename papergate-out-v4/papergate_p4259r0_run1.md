Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s strongest support comes from its argument that the proposed names match widespread terminology and that the alternative names in the other proposal are out of step with existing practice. That support, however, is concentrated almost entirely on naming, while the case for standardization itself remains largely asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any established need for a standard facility, the lack of implementation experience, and the failure to show why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper establishes that the existing alternative names are poor and that “floor” and “ceiling” division are the terms users would actually expect.
- Its claims about who is affected and about broad precedent in math and programming are plausible but not backed up with concrete evidence.
- The paper does not establish that the standard is the right place for this functionality, nor why a library implementation would not suffice.
- There is no implementation experience presented to support the feasibility or readiness of the proposal.
