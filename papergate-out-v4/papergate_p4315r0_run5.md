Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only the beginnings of a rationale for why a profiles-related facility might matter, and even that support is mostly asserted rather than demonstrated against concrete code, costs, or alternatives. Beyond those fragments, the document provides almost no self-standing case for standardization, leaving most of the required groundwork—audience, standard need, coordination, library sufficiency, and implementation experience—unaddressed.

- The strongest material is a general claim that profiles may disallow constructs that are unavoidable in small amounts of code or necessary for tooling, which at least gestures at a tension worth exploring.
- The paper also makes an unexplained claim that many profiles create safety guarantees, but does not connect that to a measurable benefit or a specific standardization need.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of evidence about who is affected and how existing code, users, or implementations would actually experience the proposed facility.
- Equally absent is any treatment of why a library cannot provide the capability, or how the proposal would coordinate with existing standards and implementations.
