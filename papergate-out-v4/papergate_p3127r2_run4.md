Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some useful groundwork by explaining why the proposed graph model matters and by situating it within existing terminology and practice, but it does not yet make a case for standardization itself. The thinnest support is around the central questions: why this belongs in the standard, how it would coordinate with existing or future library work, and what implementation experience justifies normative action.

- The strongest support is for prior art and alternatives, where the paper grounds its terminology in established sources and relates its concepts to common library operations.
- The paper also clearly establishes why the problem matters by identifying concrete cases that the current definitions cannot represent.
- The weakest area is the absence of any established argument for why the standard, rather than a library, is the right venue, alongside missing discussion of interoperability and implementation experience.
