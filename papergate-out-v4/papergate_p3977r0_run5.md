Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful conceptual support for its central claim that the existing vocabulary for contracts is inadequate, and it does useful work situating that argument against prior discussions and the current standard. That support thins considerably when the paper turns to the practical case for standardization, where the effects on users, the need for standard rather than library action, and interoperability concerns are asserted rather than demonstrated. Most seriously, the absence of any implementation experience leaves the proposal without evidence that the ideas can be realized in practice.

- The strongest part of the paper is its motivation, which credibly identifies a gap in existing contract terminology and explains why that gap matters for reasoning about non-ignorable contracts.
- The discussion of prior art is also well grounded, showing how earlier classifications and the C++26 erroneous behaviour framework fail to capture the situations the paper describes.
- The case for why this belongs in the standard is largely asserted, with little concrete demonstration of what users could not achieve through existing or library-level mechanisms.
- The paper offers no implementation experience, so it never shows that the proposed concepts can be expressed, compiled, or used in a real system.
