Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only assertions in every area where it needs to make a case for standardization, and none of those assertions is backed by evidence, examples, or analysis sufficient to move beyond a claim. The support is thinnest around implementation experience and prior art, where even the quoted material gestures at existing practice but does not demonstrate that a standardized framework is the necessary or adequate response.

- The strongest support is the reference to experience with [[gsl::suppress]], which at least points toward some in-field usage, though it does not establish broader implementation experience for the proposed framework itself.
- The paper repeatedly asserts that without a common framework suppliers will produce incompatible options, but it never shows this outcome occurring or explains why existing coordination mechanisms would fail.
- The discussion of prior art dismisses a relationship to another proposal as not competing without demonstrating what unique need this paper’s framework protocol fills.
- The most glaring omission is any substantiation for why educators, suppliers, or tool users are actually affected in ways that require a standard rather than documentation, convention, or library-level cooperation.
