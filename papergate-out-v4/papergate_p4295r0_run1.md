Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a slender case for standardization, with most of its burden carried by a single concrete implementation and a brief appeal to code structure rather than by evidence of need, reach, or fit with the standard. The supporting material is thinnest where it matters most: there is no established audience, no argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and no substantive discussion of prior art or coordination.

- The strongest support is the availability of a partial implementation, which at least shows the design has been tried in practice.
- The paper gestures at prior art by mentioning an earlier concurrent queue proposal, but does not establish how that history justifies the current design.
- The claimed benefit to code structure is asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the rationale for both interface ends underdeveloped.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why this cannot be delivered as a library or why standardization is required at all.
