Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper provides a useful reconstruction of the procedural history and a credible argument that an architectural decision is being made without an explicit poll, but it falls short of demonstrating who is concretely affected, why the standard is required, or what implementation experience teaches about the proposed design. The strongest material concerns prior art and the need to revisit the layering question; the thinnest support surrounds deployment evidence and the claim that a library solution is insufficient.

- The paper’s strongest support is its documented reconstruction of seven polls, none of which adopted the architecture now implied by the proposal.
- It also establishes that the layering question sits between two published bodies of work and is substantive rather than hypothetical.
- Its argument for affected parties relies on a single deployment cost figure and an asymmetry of deployment history that it does not develop into a clear population of users or consequences.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of concrete implementation or deployment experience showing that the standard must specify the architecture claimed, rather than leaving it to vendors or a library.
