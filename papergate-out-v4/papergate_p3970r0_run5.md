Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers a solid starting rationale by connecting its work to ongoing committee direction and the broader, well-founded pressure for safer C++, but much of the case for standardization remains asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is around interoperability with existing or competing approaches and concrete implementation experience, where the paper gestures toward activity rather than providing evidence.

- The strongest support comes from the clear need to avoid divergent tool-chain interfaces and boilerplate, which the paper ties directly to an existing specification and ongoing compiler work.
- The paper claims broad and repeated committee endorsement for Profiles as the direction, but does not substantiate that with evidence or references to specific votes.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives merely asserts that building on the Profiles framework is the way forward, without showing how other approaches were considered or why they fall short.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience, where the paper mentions an experimental implementation in a major compiler but provides no details, results, or lessons learned.
