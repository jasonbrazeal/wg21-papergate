Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful support for its core procedural point—that design goals for `zstring_view` must be settled before a constructor set can be evaluated—and it adequately grounds that point in prior art and alternative directions. Its case for standardization action is much thinner when it comes to showing why the standard specifically is needed, what the affected audience concretely loses, or how implementations would converge.

- The strongest element is the clear, established argument that the design goal must be defined before LEWG can assess constructors or even decide whether to proceed.
- The paper also establishes that it is following an existing direction and narrowing its focus to goal definition, with prior art and alternative approaches credited.
- Support for implementation experience is claimed only in a scattered way, mostly by saying many implementations exist but differ or lack stated goals, which does not by itself demonstrate what standardization would learn from them.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why this cannot be addressed by a library, leaving a central question about standardization unanswered.
