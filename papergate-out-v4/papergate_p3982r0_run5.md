Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper gives uneven support for its own standardization, with the most concrete material appearing in its implementation experience and comparison against prior art, while much of the motivation remains asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of a clear argument for why a library solution cannot address the stated gaps and why the affected audience or standardization need is actually pressing.

- The strongest support is the implementation work, including a patch series and reported benchmark details, which shows the proposed direction is at least technically executable in practice.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also grounded, citing existing practice in common languages and the earlier P2630 design rationale.
- The paper asserts that the current interface has performance and expressiveness failures, but it does not establish the concrete impact on users or use cases.
- Most notably missing is a substantive case that the problem cannot be solved adequately in a library, leaving the standardization need largely unproven.
