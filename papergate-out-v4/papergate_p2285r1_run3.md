Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers useful evidence that the relevant behavior is observable, inconsistently implemented, and tied to existing core issues, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case more asserted than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns who is concretely affected, why a standard change is the necessary remedy, and whether a library-based approach could suffice.

- The strongest support is the compilation of concrete cross-compiler behavior and references to existing core and implementation issues, which grounds the problem in current practice.
- The paper also establishes that there is implementation experience with the intended interpretation, since several compilers already follow the approach the proposal appears to favor.
- The case for affected users is weaker because it relies on general statements about what many users assume or what some compilers reinforce, without showing the scope or cost of the impact.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of why a library solution would not address the need, leaving that part of the standardization justification essentially unexamined.
