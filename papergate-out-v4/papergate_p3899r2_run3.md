Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper provides a reasonably solid foundation for its standardization case, particularly by establishing that the current wording is unclear and that the proposed behavior has genuine precedent in the library and in prior core-language discussions. The support is thinnest where the paper relies on implementation behavior without demonstrating that the affected user population is real or that a library-level solution is impossible.

- The strongest element is the paper’s demonstration that the status quo is genuinely unclear and that the proposed direction aligns with existing library conventions such as range errors in mathematical functions.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also well grounded, since it identifies concrete wording disputes and contradictions that motivate a clarifying change.
- The weakest area is the absence of any established case for why a library solution cannot address the problem, leaving a significant part of the standardization rationale unstated.
- The claims about affected users and implementation experience are asserted largely through compiler comparisons rather than evidence of real-world code impact or formal implementation reports.
