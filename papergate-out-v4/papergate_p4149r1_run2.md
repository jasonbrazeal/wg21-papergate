Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin, preliminary rationale for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on asserted goals of consistency and a passing claim about uniqueness in the language. Beyond that motivating frame, it does not substantiate who would be affected, what alternatives were considered, why a standard is required rather than a library or other mechanism, or whether anyone has relevant implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s statement that CWG wants a unified definition of “immediate context” and greater consistency across related language features.
- The only gesture toward prior art or alternatives is an unsupported claim that one rule would be “unlike anything else in the language.”
- The paper is entirely silent about who is affected, so it never establishes the practical scope or constituency for the change.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, leaving the standardization need asserted rather than demonstrated in practice.
