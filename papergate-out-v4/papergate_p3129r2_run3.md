Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a scattering of indirect support for its own standardization, with most of the case resting on references to another proposal and to an unspecified reference library. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected users, any reason the standard library is the right home, any argument that a library solution would not suffice, and any meaningful implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the claimed prior art in P3130 and a reference implementation, though both are merely gestured toward rather than demonstrated.
- The paper claims a coordinating audience across several study groups, but provides no evidence of actual coordination or interoperability with other work.
- The paper never establishes who is affected by the proposed feature or why the standard, rather than an external library, is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience beyond a one-sentence pointer to a reference library with no details about its use, maturity, or adoption.
