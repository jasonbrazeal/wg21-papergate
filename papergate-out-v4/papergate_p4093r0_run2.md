Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper is strongest when demonstrating that the described mechanism exists and works in practice, but it leaves much of the surrounding case—who exactly needs this, why alternatives are insufficient, and why the standard must act—stated as assertion rather than argument. The thinnest support sits precisely where standardization proposals are expected to be most persuasive: establishing the affected population, ruling out library-level solutions, and showing coordination with the broader sender ecosystem beyond a few quoted sentences.

- The implementation appendix and description of zero-allocation behavior on a pool worker establish that the core mechanism has been built and exercised.
- The claim that no alternative preserves both values and composition is repeated, but the paper does not develop the comparison enough to show why the proposed approach is necessary.
- The affected audience is asserted only as “the most common I/O result shapes,” with no evidence or survey anchoring that claim.
- The paper does not establish why a library cannot absorb the cost or why the standard is the right place to act, leaving the “why standardize” question largely unanswered.
