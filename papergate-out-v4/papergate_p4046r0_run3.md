Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers meaningful support in some foundational areas, particularly in its articulation of why the committee’s tacit knowledge is hard to preserve and in demonstrating that the proposed workflow has actually been used. However, the case for standardization itself is thin where it matters most: there is no established need for the standard to act, and several key claims about affected users, portability gaps, and interoperability are asserted without evidence.

- The strongest support is for implementation experience, since the paper describes a concrete framework and applies it to a real proposal with reproducible output.
- The paper also establishes prior art and alternatives by engaging directly with P4023R0 and showing how the approach follows or diverges from it.
- The weakest established ground is the lack of any case for why a library will not do, leaving the central standardization question unanswered.
- Most glaringly, the paper never shows who is concretely blocked today in a way that only a standard can resolve, instead relying on benchmarks and general statements about portable async I/O.
