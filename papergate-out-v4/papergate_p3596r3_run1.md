Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers some useful groundwork by documenting prior efforts and providing an implementation branch, but its case for standardization rests largely on unsupported assertions. The thinnest parts are the absence of any argument for why this belongs in the standard itself or why a library-based approach would not suffice, along with unsubstantiated claims about who benefits and how the work would coordinate with other efforts.

- The strongest support comes from the existence of prior art and an actual collaborative implementation branch of the working draft.
- The paper identifies earlier proposals and existing groupings for undefined behavior, which gives readers a sense of continuity.
- The claims about usefulness and affected readers are asserted without evidence or concrete examples of the decision-making value the annex would provide.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any explanation for why the standard, rather than a separately maintained reference document or library, is the necessary home for this catalog.
