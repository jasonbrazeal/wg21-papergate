Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper provides a focused rationale for why enforceable contract conditions are needed, but its support for standardization is uneven: the motivating problem is clear, while the case for a standard facility remains mostly asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who would be affected, why existing library mechanisms cannot serve, or what implementation experience exists.

- The strongest element is the established claim that there is an unmet need for function authors to specify enforced contract conditions, grounded in a concrete safety failure scenario.
- The paper gestures toward prior standardization attempts and a design direction, but does not substantively establish what was learned from those attempts or why the proposed separation of agreement and enforcement requires standardization.
- The discussion of coordination and interoperability offers only an analogy about rules versus offers, with little evidence about how the feature would interact with existing or proposed C++ facilities.
- The most glaring omissions are the complete lack of evidence regarding affected users, implementation experience, and why a library-based approach would be insufficient.
