Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gestures at the reasons constexpr support in `<random>` would be useful, but it does not develop those reasons into a concrete case for standardization. Much of the support is anecdotal or asserted rather than demonstrated, and several areas that would normally ground a proposal—coordination, library sufficiency, implementation experience—are effectively absent.

- The strongest material is the implementation change linked by the author, which at least suggests some practical experimentation with the idea.
- The paper quotes external conversations and code snippets as motivating evidence, but these remain isolated examples rather than a worked prior-art or user-need argument.
- The discussion of who is affected and why the standard is needed relies on broad claims about usefulness and duplication without showing the scope or cost of the problem.
- Most notably, the paper offers no discussion of interoperability with other standards or why the same goals could not be met by an ordinary library.
