Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin rationale for standardization, built mainly on a narrow historical correction and a passing reference to a prior specification change. The support is almost entirely retrospective, and the document does not develop a forward-looking case for affected users, standardizing rather than using a library, coordination, or implementability.

- The clearest support is the observation that the current wording changed previously and that the allowance for incomplete types was inadvertently dropped.
- The paper asserts why that restoration matters, but it does not expand on the practical consequences for users or code relying on the older allowance.
- It does not identify who is affected, how this interacts with other specifications, or what implementation experience exists.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard must address this rather than leaving it to a library or simply accepting the current wording.
