Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, largely asserted case for standardizing indexing of packs of template-names, with most of its supporting claims resting on informal confidence rather than demonstrated evidence. The thinnest areas are the absence of a motivation tied to concrete user or language impact, the lack of exploration of alternatives beyond a few cited proposals, and no discussion of interoperability, library solutions, or implementation experience beyond a brief prediction.

- The strongest support is the paper’s acknowledgement that related features and proposals must be considered, even if that consideration is mostly framed as an unresolved question rather than a completed analysis.
- The claim that lack of indexing for template-name packs has “no good reason” gestures at a rationale but does not itself establish why the standard should change.
- No evidence is offered about who is affected or why the feature matters in practice, leaving the user need inferred rather than shown.
- The paper does not address why a library cannot provide the capability or how the change coordinates with existing and in-flight features, and its implementation support is only a statement of confidence.
