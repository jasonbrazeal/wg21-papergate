Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper makes a genuine effort to situate its design against existing alternatives, but most of the essential justification for standardization rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need or experience. The thinnest support appears wherever the document relies on safety and optimization claims without showing how those benefits require a standard rather than another mechanism.

- The strongest support is the paper’s engagement with prior art, including a clear contrast with P2900 assertions and a deliberate divergence from P3911.
- The weakest support is the treatment of implementation experience, which concedes that the full proposal has not been implemented and offers only a claim that its constituent parts have been.
- A similarly glaring omission is the lack of substantiation for why a library solution would be insufficient, beyond a brief warning about opaque function definitions.
- The paper also leaves unestablished who is concretely affected and why the standard is the necessary vehicle for these guarantees, making the motivation feel asserted more than demonstrated.
