Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest support comes from its motivation and its account of practical implementation experience, but it does not yet make a complete case for standardization because several important elements are only asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The clearest strength is the established motivation: the paper shows why wrapping shift behavior is counterintuitive and why undefined behavior for overlong shifts is gratuitous.
- The paper also offers credible prior-art and implementation grounding, including identified compiler reliance and available reference code and benchmarks.
- The case for standardization is weakest around coordination and interoperability, where nothing is established about how the proposal would fit with existing practice, ABIs, or other standards.
