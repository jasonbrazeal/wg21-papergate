Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a faint outline of a case for its own standardization, resting entirely on a single sentence about improving the situation by introducing new nonterminals and relocating their definitions. That sentence is asked to carry both the motivation and the alternatives discussion, but it does not actually explain what problem the change solves, who benefits, or why existing practice is insufficient. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of any discussion of affected users, why the standard is the right venue, interoperability concerns, why a library cannot address the need, or any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s stated aim to improve the situation by adding nonterminals and placing their definitions in the relevant subclauses.
- The alternatives discussion is only gestured at through that same aim, without naming or evaluating any other approach.
- The paper says nothing about who would be affected by the proposed change.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience, leaving no evidence that the proposed approach has been tried or validated.
