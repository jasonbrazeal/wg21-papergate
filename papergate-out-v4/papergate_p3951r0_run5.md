Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in a few areas—particularly prior art, implementation experience, and the basic motivation for better interpolation ergonomics—but much of the broader case for standardization rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest support is around why this must be a language feature in the standard, who concretely benefits, and how it coordinates with existing library capabilities.

- The strongest support is the working Clang implementation, which demonstrates that the proposal is technically realizable and not purely speculative.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also substantive, clearly distinguishing this design from earlier proposals and showing deliberate comparison rather than assumption.
- The motivation for readability and safer ordering of interpolations is established, though its significance is presented as judgment rather than measured evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of a convincing case for standardization specifically, since the paper largely asserts that a library solution is insufficient and that the language needs this feature without demonstrating who is concretely blocked today.
