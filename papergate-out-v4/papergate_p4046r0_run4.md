Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper does establish why this knowledge matters and shows meaningful engagement with prior work, but it leaves the central standardization question largely unaddressed. The strongest material concerns the value of tacit committee judgment, while the thinnest support surrounds why this needs to be in the standard at all.

- The paper convincingly argues that WG21’s most valuable knowledge is tacit evaluative judgment rather than written documentation.
- The treatment of prior art is solid, particularly in distinguishing the proposed methodology from P4023R0’s code-focused approach.
- The paper claims relevance to standardization and interoperability, but never demonstrates why the standard is the right vehicle rather than a separate guidance document or community resource.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why standardization—as opposed to a standing document, process change, or library—is necessary.
