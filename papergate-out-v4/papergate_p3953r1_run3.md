Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a clear and convincing reason that the current name has become misleading, but it offers very little beyond that to justify putting the rename into the standard. Its support is thinnest on the practical, process-oriented questions that usually matter most for a standardization change.

- The strongest part of the paper is its explanation that `std::runtime_format` can now be evaluated at compile time, so the name no longer matches its actual behavior.
- The paper gestures toward improved consistency with existing terminology such as dynamic format specifiers, but it does not develop that into a case that the standard is the right place to fix the problem.
- The paper asserts there will be no impact on existing code and treats the proposed name as the obvious alternative, but it does not establish either point with evidence or comparison.
- The paper is silent on implementation experience, coordination with other proposals or implementations, and why this could not be addressed outside the standard.
