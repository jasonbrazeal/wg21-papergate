Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for its importance and for the availability of prior work to build on, but its case for standardization is uneven: several essential points are asserted rather than demonstrated. Its thinnest support lies in showing why the standard is the right vehicle, why a library cannot suffice, and what practical implementation experience already exists.

- The strongest support is the clear statement of widespread demand for improved safety and simpler C++ use, paired with the recognition that divergent experimental profiles would impose heavy boilerplate and incompatible tool-chain interfaces.
- The paper also establishes relevant prior art and alternatives by explicitly building on the proposed Profiles framework and the earlier framework-for-development paper.
- The need for coordination and interoperability is treated as established, since the paper argues that different initial profiles would otherwise require too much boilerplate and incompatible compiler options, annotations, and build settings.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: the paper merely says an experimental implementation is being conducted in a major compiler, without evidence or detail sufficient to establish that experience.
