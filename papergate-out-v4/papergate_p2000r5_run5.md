Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers modest but uneven support for its own standardization, with its strongest material concentrated in broad statements about C++’s complexity and the need for coherent direction. Beyond that general motivation, the case becomes much thinner, especially wherever the paper needs to show that standardization is the right vehicle or that the work has been validated in practice.

- The paper does establish a genuine concern that C++ risks losing coherence and that the committee underattends to ordinary developers and novices.
- The claims about who is affected rest on anecdote and a show of hands, without evidence that the described population actually exists at the suggested scale or shares the paper’s framing.
- The discussion of prior work gestures toward existing documents and TS experience, but does not show how those alternatives were compared or why they are insufficient.
- The paper offers no meaningful account of coordination or interoperability, no argument for why a library would not suffice, and no implementation experience to support standardization.
