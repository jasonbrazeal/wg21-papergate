Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper’s strongest support for standardization lies in its implementation experience, where it shows that the mechanisms it describes are already deployed in compilers and sanitisers. Beyond that, most of the case rests on asserted importance and claimed connections to existing work rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are the lack of a developed argument for why a library solution is insufficient and why the standard itself must change.

- The concrete deployment evidence for runtime checks and field-tested compiler behavior gives the paper a real foundation.
- The paper asserts that poor tool integration and undefined behavior are significant, but it does not establish who is affected with concrete consequences.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives mainly points at related papers and existing mechanisms without showing why they are inadequate.
- The absence of a substantive argument for why a library cannot address the problem leaves a central standardization justification undeveloped.
