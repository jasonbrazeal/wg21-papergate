Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper gives a credible account of a real problem—implementations falling behind the pace of standardization—but it does little to connect that concern to any particular proposal or demonstrate that standardization is the right remedy. The thinnest support is on the questions that matter most: why the standard should change, how the change would interoperate, and what experience backs it.

- The strongest part of the paper is its motivation, especially the observation that putting features in the standard is not the end and that incomplete foundations increase portability risk.
- The paper claims, mainly through a description of a Kona 2025 implementer discussion, that a relevant group is affected, but it does not establish who they are or what specific need they share.
- The paper’s prior art and alternatives are only gestured at through the same meeting description, without a real comparison to existing processes or other ways to address implementer overload.
- The paper is entirely silent on why the standard is the necessary vehicle, how the proposed direction would coordinate with existing rules, and why a non-standard or library-level approach would not work.
