Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin justification for its own standardization, resting primarily on an asserted desire to improve the organization of expression grammar. The strongest material concerns the proposed restructuring itself, while the argument thins out almost immediately when it comes to who would benefit and whether the change belongs in the standard at all.

- The paper at least gestures toward a concrete structural change by proposing new non-terminals and placing their definitions in the relevant subclauses.
- The rationale for why the current organization is a problem is asserted rather than demonstrated, with only a passing characterization of how the grammar is presently presented.
- The paper gives no account of who is affected, what alternatives were considered, or why the standard is the right vehicle for the change.
- Most conspicuously, it offers no implementation experience, no discussion of library solutions, and no coordination considerations to ground the proposal.
