Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers real support on a few narrow points, chiefly that a library-only implementation would impose avoidable overhead and that a working implementation exists, but much of the surrounding case is asserted rather than shown. The thinnest part is the lack of any identified audience or user impact, and the paper does not substantiate its claims about prior art, the need for standardization specifically, or interoperability with existing facilities.

- The strongest support is the demonstrated implementation experience, which shows the proposed facility can be realized in practice.
- The paper also reasonably establishes why a pure library composition would not suffice, given the extra function-call overhead it describes.
- The claims about prior art and the necessity of standardization are made but not backed with enough detail about how existing approaches fall short.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established description of who is affected by the problem or why that population needs the feature.
