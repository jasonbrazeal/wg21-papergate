Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin account of why its proposed work belongs in the C++ standard, with nearly all of the necessary supporting argument either absent or resting on unelaborated references to other documents. The strongest material concerns the general motivation and the existence of prior directions, but even that is asserted rather than developed, while the sections on affected users, the role of the standard, interoperability, library feasibility, and implementation experience are effectively silent.

- The clearest, though still undeveloped, support is the paper’s appeal to long-standing community interest in networking and the current focus on safety.
- Its treatment of prior art gestures toward Profiles and a referenced long-term vision document, but does not explain how those relate to this proposal’s specific standardization needs.
- The case for why a standard is needed, what would be coordinated with it, why a library cannot suffice, and what implementation experience exists is missing entirely.
