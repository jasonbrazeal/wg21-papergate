Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper gives a workable account of the problem and some of the surrounding landscape, but it leaves the central burden largely unaddressed: it does not show why this convention belongs in the standard, nor does it dispel the possibility that a library could carry the weight. The strongest material concerns the difficulty of the design space and the existence of partial results, while the case for institutional action remains mostly asserted.

- The paper convincingly establishes that compound results are a genuine, recurring shape in systems programming and that existing sender-based constructions make the programmer’s choice unnecessarily hard.
- Prior art is handled well enough to show that the problem is known and that the adapter under discussion solves the local dispatch issue.
- The discussion of affected users is suggestive but thin: the repeated echo-server examples gesture at cost without establishing who is actually blocked or at what scale.
- The most glaring omission is that no argument ties the described difficulty to standardization rather than to a missing library convention, and the proposal offers no clear account of what the standard itself needs to specify.
