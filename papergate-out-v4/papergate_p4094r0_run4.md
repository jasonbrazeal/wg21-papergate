Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers meaningful support in a few areas, particularly in showing that the question mattered and that alternatives were genuinely considered, but much of the case rests on claims rather than demonstrated evidence. The thinnest parts are the arguments that a library solution cannot suffice and that the affected communities actually require the proposed direction.

- The paper is strongest when documenting prior art and the existence of serious alternatives, including the acknowledged precedent of a hybrid `task` type already adopted by the committee.
- The discussion of why the issue matters is grounded in concrete fragmentation concerns and the real costs of bridging incompatible executor models.
- The claim that a library will not do is asserted through a general complexity argument but not shown against realistic usage or existing practice.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: production use and bridge implementations are mentioned, but the paper does not establish what that experience demonstrates about the need for standardization.
