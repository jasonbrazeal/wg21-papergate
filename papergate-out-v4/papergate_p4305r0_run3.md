Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers useful criticism of a related proposal and at least sketches a few reasons the interaction must be resolved before standardization, but it does not build a full case for standardizing its own direction. The thinnest parts concern who is concretely affected and whether there is any implementation experience, while several arguments about ABI, interoperability, and library sufficiency remain asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the paper’s identification of concrete design problems in P2964R5 that would need to be addressed consistently for user-defined element types.
- The paper also credibly positions itself against an existing proposal and explains why halting or coordinating progress may be part of the alternative.
- The case is weaker where it claims standardization is necessary because breaking ABI or making existing code ill-formed is unacceptable, since that concern is stated without sufficient proof of a standard-level problem.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of evidence about who is affected or how the proposed direction has worked in practice.
