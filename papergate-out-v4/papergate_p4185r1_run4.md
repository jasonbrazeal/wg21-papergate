Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper gives a reasonably solid account of why the proposed three-way quantity abstraction is needed and that it has been implemented and tested in real code, but it is much thinner when it comes to showing that this work belongs in the standard rather than in a library, or that the affected community and coordination case are as broad as the paper implies. The strongest material concerns motivation, prior art, and implementation experience; the weakest concerns the standard-specific justification.

- The paper firmly establishes the problem and the practical gaps in the current two-abstraction model through concrete implementation experience.
- It also establishes meaningful prior art and independent convergence, including Sequoia and Au, which supports the claim that the design is not arbitrary.
- The argument for who is affected rests mostly on assertions about committee feedback and user requests rather than demonstrated breadth of need.
- The most glaring omission is a convincing explanation of why existing or prospective libraries cannot deliver these capabilities without standardization.
