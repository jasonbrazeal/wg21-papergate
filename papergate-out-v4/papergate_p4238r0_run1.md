Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: it argues convincingly that the procedural problem matters and that alternatives exist, but it does little to show why a standard is the right remedy or why non-standard mechanisms would fail. The thinnest areas are those that connect the procedural critique to a concrete, necessary standardization action, leaving the reader with a well-documented grievance and an under-specified solution.

- The strongest support is the established account of why the issue matters, grounded in the consequences of removing oversight from consensus decisions.
- The paper also establishes prior art and alternatives by citing IETF practice, the withdrawal of trivial relocatability, and the recorded procedural history.
- The section on who is affected rests mostly on claims rather than demonstrated impact, weakening the case that a broad population is harmed.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established argument for why a standard is needed or why a library or other non-standard remedy would not suffice.
