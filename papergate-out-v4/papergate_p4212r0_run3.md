Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper’s case for standardization rests almost entirely on general claims about underspecification and portability, with little concrete support for who is affected, what implementation experience exists, or why only a standard can close the gap. Its strongest, most concrete support is the acknowledgment of prior C standardization and an earlier C++ effort, but even those connections are used more as context than as a demonstrated need for the proposed work in C++.

- The paper establishes that floating-point conversion and related facilities are underspecified and that related standardization efforts exist, which at least frames the topic as a real standards concern.
- The paper asserts rather than demonstrates that the C model is unsuitable for C++, and it does not show how the proposed specification would resolve the claimed semantic, constexpr, template, or library mismatches.
- The paper offers no evidence about who is affected, how widespread the portability problems are, or what user or vendor experience motivates the work.
- The paper gives no implementation experience, and it does not establish that a library solution would be inadequate or that standardization is the necessary next step.
