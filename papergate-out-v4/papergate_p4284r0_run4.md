Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with its clearest contribution being an explanation of why the work exists as a companion to prior catalogue annexes. Beyond that context, the case is largely unbuilt: it does not identify an affected audience, show why the standard is the right vehicle, address coordination or implementation concerns, or explain why a library solution would not suffice. The thinnest areas are not merely underdeveloped but entirely silent, leaving the proposal’s rationale resting on a claimed maintenance need rather than demonstrated necessity.

- The strongest support is the paper’s framing as a companion to P3596R3, explaining that its catalogues were immediately out of date due to concurrent papers landing at the same meeting.
- The next most substantive element is the mention of specific follow-up discussion and one related committee motion, though this is presented only as claimed prior art and alternatives rather than demonstrated.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the proposed changes, leaving the practical stakes unnamed.
- Most glaringly, it offers no implementation experience, no argument for why the standard rather than a library is needed, and no discussion of coordination or interoperability.
