Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers real evidence for the existence and significance of the compound-result problem, and it shows working implementation experience behind the coroutine-native alternative. That support thins considerably when the paper reaches the questions that matter most for standardization: why this needs to be in the standard, why a library cannot carry the design, and who beyond the authors is actually affected.

- The strongest support is the combination of compilable examples and the Voutilainen side-by-side comparison, which credibly establishes that the trade-off is demonstrable rather than speculative.
- The paper also establishes the prior-art landscape clearly, positioning the coroutine-native approach as complementary to senders rather than as a replacement.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why the standard should adopt this facility, rather than the facility remaining a library or a pattern.
