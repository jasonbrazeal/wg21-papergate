Verdict: None (0/14)

The paper offers only a single, underdeveloped claim about why profiles matter, and it leaves essentially every other question about affected users, prior approaches, the role of the standard, interoperability, library suitability, and implementation experience unaddressed, so the case for standardization is almost entirely absent.

- The strongest support is a general statement that profiles provide incremental safety guarantees beyond base-language changes, but even that is asserted rather than substantiated.
- The paper gives no account of who would be affected by standardizing profiles or what practical problem they would solve.
- It does not discuss prior art or alternatives, leaving no basis for understanding why this particular direction deserves committee attention.
- Most glaringly, it never explains why a standard is needed, why a library cannot suffice, or whether any implementation experience exists to ground the proposal.
