Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some grounding for revisiting the alignment wording, mainly by pointing to existing Core issues and divergent compiler behavior, but it does not develop that into a coherent case for the specific standardization changes it wants. The support is thinnest around why the standard is the right venue, what implementation experience exists, and why a library solution cannot address the problem.

- The clearest support is that the paper identifies live compiler divergence and open Core issues in the relevant alignment clauses.
- The paper gestures at affected users and prior art through the cited issues and implementer differences, but leaves those connections largely implicit.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any demonstrated implementation experience with the proposed wording changes.
- The paper also does not explain why the standard must be changed rather than addressing the issues through library facilities or existing core-issue resolution mechanisms.
