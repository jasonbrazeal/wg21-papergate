Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with its strongest grounding in the existence of a reference implementation and clear engagement with prior art. The case becomes much thinner when it turns to motivating the need for standardization itself, since the affected audience is never identified and the arguments about why a library solution is insufficient rely on claims rather than demonstrated limitations.

- The paper establishes that a reference implementation exists and that the underlying algorithms are straightforward, with hardware support commonly available.
- The paper adequately engages with prior art, including P0543R3 and related proposals, situating its approach within ongoing work.
- The paper claims, without substantiation, that the feature must be in the standard because portable third-party implementation is difficult, but it does not demonstrate that library-level solutions have actually failed or are inadequate in practice.
