Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a genuine and well-argued case on the importance of the problem and the suitability of a bright-line rule, but its support for standardization is uneven: the strongest passages concern why the committee should care and what alternatives exist, while the thinnest concern whether the rule itself would work across the affected processes and whether a standards change is actually the right vehicle.

- The paper is most persuasive when it explains the cost of unpredictable revision polling and shows that existing SD-4 and peer-committee practice already support an objective deadline.
- The discussion of affected parties rests heavily on personal experience and unquantified implementer sentiment, so the breadth of the claimed burden is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The argument that a standard is needed, rather than a policy or process change, is largely a statement of intent about changing author incentives, not evidence that standardization would achieve that effect.
- The paper offers no implementation experience for the proposed rule and does not establish why a library-level or process-level solution would be insufficient.
