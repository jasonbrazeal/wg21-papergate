Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives a clear account of why deferring Contracts would matter, but it offers much thinner support for the practical, comparative, and coordination arguments that would justify taking that step through a standardization process. The strongest material is the motivation; the weakest is the absence of any case against library or non-standard solutions.

- The paper establishes that the timing and readiness of Contracts are consequential because the design touches semantics, safety, control flow, and teachability across a broad user base.
- It gestures toward relevant prior art and continuing disagreement, but does not substantiate the claimed volume of NB comments or the relevance of the cited alternative proposals.
- Its discussion of coordination, interoperability, and implementation experience remains speculative, framed mainly as future possibility rather than demonstrated need or practice.
- It offers no reasoning for why the concerns raised could not be addressed outside the standard through libraries, TSes, or continued implementation experimentation.
