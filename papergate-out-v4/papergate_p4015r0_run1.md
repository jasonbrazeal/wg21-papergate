Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper establishes a genuine need for enforceable contract conditions and motivates the problem clearly, but its case remains mostly aspirational beyond that core concern. The supporting argument is thinnest where standardization typically demands the most: evidence about who is affected, implementability, and why the work cannot be done in a library.

- The strongest element is the demonstrated need for function authors to specify that contract conditions will be enforced, grounded in avoidable vulnerability scenarios.
- The appeal to C++’s character as a statement-driven language gestures at a standardization rationale, but is asserted rather than shown to require standard action.
- The paper offers no implementation experience, so there is no assurance the proposed direction can be realized in practice.
- Most glaringly, the paper never shows who is affected or why a library solution cannot meet the stated need.
