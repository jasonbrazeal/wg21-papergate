Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, self-referential justification for removing a member function from the C++26 design, and it leaves the broader question of why the remaining feature should be standardized almost entirely unaddressed. The strongest support concerns the existence of unresolved design questions, but the document does not connect those questions to a positive case for the proposal’s place in the standard.

- The paper does establish, by its own account, that a specific member function attracted unresolved design discussion during a recent committee telecon, which is the clearest reason given for acting now.
- The paper gestures at an alternative formulation for checking element fit, but this is treated as a reason for further discussion rather than as a worked comparison.
- The paper offers no evidence about who would be affected by the proposed removal or by the feature’s eventual standardization.
- Most notably, the paper never explains why the proposed behavior belongs in the standard rather than in a library, nor does it demonstrate implementation experience or coordination with related facilities.
