Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers substantive support for its relevance, domain tension, affected users, prior art, and implementation experience, but the case for standardization itself is largely absent. The thinnest parts are exactly where a proposal must be strongest: what the standard should say, why only the standard can say it, and how the resulting specification would interoperate.

- The strongest support is the documented production use and the maturity of the reference implementation behind the model.
- The paper also establishes clearly that coroutine-native I/O and `std::execution` address different domains rather than one subsuming the other.
- The most glaring omission is any argument for why this needs to be in the C++ standard at all.
