Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s strongest support is its motivation: it clearly identifies a gap between the standard’s extensive use of structural-type requirements and the absence of any user-facing query. Beyond that, however, the case rests largely on assertions and references to prior or concurrent work rather than demonstrated need, implementation validation, or concrete interoperability analysis. The thinnest areas are those concerning who specifically is affected and whether the proposed facility could credibly be supplied outside the standard.

- The paper establishes that structural types are already a pervasive standard-library and language concept that users cannot currently query.
- The paper gestures at prior art and a proof-of-concept implementation, but does not substantiate the breadth, portability, or maturity of either.
- The paper asserts that library mandates imply implementers already possess this capability, but offers no evidence about how it is exposed, coordinated, or reused.
- The paper does not identify any affected user population, making the practical demand for standardization mostly abstract.
