Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin rationale for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on assertions about confusion and past failures rather than on demonstrated need, affected users, or viable alternatives. Its strongest material is a general claim that profiles lack agreed documentation and that earlier efforts have not succeeded, but even that is not backed by specifics. The case for standardization is thinnest where it matters most: no explanation of why the standard is the necessary venue, no coordination or interoperability analysis, no implementation experience, and no argument that a library cannot address the problem.

- The paper at least gestures toward prior work by naming several recent profiles-related proposals and noting that none have succeeded.
- It claims, without establishing, that insufficient agreed-on documentation is blocking meaningful contributions to profiles.
- It does not identify who is affected by the problem or what practical harm the lack of standardization causes.
- It offers no evidence for why the standard, rather than a library or another approach, is needed to make progress.
