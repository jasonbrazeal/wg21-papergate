Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper builds its case mainly around procedural failure and the need for external scrutiny, and that part is documented convincingly; beyond the process argument, however, it offers little that would independently justify taking this design into the standard now. The thinnest areas are the ones a standardization proposal would normally need most: what the standard itself is for, how the feature coordinates with the rest of the language, why a library cannot serve, and whether anyone has real implementation experience.

- The strongest support is for prior art and alternatives, where the paper shows that established process norms, a rejected Technical Specification route, and an accelerated merge path make the present ballot check a reasonable alternative to deferral.
- The paper also establishes why the issue matters, by pointing to an unrecorded reconciliation of sustained technical opposition and to the National Body ballot as the only audience able to scrutinize those determinations.
- The weakest established element is who is affected, where the paper can cite the forwarding polls but does not actually show how the affected population extends beyond the committee process itself.
- The most glaring omissions are the absence of any case for why this belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere, why a library would not do, how the feature coordinates with existing and future language facilities, and whether there is implementation experience sufficient to standardize it.
