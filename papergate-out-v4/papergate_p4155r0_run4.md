Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some grounding in prior discussions and rejected alternatives, but it does not build a positive case for its own standardization, and much of its argument rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need or implementability. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected users, any implementation experience, and a convincing explanation of why the standard—or this particular change—is required.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s recognition of prior work, including P3858 and P3937, and its framing as a response to those existing discussions.
- The paper claims the problem matters because of perceived flaws in P3937, but it does not establish who concretely suffers from those flaws or what practical harm follows.
- The argument for changing the standard rather than pursuing a library solution is asserted mainly through a disputed reading of simplicity and constraints, not demonstrated.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no implementation experience, no evidence of affected users, and no clear account of why prior committee decisions should be revisited.
