Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers solid support in the areas that argue outward—why the gaps matter, who is affected, what prior work exists, and what implementation experience suggests—but its case becomes noticeably thinner when it turns inward to why the standard itself must change and what the coordination cost would be. The strongest evidence is empirical and comparative, while the claims about standardization being the only path are asserted more than demonstrated.

- The paper’s strongest support comes from production use and independent, multi-year, multi-group recognition of the same structural gaps.
- It also grounds its analysis in concrete prior art, including a working coroutine-native I/O implementation with tests and benchmarks.
- The case for standardization specifically is the thinnest part: the paper argues the costs and risks of the sender model but does not establish that these are problems only the standard can solve or that interoperability consequences are understood.
