Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization case, and much of that support rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need. Its strongest ground is in situating the response within prior discussions and rejected alternatives, but it does not establish who is affected, how the feature would interoperate, or why standardization is the necessary path.

- The paper is most convincing when it connects its objections to previously rejected approaches and the committee’s existing consideration of trivial relocation semantics.
- Its argument for why the issue matters is asserted through critique of another paper, but the actual stakes for users or implementers are not made concrete.
- The absence of any discussion of affected parties leaves the proposal without a clearly identified constituency or motivating problem.
- The lack of coordination and interoperability analysis is a glaring omission for a proposal concerned with type erasure and cross-component behavior.
