Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly asserted case for standardization: the rationale rests on a few claims about inconsistency and utility, but those claims are not developed into a concrete problem statement, affected audience, or demonstrated need for a standard facility. The support is thinnest around the questions of who is actually affected, why a library solution would be insufficient, and whether there is any implementation experience to validate the design.

- The strongest support appears in the paper’s claim that the existing API is inconsistent and that providing the information directly would avoid further proliferation, although even this is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper gestures toward prior art and coordination concerns by citing the interaction between independent adopted papers and a naming/ADL issue raised by an implementer, but it does not substantiate those as reasons for standardization specifically.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the inconsistency or the lack of the proposed facility, leaving the actual user need unclear.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no evidence that implementation experience, a standard-level intervention, or the infeasibility of a library solution has been shown, which are essential for justifying standardization.
