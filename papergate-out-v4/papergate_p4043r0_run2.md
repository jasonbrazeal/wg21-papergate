Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper’s strongest support is its high-level motivation: it credibly establishes that Contracts interact with deep and unsettled semantic questions, and that the resulting design instability is a real reason to consider deferral. Beyond that, however, the case is mostly asserted rather than demonstrated. The discussion of affected users, prior design alternatives, why a standard is the right vehicle, and why implementation outside the standard would not suffice remains thin, while coordination, interoperability, and implementation experience are essentially unaddressed.

- The paper establishes a coherent rationale for caution by pointing to unresolved semantic, safety, and teachability concerns around Contracts.
- Its treatment of affected parties and prior art gestures at relevant NB comments and the Concepts precedent, but does not substantiate their significance or lessons.
- The argument that a standard is needed rests largely on the existence of opposing papers rather than an independent showing.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of coordination, interoperability, or implementation experience.
