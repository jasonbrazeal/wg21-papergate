Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s support is concentrated in a few technical arguments and artifacts: it convincingly explains why the status quo is awkward, why erasing static extents would be a poor trade, and that the proposed mechanism can be implemented. The case is much thinner on the broader standardization questions, especially who is affected, how the feature interacts with the surrounding ecosystem, and why users cannot reasonably solve the problem outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the implementation evidence, which demonstrates that the tuple-interface approach works with current library implementations.
- The paper also clearly establishes the design problem and why the obvious alternative of demoting static extents is unattractive.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any account of who is affected by the missing feature or what practical codebases would benefit.
- Equally unaddressed are coordination and interoperability concerns, leaving the relationship to existing practice and adjacent proposals unspecified.
