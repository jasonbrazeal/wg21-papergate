Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper builds a substantial case for standardization across most of the necessary dimensions, with especially thorough backing for why existing workarounds fail and why compiler-level support is warranted. The support is thinnest where the paper turns to coordination and interoperability: it asserts broad importance and portability harm but does not yet substantiate those claims with concrete evidence or external confirmation.

- The strongest support is the demonstration that library-only and source-generation alternatives impose severe correctness, portability, and compiler-resource costs that standardization could address.
- The paper also convincingly establishes the prevalence of the need and the existence of real implementation experience across major toolchains.
- The most glaring omission is the coordination and interoperability case, where the paper claims broad cross-tool and cross-industry impact without providing the concrete examples or validated interactions needed to establish that concern.
