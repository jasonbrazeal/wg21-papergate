Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a clear and well-supported case that the current consensus process lacks sufficient accountability and has produced contested outcomes, but it does not establish why the international standard is the necessary remedy or how the proposed mechanism would interoperate with existing processes. The strongest material concerns the procedural stakes and the credibility of consensus claims, while the thinnest parts concern the actual need for a standards-level change and evidence that such a change is implementable.

- The paper convincingly establishes that a real problem exists: sustained opposition has been overridden without a documented rationale, and removing National Body ballot oversight weakens an existing accountability check.
- It gives credible prior art and alternatives by citing WG21’s own consensus rules, the IETF’s analogous guidance, and the successful removal of trivial relocation after National Body objections.
- It claims but does not establish that the National Body ballot is the only coordination mechanism capable of removing a working-group-adopted feature, leaving the interoperability case speculative.
- It offers no implementation experience and does not establish why the standard is the right vessel rather than some other governance or process change inside or outside ISO.
