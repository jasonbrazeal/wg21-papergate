Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers some concrete evidence of implementation experience, but its central justifications are largely asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the case for standardization resting on repeated claims about attributes and reflection rather than worked examples or documented need. The thinnest support appears where the paper argues that existing mechanisms cannot address the problem and that committee-level standardization is the right remedy.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, including a compiler explorer link and experimental details about expression comparison under profiling.
- The prior art and alternatives section is credited as established, mainly by aligning the approach with the existing `info`-based reflection design and noting prior committee feedback.
- The paper repeatedly claims that attributes are widely used and that no current way exists to appertain many attributes, but it does not substantiate who is concretely blocked or why the standard must be the solution.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of established support for why a library solution will not do and why committee time should be spent on this uniform vehicle rather than narrower fixes.
