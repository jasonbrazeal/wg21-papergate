Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, circumstantial case for its own standardization, relying on the assertion that it corrects an oversight in a previously adopted proposal and that implementations of the earlier work already exist. Its support is thinnest in showing who is affected, why a library solution would not suffice, and what the standardization-specific need actually is, since those points are not addressed at all.

- The strongest support is the paper’s claim that this change extends an already-adopted design from P2248R8 to a family of algorithms that was accidentally omitted, so the intended direction has prior committee endorsement.
- The paper also gestures at implementation experience by noting that implementations are already shipping the earlier P2248R8 behavior, though it does not show experience with the proposed change itself.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the actual user base and practical impact unspecified.
- Equally unaddressed is why the work belongs in the standard rather than remaining an implementation or library concern, leaving the standardization rationale largely assumed rather than argued.
