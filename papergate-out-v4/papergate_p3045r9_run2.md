Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper offers substantial support for most of the case for standardization, with solid evidence of user populations, prior art, coordination needs, and implementation experience. The clearest weakness is the argument for why an external library cannot suffice, which leans on indirect claims about MISRA compliance and uniqueness rather than demonstrating that the standardization process is necessary to achieve those benefits.

- The paper establishes who is affected with unusually concrete audience estimates and real user requests, grounding the proposal in identifiable communities rather than abstract need.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is thorough, showing deliberate design choices and rejected options that suggest the proposal has considered existing practice carefully.
- The thinnest support is around why a library will not do, where the paper claims uniqueness and policy pressures but does not directly show that a non-standard library fails to meet those needs in practice.
