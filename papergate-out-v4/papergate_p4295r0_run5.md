Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, concentrating on the convenience of single-ended access and the existence of a partial implementation, while leaving most of the justification undeveloped. The support is especially weak where the proposal needs to show that a standard facility is necessary and that existing or third-party solutions cannot fill the role.

- The strongest support is the partial implementation, which at least demonstrates that the proposed interface has been explored in code.
- The paper claims relevance for bounded concurrent queues and single-ended access, but does not establish why that convenience matters for standardization.
- The paper gestures toward prior work, but does not connect that history to a clear need for a standard wording change.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for who is affected, why the standard should address it, or why a library cannot suffice.
