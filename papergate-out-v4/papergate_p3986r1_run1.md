Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow, indirect basis for its own standardization, mostly by gesturing at adjacent decisions and prior papers rather than by making its own affirmative case. The support is thinnest where a proposal most needs to be concrete: necessity for the standard, interoperability, implementability outside the standard, and evidence from practice are all left unestablished.

- The clearest support comes from references to prior work and a committee decision, though even these are asserted rather than demonstrated within the paper itself.
- The paper’s account of the underlying problem and its affected audience is plausible but remains a set of claims without enough supporting argument to carry the standardization question.
- The most glaring omission is the total absence of a case for why this must be addressed in the C++ standard rather than through a library or existing mechanisms.
