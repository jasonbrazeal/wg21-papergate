Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers genuine context for why networking standardization has stalled and does credible work surveying the surrounding landscape, but its affirmative case for standardizing this particular design is thin in exactly the places that matter most: scope of impact, necessity of a standard, interoperability, and implementation evidence.

- The strongest support is the historical framing, which establishes that two decades of committee effort have not produced standard networking and that prior work lost coherence through accumulated compromise.
- The paper reasonably establishes that coroutine-native I/O and `std::execution` can be viewed as complementary approaches with different strengths.
- The claims about broad industry impact, concrete adoption, and the existence of shipping implementations remain assertions rather than demonstrated facts.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why existing libraries cannot serve the need, which leaves the central question of standardization unanswered.
