Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some grounding for the general relevance of contract enforcement, but its case for standardization remains largely asserted rather than demonstrated, with the weakest area being any explanation of why a library approach cannot meet the stated needs.

- The paper establishes that making and enforcing contract assertions declaratively is a matter of real interest and concern within the committee.
- The paper claims friction from specific compiler vendors and broader community experience with experimental implementations, but does not substantiate those claims with evidence or documentation.
- The paper asserts that a standardized mechanism is needed for recording and enforcing contract violations, without showing why existing or library-based mechanisms are insufficient.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of a rationale for why a library will not do, leaving the central question of whether standardization is necessary essentially unaddressed.
