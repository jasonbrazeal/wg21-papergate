Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers solid support in a few foundational areas, particularly prior art and feasibility, but the case for standardization is uneven overall. The thinnest support appears around demonstrating who is affected, why this must be in the standard, and how it would coordinate with existing practice.

- The paper convincingly situates the problem within prior standardization discussions and shows a working proof of concept across major compilers.
- The treatment of existing libraries and the proposed compatibility layer gives some basis for coordination, though the actual interoperability claims remain more asserted than demonstrated.
- The quantitative evidence for the affected user base is presented as raw search counts without enough context to establish the scope or representativeness of the problem.
- The most glaring omission is a sustained argument for why a standard library facility is the only viable path, since the paper repeatedly asserts the necessity of standardization without establishing it.
