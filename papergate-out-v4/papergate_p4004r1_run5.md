Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a workable motivation for reconsidering the current specification and shows that its preferred direction matches long-standing implementer practice, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case more asserted than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why a library cannot help and the lack of concrete implementation experience beyond one vendor.

- The proposal’s strongest support is its established account of existing implementation divergence and the need to align the standard with widespread practice.
- The discussion of alternatives is adequately grounded in the proposal to revert most of CWG 1395 and retain only the tie-breaker.
- The affected audience is only claimed rather than shown, since the paper points to bug reports and real-world breakage without documenting their scope or frequency.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never addresses why a library-level solution is unsuitable, leaving that requirement entirely unestablished.
