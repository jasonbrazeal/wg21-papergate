Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a slim rationale for its own standardization, with most of its case resting on stylistic consistency and redundancy reduction rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected audience, any reason the standard rather than another mechanism is required, and any exploration of implementation or interoperability.

- The strongest support is the paper’s stated motivation that repeating a long or templated type name in a default argument is redundant and inconsistent with direct-list-initialization elsewhere.
- The paper gestures at an overload-based alternative but does not establish that existing alternatives are inadequate enough to justify a language change.
- The paper gives no account of who would use this feature or how widely the repetition problem occurs in practice.
- The proposal does not establish why the standard is the necessary venue, why a library solution will not do, or how the feature would interact with existing rules and implementations.
