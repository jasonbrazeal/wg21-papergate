Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gestures toward a need for attribute introspection and hints at implementation work, but it never substantiates those gestures with concrete audiences, alternatives, or a clear argument for why standardization—rather than a library or continued design work—is required. The thinnest areas are the absence of any affected-user analysis and the lack of a case against library-only solutions.

- The strongest support is the mention of an experimental implementation available on Compiler Explorer, which at least suggests the idea has been tried in practice.
- The paper makes repeated claims about uniformity and future reflection applications, but these remain aspirational and are not tied to demonstrated use or urgency.
- It does not identify who is affected by the missing capability, leaving the proposal without a clear constituency or motivating problem.
- Most glaringly, it never explains why a library or existing extension mechanism cannot address the need, which is a central part of justifying a standard language feature.
