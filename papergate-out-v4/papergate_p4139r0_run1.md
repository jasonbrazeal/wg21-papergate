Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for standardization, resting mainly on brief claims about prior naming discussions and a cursory acknowledgment of unusual semantics. Most of the necessary justification—who would be affected, why the standard is the right venue, what alternatives exist beyond rejected names, whether a library suffices, and whether anyone has implemented it—is simply absent. The strongest support concerns the recognition that the proposed facility would be distinctive in the standard library, but even that stops short of explaining why that distinctiveness demands standardization.

- The paper’s strongest moment is its observation that this would be the first standard-library `get()` that can fail, acknowledging at least one point of novelty.
- It gestures at prior art by noting that earlier candidate names failed to gain enthusiasm, though it does not turn that into a real analysis of alternatives.
- The discussion of naming reduces the entire design question to shortness, leaving the “why it matters” argument more asserted than argued.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes who is affected, why a library would not do, or that there is any implementation experience behind the proposal.
