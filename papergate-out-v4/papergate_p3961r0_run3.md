Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides a narrow but real evidentiary basis for its proposal, chiefly by showing that a specific expression currently fails to compile and that an implementation exists. Beyond that, the argument for standardization is thin: it does not identify who is affected, explain why a standard facility is needed, or address coordination with related features. The discussion of alternatives and the insufficiency of a library solution is presented more as an open question than as a demonstrated conclusion.

- The most concrete support is the implementation experience, with a public implementation referenced for the proposed design.
- The motivation is anchored by a clear example of code that does not compile without the proposal.
- The prior-art discussion gestures toward a possible rationale but stops short of establishing why the proposed scope is the right one.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any account of who would use this feature and why the standard, rather than a library, is the necessary vehicle.
