Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers some useful grounding for its central technical concern, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case largely unargued, particularly around who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and how the proposal coordinates with existing practice. The strongest material is rhetorical and experiential: it identifies a real semantic ambiguity and reports a working implementation, but that does not by itself carry the burden of demonstrating a need for language or library standardization.

- The paper most convincingly identifies the ambiguity in the motivating example and shows that a resolution is implementable in practice.
- It situates the problem against prior work and existing wrappers such as `constant_wrapper` and `constant_arg`, giving the discussion some continuity with earlier proposals.
- The case for standardizing this behavior is asserted mainly through claims about consistency and teachability, without establishing a shared specification need or coordination path.
- The paper does not show who is concretely affected or why a library-only approach would fail to address the problem.
