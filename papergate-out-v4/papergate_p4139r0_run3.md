Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin opening toward its own standardization case, resting on asserted distinctions in the existing library interface while leaving most of the burden of justification unaddressed. The discussion is concentrated almost entirely on naming, and the broader questions of need, affected users, interoperability, and implementability receive no developed support.

- The strongest support is the observation that the proposed operation would be unlike existing `get()` functions in ways that at least gesture toward a design question worth resolving in the committee.
- The treatment of prior alternatives is limited to a passing claim that one other name was rejected, without enough context to assess whether that rejection actually motivates standardization.
- The paper does not identify who would use the facility or what problem in existing practice it would solve for them.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library solution cannot satisfy the need, which leaves the central question of standardization untouched.
