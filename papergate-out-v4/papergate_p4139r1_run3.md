Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin record of discussion around a narrow naming and API-shape question, rather than a case for standardization in its own right. The strongest material concerns why the chosen facility is unusual or contested, but even that is presented as a claim rather than demonstrated, and most of the usual evidentiary dimensions are missing entirely.

- The paper at least identifies the tension around introducing a potentially failing `get()` with a runtime-variable key.
- Its discussion of alternatives rests on prior committee reaction and weak consensus, but offers little direct evidence or analysis to support those alternatives being inadequate.
- The paper gives no account of affected users, implementation experience, or why the facility must be standardized rather than provided by a library.
- It is silent on coordination with related standards or existing practice, leaving the standardization need largely unexamined.
