Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers genuine support for the existence of a real usability problem and for following an established precedent, but its case becomes much thinner when it moves to questions of affected users, standardization authority, and practical experience. The weakest part of the argument is the absence of any discussion of why a library would not satisfy the need, which leaves a central justification for standardization unaddressed. Overall, the evidence is strongest where it describes friction and prior art, and most missing where it must explain why a standard feature, rather than a shared utility, is required.

- The paper’s clearest contribution is establishing that retrieving a stored pointer from optional forms is currently awkward and has an existing precedent in Boost.Optional.
- The discussion of interoperability is only gestured at through a list of pointer-wrapping types and a mention of inplace_vector, without a developed account of how this feature would coordinate with existing practice.
- The affected-user claim relies on a general statement about C and legacy APIs rather than showing that these interfaces create a standardization need.
- The paper does not address why a library would be insufficient, leaving the rationale for standardization itself largely unsupported.
