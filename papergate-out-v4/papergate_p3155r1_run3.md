Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers substantial support for standardizing its proposed policy by showing the Lakos Rule’s continued practical importance, its dominance in the existing standard library, and the compatibility and implementation consequences of leaving the question unsettled. The support is thinnest where it needs to demonstrate that the problem cannot be addressed outside the standard and that the proposed policy direction has meaningful implementation experience behind it.

- The paper is strongest in showing why the Lakos Rule still matters and that abandoning it would create avoidable harms, especially for users who need recoverable precondition failures.
- It also persuasively establishes that the committee has already wrestled with this design question and that the major implementations have diverged from the proposed policy, making coordination a real concern.
- The case for why this must be settled in the standard rather than through library-level techniques or implementation practices is asserted more than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established implementation experience for the proposed policy, since the quantitative study and implementation anecdotes are only claimed as support, not confirmed as evidence.
