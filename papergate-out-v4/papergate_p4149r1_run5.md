Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly declarative case for standardization: it asserts a definitional goal and some consistency reasoning, but leaves most of the necessary justification unstated. The support is thinnest around the absence of affected users, the need for a standard change rather than another mechanism, and any evidence from practice or implementation.

- The clearest support is the paper’s stated aim to define “immediate context,” which at least identifies the topic it wants to standardize.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives is present but largely asserted through references and consistency claims rather than developed into a comparison.
- The paper gives no account of who is affected by the lack of the proposed definition or why that impact rises to the level of a standard.
- Most notably, it does not establish why standardization is necessary at all, nor why a library or other non-standard approach would be insufficient.
