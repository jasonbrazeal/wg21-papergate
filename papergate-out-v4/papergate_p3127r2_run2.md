Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some grounding for why graph representations matter and gestures toward familiar terminology and prior practice, but it leaves the case for standardization largely undeveloped. The thinnest areas are exactly those a committee most needs: who is affected, why a library would not suffice, what belongs in the standard, and what implementation experience shows.

- The paper meaningfully connects graphs to sparse matrices and names common representations, which gives the problem domain a clear foothold.
- Its appeal to CLRS terminology and to operations found in other libraries suggests, but does not demonstrate, that the design aligns with established practice.
- The cited implementation material is fragmentary and explicitly unfinished, so it does not yet amount to experience with the proposed design.
- The paper provides no real account of the affected users, the limits of non-standard libraries, or the coordination and interoperability requirements for a standard facility.
