Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers mostly assertions rather than demonstrated support for its standardization case, with every category resting on claims that are not backed by concrete evidence about affected users, viable alternatives, or implementation experience. The thinnest areas are the absence of any established audience or impact and the lack of demonstrated implementation evidence beyond a single unresolved mention of Croydon.

- The paper’s strongest support is its repeated claim that shipping the current design would foreclose automatic frame allocator propagation and environment-based allocation, though even this remains asserted rather than proven.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives gestures toward related proposals and the Croydon meeting, but does not establish how those alternatives were evaluated or why they suffice.  
- The case for why only a language change will do rests on the same foreclosure argument without showing that library-level or convention-based approaches are inadequate.  
- The most glaring omission is that no affected group of users or real-world code is identified, so the paper never grounds its urgency in demonstrated need.
