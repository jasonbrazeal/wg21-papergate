Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper provides solid support for situating its question among competing published designs and showing that the bundled architecture claim was never actually adopted by the working group, but it leans heavily on assertions about deployment, implementation experience, and the insufficiency of non-standard approaches without independently establishing those claims. The thinnest support appears where the paper needs to show that the standardization path it requests is necessary rather than merely convenient, and that the named-guarantee form has enough field grounding to carry the architectural argument forward on its own.

- The strongest support lies in the reconstruction of the proposal’s seven-poll history and the demonstration that no prior poll adopted the disputed architecture.
- The paper also convincingly establishes that the layering question sits between two substantive published bodies of work, with P3100R8 making a specific choice that P3081R2’s wording still depends on.
- The most consistent weakness is that nearly every claim about who is affected, why the standard is needed, coordination, and implementation experience rests on the paper’s own characterization or on deployment evidence that is asserted but not independently shown.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established evidence that the named-guarantee form is actually deployed enough or specified precisely enough to justify separating the architecture decision from the wording that currently carries it.
