Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest support comes from its prior-art discussion and the availability of a reference implementation, but much of the rationale rests on repeated assertions about user difficulty rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are coordination with existing practice and interoperability, which are not addressed at all.

- The paper firmly establishes precedent through existing lock algorithms and notes that standard implementations already use the relevant deadlock-avoidance technique.
- A reference implementation is available, giving the proposal some concrete grounding.
- Claims that users are affected and that the standard is the right venue rely on the same unelaborated assertion that hand-rolled timed locking is error-prone and verbose.
- The proposal does not establish coordination or interoperability considerations at all, leaving a notable gap in the standardization case.
