Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a narrow but real foundation for its need, rooted in concrete process risks around in-meeting revisions, unmailed design changes, and publication deadlines, but much of the surrounding case is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support lies in the absence of evidence tying the proposed rule to the standard itself, to implementation experience, or to why non-normative mechanisms cannot suffice.

- The established process gap is clearest when the paper describes how in-meeting revisions can shift the consensus burden before national body experts have seen the changed text.
- The paper credits prior art and alternatives by referencing P2138R4, SD-4’s allowance for followup papers, and the pre-meeting mailing’s review function.
- The paper claims authorship incentives and scheduling benefits for mailed revisions, but it does not establish that a library or other non-standard mechanism would fail to provide the same certainty.
- The most glaring omission is any demonstration of why the standard itself must change: the paper never connects the procedural problem to a requirement only a normative rule can satisfy.
