Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin basis for standardization: its central claim is that a gap in `noexcept` policy exists and that filling it would be better than nothing, but it does not demonstrate who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or that the approach is workable in practice. The support is thinnest around the basic questions of need and feasibility, where the paper asserts rather than shows.

- The strongest support is a plausible assertion that codifying unconditional `noexcept` for wide-contract functions would improve on having no policy.
- The paper gestures at prior discussion and apparent agreement, but offers no actual record of alternatives or precedent that would ground the proposal.
- The most glaring omission is any indication of who is affected, leaving the problem abstract and unconnected to real users or implementers.
- Nearly as serious is the absence of implementation experience or evidence that a library solution cannot address the concern, which leaves the need for standardization unestablished.
