Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, resting on a narrow clarification around constant expression evaluation and a passing reference to a national body issue. Most of the case is asserted rather than demonstrated, and several essential dimensions of the argument are simply absent.

- The clearest support is the paper’s identification of a specific standardese ambiguity about how many times a possibly-constant expression is evaluated, tied to an existing issue.
- The discussion of prior art gestures at relevant language and implementation constraints, though it does not develop an actual comparison or justification.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the proposed clarification or why the standard is the necessary vehicle for addressing it.
- Most glaringly, there is no implementation experience, no coordination or interoperability discussion, and no explanation of why a library solution would not suffice.
