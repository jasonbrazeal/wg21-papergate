Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a partial account of why its proposed extension might be needed, with a clear articulation of the portability problem and some engagement with alternatives, but it leaves several central justifications for standardization essentially unargued. The thinnest parts concern the affected audience, why a library solution is insufficient, and whether the proposed mechanism has actually been tried in practice.

- The strongest support is the explanation that there is currently no portable way for a backend to check the receiver’s stop token.
- The paper also shows meaningful engagement with prior discussion and at least one alternative design, including trade-offs against the proposed extension.
- Less convincingly, the claims about uniformity, API surface, and alignment among user code, frontend, and backend are asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for why the functionality cannot be provided outside the standard or for who specifically is affected.
