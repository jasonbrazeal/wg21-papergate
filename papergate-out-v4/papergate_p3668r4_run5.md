The paper offers a solid rationale in some areas, particularly its treatment of prior art and alternatives, but its support is uneven overall, with several claims about standardization and interoperability asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest part of the case is the complete absence of implementation experience, which leaves the most direct evidence for feasibility unaddressed.

- The strongest support is the discussion of existing alternatives, which engages with rewrite rules, related proposals, and feature-testing choices in a substantive way.
- The paper also clearly establishes why the topic matters by grounding it in canonical definitions and visible boilerplate costs.
- The argument that standardization, rather than libraries or mixins, is necessary rests largely on asserted benefits without sufficient evidence to establish those claims.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience, which the paper does not establish at all.
