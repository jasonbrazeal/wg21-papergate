Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers a thin and largely rhetorical case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on assertions about consistency and repetition rather than on concrete evidence of user impact, implementation experience, or interoperability concerns. The support is thinnest where a proposal most needs substance: demonstrating who is affected and why the standard is the right place for the change.

- The strongest support appears in the framing of the problem as one of redundancy and stylistic inconsistency with direct-initialization elsewhere.
- The discussion of alternatives gestures at trade-offs with overloads, but it does not establish that those alternatives are inadequate in practice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any evidence about who is affected or how widely the proposed feature is needed.
