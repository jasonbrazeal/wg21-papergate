Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper lays a foundation for some parts of its case, particularly in showing that the problem is real, the affected surface is broad, and the competing approaches are now on a collision course. Its support is thinnest where it needs to show that standardization of this specific mechanism is required rather than merely plausible, and where it treats user-space or library-level responses as inadequate without fully establishing that claim.

- The strongest support is the record that two active standardization efforts now answer the same configuration question incompatibly, making the choice unavoidable.
- The paper also credibly establishes prior art and implementation lineage for the named-guarantee form in production, including measured cost and long vendor history.
- The least settled part is the claim that a library cannot carry the needed behavior, since the paper’s own discussion shows replaceable handlers and library-side adoption but does not close off that alternative.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any demonstrated implementation for the paper’s added machinery, leaving the standardizing step without the implementation experience that might anchor it.
