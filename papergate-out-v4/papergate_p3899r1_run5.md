Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers meaningful support in the areas of motivation, consistency with existing practice, and implementation experience, but it leaves the central case for committee action underdeveloped. The thinnest parts are the explanations of why the standard itself needs to change and why a library-level solution cannot address the problem.

- The strongest support is the demonstration that GCC 15 already implements the proposed behavior and that the current wording conflicts with the design rationale of the core language and mathematical functions.
- The paper adequately establishes that the issue matters by showing core-language and library divergence would be unmotivated and that existing constant expressions would break.
- The discussion of who is affected and how implementations coordinate is asserted largely through a table of compiler behavior, without a clear account of the affected user population or convergence process.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established argument for why standardization is necessary or why the problem cannot be solved through a library facility.
