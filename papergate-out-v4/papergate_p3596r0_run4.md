Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper’s case for standardization rests almost entirely on a general assertion that cataloging undefined and IFNDR behavior is useful, with little concrete evidence about affected users, feasible alternatives, or why a non-standard reference would be insufficient. Its strongest concrete support is that the proposed annex wording already exists as a collaborative branch of the working draft. Beyond that, the argument is largely aspirational, leaning on hopes for stable names rather than demonstrated need or coordination value.

- The one clearly established point is that draft annex wording has been developed collaboratively in a working-draft branch, which shows at least some implementation-shaped groundwork.
- The paper claims relevance by citing prior attempts and calls for formalization, but it does not establish what those efforts failed to achieve or how this proposal resolves their limitations.
- The thinnest parts of the case are the unestablished questions of who is actually affected and why a library or external reference would not serve the same purpose.
