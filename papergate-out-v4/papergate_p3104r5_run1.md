Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers meaningful support for its standardization case in the areas that matter most: it establishes why the operations are important, what prior art exists, why the standard is the right venue, and that the proposal has implementation experience. The support is thinnest around who is affected and around coordination, interoperability, and the limits of a library-only approach—these are asserted but not substantiated with sufficient evidence or analysis.

- The strongest support is the demonstrated implementation experience, with compiler output and a reference implementation compatible with all three major compilers.
- The paper also clearly establishes why the operations matter and why standardization is appropriate, grounded in their fundamental nature and hardware availability.
- The case for who is affected rests mainly on a single GitHub code search figure, which is suggestive but not established as representative.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish why a library will not do, since the paper repeats the need for compiler or intrinsic support without showing that a portable library implementation is insufficient.
