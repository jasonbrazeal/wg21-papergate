Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin argument for its own standardization, mostly by gesturing at related proposals and asserting that the design should be possible to implement. The support is thinnest around the basic questions of who needs this feature, why the language rather than some other layer is the right place, and whether anyone has actually tried it.

- The strongest support is the paper’s claim that existing work on pack indexing leaves a gap for packs of templates, though even that is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The discussion of prior art at least names the relevant proposals and notes their relationship to this one, but it does not build a case from their history or outcomes.
- The paper offers no account of who is affected, why a library cannot solve the problem, or how the feature fits into the broader standard.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: the author states the feature has not been implemented and offers only personal confidence that it could be.
