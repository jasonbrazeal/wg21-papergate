Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some groundwork for its standardization effort, but much of the case remains undeveloped, particularly around why the standard itself is the right venue and who concretely benefits. The strongest support lies in the existence of prior work and a collaboratively developed wording branch; the thinnest support is the absence of any demonstrated need for normative standardization rather than another kind of document.

- The paper shows clear awareness of prior attempts and existing categorization work, and it has produced a working draft annex, which counts as real implementation experience.
- The paper gestures toward stable names becoming useful as shared vocabulary, but it does not show that coordination or interoperability needs are pressing enough to justify standardization.
- The paper claims the topic matters in general terms, but it never establishes who is affected by the lack of a standardized annex.
- The paper does not explain why this information must live in the C++ Standard, nor why a non-normative library, database, or separate reference document would not serve the same purpose.
