Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only fragmentary support for its own standardization: the motivation and affected audience are asserted in passing, while the references to prior art gesture toward background discussion rather than establish a considered alternatives analysis. The thinnest parts are those one would most expect a change paper to address directly—why the standard must change, what would happen otherwise, and whether any non-standard solution exists.

- The strongest support is the identification of a compatibility concern, namely that making certain functions `constexpr` would be a breaking change in some cases.
- The discussion of who is affected rests on an unsupported prudential judgment about timing and lateness rather than demonstrating actual impact.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives defers to two cited papers, without showing how their background explains this proposal’s particular need.
- The paper is silent on why the standard is the right venue, how the change interacts with existing specifications, why a library solution is insufficient, and what implementation experience exists.
