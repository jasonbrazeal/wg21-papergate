Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides a clear rationale for why the proposed change matters and identifies prior art and alternatives, but its support for standardization becomes much thinner on questions of who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, interoperability, and implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the explanation of the breakage in ported code and the acknowledgment that the underlying concerns are troubling.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is credited as established, including the consteval overload with constexpr exceptions and the overlooked design-review history.
- The paper claims, but does not establish, who is actually affected and why the standard must be changed rather than relying on a library workaround.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established implementation experience or coordination evidence beyond the author’s own implementation and unit tests.
