Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers substantive support for the existence of a real, longstanding design problem and for its account of the competing positions that stalled prior work, but it is much thinner when it comes to showing that the specific direction it advocates is the one the standard should adopt. The material on affected users, the need for a standard facility rather than a library solution, and implementation experience is asserted more than demonstrated.

- The strongest part of the paper is its framing of the failure to reach consensus as rooted in genuine, unresolved disagreements over core semantics rather than superficial details.
- The discussion of prior competing proposals and the “sharp-knife” versus “dull-knife” split credibly establishes that the design space is well explored and that earlier efforts left the central questions open.
- The weakest part is the repeated reliance on third-party library practice as evidence without showing concretely who is affected, what exact semantics those libraries require, or how a standardized operation would fit existing ABI and reallocation-based implementations.
