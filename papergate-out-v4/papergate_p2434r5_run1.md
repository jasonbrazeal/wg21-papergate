Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a partial case for its own standardization, strongest on motivation and on situating the proposal against prior provenance models, but much thinner on who would be affected, what implementation experience exists, and why the standard rather than another layer is the necessary vehicle. The discussion of alternatives is substantive and readably connects the proposal to ongoing committee work, yet several of the places where the paper could demonstrate necessity instead assert it without enough supporting evidence.

- The clearest support is the explanation of why current wording is “overly charitable” and how existing nondeterminism already produces undefined behavior in cases the proposed model would address.
- The paper’s engagement with prior art, especially PVI, P2318R1, and P3501R0, gives a credible account of alternatives and why the chosen direction was preferred.
- The thinnest part is the absence of evidence about implementation experience, leaving the practical consequences and costs of adopting the wording largely unexamined.
- The paper also says little about who would feel the change in practice, so the affected audience and the concrete standardization burden remain hard to judge.
