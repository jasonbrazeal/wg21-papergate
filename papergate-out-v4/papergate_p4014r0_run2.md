Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper makes a credible case that its subject matter is real and already in production, but it stops short of demonstrating that standardization is the necessary next step. The strongest material concerns existing practice and a recognized gap in C++26, while the argument for why the standard library or a library-level solution cannot suffice remains largely asserted rather than shown.

- The paper’s grounding in a shipped reference implementation and production use at NVIDIA and Citadel Securities gives it a solid factual basis for implementation experience.
- The prior-art discussion is well supported by direct references to P2300R10 and related work, establishing that the paper is responding to a real and recent standardization context.
- The case for why this must be in the standard, rather than remain a library or vendor extension, is mostly a rhetorical question and does not establish the necessity it claims.
- Who is actually affected is asserted through quotations and broad characterizations, without evidence that the claimed audience experiences the problem in the way the paper describes.
