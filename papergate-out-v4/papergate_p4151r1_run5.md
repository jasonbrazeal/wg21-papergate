Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only fragmentary support for its own standardization, mostly by recounting the naming history of an existing facility, and it does not connect that history to a live problem for users or implementers. The support is thinnest wherever the proposal should explain who benefits, what alternatives exist outside the standard, and why the change must be made in the standard itself.

- The strongest material is the account of how the interface changed from a binary-invocable object to a unary one, which at least suggests a naming inconsistency the paper wants to address.
- The paper gestures at prior discussion and the author’s own remark that a change of interface warranted a change of name, but it does not turn that into an actual survey of alternatives or established practice.
- It never identifies an affected audience or what practical harm or confusion the current name causes in real code.
- Most glaringly, the paper gives no reason the change needs to happen through the C++ standard rather than through guidance, documentation, or library-level conventions.
