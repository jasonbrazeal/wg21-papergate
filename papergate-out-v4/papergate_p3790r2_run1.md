Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only one fully established element—the existence of prior art and alternatives—while leaving nearly every other justification, including the central question of why standardization is necessary and why a library solution is insufficient, asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears around the need for a standard rather than a library, where no evidence or argument is credited at all, and around implementation experience, which rests on broad production-use claims rather than concrete validation.

- The strongest support is the paper’s acknowledgment and citation of prior work, particularly P2434R4 and the existing working draft, showing awareness of the surrounding design space.
- The paper repeatedly asserts that concurrent and sequential algorithms have relied on pointer bit-manipulation in production for decades, but this claim is credited only as an assertion, not as established evidence.
- The case for why only a language or library change within the standard is required—and why a library alone will not solve the problem—is not established at all by the credited material.
