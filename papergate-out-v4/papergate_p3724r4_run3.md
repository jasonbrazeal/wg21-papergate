Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers a solid foundation in some areas, particularly its discussion of prior art, alternatives, and the existence of a reference implementation, but its broader case rests on thin assertions about prevalence and difficulty that are not backed up with evidence. The support is thinnest where the paper needs to show that user-level implementations are unreliable enough to justify standardization, since that claim is repeated rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support comes from the established prior art, including the earlier proposal, Numerics TS mention, and the available reference implementation.
- The paper also does well to establish implementation experience through its linked GitHub reference and the included wording and appendix.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper claims but does not establish why this belongs in the standard rather than remaining a library concern, despite that being central to the standardization case.
- The repeated references to an “ocean” of user mistakes are not substantiated with concrete evidence, leaving the affected-audience claim thinner than it first appears.
