Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin foundation for its own standardization, centering on a general argument that implementation resources are constrained and that more realistic planning is needed. Beyond that motivation, the support is largely aspirational or anecdotal, leaving the core standardization questions effectively unaddressed.

- The strongest support is the acknowledged resource pressure on implementers and the need for realistic expectations after features are standardized.
- The paper gestures at affected parties through meeting attendance, but does not establish who would actually be served by the proposed standardization work.
- The claim that a study group would foster collaboration is presented as a benefit without evidence of prior art, alternatives, or comparable efforts.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no case for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library, no implementation experience, and no account of coordination or interoperability needs.
