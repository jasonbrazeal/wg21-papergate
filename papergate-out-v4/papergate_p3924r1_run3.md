Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a slim, largely procedural justification for its own standardization, mostly by citing a national-body comment and noting that past misuses have been corrected. Most of the support is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the argument becomes entirely absent when it reaches the questions of why the standard is the right venue, how the change interacts with other work, and why a library solution is insufficient.

- The strongest support is the reference to NB comment US 11-400, which at least ties the proposal to a concrete request for a consistency review of “declaration” in the standard.
- The paper asserts that incorrect uses exist and that some past errors were fixed, but it does not show how widespread or consequential the remaining problems are.
- The paper offers no discussion of why this needs to be addressed through the standard itself rather than through editorial practice or other means.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of any case for standardization as opposed to ordinary defect repair, leaving coordination, interoperability, and implementation experience unaddressed.
