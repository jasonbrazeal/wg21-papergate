Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: it shows convincingly that there is a divergence worth fixing and that the current wording is not the whole story, but it leaves the affected audience, the necessity of a standard change, and the practical implementation record largely unspoken. The thinnest parts are the absence of a clear case for who is harmed today and why the problem cannot be addressed outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the concrete demonstration of implementation divergence and the observation that existing wording has left the selection of deallocation functions poorly specified.
- The paper also credibly presents the proposed wording as part of a coordinated cleanup around placement new, with prior discussion making it easier to review.
- The claim that there are two cases of implementation divergence is repeated, but the paper does not establish who is affected by those cases or why that audience needs a standardized resolution.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of implementation experience beyond a table of current behavior, with no clear evidence that the proposed direction has been tried or adopted in practice.
