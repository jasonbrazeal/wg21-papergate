Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only thin support for its own standardization: the material it presents is largely fragmentary and does not rise to an argument that the proposed change belongs in the standard. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, or how the change would coordinate with existing practice.

- The strongest credited material concerns prior art and alternatives, where the paper gestures toward existing implementation workarounds and related proposal history.
- The paper asserts significance mainly through a brief scenario about blocking futures, but that claim is not developed into a demonstrated need.
- There is no established discussion of implementation experience, affected users, or interoperability, leaving the practical case for standardization ungrounded.
- Most notably, the paper never establishes why the standard must act rather than a library, nor who the change would actually help.
