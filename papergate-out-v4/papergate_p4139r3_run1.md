Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper’s case rests most convincingly on its account of prior naming discussions and rejected alternatives, but it does not carry that argument forward into a clear demonstration of need, affected users, or feasibility. The support is thinnest where a standards document would normally show why the change belongs in the standard, how it coordinates with existing practice, and that it has been tested in real implementations.

- The strongest part of the paper is its documentation of prior art and the sequence of naming decisions, which explains how the proposal arrived at its current shape.
- The paper makes only a weak claim about who is affected, relying on a passing observation about consensus rather than identifying concrete users or codebases.
- It does not establish why the feature requires standardization rather than a library solution, leaving the central justification unaddressed.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or coordination and interoperability discussion, which leaves the practical case for standardization essentially unexamined.
