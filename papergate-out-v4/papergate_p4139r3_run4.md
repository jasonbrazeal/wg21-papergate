Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives a narrow but real account of why the naming choice matters and what alternatives were already considered, but it leaves several core parts of the standardization case unaddressed. The support is strongest on naming rationale and weakest on questions that would show why the feature belongs in the standard itself rather than in a library or as a postponed design question.

- The paper establishes why a `get`-style name would be inconsistent and why the name should signal an unbounded search with possible failure.
- It documents the rejected naming alternatives and the prior committee history around P3091 clearly enough to frame the remaining problem.
- It only claims, rather than establishes, who is affected and that there is coordination pressure from the C++26 timeline.
- It does not establish why the standard is the right venue, why a library cannot provide the functionality, or that there is implementation experience behind the proposed direction.
