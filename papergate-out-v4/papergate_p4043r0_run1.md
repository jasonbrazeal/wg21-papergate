Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gestures at a contentious and active design discussion, but it does little to establish that standardization now is necessary or even that the proposed feature requires a standard rather than a library or another form of specification. Its strongest material is the citation of substantial national body and community activity around Contracts, though even that is offered more as evidence of disagreement than as a foundation for moving forward.

- The paper best supports the point that many people are affected by the Contracts discussion through its link to more than twenty national body comments and related papers.
- Its treatment of prior art is thin but at least gestures toward the Concepts precedent as a reason to allow more design time.
- The paper offers essentially no argument for why a standard is the right vehicle or why a library solution would be inadequate.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any real implementation experience, since the paper only names the gathering of such experience as a goal rather than presenting it as evidence.
