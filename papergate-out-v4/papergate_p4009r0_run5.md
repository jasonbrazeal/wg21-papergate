Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a partial case for its own standardization, relying mainly on the existence of alternative designs and prior discussion; the affirmative argument for why this particular facility belongs in the standard, who it serves, and how it would fit with existing practice is largely left unsupported.

- The strongest part of the case is that plausible alternatives to P2900 have been identified and that the desired semantics can already be approximated through standard library functions, which shows the design space is understood.
- The paper’s stated motivation responds to objections raised against the current Contracts draft, but it does not demonstrate that the affected audiences actually require a standardized solution rather than the existing workarounds.
- The discussion of standardization necessity is thin, offering only assertions that little would change by adopting the current approach while not showing why a standard facility, rather than a library or implementation-defined mechanism, is required.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of evidence about who is affected, how the proposal would interoperate with existing or future features, or what implementation experience supports the design.
