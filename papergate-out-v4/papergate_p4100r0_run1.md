Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers substantial support in the areas where concrete artifacts and third-party activity can be pointed to, particularly its implementation experience, prior art, and coordination story. The case is much thinner where it needs to explain why this belongs in the standard rather than remaining a library concern, and the claims about who is affected rest on adoptions and evaluations that are asserted more than demonstrated.

- The strongest support comes from the working libraries and related Boost projects, which give the implementation experience and interoperability claims real weight.
- The discussion of prior art is convincing because it acknowledges what Asio got right and situates the work as building on existing practice rather than replacing it wholesale.
- The most persistent shortfall is the failure to establish that existing library mechanisms are insufficient, leaving the standard as the necessary home for these abstractions.
