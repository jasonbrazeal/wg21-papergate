Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper offers substantial support for the existence of a real coordination problem and documents considerable implementation experience, but its case for standardization is uneven: it establishes context, affected users, and prior practice convincingly, while leaving the core argument about why the standard—rather than a library or other mechanism—must resolve the question largely asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the assembled deployment record, which shows named-guarantee checking shipped at production scale across multiple vendors and independent codebases over a decade.
- The paper also firmly establishes that two committee proposals now answer the same configuration question and have become inconsistent in their stated wording.
- The thinnest part of the case is the argument that a library solution will not suffice, which is asserted but not backed by evidence that user-space or vendor mechanisms cannot carry the needed semantics.
- Most notably, the paper does not establish why the standard itself must decide the ownership question, rather than leaving the competing proposals or existing practice to resolve it.
