Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper makes a genuine start by explaining why the current lifetime rules feel too conservative and by situating its approach in past committee discussion, but it leaves too many practical questions unanswered for the case to feel complete. The thinnest areas are those that would show real-world need and viability: who is affected today, whether existing synchronization mechanisms actually fail, and whether any implementation can be pointed to.

- The strongest support is the motivation, which credibly identifies a surprising gap between what the rules require and what the paper argues should be sufficient.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives shows that the authors engaged with earlier committee reasoning and attempted to avoid changing happens-before itself.
- The paper does not establish coordination or interoperability with existing practice, leaving the standardization need largely abstract.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of implementation experience: there is no demonstrated platform or codebase showing that the proposed change solves a concrete, current problem.
