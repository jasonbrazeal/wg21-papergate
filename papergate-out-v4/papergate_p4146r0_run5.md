Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a mixed account of itself: it can point to concrete motivating failures, existing practice, and considered alternatives, but it leaves several basic justifications for standardization almost entirely unspoken. The thinnest areas concern who is actually affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the change coordinates with existing specifications, and why a library solution is insufficient.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation experience, including fixes in libstdc++ and stdexec, which shows the problem and proposed direction are grounded in real code.
- The paper also establishes why the issue matters through clear examples of reasonable code failing or behaving unnecessarily poorly.
- Prior art and alternatives are credited, showing the author weighed more than one possible resolution.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who is affected, leaving the scope and urgency of the problem unquantified.
