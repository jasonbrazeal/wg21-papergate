Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin evidentiary basis for standardization, relying heavily on unsupported assertions about utility and a single vendor’s implementation. Its strongest claims are concentrated in the existence of a reference implementation and some discussion of hardware lowering, but the document does not connect these to a clear need for the standard or to alternatives.

- The most concrete support is the statement that the functions have been implemented in Intel’s reference implementation and used in software products, though no details or scope are provided.
- The paper gestures at prior art by citing P0543R3 and mentioning native instruction mappings, but it does not develop what those alternatives lack or why a new proposal is needed.
- The discussion of who is affected is limited to the same vendor reference, with no broader user community or use-case evidence.
- Most notably, the paper does not establish why the standard is the right venue, why a library would be insufficient, or how the feature would coordinate with existing and future interfaces.
