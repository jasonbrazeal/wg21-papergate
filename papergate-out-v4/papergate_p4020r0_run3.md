Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for standardization, mainly by establishing that contract assertions are already in the C++26 CD and that concerns have been raised about them. Beyond that, the case is thin: the affected parties, prior art, rationale for standardizing, and implementation experience are asserted rather than demonstrated, and several essential requirements are not addressed at all.

- The strongest support is the uncontested observation that contract assertions have been voted into the CD and have prompted formal concerns.
- The paper asserts but does not establish that two compiler vendors have objected, who is affected, or that implementation experiments are underway.
- The argument for why the standard is the right venue rests on claims about needing confidence and limited experience, without evidence tying those claims to a concrete standardization need.
- The paper offers nothing at all on coordination and interoperability, why a library approach would not suffice, or implementation experience beyond hearsay.
