Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers substantive support on the procedural problem, the relevant prior art, and the narrowness of the proposed remedy, but the case for standardization becomes notably thinner when it moves from the mechanics of committee process to the affected population and the absence of alternative mechanisms. The thinnest areas are the unestablished claims about library-level or other non-standard remedies and the lack of any implementation experience, both of which are left essentially unargued.

- The paper most convincingly establishes why the problem matters, with concrete procedural risks about consensus burden and national body review shifting when unmailed changes are tallied.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives is also established, showing how the proposal fits within existing SD-4 rules and is narrower than current permissions.
- The paper claims but does not establish who is affected, relying on a single anecdotal meeting count and a loosely connected number of implementer requests for slowing feature additions.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish why a library or other non-standard mechanism cannot address the concern, followed closely by the absence of any implementation experience for the proposed process change.
