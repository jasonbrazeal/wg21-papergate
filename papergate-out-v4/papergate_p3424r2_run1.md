Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for standardization: its clearest contribution is showing that the problem is known and that implementations already diverge around the existing wording. Most of the argument for why this should be standardized, who it affects, and how it fits into practice is asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the identification of an existing core issue and the observed divergence among major compilers.
- The paper claims that prohibiting the construct removes undefined behavior, but it does not substantiate why that removal is a sufficient standardization rationale.
- The discussion of implementation experience gestures at compiler behavior without establishing that the proposed change has been implemented or validated.
- The most glaring omission is any account of which users or codebases are affected by the current rule or would benefit from the change.
