Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin scaffolding of justification for its own standardization, with nearly every practical question left unanswered. Its strongest moments gesture toward existing practice and the current standard, but those gestures are never developed into a concrete case, and most of the argument is simply absent.

- The clearest element is the stated goal of building on existing practice for transactional memory, though this remains a claim rather than a demonstration.
- The reference to ISO/IEC 14882:2020 as context is mentioned, but the paper does not explain what that context contributes or what alternatives were weighed.
- The proposal never identifies who would be affected by the change or why standardization, as opposed to a library-based approach, is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience, coordination considerations, or evidence that the feature has been exercised in real systems.
