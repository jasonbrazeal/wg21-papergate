Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper’s strongest, most direct support lies in its account of why a portable standard guarantee would be the wrong vehicle for a non-portable, adoption-bounded behavior, and it is equally clear that the standardized slice would add little beyond existing vendor options. The case thins where it leans on implementation practice and affected users: those passages are asserted rather than shown, so the paper never fully demonstrates that the people and products it names represent the standardization problem it asks the committee to decline.

- The paper establishes that the recurring need is already served by non-portable, opt-in vendor mechanisms, so standardization would add little portable value.
- The paper establishes that the continuing response would impose machinery implementers have said they will not carry, which strengthens the case against requiring it.
- The paper claims but does not establish who is concretely affected, beyond naming libc++ and Bloomberg’s BDE as shipping comparable capabilities.
- The most visible omission is implementation experience: the paper repeatedly cites existing opt-in implementations but does not establish enough about their scope, limits, or lessons to support the standardization question it is addressing.
