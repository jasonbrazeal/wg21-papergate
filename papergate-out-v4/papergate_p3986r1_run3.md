Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only thin, largely indirect support for its own standardization, with nearly every element of the case resting on assertions attributed to P3425 rather than developed here. Its thinnest areas are the absence of implementation experience and any discussion of coordination or interoperability.

- The paper’s strongest support is its connection to P3425, though even that is asserted rather than demonstrated in the text.
- The need for standard wording is claimed through a critique of the existing specification style, but the claim is not substantiated.
- The paper does not establish who is concretely affected by the proposed change beyond reporting attendance and a poll outcome.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience or any account of how the change interacts with existing practice and adjacent specifications.
