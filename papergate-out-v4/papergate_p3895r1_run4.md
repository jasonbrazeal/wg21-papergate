Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest, and really only fully developed, support comes from the existence of a complete implementation, while nearly every other part of the standardization rationale rests on repeated assertions rather than demonstrated evidence. The argument is thinnest wherever it depends on broad, unquantified claims about user need, widespread implementation failure, or the inadequacy of existing alternatives, since those points are stated but not backed up with examples, data, or concrete comparisons.

- The paper does establish implementation experience through a full repository, which at least shows the feature is implementable in practice.
- The claims about users needing this “all the time” and failing “miserably” appear repeatedly, but no specific cases or evidence are provided to make that need tangible.
- The discussion of alternatives and interoperability is asserted rather than examined, leaving open whether other languages’ division modes or existing libraries already address the problem adequately.
- The most glaring omission is any real substantiation for why a library cannot suffice, since the paper’s own cited implementation existence undercuts the claim that a third-party solution would be unreasonable.
