Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers firm support in two important areas: it shows real implementation experience and it identifies a concrete defect in the current customization model, including prior efforts to address it. The support is much thinner, however, on the questions that tie directly to a standards-track change—who is actually affected, why only the standard can solve the problem, and how the proposal fits with existing and future sender/receiver work.

- The strongest case is the implementation experience, with two largely independent deployments and no reported bugs.
- The paper also establishes that the problem matters by pointing to a broken customization mechanism and gaps in P3718R0’s proposed resolution.
- The least supported area is the need for standardization itself: the paper asserts standard-library vendors are in no rush and offers little evidence of current user demand or ecosystem breakage.
- A glaring omission is coordination and interoperability, where the claims about ecosystem impact and scheduler customization are asserted but not substantiated.
