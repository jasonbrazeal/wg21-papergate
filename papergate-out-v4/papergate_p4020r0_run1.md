Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some grounding for why contract enforcement might matter, but most of its case for standardization rests on assertions and references rather than demonstrated need, evidence, or experience. The support is thinnest where the proposal must distinguish itself from what can already be done in libraries and where it must show that the proposed feature has been successfully used in practice.

- The strongest support is the recognition that contract assertions in function declarations involve a different and less widely exercised model than configurable assertions inside function bodies.
- The discussion of interoperability and enforcement gestures toward a standards-level concern, but the paper does not establish that current practice or non-standard means fail to address it.
- The most glaring omission is any evidence that a library-only approach is insufficient, leaving the central question of why standardization is necessary unanswered.
