Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers a solid demonstration that the mechanism can be implemented, but most of the surrounding rationale is asserted rather than substantiated. The thinnest support is in the parts that matter most for a standardization proposal: why this is a real, standardizable problem, who exactly is harmed, and why the standard library is the only viable home for a fix.

- The strongest element is the concrete implementation evidence, including a cross-compiler proof of concept and a link to existing practice in mp-units.
- The paper states that several libraries independently arrived at the same workaround, but it does not show those libraries, their usage, or the scope of the users affected.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of a demonstrated case for why the standard must act: the jurisdictional and library-feasibility arguments are repeated, but not developed into evidence that the problem cannot be adequately addressed outside the standard.
