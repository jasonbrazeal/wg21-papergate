Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some foundation for its standardization case by pointing to a related core proposal and cataloguing candidate library operations, but it mostly asserts benefits without demonstrating them. The thinnest areas are the lack of any argument for why the library wording change must be standardized rather than handled editorially or otherwise, and the absence of implementation experience despite the existence of a review record.

- The clearest support comes from prior art and alternatives, where the paper ties itself to the design-approved P3668 and identifies 51 candidate operations that could be defaulted.
- The paper gestures toward implementation experience by noting LWG review at a specific meeting, but it does not describe any actual implementation or prototyping.
- Coordination and interoperability is only suggested by a brief claim about a universally accepted meaning, without showing how existing or future library specifications would interact.
- The paper provides no reason that a library-level solution would be insufficient, leaving the most basic threshold question about why a standard is needed unaddressed.
