Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only assertions that its central concerns matter, without grounding them in demonstrated practice, measurable impact, or evidence that existing mechanisms and specifications fail in the ways claimed. The thinnest support runs through nearly every category: the affected population, the inadequacy of prior art, and the need for standardization are all asserted rather than shown, and the implementation experience cited is too partial and unresolved to carry the argument.

- The paper’s strongest material is its restatement of a known concern from P3846R1, but even that is only quoted, not independently established as a standardization need.
- The claim that contracts cannot serve as guaranteed UB checks rests on an interpretation of P2900 that the paper does not demonstrate through deployed or specified examples.
- The interoperability concern about differently built translation units is plausible, but the paper offers no evidence that this occurs in practice or cannot be mitigated by existing build discipline.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no implementation experience or deployed usage that would show why its proposed check cannot be a library facility or why standardization is the necessary next step.
