Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers partial support for its own standardization, but the case remains thin in several areas that matter most to reviewers. Its strongest ground is in connecting the proposal to prior designs and showing that alternatives exist, while the arguments about urgency, affected users, and the need for a standard rather than a library solution are largely asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The discussion of prior art and design alternatives is the best-supported part of the paper, showing awareness of related work and plausible paths forward.
- The paper claims broad and severe dissatisfaction among audiences, but offers no substantive evidence about who is affected or how much.
- The case for why this needs to be in the standard, rather than delivered through a library or postponed, is asserted without being established.
- The paper provides no implementation experience, no coordination or interoperability analysis, and no argument that a library would be insufficient.
