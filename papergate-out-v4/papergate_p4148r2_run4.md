Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers genuine support in the areas of prior art, implementation experience, and the broad importance of dynamic structural interfaces, but much of its case for standardization rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest parts are the absence of any argument that a library solution cannot suffice and the weakly supported claims about who is affected and why the standard is the right home for this work.

- The paper’s strongest support is its clear connection to a real, recurring problem and the availability of a working reference implementation that grounds the proposal in experience.
- Prior art is handled credibly by situating the work against existing type-erasure facilities and the overlapping `proxy` proposal.
- The claim about who is affected leans on the same general statement about type-erasure solutions without showing specific user populations or concrete impact.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why a library will not do, despite the proposal itself centering on a library-driven generative approach.
