Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only thin support for its own standardization, with every point either asserted rather than demonstrated or left entirely unaddressed. The thinnest areas are those that would show real-world need and feasibility: who is affected, why a standard is required as opposed to library practice, and whether anyone has actually implemented the idea.

- The clearest point in the paper’s favor is its stated goal of replacing the status quo of having no policy, though even that is only claimed, not shown.
- The claim that prior policy proposals tend to mark wide-contract non-throwing functions `noexcept` is credited as prior art, but the paper does not establish that this represents a settled or viable alternative.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the absence of a standard policy or why the standard is the right place to solve the problem.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no implementation experience, no interoperability analysis, and no argument for why a library-level convention would be insufficient.
