Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers substantial rhetorical and comparative support for its position, but most of that support is asserted rather than demonstrated with evidence tied to this specific proposal. The strongest grounding is in the existence and claimed maturity of an alternative library-based approach, while the weakest areas are the near-total absence of coordination and interoperability discussion and the lack of convincing implementation experience for what is actually being proposed.

- The paper’s most solid footing is its identification of prior art and alternatives, including a cited, implemented library-based design and historical experience with contract-like mechanisms in other languages.
- The argument for why the standard is needed leans almost entirely on the asserted superiority of that alternative, but the paper does not establish that superiority with evidence rather than claim.
- The paper fails to address coordination and interoperability at all, leaving a central standardization question untouched.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of credible implementation experience, since even the cited library experiment is characterized as limited and explicitly not deployment at scale.
