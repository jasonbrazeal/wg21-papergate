Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, and much of its case rests on assertions about the initialization profile’s importance and expected adoption rather than on demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are those where standardization proposals usually need concrete evidence: coordination, library feasibility, and implementation experience.

- The paper’s clearest strength is its acknowledgment of prior work and alternatives, including related languages and the need to merge with the existing initialization profile.
- The rationale for why the feature matters is asserted but not grounded in examples or evidence strong enough to carry the standardization argument.
- The paper does not establish who is affected, why the standard is required, or that a library solution would be insufficient.
- Most notably, it offers no implementation experience or coordination plan, leaving the practical path to standardization largely unaddressed.
