Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers modest support for its own standardization, primarily through concrete implementation observations, but many of its central claims are asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the lack of an identified affected audience and the absence of a substantive argument for why standardization is the right remedy.

- The clearest supporting evidence is implementation experience, with named compiler behaviors and a note about a pending GCC fix.
- The paper asserts that the current wording is contradictory and that undefined behavior can be removed, but it does not establish who is affected by this in practice.
- The discussion of prior art, alternatives, and why a library solution would not suffice is largely a restatement of the proposal’s premise rather than a comparative analysis.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to identify any affected users, codebases, or real-world consequences that would ground the need for a language change.
