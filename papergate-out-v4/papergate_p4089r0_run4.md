Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper offers substantial support for its standardization case, with particularly strong evidence on ecosystem impact, implementation experience, and interoperability. The thinnest areas are the justification for why this must be a standard feature rather than a library solution, and the argument for why the standard specifically needs this change.

- The strongest support comes from the documented implementation experience and the convergence of seven independent libraries on one-parameter designs, which grounds the paper's claims in observable practice.
- The paper also establishes clearly who is affected and why it matters through structural analysis and empirical evidence of cross-library composition failures.
- A notable weakness is the treatment of alternatives, where the paper establishes what has been tried but does not fully demonstrate that a non-standard library cannot provide an adequate remedy.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish that standardization, rather than continued library development, is necessary to achieve the paper's goals.
