Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with its strongest contribution being a limited account of prior art and design inconsistencies that motivate a cleanup. The case thins quickly around the absence of any discussion of affected users, implementation experience, or why the functionality cannot be provided through a library—leaving the central need for standardization mostly asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper most clearly establishes that related facilities were adopted independently and produced inconsistent APIs and coverage gaps, as well as noting indirect access-related functionality in P2996R13.
- Its claim that the requested information is useful and occasionally asked for is asserted but not supported with evidence about who needs it or how often.
- The paper does not establish why a standard mechanism is necessary as opposed to a library-based solution.
- It offers no implementation experience, no coordination or interoperability analysis, and no discussion of the affected audience, leaving the standardization rationale incomplete.
