Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper’s strongest support is its clear demonstration that the layering question is live and consequential, backed by a credible survey of prior art and competing designs. Its weakest areas concern the standardization-specific need: it does not establish why a library solution is inadequate, and several claims about affected users, standards coordination, and implementation experience rest on the proposal’s own assertions rather than independent evidence.

- The most solid part is the established account of prior art and alternatives, showing the architectural choice is contested and grounded in published competing work.
- The claim that the decision matters is well supported by the warning that incremental approvals could unintentionally close the Profiles design space.
- The thinness is most evident in the absence of any established argument for why a library cannot address the problem.
- The user-impact, standardization, coordination, and implementation-experience claims are asserted consistently but lack independent corroboration beyond the paper’s own framing and companions.
