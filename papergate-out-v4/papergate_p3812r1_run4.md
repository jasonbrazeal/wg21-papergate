Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers a thin and largely undeveloped case for its own standardization, consisting mostly of assertions that the feature would remove a limitation and reduce code rather than evidence that the problem is widely felt or that the proposed path is workable. The strongest material is the reference to the existing constraint on defaulted copy assignment and the related Core Guideline, but even that is not turned into a sustained argument. The rest of the necessary support—audience, standardization rationale, coordination, and implementation experience—is essentially absent.

- The clearest, though still underdeveloped, support is the citation of the current lifetime rules and a Core Guideline that at least gestures toward the relevant restriction.
- The paper asserts a motivation about enforcing constness with less code, but does not establish that this need is significant or common in practice.
- The paper does not identify who would use the feature, what alternatives were seriously considered, or how implementations would validate the design, leaving the standardization case unsupported where it matters most.
