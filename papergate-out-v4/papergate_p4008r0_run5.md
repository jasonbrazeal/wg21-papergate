Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gestures toward a motivation but offers very little concrete support for the claim that standardization is needed, with most of its key points asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any implementation experience and the complete silence on coordination or interoperability with existing C++ features and ecosystems.

- The paper’s strongest support is its framing of long-standing C-compatibility hazards as structural problems worth addressing, though even this remains a claim rather than a worked demonstration.
- The discussion of prior alternatives, while undeveloped, at least acknowledges that existing safe-subset efforts are either too restrictive or unenforceable.
- The paper offers no implementation experience to show the approach is practical or has been tried in any form.
- Most glaringly, it never addresses coordination and interoperability with existing code, tooling, or the broader standard, leaving a central standardization question entirely unanswered.
