Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers real support for its implementation claims and shows awareness of the prior-art landscape, but it does not seriously establish who needs this facility, why the standard is the right venue, or how it would coordinate with the surrounding ecosystem beyond the examples it gives. The thinnest parts are the absence of any affected-user analysis and the largely asserted case for standardizing rather than shipping as a library.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation and compiled example output against two execution implementations, which backs the claim of practical viability.
- The prior-art and alternatives section is adequately grounded in specific proposals and existing bridges.
- The paper asserts relevance, standardizability, and interoperability benefits without identifying the users or codebases that would adopt the facility.
- The most glaring omission is that no affected constituency is established, leaving the need for committee action essentially unmoored from real-world demand.
