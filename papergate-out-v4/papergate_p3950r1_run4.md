Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers solid support in a couple of areas, particularly the arbitrariness of the current restriction and the existence of prior work, but much of its case rests on assertions by the author rather than demonstrated evidence. The thinnest support appears in the areas of affected users, implementation experience, and why a library cannot address the problem.

- The strongest support is the paper’s argument that banning `return_void` alongside `return_value` makes `void` an unjustified special case.
- The paper also credits prior art and alternatives, including an earlier proposal and the emergence of `std::execution`.
- A notable omission is that the claim of implementation experience is asserted without showing the implementation or its results.
- The most glaring gap is the lack of established evidence about who is affected, leaving the burden and scope of the problem largely unsubstantiated.
