Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s strongest case rests on its account of why the feature is needed and on the alternatives it surveys, but it does not carry that confidence through to the questions of affected users, standardizing versus a library solution, or practical implementation. The support is thinnest precisely where the paper needs to show that the problem is broad enough, that the standard is the right venue, and that the design is ready for real-world use.

- The paper clearly establishes the motivating gap in C++26 and gives a considered account of prior syntactic approaches and their drawbacks.
- Its claims about who is affected rely on general assertions of frequency rather than concrete examples or evidence from codebases.
- The argument that only standardization can provide the missing functionality is asserted as a “must-have” without being developed into a justification against other routes.
- There is no implementation experience presented, leaving the proposal without evidence that the feature can be implemented and used as described.
