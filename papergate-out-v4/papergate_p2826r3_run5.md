Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers some useful groundwork, particularly in its engagement with prior art and alternatives, but it does not make a complete case for standardization because large parts of its rationale rest on assertions rather than demonstrated need or evidence. The thinnest areas are the lack of any identified affected audience and the absence of implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s credible survey of prior art and its honest contrast with currently available workarounds.
- The argument for why a library cannot provide the capability leans heavily on the same copy-elision claim, which is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper never establishes who would actually use this feature or what real codebases are struggling with today.
- The most glaring omission is the total absence of implementation experience, leaving the feasibility and practical consequences of the design unexamined.
