The paper gives a partial account of why the proposed change might be desirable, with its strongest grounding in references to related proposals and previous discussions, but it leaves the core standardization arguments largely unaddressed. The thinnest parts concern the need for standard action, interoperability, and why a library solution is insufficient.

- The paper’s prior-art discussion is its most solid element, connecting the idea to existing proposals and noting the inconsistency with related wrapper types.
- The claims about who is affected and existing implementation experience are plausible but rest on little more than the author’s own library and a single compiler mention.
- The motivation for change is asserted as an inconsistency rather than demonstrated as a practical problem in standard C++ usage.
- The paper does not establish why this belongs in the standard rather than remaining a library extension, nor does it address coordination with other facilities or interoperability concerns.
