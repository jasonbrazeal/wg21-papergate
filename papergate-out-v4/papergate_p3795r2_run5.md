Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, largely asserted case for its own standardization: most of its supporting discussion gestures at usefulness, historical accident, and a desire to avoid API spread without substantiating those points. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected audience, any argument for why this belongs in the standard specifically, and any evidence of implementation experience or library limitations.

- The strongest support is the acknowledgment that related standard features arrived with gaps and inconsistencies, though the paper leans on that history rather than demonstrating it.
- The paper at least claims that the information is useful and occasionally requested, but it does not show who is asking or why existing workarounds fall short.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why this cannot be provided by a library or why standardization is the necessary remedy.
