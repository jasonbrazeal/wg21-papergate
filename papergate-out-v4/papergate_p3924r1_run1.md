Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers thin support for its own standardization: most of its claims are asserted rather than demonstrated, and the sections that would explain why the standard itself must change, how the change would coordinate with existing rules, and why a library solution cannot suffice are entirely silent. The strongest material concerns the existence of prior usage errors and a related NB comment, but even these are presented more as motivation than as evidence that a normative change is warranted.

- The paper is strongest when pointing to a previously filed national body comment and to at least one acknowledged misuse in an earlier draft, though it does not show that these amount to a need for standards action.
- The claim that some declarations are not declarations is offered as the core problem, but the paper does not establish how often this creates real-world harm or who is affected.
- The paper gives no account of why this belongs in the standard itself, what coordination or interoperability concerns would follow, or why a non-standard solution would not work.
- The absence of any established implementation experience review is the most glaring omission, since the paper invokes such a review without providing enough detail to show that it supports standardization.
