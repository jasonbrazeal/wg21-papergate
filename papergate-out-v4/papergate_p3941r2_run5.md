Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper makes a modest but real case for itself by showing that the change responds to identified problems and moves away from a discouraged design, but it offers almost no evidence about who would be affected, why standardization is the right venue, or how the feature works in practice. The thinnest parts concern the absence of implementation experience and any argument that a library solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the paper’s demonstration that the change removes a problematic mechanism and reflects concerns raised in prior discussion.
- It also provides some relevant prior art and alternatives, situating the proposal against earlier scheduling approaches and a discontinued related paper.
- The paper claims coordination with another proposal and some implementation motivation, but does not establish either with enough detail to count as evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any established explanation of what the standard library would be unable to do, leaving the standardization case essentially unargued.
