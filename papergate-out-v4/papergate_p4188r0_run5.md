Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper leans heavily on the existence of independent library workarounds and a short proof of concept, but it does not actually establish why that duplication is costly enough to require standardization, who specifically is burdened, or why existing practice is insufficient. Its thinnest support is on the central question of why the standard must intervene: the jurisdictional argument is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the affected audience is described only in general terms.

- The clearest support is implementation experience, with the proposal pointing to a concrete proof of concept and convergent mechanisms in multiple established libraries.
- The paper repeatedly invokes independent libraries as evidence of prior art and real use, but offers no detail about those use cases or the scale of duplication.
- The case for standardization is asserted through the claim that users cannot implement the bridge, without showing why existing workarounds fail for the affected users.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any concrete evidence about who is affected or why the status quo produces meaningful harm, leaving the motivating problem largely abstract.
