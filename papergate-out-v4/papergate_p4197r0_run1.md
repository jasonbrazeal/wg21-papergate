Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers a solid basis for why a general relocation primitive is needed, but it falls short of demonstrating that the specific direction it advocates has the user demand, implementation validation, or structural necessity to justify standardization now. The strongest support is conceptual, while the thinnest areas concern evidence of real-world impact and why a library solution would be insufficient.

- The paper clearly establishes the historical importance of the problem and the existence of prior, competing standardization efforts that stalled over fundamental design choices.
- Its discussion of affected libraries is suggestive, but the paper does not substantiate that those libraries would adopt or benefit from this particular proposal rather than their existing in-house mechanisms.
- The argument for why a Standard feature is necessary, rather than a library facility, remains asserted rather than demonstrated with concrete limitations of library implementations.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of credible implementation experience for the proposed design, leaving the paper without the validation that typically supports a new core language or library primitive.
