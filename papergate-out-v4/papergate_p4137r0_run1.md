Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives real support to parts of its case, particularly in showing how the proposed rules connect to existing lifetime-safety work and in honestly framing the annotation dividend as a measurement worth taking. But the argument thins out sharply where the standard itself is concerned: the document does not establish why this needs to be a language feature, how it coordinates with existing tooling or the ecosystem, or why a library cannot carry the same weight. The result is a paper that motivates investigation more convincingly than standardization.

- The strongest support is the clear lineage from established lifetime and invalidation analyses, which grounds the proposal in prior art rather than invention.
- The paper also establishes why measuring real-code coverage matters, even while admitting that the measurement does not yet exist.
- The most conspicuous gap is the absence of any argument for why the feature belongs in the standard rather than in an AST-based tool or library.
