Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a thin and uneven case for its own standardization, with only the survey of prior art and alternatives sufficiently grounded. Much of the argument rests on recollections of committee discussion rather than demonstrated user need, design rationale, or practical experience, and several foundational questions are left entirely unaddressed.

- The strongest support is the record of prior discussions and a stated preference from LEWG, which shows the proposal is responding to an actual design debate.
- The paper asserts that allocator placement matters to users, but it does not show who is affected or how that impact manifests.
- The paper does not establish why the standard should address this rather than leaving it to libraries or convention.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, leaving no evidence that the proposed requirement is workable or beneficial in practice.
