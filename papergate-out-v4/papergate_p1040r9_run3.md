Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper gives a reasonably persuasive account of why compile-time resource embedding would be useful and why existing workarounds are inadequate, but its case is uneven: it is strongest on the technical and practical motivations, and thinnest when it comes to showing who precisely would be affected and how the feature would coordinate with adjacent ecosystems.

- The paper most convincingly establishes that current techniques impose severe portability, performance, and complexity costs that standardizing a dedicated mechanism could address.
- It also demonstrates meaningful implementation experience, including measurable overhead and portability findings across major platforms and toolchains.
- The discussion of affected users remains largely a broad assertion about C and C++ programmers rather than a substantiated account of the communities or codebases that need this facility.
- The least developed part of the case is coordination and interoperability, where industry claims and build-system interactions are named but not tied to a concrete shared model or verified practice.
