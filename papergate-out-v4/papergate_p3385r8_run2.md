Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives only intermittent support for its own standardization, resting most of its case on design convenience and on passing references to implementation work rather than on demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are the failure to show why a library solution cannot serve, and the repeated use of general committee-process arguments as a substitute for concrete evidence about users and coordination.

- The strongest support comes from the discussion of prior art, where the paper ties the feature to an existing design concern in P4033R0 and reports unanimous feedback on attribute arguments.
- The implementation-experience section is suggestive, citing a compiler-explorer implementation and a specific equality question, but it does not amount to established practice.
- The paper repeatedly claims committee time would be wasted without a uniform vehicle, yet it never establishes who is concretely affected or why the problem matters beyond that internal concern.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers nothing to establish why this capability requires standardization rather than a library, leaving that essential argument entirely unaddressed.
