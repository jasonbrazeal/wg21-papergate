Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its standardization case: it credibly identifies prior art and alternatives, but most of the necessary justifications—importance, affected users, need for the standard, implementability, and why a library cannot suffice—are asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is the absence of any coordination or interoperability discussion.

- The strongest support comes from the paper’s identification of prior art and alternatives, including exclusions for iostream compatibility and related standards.
- The paper claims but does not establish why the change matters, who is affected, or why the standard is needed, relying on brief assertions about reducing duplication.
- The implementation experience is only claimed, with a single commit cited but no broader validation or portability evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of coordination and interoperability analysis, which leaves the standardization context unaddressed.
