Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper earns some credit for showing that the current names are genuinely confusing and that unabbreviated alternatives are widely used, but it leaves large parts of the standardization case unargued. Its thinnest support concerns the need for the standard itself, why a library cannot solve the problem, and whether anyone has actually tried implementing the change.

- The strongest support is the established point that `sat` is opaque and that the existing naming is internally inconsistent.
- The paper also credibly shows that alternatives using fuller or more consistent naming exist in Rust, Java, C#, and LLVM.
- A notable gap is that the affected-user evidence rests only on search-result counts, without showing how representative or actionable those results are.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard must act rather than leaving the name to libraries or future design work.
