Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers solid support in the areas that matter most for a language extension: it demonstrates existing compiler practice, points to a just-standardized C feature, and explains why the feature belongs in the core language rather than a library. The support is thinnest where the paper asserts rather than argues—particularly on who would actually benefit and why existing extension syntax cannot simply remain a de facto standard.

- The paper’s strongest support is its implementation evidence, with case ranges present in GCC and Clang for decades and now standardized in C2y.
- The paper clearly establishes why the standard is the right venue, since legitimizing existing extension code requires normative behavior rather than continued reliance on compiler-specific extensions.
- The paper establishes prior art and alternatives well by connecting the feature to C2y and contrasting it with pattern matching.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of concrete evidence about who is affected, leaving the user base and motivation more asserted than demonstrated.
