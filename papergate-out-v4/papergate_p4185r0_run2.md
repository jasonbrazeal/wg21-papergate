Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial support for several key aspects of its standardization case, particularly in demonstrating real-world need, prior art, and implementation experience, but it falls short of concretely documenting who is affected and why a library-level solution would be insufficient. The thinnest areas are the repeated claims about user demand and the argument that existing libraries cannot address the problem.

- The strongest support comes from concrete implementation experience and the convergence of two independent libraries on the same structural taxonomy.
- The need for standardization itself is well grounded in the inadequacy of the existing two-abstraction model and the requirement for domain-specific chrono integration.
- The weakest part is the “who is affected” case, which leans on general assertions of frequency and user requests without concrete evidence or quantification.
- Most glaringly, the paper claims but does not establish why a library will not do, since it does not demonstrate that existing or proposed non-standard libraries cannot deliver the same functionality.
