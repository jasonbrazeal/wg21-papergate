Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a sketch of the case for standardization, relying on broad claims about ergonomic limitation and a handful of code references rather than a developed argument for the feature’s necessity or scope. The support is thinnest on the questions that matter most for committee work: whether this belongs in the standard, whether existing mechanisms are insufficient, and whether anyone has actually tried the approach.

- The strongest element is the acknowledgment that the original structured bindings proposal explicitly left room for this extension, which at least situates the idea within prior standardization discussion.
- The affected-code examples gesture toward real-world use, but they are asserted rather than analyzed, so the paper does not show who is meaningfully burdened.
- The proposal gives no account of why a library-level solution cannot meet the need or how the feature would coordinate with existing tuple protocols and structured binding behavior.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for standardization itself: the paper never establishes that this is a language question, a compatibility question, or a portable-code question requiring a standards response.
