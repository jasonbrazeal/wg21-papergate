Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a limited evidentiary basis for its own standardization, relying heavily on a single interoperability argument that is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns the concrete need for a standard rather than a shared convention, since the same passage is reused to cover several distinct questions without additional substantiation.

- The strongest support is the appeal to field experience behind the suppression syntax, though even that is described only in passing rather than shown with examples or data.
- The discussion of prior art is at least explicit in positioning the paper relative to Sutter’s proposal, but it does not establish what alternatives were considered beyond that relationship.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established evidence for who is affected, with only an unsupported reference to educators’ interest in profiles for teaching.
