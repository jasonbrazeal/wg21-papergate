Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper grounds its central argument in existing vendor practice, and it is strongest when explaining why the continuing response does not need a permanent place in the standard because libc++ and Bloomberg’s BDE already offer equivalent opt-in behavior. That support thins noticeably around the people and implementation story: the affected population is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the deployment experience is cited without enough detail to establish it as evidence for standardization.

- The most consistent support is the repeated, credited point that vendor build options already deliver the proposed semantics, so standardization would add little beyond portability for a bounded adoption period.
- The paper also establishes its prior-art and alternatives case by tying the idea to existing direction from P2000R5 and to named companion proposals.
- The weakest credited area is implementation experience, where the paper names existing implementations but does not establish enough about their use or results to carry that part of the case.
- The clearest omission is the affected audience, since the paper claims who would benefit but never establishes that reach or need with concrete supporting evidence.
