Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a modest foundation for its standardization case, establishing only why the feature would be useful in the abstract, while leaving virtually every other essential argument as an unsupported claim. The support is thinnest where the paper most needs to persuade: in showing that a library cannot suffice, that implementation experience exists, and that the feature belongs in the standard rather than in a vendor extension or existing toolchain feature.

- The paper clearly establishes the general motivation for a compile-time, optimizer-backed assertion as a time-saving and correctness-enabling tool.
- The claim that a library solution cannot adequately control side effects or undefined behavior is asserted but never demonstrated with specifics or evidence.
- The paper gestures at implementation experience through a “prototype as a library function” but provides no details, results, or lessons that would substantiate practical feasibility.
- The most glaring omission is any real account of prior art and alternatives, since related mechanisms and existing builtins are named but not analyzed in enough depth to show why a new standard feature is warranted.
