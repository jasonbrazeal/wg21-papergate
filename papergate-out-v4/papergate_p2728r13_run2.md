Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest support is practical: it benefits from a reference implementation derived from an existing library implementation, giving the design a concrete existence beyond the proposal itself. Its treatment of alternatives is also the most complete part of the argument, situating the work against removed standard facilities and showing awareness of design choices. However, much of the case for why this belongs in the standard—rather than remaining a library—is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the paper does not address coordination or interoperability with related standardization efforts.

- The paper clearly shows implementation experience through a maintained reference implementation and a lineage connected to prior library work.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives offers a grounded comparison against deprecated standard facilities and variant designs.
- The paper only asserts, without supporting evidence or argument, that ordinary users and existing safety problems make this standardization necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any account of how this proposal coordinates with or relates to other standardization work in the same or adjacent areas.
