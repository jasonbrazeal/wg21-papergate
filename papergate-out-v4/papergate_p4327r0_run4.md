Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin argument for standardization, with most points asserted rather than substantiated. Its strongest support is the motivating observation about type repetition, but the case for who benefits, why a library solution is insufficient, and how the feature would interact with existing practice remains largely undeveloped.

- The paper identifies a plausible syntactic redundancy in default arguments and frames the change as a consistency extension consistent with existing direct-list-initialization style.
- The claim that affected programmers must repeat type names is stated, but no evidence or examples beyond the obvious one are offered to show how widespread or burdensome this is.
- The discussion of alternatives, such as overloading, acknowledges a workaround but does not establish why the verbosity or overload impact is significant enough to require a language change.
- The paper offers no implementation experience and no analysis of coordination or interoperability with existing language or library features.
