Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper provides a substantial foundation for its standardization case, with well-established arguments for why the problem matters, what alternatives fall short, and how the proposed facility would fit into the standard. The case is thinnest where it reaches beyond technical motivation into community impact and the insufficiency of existing libraries, since the paper asserts those points without demonstrating them beyond the authors’ own projects and selected examples.

- The strongest support comes from the implementation experience, which is concrete, verifiable, and tied to production feedback rather than hypothetical use.
- The paper clearly establishes why a standard library component, rather than another third-party library, is the right venue for this work.
- The treatment of prior art and coordination concerns is well grounded, especially in showing how existing approaches and naming choices collide or fail to compose.
- The most glaring omission is a demonstrated account of who is actually affected, since the audience estimates and claims of dominance among existing libraries are asserted rather than supported with independent evidence.
