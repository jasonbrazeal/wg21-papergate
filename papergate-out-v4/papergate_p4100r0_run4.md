Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial support for its standardization case in the areas of prior art, interoperability, and implementation experience, where concrete libraries and adopters are named and tied to the proposal’s abstractions. The case is thinnest on who is affected and why a library alone is insufficient, where the paper asserts relevance and necessity but does not yet show enough evidence to turn those assertions into established facts.

- The strongest support comes from the demonstrated implementation experience, with two C++20 libraries delivering the proposed mechanisms and multiple Boost projects building on them.
- The paper also establishes meaningful coordination and prior art, showing continuity with Asio’s model and active adoption across independent libraries.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established evidence for who is affected, since the production use and institutional evaluation are only claimed rather than substantiated within the paper’s own argument.
