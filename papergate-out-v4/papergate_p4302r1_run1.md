Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow but real account of a process failure, supported mainly by its explanation of how in-meeting revisions can outrun external review and move recorded consensus onto text the wider community has not examined. The support becomes much thinner once the paper moves from describing that risk to showing its frequency, severity, or the practical need for a standing rule rather than case-by-case objection.

- The strongest part of the paper is its established description of why recording a poll on an unseen in-meeting revision matters, since that shifts the evidence of consensus before national body experts can weigh in.
- The paper gestures toward alternatives by citing SD-4 and clarifying that the proposed rule would leave existing followup-paper permissions intact, but this remains asserted rather than demonstrated as a workable comparison.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper does not establish why the change must be a standardization-process rule at all, nor why existing meeting practice or library-level or organizational remedies cannot address the concern.
