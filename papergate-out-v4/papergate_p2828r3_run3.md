Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only tentative support for its own standardization, resting on informal claims about compiler behavior and the design space rather than a demonstrated need. The thinnest areas are the absence of any discussion of affected users, the failure to show why a library solution would not suffice, and the lack of substantiated implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the observation that multiple major compilers already implement copy elision in the relevant cases, though this is asserted rather than documented.
- The paper gestures at a broad design space and references a core issue, but does not establish prior art or alternatives in enough detail to justify standardization.
- It does not identify who is affected by the current behavior or what practical problem they face.
- Most notably, the paper never explains why a library-based approach cannot address the need, leaving a central question about standardization unanswered.
