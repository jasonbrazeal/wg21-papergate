Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers a reasonably grounded case for standardizing this design, with its strongest material coming from the explanation of the semantic problem, the survey of prior approaches, and the report of implementation experience in GCC. The support is thinnest where the paper asserts practical impact and distinguishes its approach from what a library could provide, since those points are stated rather than demonstrated with concrete evidence.

- The clearest strength is the established implementation experience, including a complete GCC implementation and a specific historical failure of earlier inheritance-based proposals.
- The paper also establishes the prior art landscape well, showing why existing languages and earlier C++ designs do not transfer directly.
- The rationale for needing a standard feature is stated repeatedly but not grounded in worked examples or user experience, leaving the practical necessity more asserted than shown.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish who is concretely affected, since the real-world use case is mentioned only in passing without evidence of prevalence or pain.
