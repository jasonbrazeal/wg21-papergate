Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper makes only a partial case for standardization: its opening argument about C++’s role in AI is taken seriously, but most of the necessary supporting evidence is asserted rather than demonstrated, and the case that a library cannot suffice is absent entirely. The thinnest areas are implementation experience, prior art, and any concrete account of who would be affected or how standardization would coordinate with existing ecosystems.

- The strongest support is the established claim that C++ lacks key AI components and risks fragmentation without them.
- The paper repeatedly invokes competition with Python-driven JIT compilers, but that argument is never developed beyond assertion, so it does not establish affected users, prior art, or why the standard is the right vehicle.
- The cited DataFrame project is offered as similar prior art and implementation experience, but no analysis connects it to the proposed design or shows meaningful usage.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any argument for why a library would not be sufficient, which leaves the central standardization question unaddressed.
