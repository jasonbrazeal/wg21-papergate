Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a fragmentary case for standardization, with its strongest contribution confined to placing the discussion in the context of prior proposals and distinguishing two design framings. Beyond that, the document largely gestures at the problem rather than substantiating it: affected users, the need for a standard rather than a library solution, and interoperability concerns are not established.

- The paper is most convincing when it traces the pivot away from one-way `execute` through the published record and applies the two-framing distinction to that history.
- The claim that error handling is implementation-defined is asserted as significant, but the paper does not show who is concretely affected or how variation causes harm in practice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any demonstrated reason this must be standardized, rather than delivered as a library, or how it would coordinate with existing executor and coroutine facilities.
