Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s strongest foundation is its implementation experience, but much of the case for standardizing these utilities rests on repeated assertions rather than demonstrated need or demand. The discussion of affected users, benefits of standardization, and why existing library mechanisms are insufficient is especially thin, leaving the proposal more suggestive than persuasive.

- The proposal does credibly show that a workable implementation exists and has been explored publicly.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives offers some grounding by contrasting the proposed functions with locale-dependent facilities and Unicode-aware behavior.
- The paper asserts that ASCII handling is overwhelmingly common and that existing headers are inadequate, but it does not substantiate those claims with evidence or concrete user impact.
- The argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library is asserted largely by repetition, without a developed case for interoperability benefits or standardization-specific value.
