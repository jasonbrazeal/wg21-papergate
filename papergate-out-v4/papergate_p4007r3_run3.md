Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a partial case for standardization, built on asserted consequences and references rather than demonstrated need or experience. Its support is thinnest where the argument depends on claims about foreclosed design choices and unresolved coordination questions that are not substantiated in the text itself.

- The strongest support lies in the paper’s attempt to classify issues by whether shipping forecloses a fix, which at least frames the standardization risk as a live question.
- The discussion of prior art gestures toward relevant work like P4007R3 and P2583R4, but the credited passages do not show how alternatives were actually evaluated.
- The argument for why this belongs in the standard rests on claims about the two-tier split and coroutine allocation that are not backed by evidence within the paper.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or identification of the affected audience, leaving the practical stakes of standardization unestablished.
