Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a reasonable opening case by showing that the feature addresses a widespread and recognizable need, and by situating itself within identifiable prior work. The support is thinnest where the paper asserts rather than demonstrates that this need cannot be met outside the standard, and where it gestures at implementation experience without concrete evidence.

- The strongest support is the established account of prior art and alternatives, which shows the proposal is responding to a known design lineage rather than inventing the problem from scratch.
- The paper also establishes why the feature matters by pointing to frequent boilerplate and the loss of scoped-enum benefits when users work around the limitation.
- A notable weakness is that the affected-user claims rely on a single external example repeated in different forms, without demonstrating breadth or representativeness.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience, where the paper offers only a general claim about frequent boilerplate and no usable evidence that the proposed mechanism has been tried, validated, or adopted in practice.
