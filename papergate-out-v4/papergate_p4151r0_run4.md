Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only one solid pillar for its case: it can point to prior discussion and a prior interface change that connect the naming issue to the shape of the algorithm. Beyond that, the argument for standardization is almost entirely unsupported, with no meaningful account of who would be affected, why a standard is the right venue, how the change would inter operate with related work, or whether anyone has tried implementing it.

- The strongest support is the record of prior art and alternatives, including the Croydon discussion and the later unary redesign of `affine_on`.
- The rationale for the change is asserted rather than demonstrated, resting on the claimed mismatch between the name and the unary interface.
- The paper is silent on who would feel the impact of the change, leaving the affected audience unspecified.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, coordination, and any argument for why the standard library rather than a library-level solution is required.
