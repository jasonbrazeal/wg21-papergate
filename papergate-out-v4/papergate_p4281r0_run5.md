Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives a clear account of the readability problem it targets and points to external discussion as prior art, but it leaves most of the case for standardization essentially undeveloped. The strongest support is concentrated in the motivation and the acknowledgment of existing alternatives, while the argument becomes thin around the standard’s role, feasibility, and evidence of use.

- The paper establishes that repeated spelling of complex associated types makes constraints harder to read and more error-prone.
- It credits a prior hypothetical use case from Arthur O’Dwyer, showing the idea has some grounding in community discussion.
- It asserts, but does not establish, that the change is safe for the standard merely because `using` is currently invalid in that context.
- It offers no identification of affected users, no coordination or interoperability analysis, no reason a library cannot supply the convenience, and no implementation experience.
