Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for its core claim that the library change is feasible and has meaningful precedent, particularly through implementation experience and consideration of alternatives. The support becomes much thinner when it moves from “this can be done” to “this should be standardized,” because the case for why users need it, why a standard is required, and how it will work in practice rests largely on assertion rather than demonstrated evidence.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, which is described concretely enough to establish that the proposed approach has been tried in real codebases and compilers.
- The discussion of prior art and rejected alternatives is also well established, showing the design space was explored rather than skipped over.
- The case for who is affected and why it matters remains underdeveloped, with claims about user need and committee momentum not backed by substantive evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish why a library solution will not do, since the paper repeatedly leans on compiler optimization—an argument that could equally support a non-standard library facility.
