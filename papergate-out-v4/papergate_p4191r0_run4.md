Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a partial but largely asserted case for standardization, with most of its support concentrated in references to existing practice and prior discussion rather than demonstrated need or experience. The thinnest areas are the absence of a direct argument for why a standard facility is required and the lack of established evidence for implementation or interoperability claims.

- The strongest support is the paper’s appeal to existing practice, citing stdexec’s `__nothrow_connectable` and the prior discussion in P3388R3, though these remain claims rather than demonstrated adoption.
- The paper asserts that users must currently roll their own utility, but it does not establish that this burden is widespread or that a library solution would be inadequate.
- The case for coordination and interoperability rests on a single vendor’s internal mechanism, without showing broader community alignment or need across implementations.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no established argument for why the standard is the right place for this facility, leaving the central rationale for standardization unaddressed.
