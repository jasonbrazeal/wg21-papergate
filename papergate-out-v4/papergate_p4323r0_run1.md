Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, argumentative basis for its position, and it never demonstrates who would be affected, how implementation would work, or what coordination or library alternatives exist. The strongest material appears in its discussion of prior art and the claimed cost of competing styles, but most of that remains asserted rather than substantiated. The support is thinnest around the practical dimensions of standardization, where the paper is largely silent.

- The clearest support comes from the paper’s references to P2806R4 and Rust, which at least identify the feature being opposed and a language precedent.
- The argument that competing styles impose real costs on code bases is the paper’s most concrete reason for involving the standard, though it is not backed by evidence.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the proposal or what implementation experience exists.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of why a library solution would not suffice or how the change would interoperate with existing C++ features.
