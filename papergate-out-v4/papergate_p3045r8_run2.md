Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper offers substantial support for its standardization case, with especially strong evidence for the problem’s importance, the affected communities, prior art, and implementation experience. The support is thinnest around the claim that a library alone cannot suffice, where the argument is asserted more than demonstrated.

- The paper’s strongest support lies in its concrete, production-derived examples of unit-mixing bugs and the widespread existing use of the author’s library, which together make a compelling case that the problem is real and the approach is viable.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is well developed, showing awareness of competing libraries, Unicode constraints, and existing interoperability mechanisms rather than treating the proposal as the only possible path.
- The “why the standard” argument is credible, grounded in industry policies, onboarding costs, and the need for a shared vocabulary type, though some points about MISRA compliance and interoperability ease drift toward assertion.
- The most notable gap is the “why a library will not do” section, which repeats policy and usability concerns but does not establish why those cannot be addressed by a maintained, widely adopted library rather than a language standard.
