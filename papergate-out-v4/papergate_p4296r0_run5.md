Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers real support for the urgency of the problem and for its relationship to earlier work, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case largely unargued. The thinnest areas are implementation experience, the limits of non-standard solutions, and who specifically would be affected.

- The paper clearly establishes why invalidation is central to the safety profiles effort and why a single suppression can undermine guarantees for the whole program.
- It also situates itself credibly against P1179R1 and borrow-checker approaches, with enough prior-art discussion to show where its claimed advantages would lie.
- The case for why this must be standardized, rather than pursued as an implementation or tooling strategy, is asserted but not demonstrated.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no implementation experience and no account of why a library or other non-standard mechanism would be insufficient.
