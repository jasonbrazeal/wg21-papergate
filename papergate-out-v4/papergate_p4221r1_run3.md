Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper provides only a thin case for its own standardization, offering some asserted motivation and a gesture toward prior art but leaving most of the necessary justification undeveloped. The support is thinnest around the practical need for changes to the standard itself, the affected user community, and any evidence of implementation or real-world use.

- The paper’s strongest point is its stated motivation that dedicated member functions would express intent more clearly than a manual load-and-compare sequence.
- The discussion of alternatives is largely asserted rather than demonstrated, with little concrete comparison to existing practice.
- The paper offers no evidence about who would be affected by the proposed feature or what coordination with other standardization efforts might be needed.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or argument for why a library solution would be insufficient.
