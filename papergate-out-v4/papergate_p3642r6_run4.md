Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in a few concentrated areas, particularly around performance and prior art, but leaves several essential parts of the standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is in showing who is affected, and the arguments for why the standard—rather than a library—should address the problem remain largely underdeveloped.

- The strongest evidence is the benchmark showing a naive implementation performing over nine times worse than an optimized one, which grounds the implementation-experience claim in measurable data.
- The paper credibly ties its proposed operation to established prior work and notes relevant hardware support, though it does not fully develop the interoperability consequences.
- The most glaring omission is any concrete account of who would use the facility or be affected by its absence, leaving the audience for the proposal unspecified.
