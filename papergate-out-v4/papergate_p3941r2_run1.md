Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some background for why it is making a design change, but it does not build a full case that the change belongs in the standard. Its strongest material concerns prior art and alternatives, while the thinnest concerns basic questions about the affected users, the need for standardization, interoperability, and implementability.

- The paper clearly connects its proposed change to an earlier design and to the discontinued direction, so the prior-art context is present.
- The claim that the change matters rests mainly on internal discussion concerns, without showing why readers outside that discussion should care.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by standardizing this behavior.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why the standard, rather than a library, is the right place for the change.
