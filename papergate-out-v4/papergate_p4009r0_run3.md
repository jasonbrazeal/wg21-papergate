Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a reasonable foundation for why this work matters and what alternatives were considered, but it stops short of establishing several core requirements for standardization. The thinnest support concerns the actual need for a standard facility, its interoperability, feasibility as a library-only solution, and any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the explanation of the objections to the current draft and the motivation to address them without losing existing capabilities.
- The discussion of prior alternatives and their shortcomings credibly shows that this is not a proposal made in isolation.
- The paper claims the design is simpler and can serve affected audiences, but it does not establish that those audiences truly need a standardized facility rather than a non-standard configuration path.
- The most glaring omissions are the absence of any coordination or interoperability analysis and the lack of implementation experience to ground the proposal in practice.
