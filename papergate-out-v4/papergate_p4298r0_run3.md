Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a mixed foundation for its own standardization: it can point to real implementation experience, but much of its motivation and necessity rests on assertions rather than demonstrated community need or concrete technical necessity. The thinnest parts are the absence of established coordination with adjacent contract proposals and the largely unsupported claims about who is affected and why the standard is the right place for the change.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with the semantics implemented in GCC and Clang branches available for testing.
- The paper also earns credit for engaging with prior art and alternatives, particularly the interaction with other in-flight contract and assert proposals.
- The motivation for the feature and the breadth of its affected audience are asserted but not backed by evidence from users or domains.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of established coordination and interoperability with related standardization efforts, especially the other contracts facilities the paper itself references.
