Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only scattered assertions in support of its own standardization, leaving most of the necessary demonstration absent or undeveloped. Its strongest material concerns motivation and implementation experience, but even those remain claims rather than evidence, and several core questions about affected users, the role of the standard, and interoperability are simply unaddressed. The thinnest support is where the paper most needs to show why a library solution is insufficient and why the standard is the right venue.

- The most substantive support appears in snippets describing implementation experience, including a note that a libc++ implementation uncovered an oversight.
- The paper gestures at motivation with claims about overspecification, ambiguity, and unnecessary restrictions, but none of these are developed into an established need.
- It offers fragmentary references to prior art and alternatives, such as P3941 and existing practice in libstdc++, without establishing that these were considered or rejected.
- The paper gives no account of who is affected, why the standard is necessary, how the feature coordinates with existing specifications, or why a library would not suffice.
