Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper rests almost entirely on assertion and citation rather than demonstration, so it offers only a skeletal case for its own standardization. The thinnest areas are the lack of concrete evidence about affected users, prior alternatives, implementation maturity, and why existing mechanisms cannot suffice.

- The strongest support is a bare procedural observation that a future profile change would follow the same options as a change to a language or library feature, though even this is presented as a possibility rather than a demonstrated need.
- The paper gestures toward a framework as a remedy for scattered libraries, analyzers, compiler options, and guidelines, but never substantiates the claimed incompatibility or burden with examples or data.
- The treatment of implementation experience is especially weak: prototypes and experiments are named, but there is no description of what was learned, what worked, what failed, or how that experience validates the proposed direction.
- The most glaring omission is any real engagement with why a library-based or tooling-based approach cannot provide the needed functionality, leaving the central justification for standardization essentially asserted rather than argued.
