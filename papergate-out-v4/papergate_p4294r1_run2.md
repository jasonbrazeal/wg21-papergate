Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow patch of support for its own standardization, centered on a real gap and a concrete implementation. The case remains thin almost everywhere else: it does not identify who is affected, explain why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, or show coordination with existing practice. The most serious omissions are not technical details but missing justification for standardization itself.

- The strongest support is the demonstration that no direct suffix adaptor exists and that users must currently compose other operations.
- The implementation experience is concrete and linked, showing the proposed adaptors can be built in a standard library implementation.
- The discussion of prior art is present but underdeveloped, dismissing Python-style slicing without establishing a fuller range of alternatives.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of evidence about who is affected and why a library solution would be insufficient.
