Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers its most convincing support on the factual landscape: it documents a uniform deployed practice of termination or trapping on detected core-language violations, and it shows that continuing responses are treated by implementers as adoption aids rather than production defaults. The case is much thinner where it needs to move from that observation to a standardization requirement, since the paper repeatedly leans on the same deployment record to answer questions about affected users, interoperability, and the insufficiency of library solutions without adding the distinct evidence those questions call for.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, where the survey is explicitly described as covering every identifiable production-default hardening implementation and is backed by cited implementer statements about avoiding exception-handling costs and about continuation being undefined outside an adoption period.
- The prior-art discussion is also well grounded, showing the interaction with the existing Contracts framework, the undefined state left by a competing direction, and a documented objection to unconditional termination from Stroustrup.
- The thinnest part of the case is why a standard is needed as opposed to accepting existing deployed behavior, since the paper does not establish that deployments are unable to achieve their goals without a standard or that users face genuine portability or coordination costs today.
- The most glaring omission is the affected-user argument, which is asserted rather than demonstrated: the paper claims every relevant implementation and its users as the affected population but offers no evidence about who is harmed by the current absence of standardization or how that harm manifests in practice.
