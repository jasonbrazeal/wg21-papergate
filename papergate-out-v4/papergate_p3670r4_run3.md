Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only fragmentary support for its own standardization case, mostly pointing to adjacent work and an editorial assumption rather than building a self-contained argument. The support is thinnest around the basic questions of why the feature matters, who would use it, how it interoperates with existing practice, and whether it has been tried anywhere.

- The clearest support is the identification of prior and in-flight proposals, even though the relationship between them and this paper remains asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper claims to complete an existing design, but it does not establish what problem that completion solves for real users.
- The paper gives no implementation experience, no library-only alternative analysis, and no interoperability picture.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established motivation or affected audience for the proposed change.
