Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for its standardization: it can point to prior art and alternatives, but it does not establish who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the facility would coordinate with existing specifications, or why a library would be insufficient. The thinnest support lies in the absence of any audience or ecosystem argument, and the implementation claims are little more than a pointer to external work.

- The strongest support is the discussion of prior art, including the Boost Graph Library and the considered-but-rejected algorithms.
- The paper claims the algorithms matter for usefulness and efficiency, but it does not connect those claims to a concrete user or domain.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any argument for why standardization, rather than a standalone library, is necessary.
