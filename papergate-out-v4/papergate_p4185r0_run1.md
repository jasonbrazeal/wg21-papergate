Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers real evidence for its core technical distinctions and for the existence of implementation experience, but its case for standardization rests heavily on assertions about user demand, migration value, and the inadequacy of library-only solutions that are not backed up with the same concreteness. The strongest support is for the existence of a genuine design problem and for the feasibility of the proposed taxonomy, while the thin spots are mostly about why this must be in the standard and how it will fit with existing practice.

- The paper establishes most convincingly that the current two-abstraction model fails real engineering use cases and that the three-way split has independent theoretical and implementable backing.
- It also establishes that the reference implementation has accumulated enough practical experience to motivate the proposed changes.
- The paper merely claims, without demonstrating, that users broadly need this and that standardization is required for interoperability or correctness.
- The most glaring omission is any concrete evidence that a library cannot deliver the proposed benefits, especially since the reference implementation is cited as the source of the design in the first place.
