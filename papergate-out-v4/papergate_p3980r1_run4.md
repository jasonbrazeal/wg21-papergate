Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some historical and procedural grounding for its proposed change, but it does not make a connected case that standardization is necessary or that the affected community would benefit in a clearly understood way. The thinnest parts are the absence of any account of who is affected and the lack of a reason the standard, rather than a library or implementation convention, must address the issue.

- The strongest support comes from documented discussion and preference for one wording approach at a recent LEWG meeting, which at least shows the question has been considered in the committee context.
- The paper describes the status quo and competing options, so its framing of the design space is reasonably clear even where the motivation is thin.
- The case for why this matters rests mainly on assertions about allocator flexibility and national-body comments, without tying those claims to user-facing costs or concrete scenarios.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of an explanation of who would be affected by the change, alongside silence on implementation experience, coordination, and why a library solution would not suffice.
