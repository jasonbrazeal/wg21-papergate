Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives a partial account of why a graph facility might belong in the standard, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case—especially the case against a library, the affected audience, and interoperability—effectively unargued. The strongest support is conceptual and terminological, while the institutional and practical justifications for standardizing this work are the thinnest.

- The proposal does establish that graph representations raise real ambiguities and that common terminology and relationships to sparse matrices give the topic meaningful conceptual weight.
- It also credibly situates the work against prior art such as CLRS terminology, adjacency-list alternatives, and existing library operations like `adjacent_vertices`.
- Its claim about needing the standard rests mainly on a fairly abstract distinction between graphs and their representations, without showing why that distinction cannot be handled outside the standard.
- The paper offers no established account of who is affected, how the facility would coordinate or interoperate with existing practice, why a library is insufficient, or whether the proposed design has meaningful implementation experience.
