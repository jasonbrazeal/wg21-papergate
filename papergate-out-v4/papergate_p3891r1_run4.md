Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some support for the readability problem it addresses, but much of the case for standardization remains unstated rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the nearly complete absence of discussion about affected users, implementation experience, standardization necessity, and coordination.

- The strongest support is the identification of a real editorial burden in the current grammar, where the lack of grouping and repetition causes bloat and harms readability for users including those relying on assistive technology.
- The paper asserts that the proposed notation would simplify the specification and that alternatives like existing syntactic devices have shortcomings, but these claims are not substantiated with evidence from prior practice or detailed comparison.
- The proposal does not establish why this change requires standardization rather than being handled as an editorial convention or tooling improvement outside the standard.
- Most notably, it offers no implementation experience, no account of who would be concretely affected, and no discussion of coordination with existing standard wording or external grammars.
