Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper leans heavily on assertion and external context rather than building its own case, leaving most of the burden of justification to unnamed or loosely summarized sources. The thinnest areas are the fundamental questions of why the feature matters and why a standard is required at all, since those are never developed into an argument the reader can evaluate.

- The strongest support comes from pointing to existing GCC and Clang forks as implementation experience, though even that is more a claim about what the author could cite than a demonstration the committee can assess.
- The paper names competing proposals and some published concerns, which at least shows the topic is contested, but it does not show that those alternatives were fairly compared or that this design emerged as the better path.
- The claim of affected users rests on the author’s committee role and a rejected poll, neither of which establishes who actually needs the feature or how they are impacted.
- Most glaringly, the paper does not explain why standardization matters, why a library cannot solve the problem, or how the feature would coordinate with existing standard components.
