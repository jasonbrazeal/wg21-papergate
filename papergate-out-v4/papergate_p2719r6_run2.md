Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper lays a plausible foundation for why type-aware allocation and deallocation would be useful, but it leaves most of its procedural and evidentiary case asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the motivation and the description of the problem, while the supporting evidence for usage, implementability, and necessity of standardization remains largely unproven.

- The paper does establish a coherent need by explaining how type-specific allocation enables flexibility without intrusive class-scoped operators and avoids heavy macro use.
- It gestures at practical use and prior experience, but does not provide the evidence needed to show that the feature is already used or implementable in real systems.
- The discussion of why a library solution cannot suffice is asserted mainly through limitations of intrusive mechanisms, without fully ruling out other non-standard approaches.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established implementation experience or coordination evidence, leaving the standardization claim dependent on unverified assertions about real-world demand and feasibility.
