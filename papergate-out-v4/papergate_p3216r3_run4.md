Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers concrete support for the ergonomic motivation and the feasibility of a standard slice view, but its case thins considerably when it turns to the need for standardization specifically, since the same passages are asked to carry claims about standardization, coordination, and why a library would not suffice.

- The clearest strength is the demonstrated implementation experience in libstdc++, which shows the design is workable in practice.
- The discussion of range-v3 prior art gives useful context on existing behavior and boundary-checking differences.
- The rationale for why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library is asserted mostly through a single statement about ergonomics and intent, without a developed argument for standardization need.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the absence of a standard slice view, leaving the affected-user case entirely unaddressed.
