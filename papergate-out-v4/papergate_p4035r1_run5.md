Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper makes a genuine case on the narrowest point: there is real, shipped implementation experience for the exact pattern it wants to standardize. Beyond that, the support is mostly asserted rather than shown, with demand, prior art, and interoperability all gestured at through a single GitHub count or broad claims that never get tied to concrete evidence. The thinnest part is the argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than remaining a widely used library pattern.

- The strongest support is the Boost.URL implementation experience, which shows a working validating default and an explicit unsafe escape hatch.
- The paper claims over 2,100 independent GitHub implementations, but does not establish what that number represents or how it demonstrates affected users.
- The case for standardization over a library relies on broad design-value language rather than concrete standardization need.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established evidence connecting the cited prior art and demand to a problem that only the standard can solve.
