Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some context for why a general statement of direction might matter to the committee, but it builds very little affirmative case for standardizing the particular thing it proposes. The strongest support concerns motivation and perceived committee dysfunction, while the case thins out almost entirely around the question of why the standard is the right vehicle or how the proposed work would fit with existing practice.

- The paper does establish that the committee’s lack of shared design philosophy and poor visibility into proposals are live problems worth addressing.
- It asserts, but does not substantiate, that ordinary programmers are affected by the absence of the proposed direction.
- It names some prior documents and examples, but does not connect them to a demonstrated gap that this work would fill.
- The paper offers no establishment of why the standard should change, how the change would coordinate with existing facilities, why a library or non-normative guidance would not suffice, or what implementation experience supports it.
