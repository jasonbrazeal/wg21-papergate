Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides solid support on a narrow technical point, especially through concrete implementation evidence and a clear articulation of why the current preconditions are gratuitous, but it leaves the broader case for standardization largely implicit. The thinnest areas are the absence of any account of affected users and the lack of a demonstration that the change cannot be achieved outside the standard.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, backed by a Godbolt example showing existing implementations already produce the correct result.
- The paper clearly establishes why the issue matters by arguing the preconditions offer no optimization benefit and only introduce undefined behavior.
- Prior art, the need for a standard change, and coordination with related algorithms are mentioned but not developed into persuasive justification.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to identify who is affected by the current preconditions or who would benefit from their removal.
