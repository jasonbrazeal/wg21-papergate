Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in a few foundational areas, particularly in showing that standardized SI definitions would aid interoperability and that the design reflects established international specifications and prior library work. However, the case thins considerably where it needs to explain who is concretely affected, why a non-standard library cannot suffice, and whether the proposed content has been validated through implementation experience beyond the originating project.

- The strongest support is the paper’s grounding in recognized prior art, including ISQ, the SI Brochure, and the companion quantities-and-units proposal.
- The paper also clearly motivates the practical value of standard definitions for library interoperability and usable, human-friendly quantity expressions.
- The thinnest support concerns who is affected, since the document offers no concrete account of the user population or its needs.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of established implementation experience, with only references back to mp-units and assertions about usefulness rather than demonstrated practice.
