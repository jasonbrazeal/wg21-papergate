Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s case for standardization rests almost entirely on unverified assertions, with every element of need marked as claimed rather than established. Its strongest material is a gesture toward existing practice in other languages and a cited ISO function, but the central questions of user impact, standard-library placement, and why existing alternatives fall short are asserted without supporting evidence or analysis.

- The paper at least names an existing ISO/IEC specification for `isqrt`, offering a plausible interoperability anchor if the claim were substantiated.
- The discussion of floating-point width limitations is the closest thing to a reason a library function is insufficient, though it remains asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is any concrete evidence of user need beyond a vague reference to StackOverflow questions, leaving both the affected population and the urgency of standardization unestablished.
