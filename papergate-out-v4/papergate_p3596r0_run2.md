Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization: most of its arguments are gestures toward usefulness or citations of prior discussion rather than demonstrations of need, and the thinnest areas are the absence of an affected audience, a library alternative, or any account of who would rely on the standard rather than an external catalog. The strongest concrete support is the existence of collaboratively developed draft wording.

- The paper shows implementation experience through its collaboratively developed branch of the C++ working draft.
- The proposal cites earlier attempts and related papers, though it does not establish how this attempt improves enough on those alternatives to justify standardization.
- The paper asserts that stable names would be useful common vocabulary, but it does not show that this vocabulary requires a place in the standard.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the problem or why a non-standard catalog or library would be insufficient.
