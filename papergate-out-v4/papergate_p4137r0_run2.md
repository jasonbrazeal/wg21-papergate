Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper rests its strongest case on the existence of a measurable gap between what the profile promises and what it can verify, but it leaves the central empirical question unanswered: how much real code actually falls into that gap. The discussion of prior work is concrete and useful, while the arguments about affected users, standardization need, and implementability are more asserted than demonstrated.

- The clearest support comes from the structural false positive category, which gives the committee a concrete way to understand the distance between promise and delivery.
- The paper is also on firm ground when it situates its methodology against existing lifetime and invalidation work.
- The thinnest support appears where the paper says the tools exist and a few hundred lines suffice, since no working implementation or real-codebase measurement is shown.
- The most glaring omission is any treatment of coordination and interoperability, which the paper does not address at all.
