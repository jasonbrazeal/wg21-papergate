Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful groundwork on the conceptual problem and prior attempts, but its standardization case remains thin where it matters most: demonstrating who is concretely affected, why a normative or even non-normative standard change is the right remedy, and how the proposal fits with existing practice. The strongest support is historical and explanatory, while the weakest areas involve real-world impact and coordination.

- The paper clearly establishes why out-of-thin-air behavior matters and that existing approaches have failed to gain traction among implementers.
- It also establishes that there is relevant prior art and that alternative models have been explored, including a cited decision procedure.
- The paper does not establish who is affected in practice, since the claim of no known real-world OOTA instances is offered without sufficient evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of coordination and interoperability discussion, leaving the proposal’s relationship to existing implementations and users unspecified.
