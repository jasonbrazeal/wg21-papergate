Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a partial foundation for its standardization case, strongest when arguing that current contract enforcement leaves an unmet threat of uncertainty, but thin in the areas that usually decide whether a feature is ready for the standard: affected users, implementability, and whether a library solution could suffice.

- The clearest support is the argument that C++26 contracts leave function authors without reliable assurance that contract conditions will be enforced.
- The paper gestures at prior last-minute proposals and interoperability hazards, but does not substantiate them as alternatives or coordination requirements.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or evidence that the proposed mechanism can be built and used in practice.
- The paper also never establishes who is affected or why the need cannot be met by a library, leaving the standardization rationale incomplete.
