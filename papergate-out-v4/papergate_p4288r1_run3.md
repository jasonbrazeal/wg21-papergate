Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow but genuine foundation for its standardization case, chiefly by demonstrating that reference completions matter and that the proposal has some connection to prior art and an existing implementation. Beyond that, the support becomes much thinner: the affected audience, coordination story, and evidence of real implementation experience are effectively absent, and the arguments about why this belongs in the standard rather than a library are asserted more than shown.

- The strongest support is the motivation for reference completions in asynchronous functions and the identification of a real design gap left by the removal of the earlier reference-completing algorithm.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives credibly situates the proposal against earlier standardized behavior and a reference implementation.
- The weakest presented reasoning is the claim that the facility must be standardized because a library version cannot preserve completion semantics, which is asserted without enough demonstration.
- The most glaring omission is any account of who is affected or how the proposal would coordinate with existing practice, leaving the practical need largely unexamined.
