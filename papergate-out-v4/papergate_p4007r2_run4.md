Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin evidentiary basis for its own standardization case, with most of its support resting on assertions rather than demonstrated need. The strongest material concerns alternatives and prior work, but even there the claims are largely credited as unestablished, while entire dimensions such as affected users, coordination, and library feasibility are effectively unaddressed.

- The clearest, though still unproven, support appears in the discussion of prior art and alternatives, where the paper names related efforts and design tensions it says were resolved or complemented elsewhere.
- The paper’s claim that coroutine-native I/O and `std::execution` occupy complementary domains is asserted but not backed by the kind of evidence that would establish why standardization matters.
- The connection to implementation experience is weak, with only a passing statement that one prior system resolved several issues.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for who is affected, why a library would be insufficient, or how the proposal coordinates with existing standards and practice.
