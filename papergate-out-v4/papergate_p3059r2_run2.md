Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers a modest but uneven case for standardization, anchored by a clear motivation and a plausible argument that the exposed constructors are not useful to users. Beyond that, however, much of the support rests on informal assurance, unpublished discussion, or analogy rather than direct evidence. The thinnest areas are those that would normally reassure the committee about practical viability: coordination, library-level alternatives, and concrete implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the demonstrated inconsistency in current constructor exposure, which gives the proposal a concrete and standards-relevant problem to solve.
- The paper cites implementer sentiment and a favorable mailing-list reaction, but records only assertions of low breakage risk rather than evidence from users or field testing.
- The comparison to P2711 suggests prior art, but the paper does not establish how closely that change’s circumstances and consequences apply here.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of coordination and interoperability with other parts of the ecosystem or with existing range-based code.
