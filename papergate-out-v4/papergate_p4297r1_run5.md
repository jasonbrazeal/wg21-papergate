Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s case for standardization is uneven: its framing of the procedural and architectural stakes is credible, but it leans heavily on assertions about deployment, interoperability, and the insufficiency of library-only approaches that are not backed by independent evidence within the document itself. The strongest material concerns why the layering question matters and what alternatives exist, while the weakest concerns whether the proposed split is actually required for the standard and whether the affected practice is as broad as claimed.

- The paper establishes that the layering between implicit contract assertions and Profiles is a substantive, contested design question with real procedural consequences.
- It also shows that the question sits among published, competing prior work and that no single prior poll adopted the architecture at issue.
- Its claims about who is affected and about implementation experience rest largely on uncited or internally repeated deployment figures rather than independently verifiable evidence.
- Most notably, the paper does not establish why the desired separation cannot be handled outside the standard or why the interoperability problems it identifies require its specific ballot before other wording proceeds.
