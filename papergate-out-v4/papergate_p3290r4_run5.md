Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers credible support for the underlying motivation and for some of the interoperability story, but it leaves central questions about affected users and whether a library-only solution would suffice largely unanswered. The thinnest parts concern the case for standardization specifically, since the paper argues utility without quite showing why that utility must be provided through the Standard rather than through existing implementation and library mechanisms.

- The strongest support is for prior art and alternatives, where the paper identifies related facilities and earlier naming discussions and shows the proposal has been considered by the relevant study groups.
- The paper also establishes coordination and interoperability reasonably well, particularly through its account of how legacy assertion facilities could coexist with C++ contracts without forced semantic changes.
- The case for why the Standard should act is present but underdeveloped, relying on assertions about centralization and migration without demonstrating that standardization is the necessary mechanism.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established description of who is affected, leaving the audience and the concrete pain points addressed by the proposal unclear.
