Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper leaves most of the case for standardization unproven, resting heavily on broad assertions about user need and failure without evidence, and offering no serious analysis of alternatives or interoperability. Its support is thinnest in the areas that would distinguish a standards-track feature from a well-known library routine.

- The one solid piece of support is the existence of a full implementation in the `eisenwave/integer-division` repository.
- The paper claims users need this constantly and that most attempts are wrong, but it does not establish either the frequency or the failure rate.
- The argument against libraries is asserted rather than demonstrated: a single division function being “silly” to pull in is not by itself a reason the language must have it.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of prior art and alternatives, leaving the reader with no basis for judging whether existing practice, other languages, or library designs already address the need adequately.
