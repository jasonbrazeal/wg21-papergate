Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow slice of the case for its own standardization, centered on a real but limited observation that `std::execution`’s wording strategy creates a specification gap. Beyond that, the support is thin: several essential questions about audience, alternatives, standardization need, interoperability, library feasibility, and implementation experience are left unaddressed or only gestured at.

- The strongest element is the established point that the proposal responds to a genuine specification problem in how receivers and operation states are described.
- The paper gestures at prior art and alternatives, but does not actually connect them to a demonstrated need for standardization.
- The discussion of why this belongs in the standard rests entirely on one quoted complaint about the existing wording strategy, without broader argument.
- The most glaring omissions are the absence of any affected audience, coordination story, reason a library would not suffice, or implementation experience.
