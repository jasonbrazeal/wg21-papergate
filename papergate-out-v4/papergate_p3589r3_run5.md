Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin, largely asserted case for standardization, with nearly every required justification resting on the same general statement about fragmentation rather than on concrete evidence. The support is thinnest around implementation experience and prior art, where the paper gestures toward existing practice or related work without demonstrating how either validates the need for a new standard.

- The strongest support appears in the brief reference to experience with `[[gsl::suppress]]`, which at least names a real deployment history, though even this is not developed into evidence for the framework itself.
- The repeated argument from supplier divergence is the paper’s main attempt to justify the standard, but it remains a generalized claim rather than a demonstrated problem with examples or documented incompatibilities.
- The treatment of prior art is especially weak because disclaiming competition with another proposal does not show what alternatives were examined or why they cannot serve the need.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no developed argument for why a library or existing tooling approach would be insufficient, leaving the boundary between language-level standardization and implementation-level support entirely unexamined.
