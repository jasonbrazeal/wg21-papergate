Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only a thin foundation for standardization, resting on a single well-articulated observation about the current uncoordinated landscape of C++ safety proposals. Most of the necessary supporting argument is asserted rather than demonstrated, and several essential elements—prior art, alternatives, and any evidence of implementation progress—are effectively absent from the case.

- The strongest support is the paper’s recognition that uncoordinated safety proposals continue to arrive without reference to Profiles, which at least situates the need for some coordinating mechanism.
- The paper gestures toward the burden of tool-chain-specific boilerplate, but it never shows concretely why a standard interface, rather than a library or convention, is required.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of prior art or alternatives, leaving the proposal without a baseline against which its standardization need can be judged.
