Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some useful conceptual groundwork by engaging with prior art and proposing terminology, but it does not make a substantive case for standardization. The discussion of why the distinction matters is present but remains more aspirational than demonstrated, and the absence of any account of affected users, implementation experience, or why a library solution would be inadequate leaves the standardization need largely unargued.

- The strongest element is the engagement with prior work, particularly N3248, which grounds the proposal in an existing discussion and shows the terminology would clarify rather than invent.
- The paper makes a claimed, though not established, case that the distinction matters for common language and bug detection, but it lacks concrete examples or demonstrated consequences.
- There is no identification of who would be affected by adopting this terminology in the standard.
- Most glaringly, the paper never explains why the standard is the right venue rather than a library, a guideline, or simply shared vocabulary outside the standard, and it offers no implementation or usage experience.
