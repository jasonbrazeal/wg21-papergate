Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers some concrete evidence that the design pattern has been implemented successfully, but most of the case for standardization rests on assertions and references rather than demonstrated need across the affected community. The thinnest support appears where the paper argues that only standardization—rather than continued use as a library facility—can solve the problem or coordinate the relevant ecosystem.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience, since the Boost.URL pattern with a validating default and an explicit unsafe escape hatch has been shipped and used for years.
- The paper claims broad demand based on GitHub implementations and production convergence, but does not establish who is concretely affected or how that demand translates into a standardization need.
- The most glaring omission is a sustained argument for why a library cannot remain sufficient, especially given that the cited implementation experience is itself a library rather than a standard facility.
