Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, directional statement of intent rather than a developed case for standardization, and most of the burden of justification is simply not taken up by the text. The thinnest areas are the absence of any account of the affected constituency, the need for a standard-language mechanism as opposed to other approaches, and any evidence from implementation or use.

- The strongest support is the repeated indication that CWG wants to unify treatment of several constructs to improve consistency of the language and specification.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the proposed definitional change or why their needs require standardization.
- The paper does not explain why the standard is the right venue, nor why a library or other non-standard solution would not do.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience or any concrete evidence that the proposed treatment is workable in practice.
