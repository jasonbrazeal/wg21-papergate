Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers genuine support for its standardization case in the areas of motivation, prior art, and implementation experience, but it leaves the central questions of standardization necessity largely unanswered. The thinnest support is around why this work belongs in the standard rather than in an external library or process document, and around what coordination or interoperability problems standardization would actually solve.

- The paper convincingly establishes why capturing tacit committee judgment matters and shows real implementation experience through the SAGE framework and its self-evaluation against P4003R0.
- The prior-art discussion is solid, drawing on P4023R0 and historical examples like `auto_ptr` and `async()` to ground the need for documented principles.
- The paper only claims, without establishing, who is concretely affected beyond a passing mention that the two libraries are in active use.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why a library or external process cannot suffice, nor what coordination and interoperability failures justify putting this into the standard itself.
