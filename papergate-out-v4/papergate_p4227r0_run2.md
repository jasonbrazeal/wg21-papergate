Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers little concrete support for its own standardization, repeatedly gesturing at motivations and comparisons without substantiating the need, the affected users, or the viability of the proposed design choices. The thinnest part is the absence of any established audience or implementation evidence, leaving the proposal’s relevance and urgency largely asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest support is the paper’s engagement with prior art, though even that remains a claim about design differences rather than an established case for standardization.
- The paper frames a rationale around interaction with C-style APIs, but this is asserted rather than shown to require a standardized type.
- The lack of any established affected users leaves the proposal without a clear constituency or demonstrated demand.
- Most glaringly, the paper fails to establish what standardization would add over existing library types or what problem only a standard type could solve.
