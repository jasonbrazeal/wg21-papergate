Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives the impression of a focused engineering audit, but it does not carry much of the burden of justifying its own standardization. The strongest material concerns concrete checking opportunities, while the arguments about necessity, placement in the standard, and viability outside the standard are largely absent.

- The paper’s most concrete support is its claim that the listed checks were observed to produce out-of-bounds reads or writes in at least one major implementation.
- It situates itself as a follow-up to prior hardening proposals, though the relationship and what specifically was left unaddressed are only loosely explained.
- The discussion of who benefits is thin and partially undercuts itself by acknowledging that some of the hardened functions are not widely used.
- The paper does not establish why these checks belong in the standard rather than in an implementation or a library, nor does it address coordination with existing hardening modes or deployment experience.
