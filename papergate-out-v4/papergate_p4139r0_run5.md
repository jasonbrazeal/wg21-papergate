Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a fragmentary case for standardization, doing little more than situating its naming question against one prior proposal and signaling that the status quo is unsatisfying. The support is thinnest where a standards document needs to be concrete: there is no account of affected users, no interoperability or implementation analysis, and no explanation of why existing library mechanisms cannot address the need.

- The strongest support is the acknowledgment that the current `get` is opaque and that prior alternative names failed to gain consensus, which at least frames the problem space.
- The discussion of P3091 offers some prior-art context, though it only reports that earlier options were rejected without establishing what was learned.
- The paper makes no attempt to show who is affected or why the standard library, rather than a user-level library, is the right place for the change.
- Most glaringly, it offers no implementation experience or coordination analysis, leaving the practical and committee-facing consequences entirely unexamined.
