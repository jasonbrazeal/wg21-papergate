Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some support for the readability and expressiveness problems it wants to solve, but it does little to establish that the missing grammar features need to be part of the standard itself rather than handled editorially or outside the normative document. The strongest material concerns the motivation, while the rest of the standardization case remains largely unstated.

- The paper established that grouping and repetition would address real grammar readability and conciseness problems for readers and users of the specification.
- The paper claims but does not establish that the proposed syntax is the right alternative or that it would actually simplify the grammar as broadly as suggested.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the current grammar’s limitations beyond a general nod to assistive technology users.
- The paper leaves unexamined why this change belongs in the standard, how it coordinates with other specification work, why an editorial or library approach would not suffice, or what implementation experience supports the proposal.
