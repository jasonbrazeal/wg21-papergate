Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only scattered, mostly anecdotal support for its own standardization, with several foundational questions left entirely unaddressed. The case is thinnest around who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and whether the work belongs in the standard at all.

- The strongest support is a handful of specific observations about existing practice or related proposals, but even these are asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper does not establish who would benefit from the change or what problem it solves for real users.
- It offers no account of why standardization is necessary rather than a library solution, and no evidence of coordination or interoperability concerns.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience beyond brief, unsupported claims about existing practice.
