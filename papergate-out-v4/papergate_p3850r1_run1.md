Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some contextual support for the need to continue developing contract assertions, but it largely assumes rather than demonstrates the specific standardization case for the extension it describes. The strongest support is a general statement that C++26 contracts are a minimal viable product and that extensions are needed soon, but the document is thin on evidence about affected users, alternatives, interoperability, implementation experience, and why standardization—rather than another mechanism—is required.

- The paper does establish that contract assertions are incomplete and that extensions are broadly needed, which gives the proposal a legitimate starting point.
- It gestures toward affected users and prior art by referring to a “frequently requested extension” and naming related papers, but does not substantiate those claims with concrete evidence or analysis.
- The paper offers essentially no case for why the standard is the right venue, or why a library or other non-standard solution would be inadequate.
- It similarly provides no established implementation experience or interoperability evidence beyond a single unelaborated mention of a GCC implementation.
