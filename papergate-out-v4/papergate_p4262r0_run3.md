Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers meaningful grounding for why class invariants deserve attention and shows familiarity with prior art, but it does not yet make the case that this work belongs in the C++ standard. Several of the central burdens—especially interaction with the standard, interoperability, and why a library is insufficient—are essentially unaddressed.

- The strongest material establishes that class invariants are a natural and frequently requested extension whose design questions are well-motivated by existing practice and prior language experience.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is credible, particularly in showing that different languages have faced the same core challenges and made divergent choices.
- The claim that many users are affected is repeated but never substantiated with evidence from usage, surveys, or concrete demand.
- The paper is thinnest on why the standard is the right venue, leaving coordination, interoperability, and the insufficiency of a library solution entirely unsupported.
