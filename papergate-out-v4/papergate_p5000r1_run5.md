Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper provides only a loose rationale for standardization, mainly gesturing toward current safety discussions and the hope that its priorities will aid scheduling, but it does almost nothing to ground those ambitions in the affected community, existing practice, or the unique role of the standard. The support is thinnest wherever the proposal should show why a standard is needed at all: there is no account of users, implementation experience, interoperability, or why non-standard guidance would be insufficient.

- The clearest, though still limited, support is the paper’s connection of its goals to ongoing safety debates and named related proposals.
- The discussion of alternatives is mostly a pointer to other documents rather than a comparison that shows this paper’s own necessity.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the proposed priorities or how they would interact with existing code, tooling, or committee work.
- The most glaring omission is any demonstration that the goals belong in the C++ standard, as opposed to being handled through library work, guidance, or ordinary committee planning.
