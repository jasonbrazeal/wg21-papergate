Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper provides only a thin case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on assertions about wording strategy and consequences that are quoted from or tied to P3425 rather than independently demonstrated. The support is thinnest where the proposal should show who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and that the approach has been tried in practice.

- The strongest support is a repeated claim that the proposed wording strategy would make standard-mandated consequences and future refactoring of `std::execution` mechanically burdensome, though even this is asserted rather than shown.
- The treatment of prior art consists mostly of attributing findings and changes to P3425, without establishing how those findings validate this paper’s specific standardization need.
- The paper does not identify any affected users or use cases, leaving the practical relevance of the change unstated.
- There is no evidence of implementation experience, no discussion of coordination or interoperability, and no argument that a library-level solution cannot achieve the same result.
