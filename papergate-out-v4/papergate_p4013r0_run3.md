Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers real but uneven support for its own standardization, with concrete implementation experience and acknowledgment of prior art doing most of the work, while the rationale for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library remains largely asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest parts are the justifications for user impact, standardizing specifically, and interoperability, all of which lean on brief claims without enough surrounding argument to establish them.

- The strongest support is the visible implementation on a public branch and compiler explorer, which grounds the proposal in working code rather than speculation.
- The discussion of alternatives such as templates, virtual tables, and pointer tagging is credited as establishing prior art and tradeoffs, giving the proposal a useful context.
- The paper’s most glaring omission is the absence of any established case for why a library solution cannot achieve the same effect.
