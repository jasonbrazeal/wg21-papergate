Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly anecdotal basis for its own standardization discussion, with every relevant point asserted rather than demonstrated. The support is thinnest where the document leans on expectations about limited usage, vague references to implementation divergence, and assumptions about ABI impact without concrete evidence or examples tying them to a need for standard action.

- The strongest material is the acknowledgment that attributes are not part of a type, which at least names a real language-level friction the paper is exploring.
- The claim that current implementations diverge in their attribute systems gestures toward prior art, but it is not developed enough to show why that divergence matters here.
- The paper does not establish who would actually use the proposed attributes or why their expected narrow use still justifies standardization.
- The repeated conclusion that the committee should recommend “no change” leaves the standardization rationale largely unargued, since the paper itself stops short of showing a compelling need.
