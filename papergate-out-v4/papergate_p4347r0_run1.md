Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a very thin case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on unsupported assertions about implementer interest and hypothetical implementation choices. It says almost nothing about who would be concretely affected, what prior alternatives were seriously considered, or why existing mechanisms or library solutions would be insufficient. The most serious gaps are the complete absence of a rationale for standardization as such, and the lack of any implementation experience or interoperability analysis.

- The strongest support is the paper’s vague appeal to expressed curiosity among multiple people, though it never identifies them or their needs.
- The discussion of P2900 and P3100 hints at conceivable implementation configurations, but these scenarios are offered as possibilities rather than demonstrated practice.
- The paper never establishes why standardization, rather than a library or implementation-specific feature, is necessary.
- Most glaringly, there is no implementation experience, no coordination story, and no argument that the standard itself needs to change for the proposed behavior to exist.
