Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow foundation for standardization: it clearly establishes prior art and some design continuity, but much of the case for necessity, user impact, and implementation readiness rests on assertion rather than demonstrated evidence. The thinnest support is around why a library solution is insufficient, which is not addressed at all, leaving a central justification for language change absent.

- The strongest support comes from the alignment with existing reflection directions and prior committee feedback, which grounds the proposal in ongoing work.
- The claim of implementation experience is only weakly supported by references to an experimental implementation and compiler explorer availability, without substantive detail on completeness or lessons learned.
- The paper does little to demonstrate who is actually affected or why the problem is broadly significant beyond general statements about attribute usage.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any argument for why a library approach cannot solve the described problem, which leaves the need for standardization itself undefended.
