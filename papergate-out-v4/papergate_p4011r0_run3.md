Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers thin support for its own standardization, with most of the necessary case left implicit or unaddressed. The strongest material appears in the discussion of why the distinction matters conceptually and in the brief engagement with prior art, but even those sections stop short of connecting the ideas to a concrete standardization need. The remaining questions—who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, how the feature interoperates, what implementation experience exists, and why a library cannot suffice—are essentially unanswered.

- The paper at least gestures toward a motivating conceptual problem by contrasting contract enforcement, bug detection, and undefined behavior.
- Its treatment of prior art mentions N3248 and shows an example encoding, though it does not establish how those alternatives fall short.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the proposed direction or what practical problem they face.
- It offers no evidence that standardization is necessary rather than addressable through a library, tooling, or shared terminology outside the standard.
