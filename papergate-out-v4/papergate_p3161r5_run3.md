Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is largely asserted rather than demonstrated, with only the existence of a reference implementation standing as concrete evidence. Much of the argument rests on general claims about inefficiency and portability burdens that are never tied to specific users, workloads, or measured costs, leaving the case thin where it needs to be most persuasive.

- The strongest element is the availability of a reference implementation on GitHub, which at least shows the feature can be realized in practice.
- The paper repeatedly claims that a portable third-party library would be cumbersome or impossible, but offers no direct evidence or comparison to substantiate that.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives acknowledges saturation arithmetic but does not establish why the broader facility proposed is the right next step for the standard.
- Most notably, the paper never identifies who is affected or what real-world need would go unmet without standardization.
