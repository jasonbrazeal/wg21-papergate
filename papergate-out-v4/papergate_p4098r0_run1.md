Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some suggestive context and a few concrete deployment anecdotes, but it does not assemble them into a clear affirmative case for standardization. The support is thinnest precisely where a proposal usually needs to be most direct: showing that the problem belongs in the standard, that a library cannot suffice, and that the proposed direction has been validated by implementation experience.

- The strongest material is the documented deployment of sender/receiver composition and infrastructure, which shows at least one real-world base for parts of the design space.
- The paper gestures toward broad impact and committee consensus, but those claims are asserted rather than substantiated with surveys or direct evidence.
- The discussion of coordination with existing work is mostly a claim about one group’s prior position, without showing a live interoperability story.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established argument for why this needs to be in the C++ standard rather than delivered as a library.
