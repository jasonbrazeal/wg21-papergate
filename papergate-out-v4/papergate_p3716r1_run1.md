Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers partial support for its motivations and points to some existing practice, but it does not yet make a case that standardization is the necessary or appropriate response. The thinnest areas are the ones most specific to standardization: why a standard is needed, what coordination or interoperability would result, and why a library or non-standard mechanism would not suffice.

- The strongest support is the claim of implementation experience through IAR’s “Embedded C++” mode, which suggests the general idea has been tried in practice.
- The paper gestures toward relevant prior art and alternatives, such as P3589, Profiles, and Annex K, but does not establish how the proposal improves on them.
- The discussion of affected users is largely asserted, relying on a small, decade-old sample and broad statements about demand for a “without-C” subset.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of an argument for standardization itself, including interoperability, coordination, and why a library cannot address the need.
