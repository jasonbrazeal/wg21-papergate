Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers almost no affirmative case for why the proposed process change should itself be standardized. Its support is concentrated in a brief, argumentative rejection of schedule flexibility, while the surrounding questions of need, audience, prior work, and implementation are left essentially unaddressed.

- The clearest, though still undeveloped, support is the paper’s insistence that mixing feature-driven and time-driven release models would simply recreate the status quo it hopes to avoid.
- The paper gestures at prior art by invoking a “model since 2012,” but it does not establish what that model is, where it is documented, or how the proposal compares to alternatives.
- The paper never identifies who is concretely affected by release scheduling, which makes the motivating problem feel abstract and detached from the committee’s actual work.
- Most glaringly, the paper gives no reason why the intended policy belongs in the standard rather than in WG21’s own procedures or standing documents, and it offers no implementation or trial experience to ground the proposal.
