Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers consistent but largely unverified support for its standardization case. Its most concrete contribution is the discussion of prior art and the trade-off that motivates the proposed bridge, but the remaining claims are asserted from the author’s implementation and domain experience without independent corroboration in the paper itself.

- The strongest support is the account of prior art and alternatives, which clearly situates the proposal against existing sender and awaitable designs.
- The claimed implementation experience is repeated in several places, but the paper does not show enough beyond the author’s own projects to establish broader viability.
- Why this belongs in the standard rather than a library is asserted mainly through cost and composition arguments, without evidence that those costs are unavoidable or widely felt.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established evidence for who is affected or why the problem matters beyond the author’s stated constraints.
