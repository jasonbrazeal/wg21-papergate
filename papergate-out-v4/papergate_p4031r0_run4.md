Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only scattered claims toward its own standardization case, mainly around naming concerns, and leaves the core rationale almost entirely undeveloped. The thinnest support is in the areas a proposal most needs: why the standard is the right venue, how the feature fits with existing facilities, and whether implementation experience exists.

- The only substantive engagement is with prior art and alternatives, where the paper cites P3804R1 and restates objections to names like `replacement` and `replacement_functions`.
- The paper asserts that adding the proposed namespace name would cause confusion, but it does not establish why the underlying need matters to users or the ecosystem.
- The affected audience is supported only by a bare reference to a poll, without explanation of who participated or what the results imply for standardization.
- The paper provides no established case for standardizing the facility, no coordination or interoperability analysis, no argument that a library solution is insufficient, and no implementation experience.
