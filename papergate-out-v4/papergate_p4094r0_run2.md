Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers credible evidence that the problem it addresses is real and that the relevant techniques have been deployed and tested in production, but it falls short of demonstrating why standardization is the necessary next step rather than continued library development. The thinnest parts of the argument are the absence of any showing that a library solution cannot suffice and the mostly asserted, rather than demonstrated, claims about fragmentation and interoperability burdens.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience, with over a decade of production use for one model and documented deployments of the other across major industrial users.
- The paper also establishes that the growth in combinations of async facilities and algorithms creates a genuine stewardship problem worth revisiting.
- The interoperability and fragmentation concerns are asserted as real, but the paper does not show concretely how users are harmed or what coordination the standard would uniquely provide.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why existing library-based approaches cannot address the concerns it raises, leaving the central standardization question unanswered.
