Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper puts forward a plausible motivation and points to real-world precedent, but most of its case rests on assertion rather than demonstrated need, leaving the reader to take the existence of workarounds as sufficient evidence of a standardization problem. The support is thinnest where the paper repeatedly leans on the same few passages to justify quite different requirements, without showing concretely who is blocked, what the alternatives cost, or why a library-level solution is inadequate.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with named libraries converging on the same mechanism and a compiler-tested proof of concept.
- The paper asserts that multiple libraries face the problem, but does not establish the scope of affected users or the practical consequences of current workarounds.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives is largely asserted rather than analyzed, offering little comparison of the existing approaches or their limitations.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of a demonstrated reason that a library solution cannot suffice, since the paper claims this without substantiating why the jurisdictional obstacle cannot be worked around in practice.
