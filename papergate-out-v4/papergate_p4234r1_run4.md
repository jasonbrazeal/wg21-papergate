The paper offers solid support on implementation experience and a plausible motivation for why the topic deserves attention, but much of the surrounding case is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is in showing who specifically needs standardization, why the standard is the right venue, and whether a library or other mechanism could address the problem.

- The strongest evidence is the breadth of existing compiler support, including named implementations and a concrete Clang implementation attempt.
- The paper clearly explains that C++ currently treats `$` in identifiers as ill-formed, making the motivation for some action understandable.
- The claims about widespread real-world use rely mainly on repeated assertions of popularity rather than evidence that the cited usage is representative or problematic in a standardization sense.
- The paper does not establish why a library cannot address the concern or how standardization would improve coordination and interoperability beyond what the existing extension already provides.
