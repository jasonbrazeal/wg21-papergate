Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s strongest case rests on showing that the earlier push to unify executor models produced an un-deployed standard track outcome, while the specific components it discusses have a long history of real-world use. The thinnest parts are the arguments that only the standard can deliver the benefits and that the proposal coordinates cleanly with existing systems, both of which are asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper establishes why the problem matters by tracing the unification of three working executor models into P0443 and its eventual replacement without ever being deployed as a unified design.
- The paper establishes that prior art and alternatives were considered, chiefly by examining the published record behind the unification decision and documenting how continuation framing was erased from the API surface.
- The paper claims, but does not establish, who is actually affected by the problem or why standardization, as opposed to a library, is necessary.
- The paper offers no support for why a library solution would not suffice.
