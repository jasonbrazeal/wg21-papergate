Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers some concrete implementation evidence and a clear account of the problem it aims to solve, but much of the case for standardization rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need, impact, or exploration of alternatives. The thinnest support is around who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and how the proposal coordinates with prior and ongoing work.

- The strongest support is implementation experience, with a working prototype and a compiler explorer link credited as established.
- The motivation is established as a real interaction problem with constexpr exceptions and potentially-constant initialization.
- The paper only claims, without establishing, that a standard change is needed rather than a library-level or alternative language approach.
- The most glaring omission is evidence about affected users, since the paper merely expects users to write a certain kind of function without demonstrating that they do.
