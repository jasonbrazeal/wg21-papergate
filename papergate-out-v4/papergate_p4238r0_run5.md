Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers meaningful support on the need for external accountability and on the existence of alternatives to immediate standardization, but it does not establish why the feature belongs in the International Standard itself, nor how it would coordinate with existing machinery, nor why a library approach would fail. The thinnest parts are the absence of any argument tying the proposal to the standard, interoperability, or a non-library home, and the implementation-experience claim rests on secondhand assertions rather than demonstrated deployment.

- The paper establishes why recorded public rationale and external review matter for resisting a defective draft.
- It shows that slower, lower-risk paths such as a Technical Specification or deferral were available and rejected, and that comparable features have been removed after National Body objections.
- It claims implementation concerns primarily through Stroustrup’s characterization and Finland’s comment, without showing actual lack of deployment on its own terms.
- It never establishes why the feature requires standardization, how it interoperates, or why a library could not carry it.
