Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers substantial support in some foundational areas but leaves several central claims asserted rather than demonstrated, particularly where it argues that library solutions are inadequate and that the feature would deliver broad practical benefit. The thinnest support concerns the actual population of affected users and the existence of meaningful implementation experience beyond identification of candidate operations.

- The need for a language-level mechanism is most convincingly grounded in the observation that postfix operators have a near-universal canonical definition and that defaulting them would reduce user error.
- The argument against a rewrite rule is presented as a reasoned rejection rather than an unsupported assertion, and the discussion of prior standardization interest and interaction with other proposals is adequate.
- The paper asserts but does not establish that multiple libraries would otherwise ship incompatible `incrementable` variants or that standardizing the default would meaningfully shrink library specifications.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of concrete evidence about who is affected or how commonly the feature would be used, alongside no demonstrated implementation experience beyond cataloguing candidate operations.
