Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers uneven support for its own standardization, strongest when justifying the general need for the feature and weak when explaining why that need belongs in the standard rather than in a library or coordinated compiler work. The most substantial gaps are in the absence of any identified affected audience and in claims about portability and implementation difficulty that are asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper clearly establishes why the functionality matters, describing the cumbersome, error-prone, and inefficient nature of current C++ workarounds.
- The accepted saturation arithmetic work and the cited reference implementation provide solid prior art and evidence that the feature can be built.
- The case for standardization rather than a library is asserted through repeated claims of portability burden, but the paper does not establish that this burden is unique to a library approach or that standardization resolves it.
- The paper identifies no affected developers, users, or domains, leaving the practical reach and urgency of the proposal unestablished.
