Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a clear and persuasive account of why the procedural problem matters and shows that plausible alternatives were considered, but much of the case for putting the rule in the standard itself remains asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support is in the areas where standardization is most often expected to be justified: evidence of broad practice, implementer experience, and why a non-normative or library-level solution would be insufficient.

- The strongest support is the paper’s explanation of the concrete harm caused by the current judgment-based timing rule, especially the unfair disadvantage to delegates who prepare thoroughly against the mailing.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is well grounded, including acknowledgment of the author’s competing proposals and the relevant SD-4 expectation about post-ballot changes.
- The claim that the rule belongs in the standard, rather than in guidance or process documentation, is present but rests mainly on extending the stated purpose of the pre-meeting mailing rather than on direct evidence of need.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: the evidence offered is largely a single delegate’s account and a reference to an external paper, which does not by itself establish practical experience with the proposed rule.
