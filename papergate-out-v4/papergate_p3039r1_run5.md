Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper rests its case on a narrow but real foundation: it shows some awareness of prior work and analogous language features, but much of the argument for why the feature must be standardized, who it would affect, and how it would work in practice is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest parts are the absence of any implementation experience or discussion of the affected user population, which leaves the practical need largely theoretical.

- The strongest support is the paper’s grounding in prior art and existing rewrite-rule precedent, which at least places the idea in a recognizable design lineage.
- The claim that a library-only solution cannot achieve the same result is repeated and tied to the rewrite-rule mechanism, but it is not developed into evidence.
- The paper does not establish who is affected or what implementation experience exists, leaving the standardization need without a concrete constituency or feasibility record.
