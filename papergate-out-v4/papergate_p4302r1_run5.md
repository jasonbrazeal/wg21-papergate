Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a partial but uneven case for standardizing its proposed process change, with the strongest support going to the problem statement and the inadequacy of current practice. The argument for who is affected rests largely on a single anecdotal meeting count, while the connection to why a standard is the right vehicle and whether the change is implementable in practice remains essentially unmade. The thinnest parts of the case are the absence of any argument that a library or other non-normative mechanism would not suffice, and the lack of implementation experience.

- The paper firmly establishes that in-meeting revisions create a real consensus and preparation problem for national body experts.
- The prior-art review shows that existing rules and alternative proposals have not solved the problem, though the tentatively plenary idea fell short of consensus.
- The claim that six of nineteen prepared papers changed at one meeting is offered as evidence of scope, but it is too isolated to show how widespread or recurring the problem is.
- The paper never explains why the solution must be a standard rather than a process document, policy, or library-level tool, and it provides no implementation experience to ground the proposed mechanism.
