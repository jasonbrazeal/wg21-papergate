Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a thin scaffolding of support for its own standardization, with most of the necessary argument left asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest parts are the complete absence of any discussion of who is affected and the failure to connect the framework’s claims to concrete standard-library or language-level necessity.

- The one solid piece of support is the reported implementation experience in a GCC fork.
- The paper gestures at prior art and alternatives through references to P3968, P4005, and P4009, but does not establish how they compare or why this refinement is needed.
- The rationale for doing this in the standard rather than in a library is merely asserted, as are the claims about flexibility, user-extensibility, and interoperability.
- The paper never identifies who would be affected by the proposal, leaving the need for standardization abstract and unattached to any actual user or implementer population.
