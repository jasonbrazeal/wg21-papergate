Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers a clear and well-developed rationale for why the language feature it targets would be undesirable, but it provides very little of the supporting evidence that would be needed to justify standardization work in the first place. The strongest material concerns the motivating problem and its consequences for language consistency; beyond that, the case thins out quickly, leaving most of the procedural and evidentiary questions unaddressed.

- The paper firmly establishes why the issue matters by arguing that omitting `do_return` would undermine long-standing C++ rules and teachability expectations.
- The discussion of prior art identifies the relevant proposal and gestures toward consistency, teachability, and greppability concerns, though it does not demonstrate how alternatives were systematically considered.
- The paper offers no account of who would be affected by the proposed change or what real-world codebases or user communities would experience its effects.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, interoperability considerations, or any explanation of why the problem cannot be handled outside the standard.
