Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers a limited and mostly rhetorical case for standardization: nearly every point it makes is asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the risk of prematurely locking in coroutine interaction design, but the paper does not identify affected users, provide implementation experience, or establish that the problem cannot be solved outside the standard.

- The clearest support is the concern that standardizing the current interface would foreclose alternative propagation and completion mechanisms.
- The paper gestures at prior work and alternatives, but does not develop them into evidence that this particular design must be standardized.
- The thinnest areas are the complete absence of implementation experience and any account of who is affected.
- It also fails to show why a library solution would be inadequate, leaving the central rationale for standardization unproven.
