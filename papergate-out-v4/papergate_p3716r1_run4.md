Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its standardization case: it establishes why the problem matters in constrained environments, but leaves most of the burden of proof unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why the standard is the right venue, why a library solution cannot suffice, and any demonstration of implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the clear motivation that some environments forbid dynamic allocation and that pointer arithmetic can easily escape intended bounds.
- The paper gestures at affected users and prior art, but the evidence is anecdotal or asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The paper does not attempt to show why standardization, rather than a library or vendor extension, is necessary.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience to ground the proposal in practice.
