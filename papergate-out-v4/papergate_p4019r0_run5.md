Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a partial but uneven case for standardization, centered on a genuine motivation and a clear sense of the feature’s purpose, but it leaves the broader evidentiary requirements largely unaddressed. The thinnest support concerns who would actually use the feature and how it would fit with existing implementations or ecosystem practice.

- The strongest support is the motivation: the paper establishes why a programmer would want a lightweight, optimizer-dependent assertion and how it could save real effort when tuning code.
- The treatment of prior art is suggestive but not persuasive, since related facilities are mentioned without showing how they fall short or how prior practice informs the proposed design.
- The argument for standardization rather than a library is asserted through the need for compiler-visible behavior, but the paper does not develop why that cannot be achieved today or what exactly the standard must mandate.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established affected audience or implementation experience, leaving the proposal without evidence of demand or feasibility.
