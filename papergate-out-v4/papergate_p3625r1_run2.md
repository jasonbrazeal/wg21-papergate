Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives some useful context for why the authors want the feature and includes a genuine survey of naming options and external references, but it leaves the core standardization rationale largely unargued. The thinnest parts concern the necessity of a standard facility rather than a library, the absence of any coordination or interoperability analysis, and the lack of implementation experience beyond the authors’ own projects.

- The strongest support is the discussion of prior art and alternatives, which shows the authors considered naming choices and looked at existing community discussions.
- The paper at least asserts that the feature would matter to users and that the authors encountered the need in several projects, though this remains anecdotal.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any argument for why a library solution would not suffice, which is central to justifying a standard wording change.
- Relatedly, the paper does not establish coordination with existing concepts or interoperability concerns, nor does it offer implementation experience beyond its own reported use.
