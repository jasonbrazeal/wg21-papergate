Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization. What support it presents is mostly borrowed from committee sentiment or early polling, while the areas that would most directly justify standardization—implementation experience, interoperability, and the need for a standard rather than a library—remain unaddressed.

- The strongest support comes from repeated committee indications that sender/receiver is a promising basis for asynchronous use cases, though this establishes direction rather than demonstrated need.
- The paper gestures at prior art and affected users, but relies on poll results and anonymous commentary rather than concrete evidence of use, deployment, or documented alternatives.
- The thinnest parts are the complete absence of implementation experience and any argument for why a standard, as opposed to a library, is necessary.
