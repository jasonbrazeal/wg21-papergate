Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives real substance to its motivation and to the feasibility of the approach, but it leaves several parts of the standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated, especially the need for a standard as opposed to a tool or process change.

- The strongest support is the concrete evidence that the technique is cheap, fast, and already implemented in open source with compiler-backed availability.
- The paper also convincingly grounds its rationale in a documented committee gap and the costly C++20 Contracts cycle.
- Its weakest support concerns who is actually affected: the harms are invoked repeatedly, but the affected parties and the scale of impact are not clearly established.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why the work belongs in the standard rather than existing as an ordinary library, service, or procedural tool.
