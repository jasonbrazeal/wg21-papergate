Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper provides some useful compiler evidence for the behavior it describes, but much of its rationale rests on interpretation and assertion rather than demonstrated need. The thinnest support is around the core question of why standardization is warranted at all, and what problem would remain unsolved without it.

- The strongest support is implementation experience, where the paper documents agreement among implementations on 18 of 21 cases and notes the specific points of divergence.
- The paper gestures at who is affected and at interoperability concerns, but does not show a concrete user population or practical consequence.
- Prior art and alternatives are only loosely connected to the proposal’s own direction, with historical references that are suggestive rather than decisive.
- The paper does not establish why the standard should change or why a library-level solution would be inadequate, leaving the central justification unaddressed.
