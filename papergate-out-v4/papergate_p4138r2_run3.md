Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow evidentiary basis centered on implementation behavior, but most of the contextual argument—why the change matters, who feels the effect, and what alternatives exist—is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest parts are the complete absence of discussion about why a standard change is needed rather than some other remedy, and why a library-level solution would not suffice.

- The strongest support is implementation experience, where compiler agreement is shown concretely across a range of cases.
- The paper gestures at affected users and existing practice through the same implementation comparisons, but does not develop a real picture of who is impacted or how.
- Prior art and alternatives are mostly historical references and an aside about Clang, without a substantive comparison of possible directions.
- The most glaring omission is any argument for why this belongs in the standard at all, rather than being left to implementation discretion or addressed outside the language.
