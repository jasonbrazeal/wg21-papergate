Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a thin evidentiary basis for standardization, resting nearly its entire case on a single sentence about encountering the need across several projects. The strongest material is the discussion of prior art and alternative names, but the core justifications for why the standard should act, who is affected, and what would be lost by leaving this to a library are asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The most concrete support comes from the consideration of alternative names and links to existing documentation and community discussion, showing some awareness of prior usage and naming trade-offs.
- The paper repeatedly cites project experience as the reason for the proposal, but never specifies the projects, use cases, or scale of the need.
- The argument for why standardization is necessary is essentially the same single sentence used for every other justification, with no elaboration.
- The paper does not address why a library solution would be inadequate, leaving a central question about the need for standardization entirely unanswered.
