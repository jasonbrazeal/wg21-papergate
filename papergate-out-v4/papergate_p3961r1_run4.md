Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some useful support, particularly through an implementation and a concrete motivating failure, but it leaves several central questions about audience, interoperability, and the necessity of standardization largely unaddressed. The thinnest areas are those that would show why this belongs in the standard rather than in a library or a narrower specification.

- The strongest support is the existence of an implementation, which at least demonstrates that the proposed behavior is technically reachable.
- The paper gives a clear motivating example that current code fails to compile and ties that to an existing issue request.
- The discussion of alternatives and prior art is more gesture than evidence, since the paper itself asks for real-world code to justify the change.
- The most glaring omission is any account of who is affected or how the feature coordinates with existing library and language behavior.
