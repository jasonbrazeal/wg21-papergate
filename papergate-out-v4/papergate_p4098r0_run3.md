Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a partial survey of a contentious history, but it does not itself make a coherent case that the proposed facility should be standardized. The strongest material is documentary rather than evidentiary, and the central questions of why standardization is necessary and why a library solution would not suffice are left entirely unaddressed.

- The paper’s most credible support comes from documenting real deployment experience, including Boost.Asio and the author’s own coroutine-based libraries.
- The discussion of prior art is useful as a record of competing published claims, though it stops short of analyzing what the surveyed trade-offs actually cost.
- The claim that billions of users are affected by sender/receiver-based async APIs is asserted without the population or impact evidence needed to carry that weight.
- The paper never establishes why the facility must go into the standard rather than remain a library, which is the most glaring omission for a standardization proposal.
