Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a clear explanation of the practical problem it addresses, but its broader case for standardization rests largely on assertions that are not backed up with supporting detail. The thinnest areas are the absence of an identified affected audience and the lack of concrete evidence for implementation experience or alternative approaches.

- The strongest support is the explanation of why the proposal matters, particularly the conflict between TU-level contract semantics and mixing performance-critical with safety-critical code.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives gestures toward existing proposals and ABI considerations, but it is more a claim than a worked comparison.
- The case for why a library will not do is asserted through a single observation about the ignore semantic rather than developed as a full argument.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never identifies who is affected, leaving the affected constituency entirely unestablished.
