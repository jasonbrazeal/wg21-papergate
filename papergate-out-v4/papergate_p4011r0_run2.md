Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization, resting mostly on gestures toward terminology and prior definitions rather than concrete evidence of need. The support is thinnest where it matters most: there is no demonstration of who is actually affected, why the standard is the right venue, or how existing library mechanisms fall short.

- The strongest support is the recognition that prior work defined narrow and wide contracts, giving the discussion some conceptual anchor.
- The paper claims the problem matters once syntax both encodes a precondition and removes undefined behavior, but does not show that this situation is common enough to warrant standardization.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is asserted rather than developed, with no comparison to existing practice or available mitigations.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or interoperability analysis, leaving the practical case for standardization almost entirely unsupported.
