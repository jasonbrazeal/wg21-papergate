Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers solid support for the naming question and the existence of prior art, but its case thins considerably once it moves from terminology to the specific need for a standard C++ facility. The thinnest areas are the absence of an argument that only the standard can address the problem and the lack of concrete implementation evidence.

- The paper convincingly establishes that “floor division” and “ceiling division” are the names users expect and will search for, and that alternatives have worse or inconsistent naming.
- The discussion of other languages and existing C++ proposals provides credible prior art for both the operations and their conventional names.
- The paper only claims, rather than demonstrates, who is affected or how standardization would coordinate with existing practice.
- The paper does not establish why this belongs in the C++ standard rather than remaining a library concern, which is the most glaring omission given that its own examples suggest users can implement the functionality themselves.
