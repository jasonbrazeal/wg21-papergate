Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only preliminary and largely unsupported claims about the need for its proposed customization point, with nearly every category resting on the author’s assertions rather than demonstrated evidence, examples, or experience. The thinnest support appears where the proposal should be strongest: explaining why standardization is necessary at all and why existing language or library mechanisms cannot suffice.

- The clearest statement in the paper is the repeated claim that no current way exists to detect or modify values crossing between constant and runtime evaluation, though even this is asserted rather than shown.
- The discussion of affected users and prior alternatives is framed in personal belief and tentative preference rather than evidence from real code, existing practice, or comparison with rejected approaches.
- The paper offers no implementation experience beyond a stated intention to prototype, and no evaluation of whether a library solution could address the problem.
- The most glaring omission is any argument for why the C++ standard itself must change, leaving the central case for standardization entirely unaddressed.
