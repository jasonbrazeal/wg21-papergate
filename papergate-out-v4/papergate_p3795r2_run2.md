Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin and largely asserted case for its own standardization, relying on general statements about usefulness and consistency without demonstrating who is affected, what alternatives were meaningfully compared, or whether the facility has been implemented. The strongest support is the claim that the feature is occasionally requested and would prevent API proliferation, while the least developed areas concern practical evidence and integration with the existing ecosystem.

- The paper most directly supports its need by asserting the feature is useful, occasionally requested, and preferable to indirect or inconsistent APIs.
- It gestures at prior art and alternatives through references to P2996 and observed inconsistencies, but does not establish a substantive comparison.
- The paper does not identify who is affected or provide any implementation experience to ground its claims.
- The absence of coordination and interoperability discussion, along with no argument that a library solution is insufficient, leaves the standardization rationale largely unexamined.
