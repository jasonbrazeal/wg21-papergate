Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers credible support in a few areas, particularly in tracing the history of contract assertions and showing that the proposed extension follows from an already agreed roadmap. However, the case is thin where it counts most: it does not connect the extension to a concrete need that cannot be met without standardization, nor does it explain why existing language or library mechanisms are insufficient.

- The strongest support is the paper’s placement within an established sequence of contract-related proposals and its reference to prior art and a recognized roadmap.
- The paper claims broad relevance for large codebases, independently distributed libraries, and safety-critical systems, but does not substantiate who is actually affected or how.
- The paper asserts implementation experience and mentions a working implementation in GCC, but provides little evidence beyond that statement.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why this must be a standard language feature rather than something achievable through existing or library-level mechanisms.
