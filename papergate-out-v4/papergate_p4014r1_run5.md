Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a narrow base of support, mainly through its engagement with prior art and a handful of external implementation references, but it leaves several central questions about audience, feasibility, and the necessity of standardization largely unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the absence of any demonstrated need for a standard library facility as opposed to a library solution, and the lack of evidence about who would be affected by the proposed design.

- The paper’s strongest support comes from its grounding in established prior art, including Moggi’s monadic framework and scheduler affinity primitives redesigned in recent proposals.
- Implementation experience is claimed through references to Capy, Corosio, stdexec, and sender-examples, but these citations are not developed into evidence of broad or independent implementation validation.
- The case for coordination and interoperability rests on repeated assertions about shared vocabulary and team practices without concrete demonstration.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any argument for why a library will not do, leaving the core standardization question unanswered.
