Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a reasonably clear rationale for clarifying existing floating-point value wording, but it leaves several parts of its standardization case asserted rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the current wording’s ambiguity and the history behind it, while the weakest areas are the absence of concrete evidence about affected users, implementation experience, and why the core standard—rather than some other venue—is necessary.

- The paper establishes that the existing core wording leaves floating-point representable values unclear and that this matters for a coherent language model.
- It also establishes relevant prior context, including earlier proposal history and the fact that current wording diverges from the original design’s clearer intent.
- The paper claims implementation alignment and real-world impact, but does not substantiate either with sufficient detail or evidence.
- It never establishes who is concretely affected or why standardization in the core language is required.
