Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a partial case for standardization, with the strongest ground in implementation experience and the thinnest in the core rationale for why the standard, rather than a library, is the right venue. Much of the motivating material is asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the affected audience and practical need largely assumed.

- The paper’s implementation experience is the clearest support, citing an author implementation and established equivalents in range-v3, Python, and Kotlin.
- The problem statement is present but thin: it notes the lack of suffix-oriented adaptors without showing why that gap is significant in practice.
- The discussion of who is affected and prior art leans on brief references to other ecosystems rather than evidence of C++ user demand or common workarounds.
- The paper offers no established rationale for standardization itself or for coordination with existing range adaptors, and the claim that a library solution would not suffice is stated rather than substantiated.
