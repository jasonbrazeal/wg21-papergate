Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers substantial support for the existence and severity of the problem, with especially strong documentation of divergent implementation behavior and the inadequacy of the current wording. Its case is thinnest in translating that problem into a positive standardization argument: it does not convincingly establish who is concretely affected, why only a standard change will suffice, or whether the proposed behavior has enough real-world implementation experience to be codified safely.

- The strongest support is the clear establishment that implementations diverge from each other and from the standard, and that the relevant wording is scattered and ineffective.
- The paper also does well in situating itself against prior art and known LWG issues, showing that previous attempts have failed or been incomplete.
- A notable omission is a convincing account of affected users or codebases, since the claim of substantial breakage is repeated but not backed with concrete evidence.
- The most glaring gap is the lack of a demonstrated need for standardization rather than a library solution, especially when a portable library alternative is acknowledged as well-specified.
