Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only incidental support for its own standardization, leaning on surveys of prior arguments, deployment anecdotes, and authorial belief rather than demonstrating a need for a new standard facility. Its thinnest areas are the complete absence of argument for why standardization is necessary, why a library is insufficient, and how the proposed work would coordinate or interoperate with existing and in-flight specifications.

- The strongest support is the author’s development and maintenance of coroutine-native I/O libraries, which suggests at least personal implementation interest.
- The paper gestures toward relevance by citing deployment-scale use of sender/receiver APIs, but does not connect that evidence to a need for standardizing coroutine-native I/O.
- Prior-art discussion is presented as a survey of published claims rather than as a comparative case establishing that the proposed direction is the right one for the standard.
- Most glaringly, the paper never addresses why the standard is the right venue, why existing library approaches will not suffice, or how the proposal would interoperate with the rest of the ecosystem.
