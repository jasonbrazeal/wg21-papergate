Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper carries its weight best where it argues that the layering question is genuinely contested and deserves a separate decision, but that argument rests on evidence the paper repeatedly asserts rather than demonstrates. The thinnest support is where the case for standardization should be most concrete: the absence of implementation experience, the unresolved question of whether a library would suffice, and the failure to show who would actually be affected.

- The strongest support lies in the reconstruction of the proposal’s poll history and the demonstration that no prior vote adopted the architecture, which gives the request for an explicit ballot a credible procedural footing.
- The claims about prior art are well grounded in specific named papers and the withdrawn enumerators, showing a real unresolved dependency between the competing designs.
- The paper’s insistence that the standard is the right venue is asserted as a matter of consequence rather than shown through interoperability failures or harm that a non-standard mechanism could not address.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established implementation experience for the named-check-set approach the paper asks the committee to standardize.
