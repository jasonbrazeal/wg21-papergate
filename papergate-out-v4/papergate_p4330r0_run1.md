Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers real support for its core claim that the contracts program is expanding into territory that affects profiles and could foreclose library-based or independent approaches, but that support is much thinner when it comes to showing why standardization is necessary, what production experience exists, or how the proposed mechanism would interoperate in practice. The strongest material concerns the displacement of profiles and the irreversibility of placing the mechanism in the standard; the weakest concerns implementation experience and field evidence, where the paper offers only prototypes and asserted usefulness rather than demonstrated need.

- The paper establishes that the contracts program’s routing of safety configuration through contracts would displace an independent profiles facility and make later correction require a new standard revision.
- The paper establishes that prior profiles proposals have failed and notes committee votes to include contracts, lending context to its concern about committed committee time through 2029.
- The paper claims but does not establish that affected users or implementers have demonstrated the feature’s value in practice, offering no production deployment or field data.
- The paper claims but does not establish implementation experience beyond experimental compiler branches behind flags, leaving the case for standardization resting on prototype behavior rather than proven use.
