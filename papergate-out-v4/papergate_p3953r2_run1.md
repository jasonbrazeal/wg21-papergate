Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a clear and convincing account of why the current name has become misleading, but it offers very little else to justify standardization. Beyond the motivation, the case for pursuing this change through the committee is largely asserted rather than demonstrated, with especially thin treatment of affected users, implementation experience, and why the standard is the necessary venue.

- The strongest part of the paper is its explanation that `std::runtime_format` no longer matches the behavior now that formatting can occur during constant evaluation.
- The proposal gestures toward coordination with existing terminology and the history from P2918 and P3391, but does not develop a substantive case for prior art or interoperability.
- The paper says nothing about who would be affected by the renaming or what the migration burden would be.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of implementation experience, library-level alternatives, or a specific need for standardization rather than a different path.
