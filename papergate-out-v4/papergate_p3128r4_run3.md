Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for standardization, with credible discussion of prior art and alternatives but little demonstration that the proposed facilities need to enter the standard, that they would fit existing practice, or that implementation experience justifies doing so.

- The strongest support lies in the comparison with existing libraries and algorithms, particularly the acknowledged alignment with Boost Graph Library visitor events and the stated advantages over alternatives such as Kosaraju or nested-loop intersection.
- The paper asserts relevance and affected audiences, but these claims are not backed by evidence about actual user needs or the significance of the added algorithms.
- The case for why this belongs in the standard, rather than remaining a library, is absent.
- Most glaringly, the paper provides no implementation experience, no interoperability or coordination story, and no argument that standardization is the right vehicle at all.
