Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper provides meaningful support around prior art and implementation experience, but it leaves several core justifications—especially why the change matters and why the standard is the right place for it—largely asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest parts are the complete absence of a case for why a library solution is insufficient and why standardization is necessary at all.

- The strongest support is the historical tracing of the relevant wording and intent, along with concrete implementation evidence showing current compiler behavior.
- The paper less convincingly establishes who is affected and how the current inconsistency causes real user harm, relying mainly on implementation divergence examples.
- The paper offers no argument that this cannot be addressed outside the standard, such as through guidance or tooling.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any rationale for why the standard itself must change to resolve the issue.
