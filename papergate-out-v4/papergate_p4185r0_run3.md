Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper offers a partial but uneven case for its own standardization, with its strongest material lying in concrete implementation experience and a credible account of prior art. The case thins considerably where it depends on claims about user demand, cross-library coordination, and why the work belongs in the standard rather than in a library, since those are asserted more than demonstrated.

- The paper is most convincing in showing that the proposed three-way split has been implemented and exercised in real code, with concrete examples from both mp-units and Sequoia.
- The paper does well to establish that alternative designs and independent prior art exist and point toward the same structural taxonomy.
- The least supported part of the case is the claim that standardization, rather than continued library development, is necessary now, since the paper offers little concrete evidence about lock-in risk or what the standard committee would uniquely enable.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of evidence that the affected communities actually experience the claimed problems at a scale or severity that would justify standardization, since the user reports and coordination claims are asserted but not established.
