Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper gives a mixed account of itself: it clearly motivates the portability problem and shows that existing practice, prior discussions, and compiler divergence make this a live standards question, but it does not convincingly show who is affected, why standardization is the only viable route, or that implementation experience is sufficient. The thinnest parts are the repeated use of the same compiler-observation passage to support unrelated points and the absence of evidence connecting user assumptions or vendor feedback to a demonstrated need for normative change.

- The strongest support is the established description of conflicting compiler behavior and the resulting difficulty in writing portable code.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also solid, particularly through the CWG issue, the national body comment, and the illustrative examples.
- The weakest area is the paper’s failure to establish who is affected or how widespread the practical impact is beyond asserted user assumptions and reported vendor remarks.
- The case for implementation experience is notably underdeveloped, since the same general observation about compiler disagreement is offered without concrete evidence of sustained or validated implementation practice.
