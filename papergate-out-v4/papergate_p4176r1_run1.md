Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a faint outline of a case for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on a stated desire to improve the presentation of expression grammar. That support is thinnest in the areas that would normally justify a standards change: there is no clear account of who is affected, why the standard itself is the right venue, how the change fits with existing practice, or what alternatives were seriously considered.

- The strongest support is the paper’s general observation that the current grammar organization makes definitions hard to follow.
- The only partially credited argument for prior art and alternatives is the same brief statement of intent to introduce new non-terminals and relocate definitions.
- The paper does not establish whom the change would affect, either among implementers or readers of the standard.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any reasoning for why this must be solved in the standard rather than through documentation, teaching material, or other non-normative means.
