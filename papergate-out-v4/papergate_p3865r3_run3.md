Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a reasonably grounded case that the underlying issue is a wording defect rather than a new feature, with its strongest support coming from the explanation of the intended design and the lack of a viable alternative to core language changes. The case is thinnest where the paper relies on repetition and assertion rather than concrete demonstration, particularly regarding actual implementation support and the standard’s need to act.

- The paper most convincingly establishes prior art by showing that current deductions flow from a misreading of P0091R3’s intent, not from a deliberate restriction.
- The claim that a core language fix is unavoidable is asserted through repetition of LWG 4381 rather than shown through an analysis of why alternative library approaches fail.
- The paper does not demonstrate implementation experience, since it admits no implementation yet matches the exact semantics being proposed.
- The most glaring omission is any substantive treatment of why a library-only solution would not suffice, beyond gesturing at the linked library issue.
