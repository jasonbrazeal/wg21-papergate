Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper makes a reasonably strong case for standardizing a physical quantities and units library, particularly around motivating examples, prior art, interoperability, and implementation experience. The case is thinnest where it tries to establish who is affected and why a third-party library cannot suffice, since those arguments rest largely on popularity claims and certification policy rather than demonstrated standardization need.

- The strongest support is the paper’s demonstration that existing practice and mature implementations already provide the core design and performance characteristics a standardized library would need.
- The interoperability argument is also well supported by concrete alignment with existing standard library types and evidence of external standards-body interest.
- The least developed area is the claim about the scale and diversity of affected users, which leans on unverified estimates and repository stars rather than direct evidence of adoption or need.
- The most glaring omission is a rigorous argument for why certification and company policy genuinely prevent a non-standardized library from meeting those needs.
