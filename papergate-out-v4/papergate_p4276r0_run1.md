Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper does establish why the question it addresses matters and shows that the concrete alternatives were considered, but it falls short of making a full case for standardization on its own terms. The support is thinnest where the paper relies on assertion rather than evidence: the affected audience, the need for a standard answer, coordination benefits, and the limits of a library solution are all claimed without being demonstrated, and there is no implementation experience offered at all.

- The paper convincingly grounds its question in real prior-art discussions, particularly the saturating-arithmetic review and the shift/rotate/funnel-shift precedent, and it correctly identifies when a scalar overload can lower to a better instruction than a broadcast.
- The paper establishes the core technical distinction that a scalar overload is redundant unless it enables a concrete implementation benefit the broadcast cannot provide.
- The paper does not establish who is affected beyond broad assertions that the question recurs and that an expectation has inadvertently been created.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, which leaves the practical benefit of adopting the guideline as a standardized principle unsupported.
