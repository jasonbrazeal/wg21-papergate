Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers only scattered support for its own standardization, with the strongest material concerning prior art and alternatives and the thinnest coverage falling on the affected audience and practical implementation experience. Several key arguments are asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the case for a standard change substantially underdeveloped.

- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is the most concrete part of the paper, showing awareness of existing customization mechanisms and earlier draft approaches.
- The paper gestures at why a library solution would be insufficient, but those reasons remain largely asserted rather than shown with examples or analysis.
- The absence of any identified user community or affected parties leaves the proposal without a clear constituency or demonstrated demand.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience, where even the single credited hazard is presented without evidence of prototyping, deployment, or compiler support.
