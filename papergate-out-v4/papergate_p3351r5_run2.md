Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper gives credible support in a few important areas—especially prior art, implementation experience, and the basic rationale for the operation—but its overall case is uneven, with several central standardization questions left more asserted than demonstrated. The thinnest part is the absence of any argument for why a library solution would not suffice, which leaves a noticeable gap in the proposal’s foundation.

- The strongest support comes from established implementation experience, including ranges-v3 and the author’s own Beman project implementation.
- The paper also firmly establishes relevant prior art and alternatives through references to ranges-v3 and the C++26 Ranges plan.
- It claims coordination and interoperability, but does not really establish how the proposed view fits with existing or planned standard facilities beyond a passing mention of tier status and parallel libraries.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any established case for why a library implementation would not be adequate.
