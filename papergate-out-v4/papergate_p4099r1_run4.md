Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a clear and credible argument that the original executor landscape was structurally flawed, but it does not yet substantiate most of the consequential claims about adoption, production experience, or the unique role of a standard. The support is thinnest where the paper asserts that standardization—rather than a library—is needed to obtain type erasure, ABI stability, and the demonstrated viability of the proposed alternative.

- The strongest part of the paper is its account of why the earlier executor model failed and why a decade of effort did not produce standard networking.
- The paper indicates promising implementation and deployment activity, but the reader is left without verifiable detail about breadth, maturity, or what those experiences actually show.
- The case for prior art and alternatives is asserted through comparisons and assembled findings rather than shown as a demonstrated equivalence or a completed evaluation.
- The most glaring omission is a substantive justification for why the desired properties cannot be achieved outside the standard, since the paper’s central claim on this point is repeated without being established.
