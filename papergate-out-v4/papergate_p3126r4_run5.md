Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives concrete support for its implementation experience, prior art, and the general importance of graph abstractions, but it leaves several of the institutional arguments for standardization asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the explicit identification of affected users, how the proposed design coordinates with existing standard library facilities, and why a library outside the standard cannot suffice.

- The strongest part of the proposal is its reference implementation and the authors’ demonstrated experience porting and adapting existing graph libraries.
- The paper grounds its approach in recognized prior art, including Boost Graph Library, NWGraph, and earlier committee papers.
- The claim that graph functionality belongs in the standard is repeated but not developed with evidence about specific user communities or interoperability problems it would solve.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established statement about who is affected by the lack of standardized graph support.
