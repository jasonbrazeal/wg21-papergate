Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a consistent high-level rationale for why a structural-type query would be useful, but it leaves nearly every element of that rationale asserted rather than demonstrated, with almost no concrete evidence about users, alternatives, or implementability. The thinnest areas are the absence of affected-user analysis and the reliance on a single sample implementation to carry claims that range from feasibility to standard-library needs.

- The paper’s strongest support is its brief explanation that NTTPs require structural types and that no user-facing query currently exposes that property.
- Several passages gesture toward prior art and an experimental implementation, but the paper does not show how the proposed facility compares with or improves upon existing practice.
- The paper repeatedly asserts that standard-library mandates imply the functionality already exists internally, without establishing that this is true or that exposure to users is a standardization problem.
- The most glaring omission is any discussion of who is affected, leaving the motivating need almost entirely abstract.
