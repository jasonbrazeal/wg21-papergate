Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a skeletal case for its own standardization, with every major element either asserted rather than demonstrated or left entirely unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the arguments that the problem belongs in the standard at all, that the work fits with existing committee efforts, and that a library solution is insufficient—none of which receive any supporting reasoning.

- The strongest available support is the mention of IAR’s Embedded C++ mode, which at least gestures toward prior implementation experience, though it is only asserted and not substantiated.
- The paper asserts that pointer arithmetic can escape bounds and that dynamic allocation is forbidden in hard-embedded settings, but it does not connect these observations to a demonstrated need for the specific standardization it proposes.
- The discussion of affected users and prior art relies on cited figures and references without showing how they establish the necessity of this particular feature-removal mechanism.
- Most notably, the paper offers no argument for why the standard is the right venue, how the work coordinates with related proposals, or why a library-only approach cannot meet the need.
