Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only scattered, undeveloped gestures toward a standardization case, mostly by asserting that profiles impose burdens and that some restrictions can be unworkable in small amounts of code. The thinnest areas are the ones that normally carry the most weight for a standards body: why the standard is the right venue, how the feature coordinates with existing machinery, why a library cannot suffice, and whether anyone has actually tried this in practice.

- The strongest support is the repeated observation that profile restrictions can conflict with unavoidable low-level or tooling code, which at least hints at a problem worth solving.
- The paper says almost nothing concrete about who is affected beyond a vague reference to mental load on users.
- The complete absence of any discussion of why standardization is needed, rather than a library or separate tool, is the most glaring omission.
