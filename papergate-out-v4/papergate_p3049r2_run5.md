Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers only scattered, mostly self-asserted support for its own standardization, with the sole solid point being a concrete implementation. Nearly every other necessary argument—relevance, affected users, prior art, portability, interoperability, and the need for a standard rather than a library solution—rests on the authors’ say-so rather than demonstrated evidence or external validation. The thinnest support appears where the proposal most needs to justify itself: why existing or alternative mechanisms are insufficient and why this belongs in the standard.

- The proposal establishes implementation experience through a linked, available implementation.
- The paper repeatedly claims advantages such as separation of extraction and insertion, transferability, and portability, but does not show these as unmet needs beyond the authors’ assertions.
- Prior art and alternatives are mentioned but not meaningfully compared or ruled out with evidence.
- The case for why a standard facility, rather than a library solution, is necessary remains asserted rather than established.
