Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers genuine support for its standardization case mainly through the clarity of the motivating problem and the existence of a reference implementation, but much of the broader argument rests on assertions that are not backed up with evidence about affected users, alternative approaches, or why standardization specifically is necessary.

- The strongest element is the established implementation experience, since a reference implementation is available and gives the discussion a concrete basis.
- The paper clearly establishes why the functionality matters by showing that equivalent C++ is cumbersome, error-prone, and inefficient without compiler or architecture-specific workarounds.
- The weakest areas are the repeated but unsubstantiated claims that a library solution is insufficient and that standardization is the only practical route, since these points are asserted rather than demonstrated through comparison with existing practice or detailed interoperability analysis.
- Most glaringly, the paper never identifies who is affected by the problem, leaving the audience and the urgency of standardization unclear.
