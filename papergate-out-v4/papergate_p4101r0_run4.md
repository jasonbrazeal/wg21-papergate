Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers some grounding for its premise that the consteval-only type model has proven problematic and that a value-based alternative is being considered, but it does not sufficiently establish the broader case for standardizing that alternative. The thinnest support is in showing why existing language mechanisms cannot address the need and in demonstrating that the change would carry implementation experience and interoperability weight.

- The paper most clearly establishes that the current reflection design’s consteval-only type approach has encountered limitations, motivating the search for a different rule.
- The exploration of prior art is credible, since it points to an earlier consteval-only values proposal and the abstract-class analogy as reference points.
- The claim that nearly all reflection users will need this is asserted without evidence, so the affected-user picture remains unsubstantiated.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument that a library solution is insufficient, leaving the necessity of a core language change unexplained.
