Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a credible, if partial, case for the feature’s usefulness and for why existing workarounds fall short, but it leans on assertion rather than evidence for several core standardization arguments. The strongest material concerns motivation and the limits of current alternatives; the thinnest concerns implementation experience, where the document is effectively silent.

- The paper clearly establishes why the capability would matter, particularly for wrapping C APIs and aligning function aliases with existing `using` syntax.
- Its discussion of prior art and alternatives is well grounded, showing engagement with earlier proposals and the inadequacy of current library-level emulation.
- Claims about who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and coordination with existing features are repeated but not substantiated with concrete cases or detailed analysis.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, leaving the proposal without evidence that the feature is buildable or adoptable in practice.
