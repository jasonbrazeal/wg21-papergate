Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin affirmative case for standardization: it asserts that the interface additions would improve ergonomics and align with existing practice, but it does not demonstrate who needs the facility, why it belongs in the standard rather than a library, or that any implementation experience exists. The supporting discussion is largely a series of gestures toward other documents and typical behavior, without establishing the need, scope, or readiness that standardizing a new library facility would require.

- The strongest support is a general claim that changing rounding modes is unergonomic, though the paper does not connect that discomfort to a demonstrated user population or use case.
- The paper gestures at related work such as P3375R3, ISO 60559, and N5014, but it does not show how those references constitute prior art that makes standardization of these particular overloads necessary.
- The assertion that the proposal adds overload sets to the standard library merely restates the proposed mechanism without explaining why a standard facility, rather than a user-side library, is required.
- The paper gives no implementation experience, no interoperability analysis, and no account of who would be affected, leaving the central case for standardization unaddressed.
