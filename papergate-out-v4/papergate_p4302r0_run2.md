Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers solid support in the areas most central to its argument, particularly the costs of judgment-based rules and the existence of relevant prior art, but its case becomes considerably weaker when it turns to why the change belongs in the standard, how it affects the wider community, and whether there is any implementation experience behind it. The gaps are not mere formalities; they leave the reader with little evidence that the problem is broadly felt or that the proposed rule has been tried in practice.

- The strongest support is for why the problem matters, with clear and specific illustrations of how judgment-based mechanisms penalize preparation and distort decision-making.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is also well grounded, naming direct ancestors and drawing on existing SD-4 and SD-7 language.
- The weakest part of the case is the absence of anything showing a library solution would be insufficient or inappropriate.
- Most glaringly, the paper never moves beyond individual anecdotes to establish implementation experience, and its claims about who is affected and why only the standard can solve the problem remain asserted rather than demonstrated.
