Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper provides only a thin justification for its own standardization, mostly gesturing at existing misuses and NB comments rather than developing a substantive case. Its support is thinnest where it matters most for a core-language wording fix: why the standard must change, what breaks without it, and whether implementation or coordination concerns exist.

- The strongest acknowledged support is the existence of incorrect uses in the standard, with some prior fixes cited as evidence that the problem is real.
- The paper claims relevance mainly through NB comments, but does not establish who is concretely affected beyond those requests.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is asserted rather than shown, relying on a partial fix without explaining how this proposal compares or completes that work.
- The paper is entirely silent on why a standard change, rather than editorial guidance or a library approach, is necessary, and offers no implementation experience or interoperability analysis.
