Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper makes a clear case that the proposed change would matter, but for most of the other questions it relies on assertions rather than demonstrated evidence. The strongest material concerns the motivation and the concrete problem with closed element-type lists, while the thinnest concerns the claim that a library solution is insufficient and that the feature requires standardization rather than implementation or convention.

- The paper establishes why loosening the element-type restriction matters for type safety and consistency with scalar operations.
- The paper’s claims about compiler viability and implementation experience are repeated but not substantiated with enough detail to count as established support.
- The paper does not establish that the problem cannot be adequately addressed outside the standard, since the discussion of library-based alternatives remains undeveloped.
- The treatment of coordination and interoperability is the most glaring omission, with only a brief assertion about ABI behavior and no real analysis of cross-implementation or cross-platform effects.
