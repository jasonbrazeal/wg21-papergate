Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers meaningful support in two areas—demonstrating real implementation experience and identifying substantive problems in the existing customization mechanism—but it leaves several essential justifications largely unaddressed, particularly around who is affected and why a non-standard library solution would be insufficient. The argument for standardization specifically, as opposed to adoption of the implemented library fix, is the thinnest part of the paper.

- The strongest support comes from implementation experience, with the fix shipped independently in two libraries and reported as causing no bug reports.
- The paper also credibly establishes that it addresses real, identified gaps in prior art and alternatives.
- The paper claims, but does not establish, why the standard is the right venue, resting only on the assertion that existing alternatives are hacks.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any account of who is affected by the problem or what practical consequences they face.
