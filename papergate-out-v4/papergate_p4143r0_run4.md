Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, partial justification for standardization, centered on clarifying evaluations of putative constant expressions and addressing one national body comment. Most of the support a proposal needs is absent: there is no identified audience, no comparison with alternatives or discussion of implementation experience, and no explanation of why the core language rather than some other mechanism is required. The thinnest parts are the complete silence on who is affected and why the standard itself is the right place for the change.

- The strongest support is the paper’s claim that it partially addresses a specific national body issue by clarifying what evaluations of a putative constant expression take place.
- The paper gestures toward existing practice by saying compilers tend to evaluate once and discard contract violations in non-constant contexts.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the proposed clarification, leaving the practical audience for the change entirely unspecified.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any case for why the standard, rather than guidance, a library, or existing implementation practice, must be changed.
