Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for why the Lakos Rule matters and for the existence of prior policy discussion, but it leaves several core justifications for standardization asserted rather than demonstrated, particularly around implementation practice and the necessity of a standard rather than a library-level solution.

- The strongest support is the paper’s clear rationale for why the Lakos Rule remains important, especially in light of C++26 contract assertions.
- The paper also adequately documents prior art and alternatives, including P1656R2 and the earlier policy options in P3005R0.
- The cases for who is affected and why the standard is needed rest mostly on claims about the library’s current practice and status quo, without the quantitative or institutional evidence shown to back those claims.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: references to Bloomberg’s BDE and the alleged incompatibility of major implementations are asserted but not substantiated with concrete details or evidence.
