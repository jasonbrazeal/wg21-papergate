Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a broad set of assertions about the need for always-enforced contract assertions, but almost all of its key claims are stated rather than demonstrated, leaving the case for standardization largely unsupported by concrete evidence or analysis. The thinnest support is in the recurring reliance on the same general references to existing practice without examples, details, or comparisons that would let a reviewer verify the claim.

- The strongest support is the paper’s invocation of prior EWG discussion, which at least signals that the design direction has been considered before, though the substance of that discussion is not shown.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of concrete prior art or production examples for always-enforced assertions, which leaves claims about existing practice and user familiarity entirely unsubstantiated.
- Another notable gap is the lack of any explanation for why a library solution is insufficient beyond the repeated statement that users are forced to duplicate logic.
