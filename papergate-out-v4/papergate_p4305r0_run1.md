Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization case: it substantiates why the design questions matter and shows engagement with the prior proposal, but it leaves most of the burden of justification unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified affected audience, no argument that standardization is necessary, and no treatment of coordination or implementation experience.

- The paper most clearly establishes its motivation by identifying unresolved design problems in the existing proposal, especially around structure-of-arrays layout and mathematical function support.
- It also establishes prior art and alternatives through direct engagement with P2964R5 and discussion of its layout assumptions and opt-in model.
- The claim that a library cannot fully address the problem is asserted but not substantiated beyond a single special-case remark.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any evidence about who is affected, why the standard should intervene, or how this would coordinate with existing and future SIMD and mathematical facilities.
