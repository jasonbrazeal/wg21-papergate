Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its own standardization: it establishes that prior work exists and connects its framing to Lamport’s distinction between safety and liveness, but it does not show who is affected, why a standard is the right vehicle, how the feature would interoperate, what implementation experience exists, or why a library would be inadequate. The thinnest areas concern the very questions that typically decide whether a proposal is ready for the committee.

- The strongest support is the cited prior art, which grounds the paper’s distinction between safety and liveness in established literature and follow-up work.
- A second point of support is the paper’s claim that progress guarantees belong to the correctness contract, even though the broader significance of that claim remains asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any identified user community or affected audience, leaving the proposal without a concrete constituency.
- Equally missing is any argument for why the standard, rather than a library or guideline, must provide the proposed treatment.
