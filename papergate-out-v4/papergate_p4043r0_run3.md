Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper leans heavily on general assertions that deferral is prudent, but it does not turn those assertions into a concrete case for standardization; nearly every relevant point is only claimed rather than established, and some areas are left entirely unaddressed. The thinnest support is in the domains that would most directly justify standardization: coordination, interoperability, and why a library solution cannot suffice.

- The strongest support is the invocation of the Concepts precedent, which at least gestures toward a plausible alternative path of removal and later refinement.
- The paper repeatedly references outside concerns and National Body comments, but it does not demonstrate how those concerns specifically require the proposed standardization action.
- The discussion of implementation experience offers only a hypothetical that implementations could continue experimenting, without evidence of what that experience has shown.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any treatment of coordination and interoperability needs, or of why a library-based approach would be inadequate.
