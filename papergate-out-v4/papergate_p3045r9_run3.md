Verdict: Excellent (12/14)

The paper offers a reasonably strong case in several areas, particularly in showing prior art, implementation experience, and the value of standardized vocabulary types for physical quantities. The support is thinnest when the paper reaches for broader claims about its audience, adoption, and the necessity of standardization over existing libraries, where assertions tend to outpace the evidence provided.

- The paper most convincingly establishes that the problem matters and that a standard library solution would address real interoperability and safety failures, with concrete examples and design alternatives already explored.
- Its implementation experience is well grounded in existing libraries, compiler availability, and lessons from production use, which gives the standardization discussion a practical foundation.
- The weakest part is the claim about who is affected and the scale of adoption, since statements about millions of developers, dominance on GitHub, and production feedback are asserted without supporting data in the paper.
- The argument for why a library will not suffice is the most glaring omission, as the need for certification and specification is asserted but not demonstrated with evidence that existing libraries actually fail in those settings.
