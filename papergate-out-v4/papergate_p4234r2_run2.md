Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial support for most of the burden of standardization, with concrete evidence of widespread implementation, real-world usage, and cross-language coordination. The support is thinnest where the argument depends on a brief assertion that the problem cannot be handled without a standards change, which is gestured at rather than demonstrated.

- The strongest element is implementation experience, where the paper points to default compiler behavior, compiler-explorer demonstrations, and an actual implementation attempt for one option.
- The case for why the issue matters is also well supported by conflict with compliance requirements and the practical impossibility of reclaiming `$` for other uses.
- Prior art and alternatives are meaningfully addressed, including reference to WG14 discussion and comparison among several possible approaches.
- The most glaring omission is the explanation of why a library or purely editorial change will not do, which rests on a single parenthetical observation rather than an argued case.
