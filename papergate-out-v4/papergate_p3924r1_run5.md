Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper gives only a thin account of the need for standardization, resting on a plausible observation about misleading terminology but doing little to connect that observation to a concrete problem or a demonstrated user need. The strongest material is the note that the core issue is real enough to have produced previous editorial fixes and a national body comment, but the case stops there. Most of what a proposal would need to show about affected users, alternatives, standardization rationale, interoperability, library remedies, and implementation experience is absent.

- The paper’s most concrete support is that prior mistakes of this kind have been corrected and at least one national body comment asks for related wording changes.
- It gestures at a problem by pointing to declarations that are not declarations and saying they have been used incorrectly, but it does not establish why that matters beyond the observation itself.
- The proposal does not identify who is affected or what practical harm follows from the current wording or usage.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any argument for why the standard is the right place to address the issue, especially absent consideration of alternatives, coordinate changes, or implementation experience.
