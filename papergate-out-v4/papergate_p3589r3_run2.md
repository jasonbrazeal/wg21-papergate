Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper articulates its goals clearly but offers mostly assertions rather than substantiated justification, leaving nearly every required argument for standardization unestablished. The thinnest support is the absence of concrete implementation experience, verified prior art, or evidence about affected users, which makes the case for a standard framework feel premature.

- The paper at least defines a plausible interoperability problem that a common framework could address.
- The connection to existing practice is asserted only through a brief reference to `[[gsl::suppress]]`, without detail or independent validation.
- The claims about educators, supplier divergence, and the inadequacy of libraries are repeated rather than supported with examples, data, or demonstrated alternatives.
