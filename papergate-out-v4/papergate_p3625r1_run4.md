Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper gestures toward a real authoring convenience, but it does not assemble much of the evidence needed to justify standardization. Its strongest material is the acknowledgment that related work and naming alternatives were at least considered, though even those discussions are brief and largely deferential to external sources. The case is thinnest around why this belongs in the standard, why a library cannot supply it, and whether anyone has actually implemented or used the proposed facility.

- The most concrete support is the paper’s naming discussion, which shows some thought about how the feature would read in practice and what existing practice might imply for its spelling.
- The author points to cppreference documentation and a Stack Overflow question as loose evidence of prior interest, but this supports only the existence of curiosity, not the breadth or depth of need.
- The paper offers no argument for why the standard, rather than a library or existing concept composition, is the right home for these facilities.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of implementation experience, leaving the proposal without evidence that the design is usable, teachable, or stable in practice.
