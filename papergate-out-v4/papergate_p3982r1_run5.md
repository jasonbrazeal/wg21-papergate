Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers only a thin case for its own standardization: its implementation experience is real, but nearly every argument about motivation, affected users, alternatives, and the need for a standard facility rests on assertion rather than demonstrated evidence. The thinnest support is the complete absence of any explanation for why a library solution would not suffice.

- The paper’s strongest point is that a patch series implementing the proposed wording changes exists in libstdc++.
- The claims about ergonomics, bug-proneness, and performance are stated repeatedly but never substantiated with data or examples that establish who benefits or how much.
- The survey of other languages is asserted rather than shown, so the claim that common slicing interfaces use `first, last` remains unverified.
- The paper never addresses why the desired behavior cannot be provided by a library, leaving a central standardization question unanswered.
