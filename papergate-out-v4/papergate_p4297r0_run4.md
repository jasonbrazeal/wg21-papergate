Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper’s strongest material concerns the surrounding landscape: it documents prior art, identifies the contested layering question, and shows that the relevant facilities have not received the specific architectural endorsement the proposal depends on. Where the support becomes thinner is on the need for standardization itself—especially the absence of a library alternative and the lack of demonstrated implementation experience for the proposed machinery.

- The paper establishes that the architecture claim sits between two competing published approaches and that no poll adopted the claimed layering.
- It demonstrates coordination concerns by identifying concrete dependencies and routing questions between the proposal and the Profiles work.
- It claims but does not establish that the affected population and deployment cost justify the standardization direction being advanced.
- The paper does not establish why a library-based approach would be insufficient, leaving one of the foundational arguments for standardization unaddressed.
