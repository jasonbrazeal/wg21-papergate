The paper offers only a thin foundation for its own standardization, relying on brief assertions rather than a connected argument. Its strongest material is a short statement of intent and a passing reference to existing lifetime rules, but there is no sustained evidence anywhere that the problem is real, the audience is identifiable, or the change belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere.

- The paper at least gestures toward a current language limitation, even if the claim is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The reference to [basic.life] and Core Guideline C.12 supplies the beginnings of prior art, though neither is developed into an actual comparison.
- The paper does not establish who is affected, offering no users, codebases, or motivating scenarios.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or library alternative, leaving the standardization need almost entirely unexamined.
