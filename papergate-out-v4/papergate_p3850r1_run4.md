Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a patchy case for standardization, with most of its central justifications asserted rather than demonstrated and two foundational requirements left unaddressed entirely. The thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why the standard, rather than a library, is the right vehicle, and the lack of concrete evidence connecting the proposed extensions to real user needs or existing practice.

- The most concrete support comes from the claim that most of the planned extensions already have implementations, including one in GCC, though this is asserted without detail on scope or maturity.
- The paper gestures toward established incremental practice in WG21 and points to prior work such as P2900R14, but does not connect that history to the specific extensions proposed here.
- The discussion of affected users relies on a single vendor-specific attribute in Clang and a general statement about frequency of requests, without evidence of breadth or depth of demand.
- Most glaringly, the paper never explains why the standard should adopt these extensions, nor why a library-based approach would be insufficient for any of them.
