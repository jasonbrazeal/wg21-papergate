Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, mostly rhetorical case for changing an existing name, and it does little to establish that the change belongs in the standard rather than in guidance or a library-level shim. The clearest support concerns an acknowledged usability problem with the current abbreviation, but the broader arguments about naming policy, ecosystem precedent, and implementability remain asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper does establish that `sat` is not an obvious abbreviation and that the proposed spelling improves clarity for novices.
- Its strongest claimed support is that `saturating_*` is the most common naming scheme in at least some code-search results, though this is reported rather than systematically shown.
- The paper asserts that the naming choice will set policy for future arithmetic variations, but it does not establish why that policy must be made in the C++ standard.
- It offers no implementation experience or evidence that the change cannot be adequately handled outside the standard, leaving the necessity of standardization essentially unaddressed.
