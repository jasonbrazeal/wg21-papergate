Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers scattered claims in favor of its position but very little sustained support for the case that standardization is warranted. Its strongest material concerns prior art and implementation experience, though even those points are asserted rather than demonstrated, while the burden of showing who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, and why a library suffices or not is almost entirely absent.

- The paper’s most concrete backing appears in references to existing practice, such as libstdc++ behavior and changes traceable to earlier proposals.
- Several claims gesture toward prior art but do not connect that precedent clearly to the proposal’s own design needs.
- The paper does not establish who would be affected by the change or what practical problem they currently face.
- The thinnest part of the case is the absence of any developed argument for why standardization, rather than a library solution or coordination outside the standard, is necessary.
