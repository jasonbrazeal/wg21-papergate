Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a partial but uneven case for its own standardization, with its strongest material concentrated on motivation and the claimed advantages over earlier profile-based work. The argument thins considerably when it comes to showing who is affected, how the feature would coordinate with existing tooling or standards, and whether there is any implementation experience to ground the design.

- The paper most clearly establishes why profile invalidation matters for reducing false positives while preserving safety goals, and it frames those stakes in concrete, senior-reviewer terms.
- It also meaningfully positions the work against prior art, especially P1179R1, by explaining how the proposed approach extends or upgrades that earlier reasoning.
- Support for the “why the standard” and “why a library will not do” claims is asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the standardization rationale underdeveloped.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established evidence about implementation experience, affected users, or interoperability with existing specifications and toolchains.
