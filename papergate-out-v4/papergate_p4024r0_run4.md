Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only a narrow foundation for its own standardization, centered on a general concern about domain-specific bias in proposals. That concern, while credible, is not connected to a demonstrated problem affecting identifiable users, nor to any need for a standard rather than guidance or process change. The thinnest areas are the absence of implementation experience and any argument that a library or existing practice could not achieve the same goal.

- The strongest support is the paper’s recognition that proposals from a single source may accidentally favor one domain over broader applicability.
- The paper gestures toward prior art and coordination by asking authors to communicate before conflicts reach the full working group, but it does not show what existing alternatives or processes it builds on.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience or evidence about who is affected, leaving the practical need for standardization unsubstantiated.
