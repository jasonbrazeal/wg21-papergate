Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers little concrete support for its own standardization. Its most substantial material is a set of references to past proposals and a general acknowledgment that profiles work has been ongoing, but the core claims about impact, affected parties, and necessity remain assertions rather than demonstrated findings. The thinnest areas are entirely absent: there is no discussion of coordination, interoperability, why a library cannot suffice, or any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s mention of multiple prior proposals on profiles, showing a documented history of committee interest and attempted progress.
- The paper asserts that profiles are the best known path to safer C++, but does not develop this claim with evidence or comparison to alternatives.
- The paper gestures at committee interest and broader pressure for memory safety, but does not establish who specifically would be affected by the lack of a standardized profiles framework.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any treatment of implementation experience, coordination needs, interoperability constraints, or why the same goals could not be met through a library.
