Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers some support for its standardization case, particularly in explaining the readability and expressiveness problems with the current grammar notation and in comparing the proposed notation to the existing syntax and a briefly considered alternative. However, the case is thin on several practical questions that usually matter for a change to the standard, and it does not establish who is concretely affected beyond a concern about accessibility for some users.

- The strongest support is for why the change matters, with credited passages showing that the current grammar is bloated, hard to read, and insufficiently expressive, and that purely editorial improvements could help.
- The paper also establishes that prior art and alternatives were considered, since it summarizes the limited features of the current grammar notation and mentions an alternative repetition syntax that was rejected.
- A notable omission is that the paper does not establish who is affected, so it is unclear which users, implementers, or editors would concretely benefit or how widespread the current difficulty is.
- The most glaring gap is the absence of any established case for why the standard is the right venue, why a library or external notation cannot solve the problem, or what implementation or coordination experience supports the change.
