Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a solid foundation for its standardization case in the areas that matter most: it clearly explains the motivation, connects the work to prior art and alternatives, and provides concrete implementation experience through a compiler explorer link. The support is thinnest where the paper relies on informal claims—the affected audience, the necessity of a standard solution, coordination concerns, and the inadequacy of library-only approaches are asserted rather than demonstrated with evidence or detailed examples.

- The strongest support comes from the established demonstration of prior art and alternatives, including the direct continuation of P3309R3 and the explicit discussion of why alternative implementation approaches are insufficient.
- The implementation experience is also well established, with a working compiler explorer link showing functions, member functions, and destructors in operation.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of established evidence for who is affected, where informal feedback such as emoji reactions is offered without names, contexts, or verifiable documentation.
- The paper also leaves the standardization necessity and library-only alternatives as claims, noting uncertainty about whether the standard should provide this at all without resolving that uncertainty with a clear technical argument.
