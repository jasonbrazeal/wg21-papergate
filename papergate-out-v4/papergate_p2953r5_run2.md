Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers moderate support for its own standardization, grounded mainly in implementation experience and a clear explanation of the language inconsistency. Its case is thinnest where it needs to show that the problem is significant enough to require a core-language change rather than being handled through guidance, library facilities, or existing practice.

- The paper establishes implementation experience by demonstrating a working Clang fork that compiled large codebases and by cataloguing the resulting divergences.
- The paper establishes both prior art and alternatives, including a previously proposed conservative design and evidence of EWG’s preference for the current approach.
- The paper does not establish why the standard itself must change, leaving the necessity of normative wording unsupported.
- The paper does not establish coordination or interoperability concerns with other proposals, implementations, or language features.
