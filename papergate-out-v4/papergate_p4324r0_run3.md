Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers meaningful support in one narrow respect—there is an actual implementation described—but most of the surrounding case is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the lack of a concrete affected-user story, the absence of any interoperability discussion, and the reliance on a library-oriented framing that is never tested against a clear reason why standardization is required.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, since the approach has been implemented in a GCC fork and is available for exploration on godbolt.
- The paper claims a basis in prior proposals and references related work, but it does not show how this design meaningfully refines those alternatives or answers their review feedback.
- The claim that various needs “have already been communicated” is too vague to establish who is actually asking for this or why it matters to them.
- The most glaring omission is coordination and interoperability, where the paper offers nothing about how the framework would fit with existing contracts mechanisms, tooling, or implementations.
