Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers some useful conceptual groundwork for standardization by motivating the need for clearer terminology around contracts and drawing on existing categories of erroneous behavior, but it falls short of demonstrating that standardization is warranted. The support is thinnest on the practical and procedural questions: who is concretely affected, why a library solution would not suffice, and whether the proposal has been tested in real implementations.

- The paper’s strongest contribution is its motivation, clearly explaining why existing wide/narrow contract terminology becomes counterintuitive for non-ignorable contracts.
- It also credibly engages with prior art and alternatives, situating its proposed taxonomy against C++26 erroneous behavior and earlier contract discussions.
- The case for why this belongs in the standard is asserted mainly through conceptual inconvenience rather than demonstrated through user or implementer impact.
- The most glaring omissions are any evidence of implementation experience, a meaningful analysis of who is affected, and an explanation of why a library or vocabulary outside the standard would not be adequate.
