Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers meaningful support in some areas but leaves the core standardization rationale thinly argued. The clearest strengths are its account of prior art and its implementation experience, while the case for why the work must be a standard—and cannot be delivered as a library—is essentially absent.

- The paper establishes solid prior art and alternatives by explicitly connecting its patterns to existing proposals, reference implementations, and formal models from the literature.
- Its implementation experience is grounded in named production and reference codebases, credibly demonstrating that the ideas have been exercised in practice.
- The argument for why a standard is needed rests on assertions about missing language facilities and shared vocabulary, but does not substantiate those claims against library-based alternatives.
- The paper does not establish why a library would be insufficient, leaving unanswered the most basic question a standardization proposal must address.
