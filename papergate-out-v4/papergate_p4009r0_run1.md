Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers a partial, uneven case for itself: it grounds the problem in real committee friction and shows that alternatives have been considered, but it does not seriously demonstrate who is blocked today, why a standard mechanism is required, or how the design would work in practice. The thinnest areas are the absence of implementation experience and any argument against a library-only solution, which leaves the standardization question largely unanswered.

- The strongest support is the established record of controversy and prior design discussion, which shows the paper is responding to a live and difficult committee situation.
- The paper also credibly establishes that alternative designs have been explored and found problematic, giving some reason to continue looking for a solution.
- The claims about affected users and the need for a standard rather than a library facility are asserted but not backed by evidence, weakening the urgency of the proposal.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience, which leaves no practical basis for judging whether the proposed approach is viable or beneficial.
