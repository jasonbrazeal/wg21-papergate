Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers very little support for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on a single mention of an in-review libstdc++ patch that is treated as evidence for several different requirements at once. Beyond that implementation claim, the document does not connect its proposal to user needs, standards-level problems, or the existing ecosystem in a way that would justify action by the committee. The thinnest areas are the absence of any argument for why the language or library standard is the right home, why a library solution would not suffice, and how the feature would fit with adjacent specifications or implementations.

- The strongest support is the claim of implementation experience, which at least gestures toward tested code in a major standard library.
- The same statement is stretched to cover who is affected and prior art, but it does not identify actual users, workloads, or comparable designs.
- The paper does not explain why standardization is necessary at all, including what breaks or remains impossible without committee action.
- Most glaringly, it never addresses interoperability, coordination, or why an ordinary library would not be sufficient.
