Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives credible support on naming and prior art, particularly for the claim that “floor” and “ceiling” division are the expected terms. That support thins considerably when it comes to showing who is concretely affected, how the feature would interoperate with existing standards, and whether there is implementation experience. The sharpest absences are any argument for why this belongs in the standard rather than a library, and any evidence that the feature has been tried in practice.

- The strongest case is the established precedent that floor and ceiling division are the familiar, searchable names, supported by references to other languages and existing conventions.
- The paper also credibly contrasts the proposed names with alternatives, showing that the other naming choices are worse.
- A weaker claim is that a broad range of programmers would look for these operations under those names, but the affected audience is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any case for standardization itself: the paper never explains why a library implementation would not be sufficient.
