Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers only a scattered, largely asserted case for its own standardization, with the thinnest support around who would be affected, why the standard is the right venue, and why a library solution is insufficient. It gestures at motivation, prior work, interoperability, and implementation experience, but leaves those threads as claims rather than demonstrated need.

- The strongest support is a concrete compiler fork and a compiler-explorer link suggesting at least some implementation experimentation.
- The paper names a standard library type, `std::inplace_vector`, for coordination, though it does not establish the broader interoperability case.
- It cites earlier proposals and a fork of prior ideas, but does not show why those alternatives are inadequate or why standardization is preferable.
- Most glaringly, the paper says nothing about who is affected or why a library-only approach will not do.
