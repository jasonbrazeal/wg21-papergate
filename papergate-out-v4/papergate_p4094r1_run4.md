Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers some useful historical grounding for the problem it identifies, particularly around prior executor designs and the consequences of unification, but it does not build a complete case that the proposed facility belongs in the standard. Its strongest material concerns alternatives and prior art, while its thinnest concerns the standard-specific rationale, teachability evidence, and proof that existing library implementations are insufficient.

- The paper clearly establishes that earlier executor models were unified and that this produced fragmentation concerns, supported by references to P0113R0 and the published record.
- The paper asserts broad production deployment and organizational interest, but does not substantiate those claims with evidence tied to the proposed design.
- The paper claims that standardization is needed and that a library will not suffice, but provides no measurement of teachability, no demonstration of why existing implementations are inadequate, and no coordination evidence beyond general concerns about duplication.
