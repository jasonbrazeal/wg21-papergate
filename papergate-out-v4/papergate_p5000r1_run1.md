Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin argument for its own standardization, relying on broad references to ongoing safety discussions and existing proposals rather than concrete demonstration of need. Its support is strongest when situating itself within the current Profiles work, but it leaves nearly all of the practical case—who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, what alternatives were weighed, and what experience exists—unaddressed.

- The clearest support comes from connecting the proposed goals to the existing Profiles proposals and the longer-term direction outlined in P2000.
- The paper claims the safety discourse matters, but does not move from that general observation to a specific, established need for this particular standardization document.
- The proposal neither identifies the affected users or implementers nor explains why standardization, rather than guidance or a library approach, is required.
- Most conspicuously, it offers no implementation experience, coordination plan, or interoperability analysis to ground the proposal in practice.
