Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only scattered support for its standardization case, mostly through assertions about algorithmic efficiency and Boost-inspired design, but it does not connect those claims to a demonstrated need for ISO standardization. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of evidence about who is affected, why existing libraries are insufficient, implementation experience, and how the proposed facility would coordinate with the current standard.

- The strongest support lies in the paper’s claims that the proposed algorithms offer performance advantages over alternatives and follow established Boost Graph Library visitor conventions.
- The paper gestures at prior art by comparing Tarjan’s algorithm with Kosaraju’s and by listing excluded algorithms, but it does not establish how this prior art bears on the need for standardization.
- The paper does not identify any affected users, communities, or domains, leaving the motivating problem abstract.
- Most glaringly, it offers no argument that this functionality cannot be served by an existing or proposed library, nor any implementation experience to show the design is ready for standardization.
