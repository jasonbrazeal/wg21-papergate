The paper offers only partial support for its own standardization, with its strongest material concentrated in implementation experience and prior art. The case thins substantially around the core arguments: why the problem matters, who is affected, why the standard should address it, and how the work coordinates with existing or future standardization. The most serious absence is any argument for why a library cannot solve the problem.

- The paper’s strongest support is concrete implementation experience, with both a Boost implementation of an early version and a partial standalone implementation cited.
- Prior art and alternatives are also grounded, pointing to earlier WG21 papers and existing code that informed or instantiates the design.
- The weakest established element is the absence of any reasoning for why a library will not do, leaving the burden of standardization largely unaddressed where it matters most.
