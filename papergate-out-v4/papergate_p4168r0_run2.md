Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper offers solid grounding for several parts of its standardization case, particularly in documenting divergent implementation behavior, prior standardization attempts, and existing implementation experience, but its support is markedly thinner when it comes to showing who is concretely affected and why work cannot simply continue outside the standard.

- The strongest support comes from the detailed evidence that implementations already diverge from one another and from the standard’s wording, which makes the current situation demonstrably unstable.
- The paper also establishes meaningful prior art and alternatives by linking its proposal to earlier papers and open LWG issues, showing this is not a novel or unexamined problem.
- The case for standardization itself is only claimed rather than established, since the paper relies on the general risk of breaking existing code and the persistence of an open issue rather than showing a compelling standards-level need.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of established evidence about who is affected and what real-world cost they bear, leaving the practical stakes of inaction largely asserted rather than demonstrated.
