Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin basis for its own standardization, resting almost entirely on an allusion to prior discussion rather than a developed argument. Its clearest, though still incomplete, support concerns the existence of past standardization conversation around `noexcept`, while it leaves essentially every other necessary question about need, scope, and practice unaddressed.

- The paper gestures toward the absence of a current `noexcept` policy and the historical Lakos Rule as relevant prior art, but even this connection is asserted rather than explained.
- The document does not identify who would be affected by adopting the proposed policy or what problems they currently face.
- It provides no discussion of why a standard change is required rather than guidance or a library-level convention.
- Most glaringly, the paper offers no implementation experience and no account of coordination or interoperability with the existing standard library.
