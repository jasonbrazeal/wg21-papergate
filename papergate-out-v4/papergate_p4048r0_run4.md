Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s strongest support rests on the undeniable twenty-one-year absence of standard networking facilities and the existence of a defined, multi-paper effort with shipping implementations. Its case is thinnest where it treats coordination, broad relevance, and implementation experience as self-evident consequences of listing participating libraries and adopters rather than demonstrating how the proposed standardization actually resolves the cross-cutting dependencies it identifies.

- The paper firmly establishes why standardized networking matters by pointing to the standard’s long-standing lack of sockets, DNS, and TLS despite pervasive industry need.
- Prior art and a concrete standardization path are established through the cited Network Endeavor papers, their two-stage plan, and the critique of the Networking TS’s eroded coherence.
- The paper claims but does not establish who is affected or how the two programming models will interoperate in practice, relying on assertions that coordination is the only missing ingredient.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper offers no argument for why a library will not do, leaving the threshold question of standards necessity entirely unaddressed.
