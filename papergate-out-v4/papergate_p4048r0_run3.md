Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper carries a good deal of its case in the architecture and coordination sections, where it shows that the technical groundwork exists and that the pieces are meant to fit together. The argument is much thinner when it moves from “this is technically possible and the work is underway” to “this must be standardized,” and it never explains why the existing shipping libraries could not remain the delivery vehicle.

- The strongest part of the case is the concrete prior art and the coordinated companion papers, which together show a coherent program rather than an isolated proposal.
- The paper also successfully establishes why coroutine-native I/O matters as a design direction, even if it leans on rhetoric about the twenty-one-year delay rather than detailed evidence.
- The discussion of implementation experience is suggestive but not yet persuasive, since it rests mainly on the author’s own libraries and early-stage adopters without independent validation.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library will not do, leaving the central standardization question effectively unaddressed.
