Verdict: Strong (9/14)

The paper’s strongest support comes from concrete implementation experience and a credible line of prior art, but several essential claims about affected users, the need for language-level treatment, and interoperability benefits rest on assertion rather than evidence. The case for standardization is therefore uneven: the existence and usability of the approach are well supported, while the argument that it belongs in the standard rather than in libraries remains thin.

- The paper establishes implementation experience most convincingly through multiple referenced libraries in active use and a production-ready reference implementation.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is well grounded, with explicit acknowledgment of foundational work in Boost.Asio and C++20 coroutines.
- The weakest support is in why the standard is needed, since the argument for language-level action over a library solution is asserted but not demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the failure to establish who is affected beyond the authors’ own projects, leaving the breadth and urgency of the problem unclear.
