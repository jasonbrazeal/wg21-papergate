Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, largely asserted basis for its own standardization, with most of the necessary case left implicit or gestured at rather than demonstrated. The strongest material concerns the existence of related prior work and ongoing coordination efforts, but the argument is weakest where it needs to explain who is affected, why standardization specifically is required, and what implementation experience actually shows.

- The paper at least acknowledges recent unsuccessful proposals and points to SG23 as now leading documentation and coordination, which suggests some awareness of prior art and current committee activity.
- The claim that insufficient agreed-on documentation causes confusion and blocks meaningful contributions is asserted but not supported with examples, affected groups, or concrete consequences.
- The paper never establishes who is affected by the absence of the proposed work, leaving the practical stakes of standardization unclear.
- Most glaringly, it offers no case for why a standard is necessary, why a library or other non-standard mechanism would not suffice, or what implementation experience grounds the proposal.
