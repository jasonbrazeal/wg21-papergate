Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a thin basis for its own standardization, leaning heavily on the observation that related adopted papers introduced inconsistencies. Its support is largely implicit or asserted in passing, with almost no evidence about who is affected, why a library cannot address the issue, or what implementation experience exists. The thinnest areas are the ones that usually carry the most weight in establishing need.

- The strongest support is the repeated claim that independently adopted papers created gaps and inconsistent APIs, though this is never demonstrated with specifics.
- The paper gestures at avoiding API proliferation by providing the information directly, but this is presented as a preference rather than a demonstrated need.
- It offers no substantiation of prior art or alternatives beyond the vague reference to the related papers.
- Most glaringly, it does not establish who is affected, why the standard is the right venue, or that a library solution would be inadequate.
