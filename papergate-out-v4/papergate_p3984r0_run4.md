Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization remains largely a set of assertions rather than a developed case, with every category of need identified only in broad or cited terms rather than demonstrated in the text itself. The thinnest areas are those where the reader would expect concrete evidence—prior art, implementation experience, and interoperability—but finds only invocations of prototypes and general warnings about fragmentation.

- The strongest support is the repeated identification of a real coordination problem: the proliferation of incompatible safety facilities, each with its own invocation style.
- The paper gestures toward implementation experience through references to prototypes and an experimental compiler implementation, but does not substantiate what was learned or how it validates the design.
- The discussion of alternatives and prior art remains the most glaring omission, since naming other approaches is not the same as comparing them or showing why they are insufficient.
