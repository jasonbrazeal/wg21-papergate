Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper’s internal case for standardization rests almost entirely on asserted importance and a gesture toward prior art, leaving the practical and procedural grounding largely absent. The thinnest areas are those that would show who is affected, how the work fits with existing practice, and whether there is any experience behind the approach.

- The clearest support is an aspirational argument that progress guarantees should be treated as part of the correctness contract in C++ concurrency facilities.
- The paper points to an external write-up as the main source for prior art and alternatives, but does not itself establish how those ideas compare or apply.
- It offers no account of the affected users, implementations, or domains, and no interoperability or coordination analysis.
- There is no evidence of implementation experience or any demonstration that the goals cannot be met by a library.
