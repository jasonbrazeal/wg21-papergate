Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper offers a narrow but genuine evidentiary base: its implementation patch and benchmark thread credibly demonstrate that the design has been tried, but nearly every other claim about ergonomic benefit, affected users, precedent, and standardization need is asserted rather than shown. The thinnest support lies in the absence of concrete evidence that existing practice or widespread error motivates the change.

- The submission is strongest on implementation experience, with a patch series and benchmark details showing the proposed wording has been put into practice.
- The arguments for prior art and alternatives are asserted through a general claim about other languages, but the supporting survey is not presented in the paper.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper does not establish why the standard, rather than a library extension, is the necessary vehicle for the change.
