Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper gives uneven support for its own standardization: its engagement with prior art is concrete and credible, but most of the burden of proof—why the feature matters, what the standard should contain, who it serves, and whether it cannot live as a library—rests on assertion rather than demonstration. The thinnest areas are the absence of any affected-user analysis and the lack of a case that standardization is the right venue at all.

- The strongest part of the paper is its treatment of prior art and alternatives, with specific references and evidence of compilation against existing implementations.
- The paper claims implementation experience through an included appendix and reported compiler results, but does not independently establish that this experience validates the need for standardization.
- The document asserts, without support, both why the feature matters and why it belongs in the standard rather than in a library.
- Most glaringly, the paper never identifies who is affected by the proposal, leaving the motivating audience and impact entirely unexamined.
