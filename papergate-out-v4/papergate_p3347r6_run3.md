Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives real support to the need for standardization in the areas of motivation and prior art, but it leaves several essential parts of the case as bare assertions, especially the scope of affected users and the argument that a library solution cannot suffice. The thinnest support is in demonstrating who is concretely affected and why the work cannot be done outside the standard.

- The paper clearly establishes that current lifetime rules make ordinary operations on invalid pointers dangerously unspecified and inconsistent with long-standing practice.
- It credibly connects the proposal to existing related papers and describes a compatible, building-block relationship with prior work on pointer lifetime-end zap.
- It asserts, but does not demonstrate, that the affected community is broad or that the standard is the only viable venue rather than a library or coding discipline.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any established evidence about who is affected, leaving the practical reach of the problem largely hypothetical in the paper.
