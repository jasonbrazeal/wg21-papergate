Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: it points to some collaborative drafting work, but it does not make a clear case for why this material belongs in the standard, who would be affected by its absence, or why non-standard venues would be insufficient. The thinnest parts are the lack of any demonstrated need for normative action and the absence of alternatives analysis beyond naming a few prior documents.

- The most concrete support is the existence of draft wording developed collaboratively as a branch of the C++ working draft.
- The paper gestures toward prior work and possible organizational approaches, but does not show that those alternatives were assessed against the proposed approach.
- The paper offers only general claims about the usefulness of cataloging undefined and IFNDR behavior without connecting that usefulness to actual user or implementer impact.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why the standard, rather than a separate document, is the right venue.
