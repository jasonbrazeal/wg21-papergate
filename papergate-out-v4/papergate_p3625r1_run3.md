Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers meaningful groundwork in its discussion of naming alternatives and its pointers to existing community requests, but it does not yet build a case that this facility belongs in the standard. The support is thinnest where the proposal needs to show why a library solution is insufficient, what implementation experience exists, and why standardization rather than shared code is the right remedy.

- The paper reasonably documents prior usage and weighs alternative names against likely misinterpretations.
- The motivating need is asserted through the authors’ own project experience, but the affected audience is not broadened beyond that claim.
- The paper gives no account of implementation experience or coordination with existing practice.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any argument for why a library-level concept cannot already serve this purpose.
