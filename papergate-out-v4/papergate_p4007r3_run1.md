Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gestures toward a case for standardization, but much of its support remains asserted rather than demonstrated, and the thinnest parts are exactly where the standards committee most needs concrete evidence: the necessity of a standard solution, interoperability, and viability outside a library.

- The strongest support is the mention of Croydon resolving several classified issues, though the details are too thin to establish implementation experience.
- The discussion of coroutine-native I/O and `std::execution` as complementary domains suggests the authors are thinking about design fit, but it does not yet ground the proposal in prior art or alternatives.
- The claimed stack-growth problem and affected users are stated rather than shown, leaving the motivating harm unestablished.
- The paper does not establish why a standard is needed, how it would coordinate with existing facilities, or why a library would not suffice.
