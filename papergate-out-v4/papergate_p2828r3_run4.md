Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only preliminary support for its own standardization, mainly through claims about existing compiler behavior and a reported experimental implementation, but it does not yet demonstrate why the standard itself must change. The thinnest areas are the absence of a rationale tied directly to normative wording and the lack of evidence that a library solution is impossible or that the affected community extends beyond anecdotal reports.

- The strongest support is the statement that major compilers already accept the relevant code through copy elision, which at least suggests some industry convergence.
- The paper also reports an implementation of its proposed approach and lists examples it accepts or leaves unchanged, though this is asserted without detail.
- The discussion of alternatives and compiler design space is presented, but the paper does not establish that any of those alternatives are unsuitable enough to require standardization.
- Most notably, the paper offers no argument for why the standard needs this change or why a library-level solution would not suffice.
