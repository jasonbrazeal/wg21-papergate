Verdict: Strong (8/14)

The paper does establish that the problem is real and that a working implementation exists, but it leaves most of the surrounding case for standardization asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support concerns who is actually affected and what alternatives have been tried, which makes the need harder to weigh against the cost of adding a standard facility.

- The strongest support is the concrete implementation, which shows the proposed mechanics can be made to work.
- The paper also credibly grounds the motivation in independent code-review findings, suggesting the need is not purely hypothetical.
- The claims about why a standard facility is preferable, and why a library solution is insufficient, are stated but not backed by evidence or comparison.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any discussion of who would use this or what alternatives were considered.
