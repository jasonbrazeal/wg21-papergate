Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers some genuine grounding for its motivation and its account of prior art, but the overall case for standardization remains thin where it matters most: it does not establish why this belongs in the standard or why a library would be insufficient. The strongest material concerns the recognized fragmentation problem and the historical precedents, while the weakest concerns the absence of demonstrated need, implementation breadth, and coordination evidence beyond the author’s own projects.

- The paper most credibly establishes that executor-model fragmentation is a real concern and that the committee has precedent for fusing async models, particularly in `task`.
- The discussion of prior art is appropriately restrained and grounded in the published record, especially the documented terminology shift away from continuation framing.
- Claims about production use and interoperability rest largely on the author’s own libraries and on assertions about affected organizations, without independent confirmation of how widespread the need is.
- The paper does not establish why standardization is necessary as opposed to a library solution, nor does it show implementation experience sufficient to support a standard.
