The paper’s strongest support lies in its implementation experience, which is concrete and tied to real compilers and architectures. Its thinnest support is in explaining why a library-only solution cannot suffice and why this change belongs in the standard itself, where the reasoning is asserted more than demonstrated.

- The paper clearly establishes that the change matters and that the proposed direction has been implemented and tested in real toolchains.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is well grounded, showing the design was shaped by committee feedback and hardware constraints.
- The case for coordination and interoperability, and for why a library extension would not be enough, is claimed but not adequately demonstrated with evidence.
