Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s support for standardization is uneven: it demonstrates real implementation experience, but the core rationale—why the feature matters, who needs it, and why only the standard can provide it—rests on assertions that are mentioned rather than substantiated. The thinnest part is the absence of a clear, evidence-backed case for the problem’s significance or the limitations of existing library-level solutions.

- The strongest element is the concrete implementation experience cited from libc++, Clang, and related parallel proposals, which shows the feature is feasible in practice.
- The paper claims broad existing use and recurring requests from the community, but these are listed without the depth of examples, frequency data, or impact explanation needed to establish who is affected.
- Glaringly, the paper never establishes why the problem matters in the first place, leaving the motivating need for standardization essentially unargued.
