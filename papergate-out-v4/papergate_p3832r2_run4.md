Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is largely asserted rather than demonstrated: it names a plausible user need and provides a reference implementation, but does not develop the surrounding case for why that need is common, why existing alternatives are insufficient, or why the facility belongs in the standard rather than in a library. The thinnest areas are those that require evidence beyond the motivating scenario, especially who is affected in practice and what prevents a non-standard solution from serving those users.

- The only fully established element is implementation experience, backed by a linked reference implementation.
- The motivating claim about error-prone, verbose user code is repeated frequently, but it is not connected to concrete cases, usage patterns, or reported defects that would show the problem is significant.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives identifies related standard facilities but does not establish why they cannot adequately cover the stated need or what a new standard facility would improve for real users.
- The case for placing this facility in the standard, rather than in a library, is asserted but essentially unsupported, leaving the proposal’s standardization rationale incomplete.
