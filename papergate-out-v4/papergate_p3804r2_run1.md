Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers only selective support for its own standardization, and much of its reasoning rests on assertions rather than demonstrated need. The strongest material concerns design history and the considered alternatives, but the case is thinnest when it comes to showing who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, and what implementation experience actually teaches.

- The paper establishes that it is responding to known design concerns and that an alternative backend-level approach was considered.
- The paper claims, without fully establishing, that there is no portable way for a backend to check a receiver’s stop token and that the frontend/backend separation creates meaningful coordination challenges.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the proposed change, why standardization is necessary, or why a library-only solution would be inadequate.
- The paper does not present implementation experience in a way that substantiates the proposal’s readiness for standardization.
