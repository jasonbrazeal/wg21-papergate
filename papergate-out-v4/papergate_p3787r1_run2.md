Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, mostly self-referential case for standardization: it identifies a likely oversight and points to consistency with P2248R8, but does not develop the affected audience, alternatives, coordination concerns, or why a library solution is insufficient. The strongest support is the assertion that the change mirrors an already-adopted pattern, while the weakest areas leave the reader without evidence of real-world impact, implementation specifics, or standardization necessity.

- The paper’s strongest support is its claim that the proposal simply extends an existing, already-standardized change from P2248R8 to a previously omitted algorithm family.
- Its treatment of prior art is minimal, relying mainly on the parallel to P2248R8 and a passing mention of P3217R0 without substantial comparison or analysis.
- The most glaring omission is any discussion of who is affected by the current specification or why the problem cannot be addressed through a library solution.
