Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper gives its most convincing support to the underlying motivation and to the existence of plausible precedent, but it leaves several essential parts of the standardization case largely unargued. The thinnest areas are the absence of any description of who is affected, how implementations would coordinate, or what practical experience supports the change.

- The paper clearly establishes why the current rules can be too conservative and why that matters for real synchronization patterns.
- It also establishes that prior art and alternatives favor adjusting the rules without rewriting the happens-before relation itself.
- The case for why standardization, rather than a library solution, is needed remains asserted but not demonstrated.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any account of who is affected or how the change would fare across implementations.
