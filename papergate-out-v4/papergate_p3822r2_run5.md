Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper gives only narrow, uneven support for its own standardization, mainly through a working implementation and links to examples. Its argument is thinnest around the core justifications: why the feature matters in practice, who is affected, what alternatives exist, why the standard is the right vehicle, and how it interacts with the wider ecosystem remain largely unsubstantiated.

- The clearest strength is implementation experience, with a Clang fork and Compiler Explorer examples demonstrating the proposed syntax in use.
- The paper asserts a reason the feature matters, but supports it with only a brief statement about conditional non-throwing assertions in generic programming.
- Prior art and inconsistency with function declarations are mentioned, but not developed into a convincing case that the language must change.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any demonstrated need for standardization specifically, including why a library-level solution would not suffice.
