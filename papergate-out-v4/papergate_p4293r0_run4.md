Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow, conditional case for its own standardization, resting mainly on a disagreement with another proposal rather than on demonstrated user need or implementation demand. The support is thinnest where the paper never moves beyond assertion: it does not identify who would use the facility, why a library solution is insufficient, or what experience exists with the proposed design.

- The strongest support is the paper’s connection to a live design dispute over decay-copying in sender/receiver completion signatures, which at least gives the proposal a context in which it might be useful.
- The discussion of prior art leans entirely on P4288 and its taxonomy of completion signatures, so the proposal has some grounding in existing committee conversation but little independent standing.
- The paper does not establish who is affected by the current state of affairs, which leaves the motivating problem abstract and unverified.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience, coordination analysis, or argument for why the standard library cannot host the facility.
