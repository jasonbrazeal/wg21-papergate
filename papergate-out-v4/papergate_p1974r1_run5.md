Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow basis for its own standardization: it acknowledges prior work and alternatives, but leaves most of the motivating case, the affected constituency, and the need for standard intervention either unstated or merely asserted. The thinnest support lies in the absence of any account of who is affected, why a library solution is insufficient, or what implementation experience suggests about the proposal’s viability.

- The paper’s strongest support is its recognition of prior proposals and alternative approaches, which situates the idea within existing committee discussion.
- The claim that persistent `constexpr` allocations would unlock complex compile-time data structures is asserted as motivation, but not developed into a concrete case for standardization.
- The paper does not identify any affected users, domains, or codebases, leaving the practical demand for the feature unestablished.
- Most glaringly, there is no implementation experience or evidence of coordinated use, and no explanation of why this cannot be achieved as a library solution.
