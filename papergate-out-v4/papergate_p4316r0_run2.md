Verdict: None (0/14)

The paper offers only a bare, high-level assertion about why profiles matter, and almost no supporting argument for standardization itself. Its thinnest areas are the complete absence of discussion about affected users, prior work, standardization rationale, interoperability, library alternatives, or implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the paper’s statement that profiles are an incremental path toward stronger safety guarantees beyond changes to the base language.
- The paper does not identify who would be affected by the proposed feature or how existing code and tooling would interoperate with it.
- The paper gives no account of prior art, alternatives, or why the work belongs in the standard rather than in a library.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience, which leaves the standardization need almost entirely unsubstantiated.
