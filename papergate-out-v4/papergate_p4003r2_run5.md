Verdict: Strong (10/14)

The paper’s strongest asset is concrete evidence: it demonstrates a working implementation on multiple platforms and situates the design clearly within existing practice and a companion rationale. The case is much thinner, however, when it moves from “this works” to “this must be standardized,” especially in showing why a library cannot provide the same capability or how the feature would interoperate with the rest of the ecosystem.

- The implementation experience is well established through named, accessible projects and a reproducible demonstration.
- The paper credibly grounds its design in prior art, including Boost.Asio and a companion standards proposal.
- The argument for why the feature belongs in the standard rests mostly on general assertions rather than demonstrated library insufficiency.
- Coordination with other async models is asserted as a boundary benefit, but the paper does not establish how that interoperability would be realized in practice.
