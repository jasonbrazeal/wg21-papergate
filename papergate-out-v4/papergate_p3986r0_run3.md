Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers a focused but narrow justification for its own standardization, with its strongest support concentrated in explaining the wording problem it addresses and, to a lesser extent, gesturing at alternatives and the inadequacy of a library-only approach. The case is thinnest around who is concretely affected, why this belongs in the standard rather than elsewhere, how it coordinates with existing specifications, and whether there is any implementation experience to validate the proposed strategy.

- The paper clearly establishes that the current wording strategy for `std::execution` creates a real specification burden, which gives the proposal its most solid footing.
- It claims, without fully establishing, that a library-only fix would inherit the same mechanical and normative rigidity of code-based specification.
- It gestures at prior art and alternative wording strategies but does not develop them into a comparison that shows this approach is preferred.
- The paper offers no evidence about who is affected, no coordination or interoperability analysis, and no implementation experience, leaving major parts of the standardization case unaddressed.
