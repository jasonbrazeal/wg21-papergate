Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only scattered, often conclusory support for its own standardization, with several key burdens left entirely unaddressed. The strongest material is a gesture toward prior work and some rationale for reifying a condition already present in the standard, but the absence of implementation experience, affected users, or a library-only alternative leaves the case thin.

- The paper at least identifies an existing in-standard formulation of the requirement it wants to replace, giving a starting point for interoperability concerns.
- The rationale for broader generic programming use is asserted rather than demonstrated with concrete code or user need.
- The paper never establishes who is affected by the absence of the proposed facility or what practical problems they encounter today.
- The most glaring omission is the complete lack of implementation experience, leaving no evidence that the concept works as specified or would be adopted in practice.
