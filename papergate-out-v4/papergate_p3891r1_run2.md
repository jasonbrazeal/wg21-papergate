Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper makes a plausible editorial case that the C++ grammar notation would become more readable, but it offers little evidence beyond that assertion. Its support is thinnest on the questions that would turn an editorial convenience into a standardization case: who is actually burdened today, what prior practice exists in other standards or tools, and whether there is any implementation or drafting experience.

- The strongest support is for the motivating readability problem, since the paper identifies concrete grammar rules that would be simplified by grouping and repetition.
- The treatment of prior art and alternatives is present but underdeveloped, mentioning only a briefly considered alternative and a summary of the current notation without a comparison to established practice elsewhere.
- The paper asserts that the new notation is feasible in the standard draft but does not establish why this requires standardization rather than remaining an editorial convention.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any implementation experience or demonstration that the new notation can be parsed, rendered, and maintained across the standard’s existing tooling and workflows.
