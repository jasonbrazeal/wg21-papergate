Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin, almost entirely unsupported case for standardization: a single sentence gestures at the benefit of restructuring nonterminals, and nothing else in the document establishes that the problem affects real users, that the standard is the right place to solve it, or that the change is workable in practice.

- The only support the paper provides is a general statement that it aims to improve the situation by introducing new nonterminals and relocating their definitions.
- The argument for why this matters is asserted rather than demonstrated, with no concrete motivation or consequences tied to the change.
- The paper leaves every other question about need unanswered, including who is affected, what alternatives exist, why a library or non-standard approach would not suffice, and whether there is any interoperability or implementation experience.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any supporting evidence beyond the initial claim, leaving the standardization need effectively unargued.
