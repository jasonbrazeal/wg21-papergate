Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is largely assertive rather than demonstrative: it repeatedly states the problem, the affected audience, and the need for a standard facility, but it does not develop those claims with evidence, comparison, or analysis. The strongest concrete support is the existence of a reference implementation, while the rest of the case rests on generalizations and brief appeals to consistency with existing facilities. The thinnest area is coordination and interoperability, where the paper offers no indication of how the proposal would fit with related standardization efforts or existing practice.

- The paper establishes implementation experience through a publicly available reference implementation, which is its most concrete form of support.
- The paper’s claims about why the facility matters and who is affected are repeated but not substantiated with examples, usage data, or detailed scenarios.
- The paper gestures at prior art and consistency with `std::lock` and `std::try_lock` but does not actually compare alternatives or show why existing non-standard solutions are insufficient.
- The paper is silent on coordination and interoperability, leaving unaddressed how the proposal relates to other work in the committee or the broader ecosystem.
