Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only scattered, undeveloped signals that there may be problems worth addressing, but it does not assemble them into a case for standardization. Its strongest material is a handful of implementation and design observations, while the foundational questions of affected users, the need for a standard solution, and feasibility outside the standard are left entirely unargued.

- The clearest support comes from concrete notes that existing implementations or related proposals have already encountered the relevant behavior.
- The discussion of prior art gestures at comparable designs and a possible alternative, though it stops short of weighing them as viable paths.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the issue in any substantive way.
- Most glaringly, it never establishes why the problem cannot be solved by a library or why standardization, rather than another remedy, is required.
