Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a narrow statement of purpose and does not build a surrounding case for standardization; most of the reviewers’ questions are left entirely unaddressed. The thinnest area is the complete absence of evidence about how the proposed grammar changes would work in practice or fit into the existing ecosystem.

- The strongest support is a single sentence indicating the paper’s goal of adding non-terminals and placing definitions in the relevant subclauses.
- It does not establish who would be affected by the change or what problems those users currently face.
- It provides no discussion of prior art, alternative approaches, coordination with other specifications, or why a library solution would be inadequate.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of any implementation experience to show that the proposed change is viable beyond the paper’s own assertion.
