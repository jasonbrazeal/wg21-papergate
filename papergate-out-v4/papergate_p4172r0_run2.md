Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers substantial support for the technical necessity and implementability of its proposed vocabulary, with the strongest evidence concentrated in the analysis of prior art, the interoperability model, and the existence of production-oriented implementations. The case is much thinner where it moves from asserting that these facilities affect every audience to showing that the claimed populations actually need the standard rather than merely benefit from it, and the argument that a library solution cannot suffice relies on several statements that remain asserted rather than demonstrated.

- The paper establishes most convincingly that no adequate prior art or alternative design removes the need for a shared vocabulary, and that the proposed protocol boundary is a deliberate, interoperable choice.
- The implementation experience section is well supported by identifiable, maintained codebases and recorded design decisions.
- The discussion of why a standard is required rests partly on broad historical claims and the asserted impossibility of library-level solutions without sufficient demonstration of those constraints.
- The most obvious gap is the failure to substantiate who is affected, beyond general statements about application developers and a brief reference to ecosystem history.
