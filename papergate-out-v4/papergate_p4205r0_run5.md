Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers a workable core of implementation experience and prior-art discussion, but its broader argument for standardization rests largely on assertions about inconvenience and inconsistency rather than demonstrated impact or interoperability. The thinnest support appears wherever the paper needs to move from “this is awkward” to “this is a problem the standard must solve,” especially regarding affected users and why a library cannot address the gap.

- The strongest support is the existence of a concrete implementation in the Beman Project, which shows the API can be built and tested.
- The paper also credibly establishes relevant prior art by connecting its approach to existing searcher practice and implementation techniques.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of evidence showing who is actually affected and how seriously current limitations disrupt real codebases or use cases.
