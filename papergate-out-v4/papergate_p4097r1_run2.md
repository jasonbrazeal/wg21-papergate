Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper leans heavily on committee sentiment, polling, and a handful of product references, but it does not close the gap between general enthusiasm for a model and a demonstrated need to standardize this particular facility. The thinnest areas are the positive case for ISO involvement, the limitations of non-standard solutions, and concrete implementation evidence beyond author-maintained projects and secondhand reports.

- The strongest support appears in the cited LEWG poll and shipping-product claims, which at least gesture toward real interest and adoption for the sender/receiver approach.
- The treatment of coordination and interoperability rests largely on reported national-body concerns about teachability and beginner experience, without showing how standardization would resolve them.
- The paper is notably silent on why a library solution would be insufficient and why the standard itself is the necessary venue.
- The most glaring omission is implementation experience: despite naming projects and products, the paper does not establish broad, diverse, and independent use sufficient to validate the design for standardization.
