Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper assembles a credible case that the current contracts design has generated unusually strong opposition and that plausible alternative spellings exist, but it does little to establish what a standardized alternative would actually require or why the existing facility cannot serve as the substrate for those audiences. The support is thinnest where the standardization question is most direct: there is no demonstration that a library solution is insufficient, and no implementation experience is offered.

- The strongest support lies in the paper’s evidence of real, vehement objections to the draft facility and the existence of design alternatives palatable to those opposing audiences.
- The paper credits prior suggestions such as D3894R0 and shows how they could be adapted, which establishes that the design space has been actively explored.
- The claim that affected audiences can build what they need in C++26 is asserted rather than substantiated, leaving the population and their constraints vague.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why a library will not do, nor does it offer implementation experience for the proposed direction.
