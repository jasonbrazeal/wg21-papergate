Verdict: Weak (1/14)

The paper offers only a thin, largely anecdotal foundation for its own standardization case, leaving most of the necessary justification unaddressed. The strongest material concerns the motivation and a passing reference to prior practice, but even those remain asserted rather than demonstrated, while questions of affected users, interoperability, implementability, and the unique role of the standard are essentially untouched.

- The paper at least gestures toward why the problem matters by invoking historical delays in compiler adoption, though it does not connect that concern to the present proposal.
- It makes a single claim about a post-2012 model as prior art, but offers no evidence that this model exists or that the proposal preserves it.
- The most glaring omission is the absence of any account of who would be affected by the change or how it would interact with existing implementations.
