Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a thin, largely self-referential case for standardization, resting almost entirely on general assertions of usefulness and observed inconsistencies rather than evidence, examples, or concrete demand. The thinnest parts are the complete absence of discussion about why a standard is needed, why a library solution would be insufficient, coordination with existing features, or any implementation experience.

- The strongest support is the acknowledgment that related adopted papers introduced inconsistencies and gaps that a unified facility might address.
- The paper also gestures toward prior discussion by noting that an earlier direction involving `is_constexpr(r)` was dropped after LEWG questions.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper never establishes why the standard, rather than a library or existing mechanism, is the right place for the facility.
