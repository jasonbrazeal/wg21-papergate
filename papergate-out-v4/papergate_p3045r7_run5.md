Verdict: Strong (11/14, close to Excellent)

The paper offers solid support in the areas that traditionally matter most for a mature library proposal: prior art, interoperability concerns, and implementation experience are all well established, with concrete references to existing libraries and use cases. The case is thinnest where the proposal most needs to persuade a committee—namely, in showing why only a standard library facility can meet user needs rather than a widely adopted third-party library, and in demonstrating the breadth of the affected audience beyond a handful of named projects.

- The strongest support is the implementation experience, with working examples in Compiler Explorer and recognition of the lead author’s established units library.
- The paper also convincingly establishes prior art and alternatives by citing multiple existing approaches and Unicode-based object identifiers already in use.
- The weakest established part is the who-is-affected claim, which relies on production feedback and committee expressions of interest rather than demonstrated broad adoption or standardized quantitative evidence.
- The most glaring omission is the lack of a compelling argument for why an existing, widely used library cannot remain the vehicle for this functionality, since the paper claims uniqueness for mp-units but does not establish why that uniqueness requires standardization.
