Verdict: Adequate (5/14)

The paper offers a partial but uneven case for standardization, with its strongest material concerning alternatives and the absence of useful implementation freedom, while much of the motivating evidence and practical grounding is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest support appears where the paper claims broad implementation agreement, user impact, and interoperability consequences without supplying the underlying data or analysis to substantiate those claims.

- The discussion of prior art and alternatives is the most convincing part of the paper, credibly arguing that existing implementation freedom lacks benefits and that a defect-report approach matches practice.
- The claim that a library solution is merely unergonomic is stated without enough explanation to establish why standardization is necessary rather than convenient.
- The assertions about who is affected and about major implementations’ current behavior are treated as established findings but are not backed by reproducible evidence in the paper.
- The coordination and interoperability argument rests on an external ABI intention rather than on demonstrated standardization need, leaving that part of the case largely unsupported.
