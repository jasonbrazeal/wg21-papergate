Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is narrowly concentrated: it argues clearly that the feature under consideration would be a bad idea, but it does little to establish the broader context, affected users, or the need for committee action. The thinnest areas are all of the practical dimensions—who is harmed, what alternatives exist beyond the cited proposal, and why the standard is the right venue—where the paper offers assertions rather than evidence.

- The paper’s strongest support is its stated rationale for why the proposed change matters, grounded in consistency, teachability, and stylistic concerns.
- It gestures at prior art and alternatives by naming P2806R4 and languages such as Kotlin and Rust, but does not develop this into a comparative or evaluative case.
- The paper does not identify who is affected by the proposal, leaving the practical stakes largely abstract.
- There is no discussion of why standardization is necessary, how the change would interoperate with existing features, why a library solution would not suffice, or whether any implementation experience exists.
