Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper offers some gestures toward motivation and implementability, but most of its case for standardization rests on claims that are not backed by argument, examples, or evidence. The thinnest area is coordination and interoperability, where the paper offers nothing at all.

- The paper’s strongest support is its acknowledgment of unresolved questions about literal rounding under the current standard, which at least points to a real area of uncertainty.
- Its discussion of prior art and alternatives is mostly asserted rather than developed, though it does name a related proposal and a previously agreed-upon design direction.
- The paper gives no account of coordination with other standards bodies, existing practice, or related C++ facilities.
- The most glaring omission is any demonstration that the proposed behavior can realistically be implemented as described, since the only implementation cited does not yet exist.
