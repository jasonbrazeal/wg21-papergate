Verdict: Weak (2/14)

The paper offers only a narrow sliver of support for its own standardization: it points to an in-progress implementation and test patch in libstdc++, but otherwise leaves the motivating problem, the affected constituency, the need for a standardese change rather than a library solution, and the coordination landscape essentially unargued. The thinnest areas are the complete absence of a stated reason why the issue matters and why the standard is the right place to fix it.

- The strongest support is the mention of an implementation and test patch currently in review for libstdc++, which at least gestures toward practical experience.
- The paper claims to resolve a specific LWG issue and include a drive-by fix, but does not establish who is affected by those changes.
- The paper offers no established discussion of prior art, alternatives, or why a library-level solution would be insufficient.
- Most glaringly, the paper never establishes why the underlying problem matters enough to standardize in the first place.
