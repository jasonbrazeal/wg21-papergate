Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper’s strongest support comes from its articulation of the structural costs of current practice and its engagement with prior process rules, but it leans heavily on assertion where it needs evidence of who is affected, why a standard is the right remedy, and how the change would work across the committee’s existing workflows.

- The case that the problem matters is well grounded in concrete descriptions of how late revisions undermine preparation and decision-making.
- The discussion of prior art and alternatives credibly ties the proposal to earlier efforts and existing SD-4 language.
- The thinnest support lies in the repeated reliance on individual experience and unverified claims about affected parties, rather than broader evidence.
- The paper also does not establish that a standard is necessary or that the proposed mechanism is workable in practice, since coordination, implementation experience, and the limits of non-standard fixes remain asserted rather than demonstrated.
