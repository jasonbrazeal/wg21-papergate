Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper gives a partial and selective account of its own standardization case, strongest when describing the performance constraints of its intended users but thin everywhere else. The most serious gaps concern the basic burden of identifying an affected constituency and explaining why the standard, rather than a library or existing practice, is the right vehicle.

- The paper clearly establishes that the constituency it invokes requires zero-overhead, deterministic behavior, especially in low-latency and state-driven code.
- The paper gestures at implementation experience and prior proposals, but does not substantiate either with concrete evidence or detail.
- The paper never identifies who is actually affected in a way that would let reviewers assess the breadth or urgency of the need.
- The paper offers no argument for why the standard must act, why a library would not suffice, or how the feature would coordinate with existing and in-progress standardization work.
