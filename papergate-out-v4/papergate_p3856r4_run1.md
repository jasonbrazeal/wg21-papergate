Verdict: Adequate (4/14)

The paper’s support for its own standardization is uneven: the strongest material concerns prior art and the relationship to P2996, while much of the affirmative case is asserted rather than demonstrated. The thinnest areas are the absence of any identified user population and the lack of concrete implementation experience, which leaves the motivating need largely abstract.

- The paper does establish that reflection-based alternatives exist and that a sample implementation can be sketched using P2996 metafunctions, grounding the discussion in current standardization work.
- The claim that library mandates require implementers to have this functionality is made, but the paper does not show what users are unable to do today or who specifically is affected by the missing query.
- The implementation experience is only claimed; a code snippet and a reference to a fork fall short of evidence that the feature has been built and used in practice.
- The most glaring omission is the complete absence of any affected audience, so the proposal never connects the technical gap to a real need among C++ developers.
