Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper offers solid support on the intellectual landscape and the cost of getting the layering wrong, but much of the case rests on asserted deployment and implementation claims that the document does not itself substantiate. Its thinnest ground is the absence of any argument for why the facility must be standardized rather than delivered as a library, followed by a coordination story that is asserted more than demonstrated.

- The strongest part of the paper is its account of prior art and alternatives, showing the proposal sits between two published bodies of work and documenting a concrete break where one paper’s wording depends on an API another later withdrew.
- The paper also establishes why the layering question matters, since it identifies who controls runtime checking configuration and warns that intervening now is cheaper than reversing a default later.
- The implementation experience is claimed but not established, because the cited shipping tools implement a named-guarantee form rather than the proposal’s distinguishing per-assertion Labels and replaceable handler.
- The most glaring omission is that the paper does not establish why a library would not suffice, leaving the need for standardization itself essentially unargued.
