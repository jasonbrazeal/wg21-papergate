Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only fragmentary support for its own standardization, mostly in a brief discussion of naming alternatives, and leaves every broader precondition—scope, affected users, need for ISO action, interoperability, library viability, and implementation experience—unaddressed. The thinnest areas are the ones that would normally ground a proposal in actual practice or demonstrated need.

- The strongest material offered is a short comparison of naming choices, with a mild preference for *replaceability* because it conveys the feature rather than a set of APIs.
- The paper gestures at community disagreement by quoting a single poll, but does not show who is concretely affected or how the choice would resolve their problem.
- The discussion of prior art is limited to repeating that another paper offered options, without examining why those options failed or what standards-level decision is actually needed.
- The paper provides no showing of implementation experience, no argument for why a library cannot suffice, and no account of coordination with existing facilities, leaving the case for standardization effectively undeveloped.
