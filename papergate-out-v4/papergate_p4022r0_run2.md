Verdict: Weak (3/14, close to Adequate)

The paper offers only a narrow, discussion-shaped rationale for removing one member function and deferring its design, rather than a sustained case that the remaining facility needs standardization now. Its support is thinnest on the basic questions a standards proposal must answer: who is affected, why ISO C++ is the right venue, how the feature interoperates, and whether implementation experience exists.

- The strongest support is the paper’s candid framing that recent committee discussion revealed unresolved design questions about `try_append_range`, especially around partial insertion and naming.
- The paper points, though only briefly, to a related national-body issue and an alternative all-or-nothing formulation as context for the change.
- The clearest omission is any account of who would use the feature or what practical problem standardizing it would solve for them.
- The paper is silent on implementation experience, interoperability, and why a library solution would be insufficient, leaving the standardization need largely unargued.
