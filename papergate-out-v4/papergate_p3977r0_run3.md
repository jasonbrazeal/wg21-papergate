Verdict: Adequate (6/14)

The paper offers some conceptual groundwork for distinguishing non-ignorable contracts from the existing wide/narrow taxonomy, but it does not come close to carrying the full burden of justifying standardization. The strongest material is definitional and comparative, while the case for putting this into the standard—rather than leaving it as a design discussion or library convention—remains largely asserted.

- The paper does its best work in articulating why the current contract classification is incomplete and in connecting the proposed distinctions to prior art and familiar examples like `std::sqrt`.
- The argument that the standard is specifically needed rests on indirect claims about callee-side emission and ABI, but these are asserted rather than developed into a standardization rationale.
- The paper does not identify any actual implementer experience, which leaves the practical case for standardizing the feature almost entirely unsupported.
- Most glaringly, the affected audience is never established, so it is unclear whose problem this standard wording would solve or why the committee should act on it.
