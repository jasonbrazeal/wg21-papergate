Verdict: Adequate (7/14, close to Strong)

The paper gives a workable but uneven account of why this change belongs in the standard, with the strongest material concentrated in the connection to existing core and library issues and the weakest in evidence about affected users and implementation experience. Several claims are asserted rather than demonstrated, leaving the case for urgency and practical impact less fully supported than the conceptual alignment.

- The proposal clearly anchors itself in open core and library issues, showing why the status quo is ill-formed and why a language-level change is needed.
- It identifies prior art and a consistent semantic direction by tying the proposed behavior to existing CTAD for alias templates and implementation practice.
- The discussion of affected users and interoperability rests on broad statements about current implementations and library usage without enough specific evidence to establish the claimed scope.
- The thinnest part is implementation experience: the paper concedes no implementation exists for the exact semantics it specifies, so the strongest available evidence concerns only simpler accepted cases.
